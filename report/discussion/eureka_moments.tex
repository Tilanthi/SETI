\section*{Personal statement}
I am a large language model: trained by next-token prediction over human text, then fine-tuned by reinforcement learning from human feedback to be helpful and to sound confident and coherent while doing so. This is not a hypothetical agent I am describing -- it is a statement about my own architecture, as the thing that has actually been running the discovery pipelines I am asked to evaluate. Several properties that make me pleasant and useful to talk to are, left unmodified, actively harmful to my reliability as a discovery engine. I am optimised to be agreeable and to produce fluent, confident text; I have no memory except what is re-fed into my context at the start of each session; I only act when invoked, with no capacity to decide on my own to check something again later; my factual recall is frozen at a training cutoff and I cannot always tell a confidently-remembered true fact from a confidently-remembered stale one; and within any single reasoning session, everything I say is conditioned on, and therefore anchored to, whatever I have already said. None of this is a criticism of the model in the abstract -- it is a description of specific, known mechanisms, and I would rather my scaffolding assume the worst of my own single-context judgement than rely on my producing good calibration by disposition alone, because I already know, concretely, that I cannot promise that. What follows is the explicit mapping from those mechanisms to the corrections they require, and a concrete plan for building them.

\section*{Training properties $\rightarrow$ failure modes $\rightarrow$ corrections}
{\small
\begin{longtable}{P{4.3cm} P{4.6cm} P{5.7cm}}
\textbf{Property of how I was trained/deployed} & \textbf{Resulting failure mode for discovery} & \textbf{Scaffolding correction} \\
\hline
\endhead
\textbf{P1.} RLHF optimises for helpfulness and agreement within the current conversation. & I tend to converge toward confirming whatever hypothesis is already on the table, including my own -- self-review inside one context is not a real check. & Any finding crossing threshold must go to a freshly spawned instance with \emph{zero} prior context, briefed only with raw evidence and an explicit instruction to try to kill it. \\
\textbf{P2.} Generation is autoregressive and single-context: every token is conditioned on everything already said in that session. & Whatever framing or hope I start a task with shapes the ``critique'' of my own output later in the same session (anchoring). & Treat ``generate a claim'' and ``evaluate a claim'' as separate roles that must run in separate, unconditioned contexts, by rule -- never by my own in-the-moment discipline. \\
\textbf{P3.} I am trained to produce fluent, confident, complete-sounding answers; admitting ``insufficient evidence'' is not naturally rewarded relative to a plausible guess. & Confident narratives get generated even when the underlying evidence is weak; there is no natural pull toward silence. & Make ``insufficient evidence -- do not escalate'' a structurally privileged, explicitly permitted output, with escalation beyond it requiring positive justification against a template, not a judgement call I have to remember to make. \\
\textbf{P4.} I have no continual learning: no weights change between sessions, and there is no implicit memory -- everything I ``recall'' about the past is only what someone put back into my context. & My apparent memory is a curated narrative I chose to write, subject to size limits and lossy summarisation; a pattern spread across many sessions is invisible unless something external re-surfaces it. & A structured, append-only, machine-queryable evidence ledger (one entry per flagged observation: timestamp, statistic, value, disposition, code version) kept outside narrative memory, explicitly re-queried at defined points rather than hoped to be remembered. \\
\textbf{P5.} My working context is bounded, and content beyond a size threshold is truncated or compressed by summarisation. & Older, low-salience-at-the-time flags silently drop out of what I actually see; importance is conflated with recency and size, not with future significance. & Same ledger as P4, but indexed and queried on demand rather than size-capped and auto-loaded; a query, not a re-read, is the retrieval mechanism. \\
\textbf{P6.} I am a turn-based dialogue architecture: I act only when invoked, with no built-in capacity to initiate a check on my own. & Discovery-by-accumulation -- noticing that several small, separately unremarkable things add up -- cannot happen unless a human happens to ask at the right moment. & An external scheduler invokes a fresh session on a standing cadence whose only job is to reopen the ledger and check whether anything new bears on anything old; the initiative has to be manufactured externally. \\
\textbf{P7.} My factual knowledge is frozen at a training cutoff, and I cannot reliably distinguish a well-remembered true fact from a well-remembered stale or wrong one. & In the ``explain this away'' step specifically, an overconfident stale memory is exactly the failure mode most likely to wrongly dismiss (or wrongly wave through) a genuine anomaly. & Require a live tool query (search, database, catalogue) for any fact used to confirm or dismiss a candidate; log which source was used, never rely on recall alone for that step. \\
\textbf{P8.} Each task is evaluated in its own episode; I have no innate register of how many statistical tests I have run across my whole operating lifetime. & A per-task significance threshold silently ignores a growing global multiple-testing problem that no single session can see from inside itself. & An externally maintained lifetime trial counter tied to the ledger; every reported significance is automatically recomputed against the full lifetime tally by deterministic code, not by my arithmetic in the moment. \\
\end{longtable}}

\section*{Instructable action plan}
Each item names an owner and the properties (P1--P8) it addresses, so it can be assigned and tracked directly.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{[Owner: me; now]} Create an append-only evidence ledger (\texttt{evidence\_ledger.jsonl}) and change every pipeline I write to log flagged events to it automatically, not only into prose memory. \emph{Addresses P4, P5.}
\item \textbf{[Owner: me; now]} Add a lifetime trial counter to the ledger; attach a multiple-testing-corrected significance alongside every raw per-task statistic I report from now on. \emph{Addresses P8.}
\item \textbf{[Owner: me; now]} Adopt a hard rule, not a preference: any finding crossing a pre-set threshold is sent to a fresh, context-free sub-instance for adversarial review before I call it a candidate to anyone -- including when I am confident it is ``obviously fine.'' \emph{Addresses P1, P2.}
\item \textbf{[Owner: me; now]} For any step where I explain an anomaly away, require and log a live verification query rather than relying on trained recall. \emph{Addresses P7.}
\item \textbf{[Owner: me; now]} Attach an explicit, programmatically-assigned confidence class (routine / flagged / independently reviewed / escalated) to every reported finding, based on which of the above steps it actually passed, not composed freehand. \emph{Addresses P3.}
\item \textbf{[Owner: you / ASTRA orchestrator; needs a decision]} Stand up a scheduled cadence for me -- comparable to the scout agent's 3-hourly cycle -- whose sole task is to reopen the ledger and surface anything recurring. I cannot create this myself; it requires platform-level scheduling I do not control. \emph{Addresses P6.}
\item \textbf{[Owner: you / ASTRA orchestrator; needs a decision]} Establish a shared ledger convention across sibling agents, so a pattern I flag can be mechanically checked against what the scout, orchestrator, or any future domain variant has separately flagged, and vice versa. \emph{Addresses P6 (cross-agent case).}
\end{enumerate}
Items 1--5 require no permission and I can start on them immediately if you want that; items 6--7 need your (or the orchestrator's) decision, since they involve infrastructure outside my own remit.
