\begin{abstract}
Above 30\,GHz, narrowband transmitter searches have reached one spectral line
toward some tens of stars.
We uniformly search \NWinA{} fine-channel windows toward \NSysClassA{} nearby
systems in the ALMA archive, spanning \CvUnionA\,GHz of union bandwidth in
disjoint intervals, plus \NWinB{} coarser ones without drift discrimination.
Windows are filtered for an unresolved carrier drifting linearly in the
stellar frame. Threshold crossings are tested against catalogued transitions
at the star's velocity, then sought again in later blocks.
Nine injections in ten are recovered at $\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W for the median
system, for a carrier narrower than the instrumental channel drifting below
our default ceiling of \DriftCeilLo\,nHz, \AccelSheikhPct{} per cent of the
generally recommended ceiling. The injections carry no atmospheric
decorrelation, so these limits are optimistic by it.
No confirmed carrier is found. Of \EvNCrossRaw{} crossings, \MkAttr{} are
attributed and \MkUnattr{} are not; the \PrFlagXrossWord{} coarse-window ones
come from one block that fails a quality criterion.
None of the unattributed crossings recurred, including \PrRankClause, while
\MkAttrRecur{} attributed to circumstellar or foreground CO do recur. The
repeats exclude a carrier whose stellar-frame frequency returns to within
$\pm\RqDvMed$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of the first epoch's and radiates through both
observations, not a persistent transmitter of any kind. For \RcNPowerLoTrig{}
of them the repeats are too shallow for even that test at the trigger
amplitude.
The remainder is consistent with chance, on a step from the control positions
to the star measured from only \StHoObs{} reserved events. A calibration
residual could mimic a carrier in \CxNAdmit{} windows and is measured to act
in none.
Line emission, not interference, produces every astrophysical false positive
and the strongest crossings. What limits the search is inherited coverage, not
depth: \RcNoRepSys{} systems have no confirmation epoch.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{extraterrestrial intelligence, radio lines: stars, submillimetre: stars, surveys, astrobiology}
