# Response to the two reports on v3.60 — changes in v3.61

**Manuscript**: `technosignatures_40pc_v3.61.tex` (renamed; see R2-m2), 31 pages,
**now two-column** in the Open Journal of Astrophysics layout, all build gates at
zero, clean regeneration 51/51 generated files byte-identical, arXiv set 46 items
building from an empty directory.

Both reports recommend major revision and both converge on the same two things: the
control-ring null is not exactly exchangeable, and the paper should say so as a
result rather than a caveat; and the mid-analysis change of statistic needs a
justification that does not depend on the window it removed. We have done new work
on both, not only rewriting. The headline additions are:

* a **radial re-analysis of the spatial null** which shows the rank displacement is a
  property of *position in the field*, not of stars (R1-2, R1-1, R2-M2);
* an **empirical false-alarm calibration** measured two independent ways and
  propagated into every per-window probability (R2-M2, R1-1, R1-12);
* **both statistics for all 443 windows**, in the released catalogue and in the text,
  with an algebraic argument for the revision that owes nothing to AU Mic (R2-M1).

Everything quoted below is generated by a script in the release folder; nothing is
hand-typed into the manuscript.

rate, measured instead of assumed*):

| quantity | exchangeable | measured |
|---|---|---|
| P(a position ranks first) | 0.19 % | **0.28 %** (pseudo-stars, held out) |
| | | **1 in 599** (held-out stars, 0.9× exchangeable) |
| expected unattributed stage-1 outliers over 443 windows | 0.86 | **1.2**, P(≥1) = 71 % |
| observed | | 1 |

and the text states that every per-window probability in the paper should be read as
multiplied by 1.4. No disposition changes: no window was ever claimed significant
after the survey-wide correction, and CP−72 2713 is *expected* at the measured rate.

### Major 2 — a physically matched null experiment

Done, on both samples, from stored products; no new search was needed. The pipeline
draws its 512 probes from **one fixed seed** as r = √U(r_in², r_out²) with
r_in = 0.14 θ_PB, r_out = 0.78 θ_PB, so the fractional radii are identical in every
window and every stored control statistic can be paired with the radius at which it
was measured (`radial_null_v361.py`). Three tests, new §5.3.1:

1. **The control level itself depends on radius.** Pooled and normalised by each
   window's own noise scale it runs +0.20 σ at u = 0.24, through a minimum of
   +0.10 σ at mid-annulus, back to +0.14 σ at the outer edge (held out: +0.15,
   +0.07, +0.18). The star sits at a median 0.0035 θ_PB, an order of magnitude
   inside the inner edge, where no probe is.
2. **Radius matching removes most of the departure.** Ranking the star against only
   the 54 innermost probes moves the survey median rank 0.455 → 0.473 and the KS
   statistic 0.066 (p = 0.06) → 0.050 (p = 0.28). The 35 windows whose star happens
   to lie *inside* the annulus have median rank 0.489 against 0.450 for the 345 that
   do not.
3. **Pseudo-stars.** Treating an innermost probe as a pseudo-star and ranking it
   against the other 511 gives, out of sample, median 0.443 (D = 0.073, p = 0.003)
   against 0.429 for the real stars: **positions containing no star are displaced as
   far as stars are.** In the frozen survey the pseudo-stars are uniform (0.506).

The conclusion is that the displacement is instrumental, and Figure 4 now shows both
panels (rank CDFs including the pseudo-stars; the radial profile with the strip that
holds no probes marked). We did not build a new iso-primary-beam extraction: that
needs re-extraction beneath the frozen release, which the release does not carry.
What can be done with stored products has been done, and it answers the question.

## Referee 2

### M1 — the mid-analysis statistic revision

Three things were asked for; all three are done.

1. **Stated in the main text, not only the appendix.** §1 now says the statistic was
   revised once, from a maximum over a 135-position region to the symmetric
   single-position statistic, and that the revision postdates the first flag it
   removes.
2. **An argument that does not depend on the data.** Comparing a region *maximum*
   against single-position controls gives the star a rank floor of
   n_src/(n_src+512) = 135/647 = **21 per cent** instead of 1/513. This is geometry,
   not hindsight. Its consequence is measurable across the whole survey: the
   superseded statistic crosses threshold in **119** windows and exceeds the controls
   in **27** of them, **23 per cent** — its own floor. The symmetric statistic gives
   20 and 4. The revision therefore removes a systematic inflation affecting about
   **seven times as many windows** as the single candidate whose disposition changed.
3. **Both statistics for the whole survey.** The released catalogue now carries
   `star_snr_regionmax` and `stage1_flag_regionmax` for **all 443 windows**, not just
   the seven of Table 8, so any reader can recompute the headline claim under either
   statistic without rerunning anything.

We cannot produce a dated record *predating* the AU Mic flag, and the manuscript
continues to say so plainly rather than claiming otherwise.

### M2 — the non-conservative false-alarm rate

This is now propagated, and diagnosed. See R1-1 and R1-2 above: the tail rate is
measured (1.4× exchangeable), every per-window probability is declared to carry that
factor, the survey expectation becomes 1.2 against 0.86, and the displacement in the
*body* of the distribution is shown to be a radius effect reproduced by positions
containing no star. The phrase "we do not present it as more" now applies only to the
preliminary status of the campaign's size, not to the calibration.

### M3 — Class B prominence

Done: see R1-5. The specific number the report asks for — how many *systems* have a
drift-resolved window — is 59 of 81, now in the abstract and §1.

### M4 — polarisation bounded

Done: see R1-16. The scope box has its own polarisation bullet, and the ×1.41 /
factor-two statements are quantified rather than left open.

### M5 — the disc-host lesson

Done, as an actionable recommendation in §6.4: three of four stage-1 outliers are
circumstellar or foreground molecular emission toward disc hosts because 73 of the 88
searched stars carry a debris-disc keyword; any future archival search inherits the
same list; two remedies are named, running the continuum lane *before* promotion
(which would have caught HD 48370 and both β Pic windows at the first gate) and
attaching an explicit lower prior to disc hosts at the common transition frequencies.

### M6 — the occupancy check is underpowered

Agreed, and now quantified rather than implied. Clustering the tabulated hits into 31
independent crossing groups over 14 systems leaves **72 cross-system pairs with
overlapping coverage**; under a uniform-frequency null they would yield **0.03**
chance coincidences inside the 46-kHz kernel, so **P(≥1) = 3 per cent** and finding
none is consistent with the null. What the null *does* exclude, by the rule of three,
is a line recurring at fixed sky frequency in more than ~4 per cent of overlapping
pairs. The appendix says this in those words.

On satellite constellations: the highest allocations are the second-generation
E-band links, 71–76 GHz space-to-Earth and 81–86 GHz Earth-to-space. **The searched
union begins at 89.6 GHz**, so no operational downlink allocation lies anywhere
inside 89.6–873.1 GHz and a constellation cross-match has nothing to match against.
That is now stated with the reference.

### M7 — the completeness-transfer grid

Done, with the honest accounting. New paragraph in Appendix B: the grid spans Bands
3, 6, 7, 8 and 10, 15.3–977 kHz channels, 45–485 integrations and both arrays. Every
Class A window lies in a band the grid samples; **108 of 126 (86 per cent) lie inside
it on all three axes**; the **18** that do not are extrapolations, 17 in integration
count and one in channel width. The five deepest Class A thresholds are all
interpolated, so the headline sensitivity does not rest on an extrapolation.
