\begin{abstract}
No star within 40\,pc has been searched above about 30\,GHz for a narrowband
transmitter. The ALMA archive reaches it incidentally: dust-disc and
planet-formation observations record a spectrum of the star at every field
centre. Re-reduced uniformly from the raw visibilities, they are searched for
a drifting, unresolved carrier at the stellar position in \NWinA{}
fine-channel windows toward \NSysClassA{} systems, beside a
coarse-channel bound discriminating no carrier. No event met the confirmation
criterion of recurrence. Of the \EvNCrossRaw{} threshold crossings,
\PrFlagXrossWord{} arise in one execution block failing a quality criterion
devised after it was seen, and many of the rest coincide with a catalogued
molecular transition, among them $\beta$~Pictoris's carbon monoxide,
recovered blind. Each unattributed crossing was tested at the predicted
stellar-frame channel and across every later covering window and drift; none
returned, including \PrRankClause. That excludes a carrier holding its
frequency inside the window searched and radiating through both observations,
not a persistent transmitter of any kind; the remainder is consistent with
chance, to the width the control positions allow. Confirmation was possible
for \ScSysIndep{} of the \NSysClassA{} systems and impossible at any power
for the \ScSysNoConf{} observed once or within a single day. Depth is a
recovery fraction through the single-epoch trigger and not a threshold:
injected carriers come back nine times in ten at $\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W for
the median system. Molecular-line confusion and sparse repeat coverage, not
sensitivity, limit a search of this kind.
\end{abstract}
