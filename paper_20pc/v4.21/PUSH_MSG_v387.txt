The false-alarm accounting was mis-conditioned and fixing it changes an
answer. Every expectation the paper quoted (3.2, 3.1, 3.9, 4.4, 4.7, 1.1)
predicted the number of windows in which a no-signal position would rank
first among the 512 controls. The observed quantity is unattributed
stage-1 events, which require the trigger as well as the rank, so none of
those figures was comparable with the 4 observed. Imposing both conditions
(stageonenull_v387.py: per-window probability 1/(N_ctrl+1) if that
window's control maximum reaches the trigger and zero otherwise,
block-resampled 20,000 times with the measured x1.4 tail factor) gives
4 observed against 1.07 expected, P(>=4) = 0.025. That replaces "a modest
population excess" in the abstract, S5.3 and the conclusions.

The same calculation dissolves the reference-class question the paper had
been arguing with itself: only 460 of 1614 windows can reach the trigger
from noise at all, and 391 of those are Class A. That is why every stage-1
event in the survey is Class A. The superseded tab:chance, which existed
to display that ambiguity, is deleted.
