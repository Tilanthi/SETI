\begin{abstract}
Could a nearby star be running a narrowband millimetre transmitter bright
enough that ALMA has already recorded it? Almost nothing above 30\,GHz has
been searched, and never toward stars this close. We therefore searched the
public ALMA archive at each star's position for an unresolved carrier whose
frequency drifts: \NWinA{} spectral windows
toward \NSysClassA{} stellar systems within 40\,pc, with channels fine
enough to follow a drift, covering \DnuA\,GHz of sky frequency in
disconnected intervals from \SurvFreqLoA{} to \SurvFreqHiA\,GHz. Of the
\NCross{} threshold crossings, \NAttributed{} fall on catalogued molecular
transitions, including the $\beta$~Pictoris disc, recovered blind; the rest
are no more numerous than each field's own control positions predict,
though those positions are not a perfect null. Only \NUnattrRankFlagged{}
unattributed crossing is brighter at its star than at any control, and it
is absent when that frequency was next observed. No carrier both passed
the selection and recurred in an independent observation. Sensitivity is
measured by injecting carriers into the calibrated visibilities and
recovering them through the whole selection. The median system's best window
reaches $\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W --- the power at which nine carriers in ten
survive every stage, a recovery fraction rather than a detection probability.
Coarser windows add a supplementary limit on unresolved spectral excess.
What limits such a search is line confusion and how often the archive
revisits a star at one frequency, not sensitivity; and because ALMA chose
these stars, the result describes them, not the solar neighbourhood.
\end{abstract}
