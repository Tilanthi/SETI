\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The public ALMA archive holds thousands of hours of millimetre and
submillimetre observations taken for other reasons, mostly studies of dust
discs and planet formation. Their visibilities allow a spectrum at the
position of the star at the centre of each field, at a resolution and depth
set by the original observer's correlator setup. This paper searches those
spectra for a narrowband transmitter at the star's own position. No such
signal was found.

Searches for \emph{technosignatures} -- detectable imprints of technology,
intended or not \citep{Sheikh2020,SETIreview2025} -- have examined many
thousands of stars over six decades, from the first proposals and targeted
experiments \citep{CocconiMorrison1959,Drake1960} through Project Phoenix and
the programmes reviewed by \citet{Tarter2001} and \citet{Backus2002} to the
present generation of targeted and commensal surveys, several riding back-ends
built for other science
\citep{Zhang2020,Czech2021,FAST3IATLAS2025,LOFARdualsite2023,Choza2024,Morrison2023,Tremblay2024,SRT2025}
and now centred on machine-learning interference rejection
\citep{Ma2023,GLOBULAR2025,AnomalyParkesGBT2025}; yet the last published
accounting of that effort covers only \HaystackFrac{} of the space of
transmitter position, power, frequency, bandwidth, polarisation and repetition
rate \citep{Wright2018}, and essentially all of it lies below
${\sim}30$\,GHz. The search used here has no such interference rejection and
tests the star instead against control positions within its own primary beam,
which a single dish cannot resolve; broadband planetary-scale leakage
\citep{BRaTs2026} is outside it by construction.

Above about 30\,GHz the record is nearly empty, and that is what makes this
frequency range new. \citet{Steffes1994} observed \SbPriorNStarA{} solar-type
stars within \SbPriorDistA\,pc at \SbPriorFreqGHz\,GHz and
\citet{Mauersberger1996} included $\tau$~Cet and $\epsilon$~Eri among
\SbPriorNStarB{} targets at the same frequency, each covering no more than
\SbPriorBandMHz\,MHz of a single line, with no search over drift rate and no
epoch-to-epoch test of persistence. Of ALMA's receiver bands only Band~3 has
been searched for technosignatures \citep{Mason2025}: the
\SbBandOneLoGHz--\SbBandOneHiGHz\,GHz band does hold observations of
\SbBoneNStar{} stars of this sample, but all proprietary until \SbBoneRelYear{}
and all in a continuum mode whose channels are \SbBoneChanRatio{} times coarser
than the narrowest channel searched here, so that coverage cannot carry a
carrier search, and the band above it holds none at all. Every window reported
here was re-reduced and re-searched from the raw archive with the pipeline of
\S\ref{sec:method}.

The band has physical advantages and physical costs. Interstellar dispersion
and scintillation are negligible at these frequencies, so a carrier arrives
essentially undistorted, and a given aperture delivers far more gain per watt
supplied. Against that, receiver system temperatures are higher, the atmosphere
is opaque in places at both ends of the link, and the same high gain narrows
the beam: the \BenchDiam\,m benchmark of \S\ref{sec:excludes} radiates into a
beam \BmArcsec{} arcsec wide at half power, a solid angle of $\BmOmegaSr$\,sr,
so the Earth must lie inside a beam that was deliberately aimed. The limits of
this paper are stated as EIRP and need no transmitter model.

\citet{Mason2025} identified the obstacles that shape the present method: drift
rates scale with frequency, smearing erodes sensitivity unless drift is
searched for, and the molecular line forest raises the confusion
background. The quantity that makes the two surveys commensurable is the
minimum detectable \emph{received} flux $F_{\rm min}=S_{\rm min}\,\delta\nu$,
compared at the channel width each experiment recorded, because $S_{\rm min}$
falls as $\delta\nu^{-1/2}$ while $F_{\rm min}$ rises as $\delta\nu^{1/2}$ and
putting a coarse window on a fine channel arithmetically credits it with a
depth it never reached. On that convention and on native channels throughout,
Mason et~al.\ reach \MasonFluxLo--\MasonFluxHi\,W\,m$^{-2}$ in a
\MasonChanKHz\,kHz channel at a bare $5\sigma$ flux threshold, against
\FluxTrigBest\,W\,m$^{-2}$ in a \FluxChanBestKHz\,kHz channel for the deepest
window that lies outside the line mask -- the only bandwidth this survey
constrains (\S\ref{sec:summary}) -- a factor \FluxNatBestRatio{} deeper, and
\FluxTrigMed\,W\,m$^{-2}$ in a \FluxChanMedKHz\,kHz channel for the median
window, a factor \FluxNatMedRatio{} shallower: per channel this survey is the
shallower of the two except in its finest windows.

Figure~\ref{fig:context}(a) places this survey among published searches, in
minimum detectable EIRP against distance -- a total radiated power and not a
spectral power density, for a carrier narrower than every programme's channel,
so the axis is commensurable. One non-equivalence governs that comparison and
every other in this paper: a limit holds only for a signal narrower than the
native channel that set it, and those channels differ by \LtChanSpanDex{}
orders of magnitude, from \LtChanNarrow{} to \LtChanWide, so a signal
detectable by one programme need not be detectable by another at any
power. Table~\ref{tab:litthresh} puts the \LtNRescaled{} of \LtNProg{}
programmes drawn that quote another threshold onto a common
$\LtCommonSnr\sigma$ basis, which is an arithmetic convention and not a matched
false-alarm probability (Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}); the filled symbols are
those bare thresholds and the open ones the $\mathrm{EIRP}_{90}$ quoted
everywhere else in this paper, the power recovered nine times in ten through
the single-epoch trigger stage and the only symbols on the panel carrying a
measured completeness; and no limit drawn in either panel carries a
confirmation criterion of any kind. The \NgNDecBOnly{} stars drawn as grey
triangles were covered in coarse channels alone, and so constrain an unresolved
excess and not a drifting carrier (Table~\ref{tab:quantities}). Panel~(b) is
the other axis, the native channel width of every window searched here beside
the channel of each comparison programme: this survey is distinguished by its
frequency range and by \NSysClassA{} stellar systems within 40\,pc against
distant stars in \MasonNCal{} calibrator fields, and not by its depth in any
one channel.

The signal sought is an \emph{unresolved spectral carrier}: emission confined
to a few channels, unresolved at the stellar position, allowed to drift as it
must for a transmitter carried on a rotating, orbiting body
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}), and present at more than one epoch. ``Narrowband''
describes the assumed transmitter and not the resolving power of the archived
channels. Recurrence, and not brightness, separates an interesting event from a
detection, a signal of interest needs detailed follow-up \citep{Sheikh2021},
and the archive holds a second separated epoch for some of these systems and
not for others, so the extent that could be searched and the extent that could
be confirmed differ.

A non-detection becomes a quantitative statement only through a stated search
volume \citep{Wright2018}, and through its conversion into an upper bound on
the fraction of stars hosting a transmitter
\citep{Margot2023,EarthDetectingEarth2025} -- which is valid only if the sample
represents the population it is meant to describe. This one does not: the
targets were chosen by other programmes for other purposes, so every
statistical statement here is about an archive-selected sample
(\S\ref{sec:bias}), and \S\ref{sec:sample} states what it is instead.
