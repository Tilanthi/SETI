\begin{abstract}
We searched \NWinA{} fine-channel archival ALMA windows toward
\NSysClassA{} systems within 40\,pc for narrowband, drifting radio carriers.
They cover \CvUnionA\,GHz in \CovNIslands{} separate intervals; \NWinB{}
coarser windows constrain only unresolved emission. Drift-corrected spectra
are compared with control positions in each field. Injection into
\SensNInj{} windows, transferred to the rest, gives a median single-epoch
sensitivity of $\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W equivalent isotropic power at ninety
per cent recovery, for a carrier narrower than the instrumental channel;
recovery is not confirmation. Of \CsNCross{} peaks reaching
\StageNullTrig{} times the scatter at control positions, \MkNCoinc{}
coincide with catalogued molecular lines, \LnAttrSupp{} with further
evidence of line emission. The other \CeObs{} are consistent with chance,
against \CeExp{} expected from control positions ($P=\CeProbNull$),
which does not exclude a smaller added population. No carrier is detected or
confirmed: a non-detection, not an exclusion. Confirmation needs a repeat at
a covered frequency, which \ZcNoRepSys{} systems lack, and tests only a
carrier returning to its predicted frequency. The archive usefully
constrains detectable carriers, but heterogeneous spectral and temporal
coverage, more than depth, limits confirmation.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Technosignatures, Search for extraterrestrial intelligence, Submillimeter astronomy, Radio spectroscopy, Debris disks}
