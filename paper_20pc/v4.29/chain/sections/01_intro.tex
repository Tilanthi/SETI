\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The public ALMA archive holds thousands of hours of millimetre and
submillimetre observations taken for other reasons, mostly studies of dust
discs and planet formation. This paper searches them for a narrowband
transmitter at the position of a star in the field. No such signal was found.

Searches for \emph{technosignatures} -- detectable imprints of technology,
intended or not \citep{Sheikh2020,SETIreview2025} -- have examined many
thousands of stars since the first proposals
\citep{CocconiMorrison1959,Drake1960}, from Project Phoenix
\citep{Tarter2001,Backus2002} to the targeted, commensal and
machine-learning-assisted surveys of today
\citep{Zhang2020,Czech2021,FAST3IATLAS2025,LOFARdualsite2023,Choza2024,Morrison2023,Tremblay2024,SRT2025,Ma2023,GLOBULAR2025,AnomalyParkesGBT2025}.
The last published accounting of that effort covers only \HaystackFrac{} of
the space of transmitter position, power, frequency, bandwidth, polarisation
and repetition rate \citep{Wright2018}. Essentially all of it lies below
${\sim}30$\,GHz, and above that the record is nearly empty.
\citet{Steffes1994} and \citet{Mauersberger1996} observed \SbPriorNStarA{} and
\SbPriorNStarB{} nearby targets at \SbPriorFreqGHz\,GHz, each in no more than
\SbPriorBandMHz\,MHz of a single line, \SbPriorRatio{} times less than the
union searched here, and with no search over drift and no test of
persistence. Of ALMA's receiver bands only Band~3 has been searched
\citep{Mason2025}. The lower Band~1 holds observations of only \SbBoneNStar{}
stars of this sample, proprietary until \SbBoneRelYear{} and in channels too
coarse for a carrier search.

At these frequencies interstellar dispersion and scintillation are negligible
and an aperture delivers far more gain per watt. But system temperatures are
higher, the atmosphere is opaque in places at both ends of the link, and the
narrow beam -- \BmArcsec{} arcsec for the \BenchDiam\,m benchmark of
\S\ref{sec:mmdifferent} -- must be aimed at the Earth deliberately. Limits are
therefore stated as EIRP, which needs no transmitter model. The targets were
chosen by other programmes, so the sample supports no bound on the fraction of
stars hosting a transmitter \citep{Wright2018,Margot2023,EarthDetectingEarth2025}
(\S\ref{sec:bias}).

The signal sought is an \emph{unresolved spectral carrier}: emission confined
to a few channels at the stellar position, present at more than one epoch, and
allowed to drift as it must for a transmitter on a rotating, orbiting body
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}). Broadband planetary-scale leakage \citep{BRaTs2026} is
outside the search by construction. Recurrence, not brightness, separates an
interesting event from a detection \citep{Sheikh2021}. \citet{Mason2025}
identified the obstacles in this band: drift rates that scale with frequency,
and the molecular line forest. The method meets them with a drift search, a
line catalogue against which every event is compared, and a recurrence test in
a separated epoch. In place of interference rejection it compares the star
with control positions inside its own primary beam, which a single dish cannot
resolve, and every window was re-reduced from the raw archive
(\S\ref{sec:method}).

Experiments are compared here on the minimum detectable received flux
$F_{\rm min}=S_{\rm min}\,\delta\nu$ at each one's own channel width, because
$F_{\rm min}$ rises as $\delta\nu^{1/2}$. A limit holds only for a signal
narrower than the channel that set it, and the channels searched here span
\LtChanSpanDex{} orders of magnitude, from \LtChanNarrow{} to \LtChanWide.
Figure~\ref{fig:context} draws each published threshold beside its value
rescaled to a common $\LtCommonSnr\sigma$, an arithmetic convention and not a
matched false-alarm probability (values in \texttt{figures/figure\_data.csv}
of the data archive; \citealt{SIdeposit}). Only this survey's
$\mathrm{EIRP}_{90}$ is calibrated with injected carriers, the step
\citet{Price2020} name as missing from their limits and \citet{Margot2023}
measure but do not fold into theirs. No plotted limit carries a confirmation
criterion. At a bare $\LtCommonSnr\sigma$ threshold \citet{Mason2025} reach a
median \MasonFluxMed\,W\,m$^{-2}$ in a \MasonChanKHz\,kHz channel. The deepest
window here outside the line mask reaches \FluxTrigBest\,W\,m$^{-2}$ in
\FluxChanBestKHz\,kHz, a factor \FluxNatBestRatio{} deeper, and the median
window \FluxTrigMed\,W\,m$^{-2}$ in \FluxChanMedKHz\,kHz, a factor
\FluxNatMedRatio{} shallower. Per channel this survey is therefore the
shallower except in its finest windows. What distinguishes it is its frequency
range and its \NSysClassA{} systems within 40\,pc, against distant stars in
\MasonNCal{} calibrator fields.
