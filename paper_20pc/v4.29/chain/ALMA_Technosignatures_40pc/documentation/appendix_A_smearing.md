# Spectral response, smearing, primary beam and position: the full treatment

This file holds the full text of paragraphs the article summarises, as the article argues them, with every number taken from the same computation the article prints. Where the article points here, the summary it keeps is in the paper and the complete argument is below.

Every frequency axis here – searched spectra, tabulated ranges and crossing frequencies alike – is *topocentric* as delivered by the ALMA correlator, and the search applies no velocity-frame shift. Velocities enter at the vetting stage, where the line mask carries every crossing frequency to the barycentre at its own block's epoch and then to the stellar frame before comparing it with a transition (the section of the paper on “The line mask”). The residual error is then the catalogue radial-velocity uncertainty alone, a few km s^-1 within 40 pc; second-order relativistic terms at the 50 km s^-1 scale are ≲5 kHz at 350 GHz, below the finest native channel searched. The stacking is drift-aligned and inverse-variance weighted: coherent in frequency trajectory, not in electric-field phase.

The native channel width is also the linewidth convention for every EIRP threshold. The statistic is the single-channel flux density after drift correction, so EIRP_trig is the minimum *total* power of a channel-confined carrier, flat in the assumed linewidth below the channel and never a spectral power density. Above the channel it is not a threshold at all: spreading a fixed total power over N channels lowers the statistic as N^0.81, measured on the corresponding equation of the paper as the power recovered nine times in ten, so a feature as wide as the widest line these fields resolve (69 channels) must carry 32× the power of a carrier, where a filter matched to it would pay 8.5× and this search uses no such filter. The Hz-resolution surveys' limits are total powers in the same sense, each the best case of its own channelisation, which makes the axes of the figure of the paper headed “Where this survey sits among published radio technosignature searches (the section of the paper on “Introduction”). **(a)** Minimum detectable EIRP against distance; this survey's deepest window per star – blue, the carrier search; grey, coarse channels only; filled, the bare threshold; open, EIRP_90. **(b)** Native channel width against sky frequency (filled, Class A; open, Class B)”(a) commensurable. Channel widths come from the measurement sets' own CHAN_WIDTH; MS v2 defines no “EFFECTIVE_BANDWIDTH” column, so the quoted channelisations are separations and are not claimed to be noise bandwidths.

ALMA applies online Hanning smoothing by default (Cortes et al. 2026), so neighbouring channels are correlated: for the 0.25/0.5/0.25 kernel ρ_1=2/3=0.667, ρ_2=0.167 and σ_smoothed/σ_raw=0.612, and the peak channel holds 0.50 of an unresolved carrier's flux at a channel centre and 0.42 on a boundary, a median 0.48 over sub-channel phase. *That loss enters the limits exactly once; the thresholds are free of it and the limits are not.* A window's trigger flux is the bare 5σ of its own spectrum, so the *total* power of a channel-confined carrier set against it would be optimistic by ×2.00–×2.36. The injected carriers are not set against it in that way: each is deposited with the correlator's own channel response, at a sub-channel phase drawn afresh, and recovered through the same trigger, which reads the peak channel. The measured completeness therefore already contains the loss, and factorises into it exactly: 3.03 P_trig is ×2.29 for the channel response and ×1.325 for the margin a peak channel needs above the nominal trigger for nine carriers in ten to return. So P_trig, the sensitivity S_min below and the filled symbols of the figure of the paper headed “Where this survey sits among published radio technosignature searches (the section of the paper on “Introduction”). **(a)** Minimum detectable EIRP against distance; this survey's deepest window per star – blue, the carrier search; grey, coarse channels only; filled, the bare threshold; open, EIRP_90. **(b)** Native channel width against sky frequency (filled, Class A; open, Class B)”(a) are nominal throughout, which is what makes them commensurable with other programmes' detection thresholds, while EIRP_90 carries the correction once. That factor is 1/0.4375, the phase-mean peak-channel share a deposited tone keeps, which the injections deposited in every window: 8 per cent short of the exact 0.4741 without online spectral averaging (N=1, 290 Class A windows) and 40 per cent short of 0.726 with it (N≥2, 115). The completeness is conservative by that much – for N=2 the exact factor is 1.83 P_trig, which a refit of the campaign's own tones reproduces – and with the exact response everywhere the median limit would be 1.3×10^15 W, 24 systems still reaching 10^15 W. That correction was never injected, so it is applied to no limit the article prints; a future injection campaign with the averaged response is the fix.

*Intra-integration smearing, which the injections do not model.* Each injected tone is deposited at one frequency per integration, so the campaign carries the channel response and the drift between integrations but not the sweep within one, where η_smear is the mean of that response over the sweep and every limit in this paper is divided by it once; the sweep cannot act inside the transform, these channels being 9×10^4 times wider than the reciprocal of an integration, so it redistributes dwell time rather than destroying coherence. The correction exceeds one per cent in 9, reaching ×1.05; it is measured where the integration length is recorded, for 1054 of the 1663 windows, and *bounded* for the other 609 at 11.6 s, the longest integration delivered anywhere, where none falls below 1.000. Re-scoring the 49 injected windows' own ladders with every tone de-rated by its own η_smear gives a penalty never larger than the one applied, so the correction errs the safe way.

The sensitivity quoted for a window is S_min=5σ/A, where A is the primary-beam response at the star's offset from the phase centre: the noise σ is measured in apparent flux density, the limit is quoted in true flux density, and the two differ by A. Archival fields are pointed at a catalogue position, often decades old, while these are high-proper-motion stars, so the star can sit well off axis. Over the 1663 windows the offset has a median of 0.089” and a maximum of 17.99”; the response A runs from 0.999982 in the median down to 0.2308, falling below 0.9 on 46, every one of those being a J2000 pointing on a fast-moving star rather than an error.

*The response applied is a Gaussian of the full width the observatory documents*, 1.13 λ/D (Cortes et al. 2026), evaluated at each window's *own* dish diameter. The dish matters: the 1058 rows taken on the 1.7× smaller compact-array antennas have a correspondingly wider beam, and a Gaussian forced to D=12 m reproduces the per-window geometry only to 7.7×10^-2 against 8.7×10^-5 for the per-dish form. The blocked-aperture response, which carries the shadow of the secondary and is what casa applies (CASA Team 2022), has a half-power width of 1.15 λ/D on a 12 m antenna and 1.14 λ/D on a 7 m one; the Gaussian used here is that beam to 2 per cent in width, while a Gaussian of the Airy first-null width departs from it in response by up to 30.8 per cent, which is why the documented width is the one adopted. The two forms part company as the offset grows, which is what sets the retention rule of the section of the paper on “The sample”, and *the furthest window of the primary census lies at 0.82 λ/D, where they agree to 6.2 per cent: that is the largest beam-model uncertainty any sensitivity quoted here carries.*

*One star is covered far off axis.* ε Eridani, at 3.22 pc the nearest star the fine-channel search covers in Band 6, has 20 of its 44 windows at the pointing centre and 24 lying 0.75–0.83 λ/D off it, where the response is 0.22–0.29, inside the main lobe: the blocked-aperture response does not reach its first null until 1.36 λ/D. 12 of the 44 fall inside the primary census, 8 of them off axis, and carry its statistics, counted in the table of the paper headed “**The survey ledger, part one: execution blocks and windows.** Each row carries its own number, and a group sums to the row whose number heads it”; the deepest of them is the limit the table of the paper headed “**Per-system sensitivity of the carrier search.** For each of the 61 systems the fine-channel search covers, the EIRP_90 of its best window with its own primary-beam, annual-parallax and smearing retentions applied, ordered by the limit” gives for this star; no window of it reaches the trigger; and the 32 outside the census, 16 of them off axis, are reported with the archival extension. Nor does any block of this star carry an annual-parallax record, so its limit omits a displacement of 0.22” – 0.20 of that block's synthesised beam, from this star's parallax of 310.6 mas, the largest of any the fine-channel search covers – which is smaller than the 0.25 of a beam at which the retained amplitude below was measured, so that measurement bounds the loss instead of being extrapolated to it and the limit is optimistic by at most 9 per cent on this account.

*The correction is applied exactly once.* The offset is computed for every measurement set from its own `FIELD::PHASE_DIR` and the propagated stellar position, and it enters in one place only: S_min=5σ/A closes on all 1663 catalogued rows to 6.7×10^-5 in relative terms, and on the 1653 of them whose A comes from the search's own stored geometry to 6.7×10^-5 – the other 10 having A back-solved from 5σ/S_min and flagged as such. Nothing else needs correcting: the statistic of the corresponding equation of the paper, the control ranks and the attribution are *ratios* formed inside one window, in which A divides out identically.

*The star's apparent direction includes the annual parallax, and the amplitude a compact source retains when that is left out of the assumed phase centre is measured rather than modelled.* Re-extracting the 8 windows of 2 blocks furthest out at the full apparent position – proper motion, parallax and radial velocity where catalogued – leaves each window's noise unchanged to 0.20 per cent and alters no disposition. A carrier injected at the apparent position and recovered from the *same* visibilities at both does return the normalised dirty beam, with no model in it: 0.92 against a predicted 0.89 at 0.25 of a beam, and 0.58 against 0.49 at 0.67. The measurement exceeds the model at the larger displacement, so a limit corrected by the model is shallower than it need be.

*A component of a bound pair does not travel in a straight line, and the propagation assumes it does.* Of the 91 searched positions, 30 have a companion inside 400 arcsec (Mason et al. 2001; Matson et al. 2026) and 13 more have a disturbed astrometric solution with no companion found. Where the pair has a published visual orbit the displacement follows from it, where it has not the projected separation puts a ceiling on the orbital acceleration with the companion's mass taken from Pecaut & Mamajek (2013), and the Hipparcos–Gaia proper-motion anomaly (Kervella et al. 2022) covers 23 of them. The largest displacement anywhere is 0.20 arcsec at UCAC2 20312880, 0.28 of that block's synthesised beam, no crossing lies more than 0.001 of a beam from its predicted position and the deepest limit of any system moves by at most 16 per cent – except for Barta 161 12, HD 155555C and LHS 1140, whose solutions are disturbed with no companion located and no Hipparcos epoch to measure one from, so that the 24 windows of those 3 stars carry limits optimistic by an unmeasured amount.
