# Spatial normalisation and exchangeability: the full treatment

This file holds the full text of paragraphs the article summarises, as the article argues them, with every number taken from the same computation the article prints. Where the article points here, the summary it keeps is in the paper and the complete argument is below.

The star lies at the phase centre, inside the control annulus, and the displacement is real: the star carries more above-threshold cells than a control position at every level below the trigger. The chance expectation of the section of the paper on “The unattributed count is what the control positions predict” is not stepped by it, since the ratio at the trigger is formed from the cells under test, and what carrying it in would do is measured in the section of the paper on “The false-alarm expectation and the control ensemble”. *It is not a primary-beam effect.* Spectra are extracted from phase-rotated visibilities in apparent flux density and are never corrected for the primary-beam response (the section of the paper on “Spectral response, frequency conventions and the primary-beam response”), so the familiar rise of image noise with distance from the pointing centre cannot operate here; were it operating it would make the control probes noisier than the star and place the stellar rank *above* one half, the opposite of what is measured. The control ensemble does show a weak rise of its own level with radius, and each probe is therefore standardised within its own radius bin, with no free parameter.

*A calibration error that varied with frequency would explain the displacement, and it is one mechanism considered here.* An error identical in every channel is removed exactly by the per-integration median along frequency; a channel-dependent one is not, and two ordinary errors are channel-dependent: a residual in the bandpass, and a system temperature measured in the coarse correlator mode and interpolated onto the science channels, which near a telluric line leaves a narrow feature behind (Hunter et al. 2018). Both multiply the continuum, so both act at the star and not on empty sky, both stand still in topocentric frequency and both are coherent between integrations: they would stack at near-zero drift and put the stellar rank *below* one half, the sign observed. *Their amplitude is too small, measured where the search looks.* Such an error multiplies the continuum at the extraction position and must beat the noise of the de-drifted, block-combined spectrum, since a coherent residual stacks as a carrier does, so a fractional error ε depositing ε S_c at the star needs ε=5σ/S_c to reach the trigger. That continuum is measured for each window in its own execution block, as the time average of the window's own retained spectra, over 1557 of the 1663 windows, and over 214 blocks it agrees with independent continuum images of the same fields with no offset between the two (-0.001 mJy). No window requires as little as the 5–20 per cent a coarse system temperature is documented to leave at an ozone line – the smallest requirement anywhere is 21.4 per cent and the median 1073, and over the 49 crossings with a measured continuum the least demanding needs 63 per cent – while the bandpass branch fails by amplitude everywhere, needing 21× the 1 per cent variation the observatory quotes for its worst bands (ALMA Science Portal 2026; Kameno 2014); and of either sample 0 crossing lies within 62.5 MHz, two of the 31.25 MHz channels on which the system temperature is measured, of a catalogued atmospheric transition (the section of the paper on “Radio-frequency interference screening”). The other mechanism considered, an additive visibility error imaging at the phase centre, is tested by splitting the windows at one synthesised beam from the phase centre: the excess is not confined to the inner set, it appears only in the 7 m-array data, and its cause is not identified. The shared noise scale raises one further question, and the same arithmetic settles it: over the 1614 of the 1663 census windows holding none of the adopted crossings, the stellar statistic divided by the median of the 512 control statistics is exactly the ratio of the two noises, its median 1.002 (0.997–1.005), so the two scales agree.

The statistic and the mask are set on the searched sample and cannot also calibrate themselves, so a further sample is separated by an identifier rule: a block is held out if and only if sha256(uid)_[:8] mod 5 = 0, subject to two guards that use identifiers alone – a block on a named list stays in the survey, and no system loses every block. That this sample is independent of the choice of statistic and of mask is an assertion of ours and not something a reader can check. Of 484 blocks processed, 77 are reserved, 15.9 per cent of them, carrying 315 windows toward 36 stars, with no star and no system lost from the headline sample. The rule hashes the execution block, and ALMA's identifier carries the observing session above it, so 64 per cent of the reserved set – 49 of the 77 blocks, across 40 sessions – shares calibrators, weather and correlator setup with a searched block, where a session-level hash would have been the stronger choice; every quantity drawn from this sample is therefore given twice, over the whole reserved set and over the 28 blocks that share no session with it. One further quantity is calibrated on those blocks and on nothing else, the radial behaviour of the control ensemble: the standardised level runs +0.14, +0.11, +0.12, +0.13 across the annulus, a mean per-window slope of -0.027±0.024, small and of the wrong sign to account for the displacement.
