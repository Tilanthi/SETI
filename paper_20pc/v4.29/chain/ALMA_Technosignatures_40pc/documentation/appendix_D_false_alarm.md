# False alarms and chance expectations: the full treatment

This file holds the full text of paragraphs the article summarises, as the article argues them, with every number taken from the same computation the article prints. Where the article points here, the summary it keeps is in the paper and the complete argument is below.

This appendix defines the chance expectation for the unattributed crossings, gives the resampling the observed count is compared through, and states what the control ensemble that calibrates it can and cannot support.

*One attribution set serves both sides of the comparison*, the set of the section of the paper on “Threshold crossings and how each was disposed of”: the mask of the section of the paper on “The line mask” within ±50 km s^-1 in the star's own rest frame, so a control maximum inside it is attributed exactly as a crossing there is.

Each of the 405 Class A windows of the primary census contributes two quantities measured on itself. The fraction of its own 512 control positions that reaches the trigger is the probability that a position in that field does so – no cell count enters it and no form is assumed for the tail – and summed over the windows 36.5 are expected to trigger by chance. How many of those would be attributed is measured on the channels the control maxima land on. The channel of a maximum exists only for the 8 control positions per window that retain a dynamic spectrum (the section of the paper on “The two crossings that lead their own control fields”), so the share is measured on the 3 192 such positions of 399 of the 405 windows, which carry 98.6 per cent of the expectation above. The full ensemble holds 18 663 exceedances, which divided by 512 is the 36.5 above; the 8 are indistinguishable from a uniform draw from their window's own 512 by rank (P=0.88) and their exceedance rate from the full ensemble's by +0.1 standard deviations. 92 of 289 exceedances fall inside the mask, carried to the same star's rest frame through the same two steps and against the same transition list as a cell at the stellar position, so the attribution takes 32 per cent of the expectation where a maximum free to land anywhere on its own grid would take 23 per cent: the 15 line-tuned windows lie wholly inside the mask, and a uniform landing would under-attribute them. The atmospheric screen of the section of the paper on “Radio-frequency interference screening” is then applied to these same positions, on the same radius and in the same topocentric frame as to the crossings, and takes 9 of the 197 exceedances the mask leaves. That leaves 23.8 expected unattributed Class A crossings against 26 observed, as a sum over rows with nothing carried at an assumed rate. Both sides are read over the whole primary beam the section of the paper on “The de-drifted search statistic” defines, with each block on its own antenna geometry; the narrower field of the section of the paper on “The unattributed count is what the control positions predict” disagrees with it on 5 of the 405 windows and in both directions: the whole beam holds a crossing the narrower field does not toward HR 1010 and HD 139084 B, both attributed to carbon monoxide, and the narrower field one the whole beam does not toward τ Cet and HR 1010 (twice). System by system, each system's expectation formed from its own control positions, 0 of the 61 systems fall below one chance in twenty against 3 expected. A further 14 searched Class A windows lie outside the comparison because the deposit does not tabulate them, together with the two unattributed crossings toward HD 14055 they hold, and enter neither side.

*A count is compared with the distribution of counts chance delivers, and not with a range on its own mean.* A rate is drawn from 200 000 resamplings – each window's rate redrawn from its own 512 probes, the attributed share from its own 289 exceedances, and the execution blocks resampled because windows share them and are not independent trials – and the count scatters about that rate. A count of 26 or more then arises with probability 0.36 at the control positions alone, a probability conditional on the control-position model set out here and not on a null distribution established independently of it. The same resampling bounds what the comparison could have missed: added to each resampled rate, 12 further crossings still leave a count as low as 26 credible at 0.058, and one more does not, at 0.042 against the 5 per cent level. The uncertainty in 23.8 is carried inside that bound by the same resampling.

*The one assumption the searched data cannot test is that a star behaves like a control position in its own field.* In the census the star carries 1.108 times as many above-threshold cells as a control position of the same field, measured in the band immediately below the trigger, 4≤ T<5, with 1.053–1.163 allowed at 95 per cent when the execution blocks are resampled; no cell under test enters it, a band count being the difference of two cumulative counts. Calibrating the stellar rate against that ratio instead, the sensitivity test of the section of the paper on “The unattributed count is what the control positions predict”, lowers the bound to 9; the control positions' own rate is adopted as the smaller of the two and the more demanding of the count under test. The requirement runs the other way in any case – the 26 observed would stand above chance at the 5 per cent level only if stars reached the trigger at 0.76 times a control position's rate or less, and every ratio measured here is an excess instead. The reserved blocks measure the same quantity where the search did not reach: their stars trigger in 5 of 62 fine-channel windows against 6.5 predicted, a ratio of 0.77 – though every one of those events lies in a block that shares an observing session with a searched one, and the 28 reserved blocks that share none hold one trigger event between them, so they neither confirm the ratio nor contradict it (the section of the paper on “The reserved hold-out, and what it calibrates”).

The comparison assumes each block's control ensemble behaves like noise, and one block makes that false: every one of its 512 probes exceeds the trigger, the smallest at 6.6. A criterion is therefore applied to every block and not to the one that prompted it, built from the median of a window's own control ensemble, which separates a field with a source in it from a field that is no null at all: a block is judged on that median divided by the median over the windows of its own class, since a maximum over a larger channel×drift grid sits higher, and the same statistic flags 5 of the 1663 windows individually at twice the survey median, 5.5 against 2.74. *Neither form was fixed in advance*: the condition was noticed in one block and the criterion written afterwards, so it is post hoc, and what makes it trustworthy is how little the position of the line matters – the failing block sits at 4.1 and the next most extreme at 1.12, a factor 3.7 apart, and over 41 thresholds spanning that interval the same single block is selected every time. Of the 407 primary-census blocks, 1 fails; its 4 windows are all coarse-channel and hold the survey's entire Class B crossing population, 0 of the 51 threshold crossings, which appear in the crossing ledger with their block named and enter no count elsewhere.

Ranking the star against 512 control positions of the same field decides which windows deserve examination, but 1/(512+1)=1.95×10^-3 is the *resolution* of the ensemble – the smallest rank it can express – and not the probability that noise produced the window. A survey of 1663 windows would need a rank 65 times finer, and correlation makes it coarser still: controls inside the synthesised beam are not independent, so an annulus resolves no better than 0.023–0.032 however many probes are drawn into it. That floor is the design's central limitation: no single window here can be significant on the rank alone, which is why a crossing is followed up by its recurrence and not by its rank. The rank distribution is weak evidence in either direction for the same reason, a shift smaller than about 0.042 being invisible in a sample of this size.

The searched cells counted in the section of the paper on “From a crossing to a confirmed signal” are not independent trials either: channel smoothing and a drift grid stepped twice per channel correlate neighbouring ones, which alone would leave 0.19 of them independent, while fitting each window's effective count against its own control ensemble returns 0.81 and those ensembles' maxima sit at 0.996 of the prediction for the maximum of the full searched count treated as independent and Gaussian. Nothing turns on which is right, because the expectation above is summed from measured rates and not from a cell count: by the cell-count route the number reaching the trigger is 36.6 against 36.5, 2 per cent away. A Gaussian tail over every searched cell predicts 37.9 stellar exceedances against the 51 found; the attribution, the failing block, the archival tail and the atmospheric screen account for the difference.

The widened recurrence window of the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings” is counted in the unit it is measured in. A window covering more than one crossing is one window and one measurement, since the statistic read is that window's own maximum and does not depend on which crossing it is referred to: the 194 pairings of a crossing with a covering window are 113 distinct windows, 47 covering more than one. Of those, 14 hold a statistic above the trigger against 11.0 expected at P=0.21, or 10.0 at P=0.12 when each window's own control positions supply the rate in place of its cell count; the larger probability is quoted. 8 are toward ALMA J153702653-33192492 and HD 14055 alone, and over the other 62 windows the comparison is 6 against 4.5 at P=0.30; those two hold 51 of the windows and 6.5 of the expectation, and the section of the paper on “HD 14055 and ALMA J153702653-33192492” splits the departure between them.

The recurrence test of the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings” reads one channel at one drift rate, and the cell is registered between two epochs to 0.75 of a channel in every window – half a channel for the prediction, half for the discovery channel it is referred to, and smaller terms for the drift, the systemic velocity and the ephemeris – while the frame correction, the one term that is large in channels rather than small, moves it by up to 116 channels, and the stellar-frame centroid of a real recurring line sits a median 0.06 km s^-1 from the predicted cell over 61 block readings of the line-attributed recoveries. Each reading was therefore re-formed from the worst cell within 0.45 km s^-1 of the predicted one with the threshold raised to 5.30 so that the wider reading keeps the single cell's own false-alarm probability, it was pinned at every anchor across the span the discovery track covers, which moves it by at most 14.5 km s^-1, and the bar itself was reset to 4.0 to hold the chance of one false recovery across all 296 readings at 1 per cent – and no disposition rests on any of the three, each crossing's own tolerance, cell count, threshold and resulting bound accompanying this paper as data. What the recoveries establish about the registration of a single cell rests on the 3 recovery readings of HD 285968 alone, whose line is confined to one channel and which register to 0.06 of a channel, 0.038 km s^-1; the rest test the frame correction only to the width of a resolved line, up to 69 channels.

Two counts of the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings” are counts of different objects. The 18 above-trigger readings are window readings, one for each pairing of an unattributed crossing with a window that covers its frequency in another block – 194 such pairings over the 28 crossings a later catalogued block covers, row 2.3.3 of `catalogues/count_accounting.csv` – and they fall in 14 windows of 12 blocks, so a pair whose two members are both unattributed crossings is read twice: 6 of the readings are such a second reading, representing 12 distinct pairs, of which 2 lie outside the interval the pair test admits – the median covering window's half-width, by up to 2105 km s^-1 – and 10 remain, which is the set the table of the paper headed “**Can two crossings be one carrier?** The 9 pairs toward one star with an unattributed member (the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings”); the note below defines the columns and the verdicts” is formed over, the other 1 of the 10 wanting a discovery spectrum or a fitted drift. The accounting for each pair is in the data archive (White & Dey 2026), `candidates/drift_pair_accounting.csv`. Of the above-trigger windows the strongest reaches 5.95, and the nearest lies 106 km s^-1 from its crossing's frequency, the median 440. A catalogued window covers 28 of the 28 crossings that carry an amplitude bound and spans the orbital excursion of the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings” in 24; for 4, toward β Pic, HD 110411 and HD 69830, the widest covering window is narrower. The observatory's own line-of-sight acceleration is removed from both fitted drifts before they are compared, at most 40 Hz s^-1 here. Of the 5 pairs whose epochs are close enough together for the predicted offset to be tighter than the searched window, every one fails a drift changing at a constant rate, by 3.6 times that resolution in the mildest case; the offset (1)/(2)(ν̇_1+ν̇_2)Δ t such a trajectory predicts has no freedom left once both rates and the interval are fixed, and the one pair the searched grid itself separates is HD 14055's, whose crossings lie 717 km s^-1 apart in blocks 2.2 hours apart, a single carrier having had to drift at 25 times the grid's ceiling between them. Nothing measured here bounds the curvature in between, the ceiling bounding the line-of-sight component of a carrier's acceleration and not how fast it can turn over; the velocity excursion *is* bounded, to within 1.7 times the circular amplitude over every eccentricity and orientation, which is why the widened window of the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings” can be set against that excursion and a reversal time cannot. The control row is a line that does not drift: the CO(2–1) line of HD 285968 is recovered at one stellar-frame frequency 5.0 days apart, within 0.6 of the comparison's own resolution of the prediction, so between these two blocks it resolves a mean drift to 51 Hz s^-1: a grid step carried over the gap, which is why a longer gap does not sharpen it. For the 9 pairs it cannot separate – 5 of them resolved and failed by the constant-acceleration comparison – nothing measurable tells one carrier from two.

β Pictoris is the closest case, and it is the one the comparison cannot dismiss; its orbit is argued here and nowhere else, the methanol identification of its two crossings being excluded in the section of the paper on “The line mask”. They sit at 241.7739 and 241.8594 GHz, 106 km s^-1 apart in the star's frame, in blocks 24.4 hours apart, with both fitted drifts negative (-1611 and -679 Hz s^-1), so a single carrier would have had to rise in frequency between the epochs and under constant acceleration misses by 132 times that resolution – but the mean acceleration it needs, 1.21 m s^-2, is inside the 3.6 m s^-2 ceiling. The pair fixes three numbers, the acceleration at each epoch and the frequency offset between them, and a circular orbit about a star of 1.75 M_⊙ (Crifo et al. 1997) has three free parameters: one orbit reproduces all three at once, at 1.54 d in period, 16.5^° in inclination and 0.0314 au in radius, eccentric orbits fit up to e of about 0.27 before the periastron acceleration leaves the ceiling, and the pair is therefore not separable.

The drift trials of a window run from -ν̇_max in equal steps of half a native channel across the retained track, and the last trial is the first at or beyond +ν̇_max, so the interval searched is asymmetric by up to one step and the ceilings themselves, computed window by window from the corresponding equation of the paper, reproduce it to 1×10^-4 over all 405 Class A windows. Each window's grid is recovered from two quantities that enter no common calculation – the trial count recorded for it and the channel width and retained span its own crossing record carries – and the two agree on 45 of the 52 crossings that carry a fitted drift, on every one of which the fitted rate is a trial of that grid exactly; 2 lie on an outermost trial (the table of the paper headed “The crossings whose fitted drift lies on an outermost trial of their window's own grid”), toward HD 161868, TRAPPIST-1, and are reported as lower bounds. 0 are among the 28 crossings the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings” bounds; the other 2 carry no amplitude bound, lying in the reserved hold-out, in the quality-failing block and in the one crossing with no reading of its own, which is why this table reaches above that section's range. The remaining 2 are in archival-tail windows for which no grid is recorded anywhere.

Every control rate is measured on the data its window's statistic is read from, the 28 Class A windows with recombined in-beam fields included; on the first extraction they would carry 504 fewer control exceedances and the expectation would be 0.7 lower. The reserved blocks trigger in 5 of 62 windows against 6.5 predicted, 4 of them in blocks sharing a session with a searched one, and the 28 sharing none hold one trigger event against 1.9 expected, so they neither confirm nor contradict the ratio (the section of the paper on “The reserved hold-out, and what it calibrates”).
