# Per-system limits: the full treatment

This file holds the full text of paragraphs the article summarises, as the article argues them, with every number taken from the same computation the article prints. Where the article points here, the summary it keeps is in the paper and the complete argument is below.

The limit the table of the paper headed “**Per-system sensitivity of the carrier search.** For each of the 61 systems the fine-channel search covers, the EIRP_90 of its best window with its own primary-beam, annual-parallax and smearing retentions applied, ordered by the limit” quotes for a system is that of its best window, so a system whose coverage is wide is not penalised for the shallowest window in it; the window-by-window distribution is the per-window result of the section of the paper on “Sensitivity: the power at which a carrier would have crossed the threshold”. Each row is one system, a gravitationally bound pair appearing under its primary's name. A window inside the attribution mask constrains nothing (the section of the paper on “From a crossing to a confirmed signal”) and cannot be a best window: a double dagger marks a row keeping less than half its channels, and HIP 10679, whose 2 windows are all inside it, is tabulated with dashes. Beside each limit the table gives that window's centre frequency ν and native channel width Δν, the system's merged coverage Δν_cov, the percentage *dec.* by which atmospheric decorrelation leaves the limit optimistic, and whether two blocks of different observing sessions cover a common frequency more than a day (d) or more than a year (yr) apart, over the primary census together with the 142 blocks the recurrence test of the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings” reads, of which 38 lie outside the census – 6 in the archival extension, 18 in the reserved hold-out and 14 in the epoch-extension campaign. The frequency at which a system's best limit falls is set by what the archive holds toward it and not by any property of the star, and for 21 of the 61 systems no sensitivity would have been enough.

*Because a system's limit is its best window, a minimum over several estimates could favour windows whose own completeness factor falls below the class average*: drawing each window's factor from the 49 measured ones instead, over 4 000 trials, shifts the per-system median by 5.6 per cent of the adopted 3.03. A dagger marks the 6 of the 60 systems whose own best window was one of the 49 injected into; the other 54 limits are transferred from the class average and carry the dispersion observed between injected windows, ×0.80 to ×1.58, and for the 8 systems whose best window lies outside the injected range in band, channel width, array configuration or integration time that dispersion is itself transferred. A dagger marks where the class factor was calibrated by injection, not a different convention: every limit, daggered or not, carries the one class factor.
