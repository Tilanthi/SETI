# The molecular-line mask: the full treatment

This file holds the full text of paragraphs the article summarises, as the article argues them, with every number taken from the same computation the article prints. Where the article points here, the summary it keeps is in the paper and the complete argument is below.

A crossing that falls on a known molecular transition has an astrophysical explanation to hand, and the mask exists to say which crossings those are, reporting the frequency coincidence and the judgement about the coinciding species separately.

*The list, and when it was made.* The mask holds every transition of the 12 species these spectral setups cover that are detected in discs or molecular clouds – the three CO isotopologues, HCN, HCO^+, CS, CN, SiO, H_2CO, SO, [C i] and hydrogen recombination – that falls inside one of the 46 searched frequency intervals with an upper-state energy below 150 K. That rule is the whole of it, and it admits 111 transitions (the table of the paper headed “The line mask, by species: the number N of transitions, the frequency range they span inside the searched intervals and their upper-state energies”). No strength cut is applied across species: any threshold admitting HCN's strong lines would discard the brightest lines in these fields. Within a species the main hyperfine components are kept and the satellites dropped. The list is a single Splatalogue query (Remijan et al. 2007) of CDMS and JPL (Pickett et al. 1998; Müller et al. 2005) made on 2026 October 6, and the query is dated and repeatable, so the mask itself can be rebuilt.

*The species are the ones the observers went to the telescope for, and that is a limitation.* The spectral setups are tuned to them: 39 per cent of the 405 Class A windows are centred within ±50 km s^-1 of a transition of the list and 88 per cent contain one somewhere, against 1–8 per cent for the same windows displaced bodily. The list is therefore set by the science goals of the programmes that took the data and not by anything in this search, and *unattributed* means unattributed against it. A wider list measures what that costs: every transition below 150 K of all 225 species with a published interstellar or circumstellar detection (Pickett et al. 1998; Müller et al. 2005) gives 24 976 transitions inside the searched intervals, covering 98 per cent of the Class A union against 6.4 per cent for the list adopted here, and it attributes every one of the 28 unattributed crossings together with 100 per cent of the control positions' own exceedances, so it empties the category for star and control alike; narrowed to ±1 km s^-1 it would attribute 3 of the 28 and the unattributed count remains what the control positions predict, 23 against 28.3 expected. The nearest transition of that wider list is reported beside every unattributed crossing in the accompanying data – and at a density where 98 per cent of the band lies within 50 km s^-1 of something, proximity is not an identification.

*What an identification would look like, on the pair that comes closest to one.* The 2 unattributed crossings of β Pictoris lie 6.7 and 7.1 MHz above components of the methanol 5_K→4_K ladder (5_-1→4_-1 E and 5_-3→4_-3 E, E_u=40 and 98 K, at frequencies on which CDMS and JPL agree), 8.3 and 8.8 km s^-1 in the star's frame: offsets agreeing with each other to 0.48 km s^-1, which is the configuration a real line identification produces and which arises by chance in 0.7 per cent of pairs drawn uniformly in the window they were found in. Four measurements decide against it, all in the one window that covers the whole ladder in both blocks, 241.576–242.007 GHz in the stellar frame. The brightest member of the complex, 5_0→4_0 A^+ at 241791.352 MHz, would reach T_⋆≥5.7 at any excitation temperature and would therefore have been the window maximum, where the maximum over the whole channel–drift grid is the crossing itself. The weaker of the two claimed components cannot exceed 0.65 of the stronger at any temperature and is observed at 0.94 of it. Both fitted drifts are 71 and 30 steps of their own grid from the zero a transition at rest requires, each track crossing 35.7 and 15.2 channels inside its own block. And the two are not in one block: the first is in `Xd9668b_X3a90` and the second in `Xd9668b_Xa9df`, 24.4 h apart, and each is absent from the other, where a persistent line would have been recovered at 5.7 and 4.8σ and the statistic at that cell reaches only 2.45 and 2.88 over every drift of the grid (the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings”). A real pair of components would have stood above the trigger in both blocks; one in each and neither in both is what a noise pair gives. Where this system's own molecular gas is seen, in 10 blocks, it registers to 0.53 km s^-1 of rest.

*Every crossing is read in the local standard of rest as well, where interstellar gas is at rest, and that second reading establishes which coincidences are interstellar.* Of the 25 stars carrying crossings, the composite CO(1→0) survey of Dame & Thaddeus (2022); Dame et al. (2001) covers 12 and holds emission toward 6 of them, while toward every other star, including the 13 outside its footprint, the all-sky Planck CO map (Planck Collaboration et al. 2016) stays below 0.178 K km s^-1. Toward ALMA J153702653-33192492, HD 139084 B, HD 285968 and HD 48370 the CO spectrum is non-zero at the crossing's own LSR velocity, within 3 km s^-1 of its own peak and at 0.23 K or brighter against a 0.18 K channel, so 6 of the 23 coincidences are interstellar gas and not circumstellar. Two are worth naming. ALMA J153702653-33192492 is a serendipitous field star toward the Lupus clouds, with the clouds far behind it, and its CO(2→1) crossing lies 0.5 km s^-1 from a 6.6 K km s^-1 cloud line at +4.9 km s^-1: a molecular sightline and not a disc, on which the wider chemistry a cloud carries is a real limitation, 1 of the species such a sightline would show reaching one of its crossings within the mask's own half-width, OCS at 15 km s^-1. HD 48370 is the case where the two readings part: its coincidence lies -17.8 km s^-1 from the star's own CO(2→1) while in the local standard of rest it sits at +23.9 km s^-1, on a catalogued cloud line peaking at +21.8, so the offset follows the interstellar gas and not the star and that window's extended emission is a cloud and not a resolved disc.

*The coincidences.* Of the 49 quality-passing census crossings, 23 fall within ±50 km s^-1 of a transition of the list in their own star's frame. The list holds no species whose coincidences are reported and not acted on, so those are also the 23 attributions and the two counts are one count. The three on sulphur monoxide (CD-57 1054, CP-72 2713 and HD 161868, at -1.7, -12.3 and -48.0 km s^-1 from transitions of upper-state energy 21–87 K) rest on the coincidence alone. Interstellar sulphur monoxide is a measurement rather than a preference: in the local standard of rest the three offsets are -3.5, -12.8 and -53.4 km s^-1, so for one of them the coincidence does not survive the change of frame at all, 2 lie outside the composite survey's footprint on sightlines carrying at most 0.10 K km s^-1 of integrated CO against the 1.0 K km s^-1 a detection needs, and the third, HD 161868, is a molecular sightline whose crossing nevertheless sits 53 km s^-1 from the emission, where this survey's own cloud test allows 3 km s^-1. Each of the three windows also covers CO(3→2) and in each the maximum over the whole channel–drift grid is the crossing itself, so no CO(3→2) feature above the trigger accompanies any of them – and carbon monoxide, not sulphur monoxide, is what a cloud shows first. The reserved hold-out is treated identically, leaving 1 attributed and 4 unattributed there (the section of the paper on “The reserved hold-out and the control beyond 40 pc”).

*The half-width, and what it costs.* A one-channel test is too tight for circumstellar gas, whose emission is shifted by many channels: the width follows from Keplerian motion where these molecules survive, ≲13 km s^-1 beyond 10 au, and is ±50 km s^-1. At that width the mask removes 3.05 GHz of the 47.7 GHz Class A union, 6.4 per cent of it, and 5.04 GHz of 118.1 GHz over both classes together, 4.3 per cent, so the cost falls on the primary experiment, because its windows are the line-tuned ones. Attributing on either frame instead would mask the union of two tubes separated by the star's LSR velocity, 3.72 GHz or 7.8 per cent of the Class A union – 22 per cent more bandwidth for no further attribution, which is the measurement behind deciding in one frame.

*The labelled crossings are tested for recurrence too* (the section of the paper on “Recurrence, applied across the unattributed crossings”). For the 6 not recovered a repeat window's own largest statistic bounds the predicted cell from above and the ledger prints the row as a bound, at T_rep<4.6 and T_rep<4.2 against the T_pers=7.9 and T_pers=6.2 real carbon monoxide at the discovery flux density would have given. One of those two attributions rests on a velocity rather than on the recurrence test, the sightline carrying catalogued interstellar emission 0.5 km s^-1 from the crossing's own LSR velocity, and its repeat blocks show where that gas lies rather than whether it can be recovered: in every one of them between 192 and 491 of the 512 control positions stand above the star, so the gas is bright and it is not at the star.

*Attribution is decided in the star's own rest frame, and the sign is not the usual one.* A crossing's frequency is carried from the topocentric value to the barycentre using its own block's pointing and mid-time, and then to the stellar rest frame using the star's systemic velocity. That frame, and no other, is where a coincidence is judged, a tolerance applied in the observed frame spending most of its budget on the Earth's motion. The 4 HD 285968 epochs show the transform working in the only way that can be checked: 12 km s^-1 apart as observed, they agree to 0.03 km s^-1 in the star's frame, a twentieth of one of their channels. Three are census crossings and the fourth is a hold-out block. The observatory set all four executions to one source-frame frequency, so the agreement bounds the part of the correction that changes with epoch, which is the large part, and not a constant error in the systemic velocity. Throughout, Δ v_⋆ = c (ν_⋆-ν_0)/ν_0, so a positive offset means the crossing lies *above* the transition in frequency, the opposite sign to the velocity convention of a spectral line.
