\title{An Archival ALMA Search for Unresolved Spectral-Carrier
Technosignatures toward \NSysClassA{} Stellar Systems within 40\,pc
at \SurvFreqLoA--\SurvFreqHiA\,GHz}

\author{Glenn J. White\altaffilmark{1,2} and Robin Dey\altaffilmark{3}}
\altaffiltext{1}{School of Physical Sciences, The Open University, Walton Hall, Milton Keynes, MK7 6AA, UK; glenn.white@open.ac.uk}
\altaffiltext{2}{RAL Space, STFC Rutherford Appleton Laboratory, Harwell Campus, Didcot, OX11 0QX, UK}
\altaffiltext{3}{VBRL Holdings, 50/5 Huay Kaew Road, Chang Phueak, Chiang Mai, Thailand}

\begin{abstract}
\emph{Experiment.} We have searched the public ALMA archive for an
unresolved, drifting spectral carrier from nearby stars: \NWinA{}
fine-channel windows toward \NSysClassA{} systems over \UnionClassA\,GHz
of unique sky frequency, a median \SysUnionAMed\,GHz per system. A
secondary search of \NWinB{} coarse windows adds \UnionBonly\,GHz of
spectral-excess coverage that cannot discriminate drift.

\emph{Sensitivity.} It is measured end to end, by injecting carriers into
calibrated visibilities and recovering them through the unmodified
pipeline, trigger and control rank together. The median system's best
window reaches $\SdMed$\,W EIRP, and the distribution matters more than
the median: of \SdNSys{} systems, \SdNFifteen{} reach $10^{15}$\,W and
\SdNSixteen{} reach $10^{16}$\,W. These are upper bounds on reach; the
calibration is uncertain by
$\times\SelTransFineLo$--$\times\SelTransFineHi$.

\emph{Result.} \textbf{No independently confirmed carrier among the
crossings tested.} All \LgNCross{} threshold crossings are tabulated with
their dispositions: \LgNAttr{} are circumstellar or foreground CO,
including the $\beta$~Pictoris disc recovered blind; \LgNUnattr{} are
unattributed, of which \LgNUnattrScreen{} also outrank all \NCtrl{}
spatial controls, and neither of those recurs. A crossing is not itself
unusual --- one control position in a fine-channel window reaches the
survey's smallest crossing statistic \EpCtrlRateA{} per cent of the time
--- so the evidence is attribution and recurrence. A drift-following
visibility fit is reported for every crossing as a check on position and
epoch, not as a filter; \LgNUntest{} are not yet fitted.

\emph{Limitations.} Only \EpIndep{} of \EpTot{} systems have epochs more
than a day apart, so the result constrains persistent carriers and says
little about intermittent ones; and the targets were observed for other
purposes, chiefly disc and planet-formation programmes, so no inference to
the 40\,pc stellar population is attempted.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{extraterrestrial intelligence, radio lines: stars, submillimetre: stars, surveys, astrobiology}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A technological civilisation, signalling deliberately or going about
its affairs, may imprint the electromagnetic spectrum detectably,
a \emph{technosignature} \citep{Sheikh2020,SETIreview2025}.
Six decades of radio searches cover many thousands of stars yet sample only
\HaystackFrac{} of the haystack of transmitter frequency, bandwidth, power and
duty cycle \citep{Wright2018}. Two gaps motivate this work. The millimetre and submillimetre regime is
very sparsely searched, and everything above that axis's
${\sim}115$\,GHz upper limit more sparsely still; and few searches
reprocess \emph{existing} interferometric archives, although those
archives already contain a spectrum of every target they point at.

This paper is an archival ALMA search of nearby stellar systems over a
largely unexplored frequency regime, with an explicit empirical
characterisation of its sensitivity and selection function. It is not, and
does not attempt to be, a survey of the local stellar population: the
sample is whatever ALMA happened to observe, and \S\ref{sec:bias} sets
out how far that is from representative. The signal class searched is an
\emph{unresolved spectral-carrier technosignature}: emission confined to
a small number of spectral channels, spatially unresolved at the stellar
position on the relevant baselines, and persistent enough to recur
between epochs. Each qualifier is a real restriction, since a carrier
broader than the searched channel width, one that fills the synthesised
beam, or one that appears in a single epoch and never again would not be
recovered here whatever its power. The fine channels used are
\ChanLoA--\ChanHiA\,kHz wide, orders of magnitude broader than a
centimetre-wave narrowband backend, so ``narrowband'' throughout
describes the assumed transmitter and never the resolving power of the
data (\S\ref{sec:method}); no hertz-scale spectral resolution is claimed.
We mine the archive, correcting for proper motion and Doppler drift, for
such carriers. Channelisation sets
which of two search classes a window belongs to: \ClassBPct{} per cent of
the searched windows carry no drift discrimination at all, and only
\NWinA{} support the fine-channel search that ``narrowband SETI''
normally means.

Three contributions follow. The first is the systematic exploitation of
archival ALMA stellar fields, \NStarBands{} target/band datasets mined from
public data with no new observing time. The second is spatial-control screen
calibration for interferometric SETI, in which the same statistic is evaluated
at the star and at \NCtrl{} control positions so that the false-alarm rate is
measured and not assumed. The third is a quantitative accounting of the
frequency, power and drift domain actually searched, which gives the
non-detection explicit boundaries. One worked case
runs through the paper, a crossing towards $\beta$~Pictoris, which is the
largest star-versus-control contrast here and is attributed to the disc's
known circumstellar CO across two transitions and
\NStageOneBpicEb{} execution blocks
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). Figure~\ref{fig:funnel} is the roadmap: it gives
the selection from catalogue to searched sample on the left, and the decision
chain from window to disposition on the right.

Two properties of the design bear on how every number below should be
read. The statistic and the molecular-line mask were defined on these
data, so every rate quoted here is post hoc; \S\ref{sec:statistic},
\S\ref{sec:heldout} and Table~\ref{tab:decisions} set out what was decided
when, and against what had already been seen. And the statistic compares a single position with single positions,
which rules out the obvious region-maximum alternative on grounds of
exchangeability; both forms are scored for every window and released, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:aumic} audits the comparison.

One convention holds throughout: \textbf{wherever this paper
says ``sensitivity'' without naming another quantity, it means
$P_{90}^{\rm sel}$.} Four power scales appear below and only the last is a
sensitivity: the trigger level $P_{\rm trig}$, the power
$P_{\rm eff}$ a transmitter must radiate to reach it, the trigger's own
90 per cent recovery $P_{90}$, and $P_{90}^{\rm sel}$, the power the
\emph{complete} automated selection recovers nine times in ten.
$P_{90}^{\rm sel}$ is the only one of the four quoted in the abstract, the
discussion and the conclusions; Appendix~\ref{app:notation} defines them
and every other term used here in a particular sense.

The targets were not chosen for a technosignature search but are the
stars that archival disc and planet-formation programmes happened to
observe, so the sample is enriched in F and G stars and depleted in M
dwarfs relative to the 40\,pc census, with only \NMSearched{} of
\NMCensus{} catalogued M~dwarfs within 40\,pc in it at all. Every
statistical statement below therefore concerns \emph{the ALMA
archive-selected stellar sample within 40\,pc} and not the solar
neighbourhood; in particular the survey cannot constrain the local
habitable-zone planet population, which M~dwarfs dominate
(\S\ref{sec:bias}).





\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Radio searches, from \citet{CocconiMorrison1959} and \citet{Drake1960} through
the programmes reviewed by \citet{Tarter2001} and Project Phoenix
\citep{Backus2002}, divide into wide-field commensal and targeted surveys
\citep{Zhang2020,Czech2021,Morrison2023,FAST3IATLAS2025,LOFARdualsite2023},
with wide-field low-frequency coverage from the MWA \citep{Tremblay2024} and a
single-dish C- and K-band programme at 6 and 18\,GHz with the Sardinia
Radio Telescope \citep{SRT2025},
with machine-learning interference rejection now central
\citep{Ma2023,Choza2024,GLOBULAR2025,AnomalyParkesGBT2025,BRaTs2026}, a
capability our classical threshold search lacks. Essentially all of that work
lies below ${\sim}30$\,GHz, above which only \citet{Mason2025} has searched,
in ALMA Band~3. There are physical reasons to go higher: interstellar scattering and
dispersion fall steeply with frequency, a given aperture delivers higher
gain and hence higher EIRP per watt of transmitter power, and directional
communication, radar and beamed power
are already engineered as coherent mm-wave emission. Atmospheric transmission,
receiver noise and phase stability raise the cost without forbidding it.

\citet{Mason2025} searched archival Band~3 windows toward \MasonNStar{}
Gaia~DR3 stars lying in the fields of \MasonNCal{} ALMA calibrators,
observed for other science and usable for free,
placing EIRP$_{\rm min}>{}$\MasonNearEirp\,W for the nearest, at
\MasonNearKpc\,kpc, and
identifying the challenges of high-frequency work: drift rates scale
with frequency, smearing erodes sensitivity without a drift search, and
the dense forest of molecular lines raises the confusion background. Every window reported here was re-reduced and
re-searched from the raw archive data with the pipeline of
\S\ref{sec:method}, so the numbers to cite for any star in common are
this paper's.

Figure~\ref{fig:context} places this survey among published searches. The
vertical axis is physically commensurable: for a carrier narrower than
every programme's channel the minimum detectable EIRP is a total radiated
power, not a spectral power density, so the same quantity is plotted for
each. What differs is not the unit but the convention --- each programme
adopts its own detection threshold and its own completeness level --- and
the difference runs one way here. The value plotted for this survey is
$P_{90}^{\rm sel}$, a 90 per cent recovery through the complete automated
selection, whereas most published limits are nominal thresholds at which
recovery is not stated. The figure also shows that ALMA occupies
a different combination of frequency and spectral resolution from cm-wave
SETI. Two numerical comparisons differ. Against
centimetre-wave work, essentially all of which lies below
${\sim}30$\,GHz, a search spanning \UnionLo--\UnionHi\,GHz raises the
frequency by about two orders of magnitude. Against the only prior
millimetre search, \citet{Mason2025} in Band~3, the increase is about a
factor of nine. Any EIRP advantage toward the nearest
systems is a $d^{2}$ effect and not an instrumental one, decomposed in
\S\ref{sec:null}. This survey samples far fewer stars, far fewer epochs
and a channelisation seven orders of magnitude coarser than a dedicated
narrowband search; it is complementary to those surveys rather than a
deeper version of them.

A non-detection must become a quantitative exclusion statement:
\citet{Wright2018} formalised the ``Cosmic Haystack'' search volume,
\citet{Margot2023} its conversion into an upper bound on the
transmitter-hosting fraction, and \citet{EarthDetectingEarth2025} asked
where Earth's technosignatures would be detectable. That bound is valid
only if the sample represents the underlying population, which
interest-selected samples do not: most surveys select by proximity,
spectral type, planets or Earth Transit Zone membership, and the
ALMA-specific form, disc selection, skews the sample young and
dynamically active. We instead take every star within 40\,pc lying in the
usable field of at least one public ALMA observation
(\S\ref{sec:sample}). That sample is \emph{work-list complete but
epoch-incomplete}, and this paper uses no other completeness formulation:
every star on the frozen work list that proves genuinely covered is
searched, but not every execution block those stars hold is in this
release, and no completeness is claimed for the local population itself.
The \NCensus{} sample entries are ${\sim}1$ per cent of the catalogued
population within 40\,pc (the \NStars{} actually searched
${\sim}0.5$ per cent), and the residual coverage selection is
characterised as a completeness function (Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}).

\section{Sample selection}
\label{sec:sample}

The sample is \emph{work-list complete but epoch-incomplete} within
40\,pc (Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}), a much weaker condition than volume
completeness. The execution-block accounting closes as follows, and every
number in this paper refers to one of these populations.

\begin{enumerate}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt,parsep=0pt,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \LedProgenitor{} public execution blocks lie in the parent member
  observing-unit sets of the sample stars. This is the population in
  scope, and it splits in two.
\item \LedInScope{} of them have been downloaded, calibrated and
  searched; \LedNotProcessed{} have not. Every one of the
  \LedNotProcessed{} is repeat coverage: each sits in an observing-unit
  set from which another block was already taken, and the de-duplication
  rule below keeps one block per star and tuning. \LedNoCalib{} of them
  the archive offers as raw visibilities with no pipeline calibration at
  all. That is the first identity,
  $\LedInScope+\LedNotProcessed=\LedProgenitor$, which splits finer as
  $\LedSciInScope+\LedHoldoutInScope+\LedNotProcessed=\LedProgenitor$
  into science-sample blocks, the reserved hold-out of
  \S\ref{sec:heldout}, and blocks never searched.
\item \LedProcessed{} blocks were processed in total,
  \TotalDataTB\,TB of raw data through one frozen pipeline. This is a
  \emph{different} population from the \LedInScope: it is those plus
  \LedNoProgLink{} whose member-OUS progenitor link the archive does not
  expose (\LedSciOutScope{} of them in the science sample), so they
  cannot be placed in the ledger above. That is the second
  identity, $\LedInScope+\LedNoProgLink=\LedProcessed$. The two counts
  must not be added to each other.
\item Of the \LedProcessed, \LedCatalogue{} form the \emph{frozen science
  sample} reported here, \LedHoldout{} are the reserved calibration
  hold-out of \S\ref{sec:heldout}, and \LedNoWindow{} left no surviving
  window after the quality cut.
\end{enumerate}

No claim is made to have searched the whole ALMA archive, only every star
on the work list; Table~\ref{tab:partitions} gives the provenance of
every dataset used anywhere in this paper.

The procedure admits every star within 40\,pc of the Sun (Gaia~DR3
parallaxes, distances direct inversions $d = 1/\pi$; every entry $\pi \ge
25$\,mas with a parallax measured to better than \PlxAdqlPct{} per cent, which
is the condition the archive query applied) whose proper-motion-corrected
position at the observation epoch falls in the quoted field of view of at
least one public ALMA execution block. The star need not sit at the pointing centre,
and \S\ref{sec:exclusions} shows the quoted field of view admitted
\NNeverObsStars{} stars no observation in fact contains, so ``covered'' is a
property of the selection and not a guarantee. Disc, planet and
spectral type enter as descriptive metadata only, and units are defined
in \S\ref{sec:units}. This gives \NCensus{} unique target stars,
processed nearest-first; because trigger thresholds scale with $d^{2}$ at
fixed noise that order is sensitivity-ranked, so the prevalence
statements of \S\ref{sec:discussion} are conditional on it.

Composition follows from the archival selection and is tabulated against the
reference census in Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}. The sample is F- and G-enriched
and deficient in M~dwarfs, and \ConcThreeWin{} of the \NWindows{} windows come
from \ConcThreeSys{} systems; \S\ref{sec:bias} gives the figures and
their cost. The spectral-class proxy is \texttt{teff\_gspphot}, available
for \NTeffSample{} of \NStars{} sample stars and missing preferentially
for the coolest objects, so the M fraction is a lower limit and the
earlier-type ratios upper limits. Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions} lists the cross-match and exclusion edge
cases, including the ``$\gamma$~Lupi'' relabelling.





``Usable public ALMA coverage'' is the five-criterion decision tree of
Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}. No star or window was excluded by an
unlisted criterion, and none for short integration.

\section{Data and methodology}

Three abbreviations recur below: QA2 is ALMA's second-stage quality
assurance, the stage that delivers calibrated data; a member observing
unit set (MOUS) is the scheduling container holding one or more execution
blocks; and TDM and FDM are the correlator's time- and
frequency-division modes, giving coarse and fine channelisation. Symbols,
including $T_\star$, $T_{\rm trig}$ and the power scales, are defined in
Appendix~\ref{app:notation}.
\label{sec:method}

One shared archive-ingestion stage feeds two independent search lanes,
and Table~\ref{tab:algorithm} gives the data path of the primary lane in
order. For each target the calibrated archival execution blocks covering
the star's Gaia-propagated position are mined within a bounded budget.
Their visibilities feed (i) the \emph{primary} lane, a
Doppler-drift-corrected spectral-carrier search following \citet{Mason2025}
and \citet{Enriquez2017}, which converts each non-detection into an equivalent
isotropic radiated power limit
$\mathrm{EIRP_{\rm trig}} = 4\pi d^{2} S_{\rm min}\Delta\nu$. The product
$S_{\rm min}\Delta\nu$ is an integrated power only if the channel's effective
noise bandwidth equals its separation, which for a smoothed correlator it does
not; the response factor $C_{\rm response}$ corrects that. It is computed
from the correlator's own lag window and runs
$\times\HanFacBest$--$\times\HanFacWorst$ with sub-channel position, median
$\times\HanFacMed$, and is validated against ALMA's stated channel
resolutions --- including the effective resolution the archive reports for
every window in this survey (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). So
$P_{\rm eff}=S_{\rm min}\Delta\nu\,C_{\rm response}C_{\rm smear}$
recovers the injected power, with $S_{\rm min}$ the
primary-beam-corrected trigger-level flux density defined below,
$\Delta\nu$ the native channel width and $d$ the distance (worked
numerically in Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). EIRPs are quoted to two
significant figures, the precision the flux scale supports. Five terms
enter the systematic budget, each multiplying $S_{\rm min}$ and hence
EIRP$_{\rm trig}$ directly.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.9em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt,parsep=0pt]
\item The ALMA absolute flux scale: \BudFlux{} per cent band-weighted,
up to \BudFluxWorst{} per cent at Bands~9 and~10, and epoch-dependent.
\item Residual atmospheric phase after water-vapour correction and phase
referencing: a coherent point-source loss $\exp(-\sigma_\phi^{2}/2)$,
$\BudDecorLo$--$\BudDecorHi$ per cent at 230--345\,GHz. It is common to
star and annulus within the isoplanatic patch and so no threat to
exchangeability, but it is \emph{not} common to the injected tones that
calibrate $P_{90}$, which is why \S\ref{sec:excludes} carries it as a
one-sided bias.
\item The primary-beam model at the stellar offset: negligible on axis,
\BudPoint{} per cent at the worst pointing error.
\item Visibility calibration: bounded empirically at
\VisCalPct{} per cent. An inconsistent delivered amplitude scale would
make the ratio of each window's measured per-integration rms to its
radiometric prediction scatter from block to block beyond what thermal
noise explains. Over \VisCalWin{} windows in \VisCalBlocks{} blocks the
per-block median of that ratio is \VisCalMed{} with a robust scatter of
\VisCalPct{} per cent, against \VisCalWithinPct{} per cent within a
block. It enters the combined budget of Table~\ref{tab:p90budget} in
quadrature with the other two-sided terms.
\item The instrumental spectral response: median $\times\HanFacMed$,
carried explicitly as $P_{\rm eff}$ by the reading rule below. It is a
definition rather than an uncertainty, and most of it is a change of
units, as the next paragraph separates.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{How much of the response factor is a real loss.} The
factor $\times\HanFacMed$ is large, but it is not mostly a penalty. It
factorises exactly into two parts. $P_{\rm trig}$ is quoted against the
noise of the \emph{delivered}, smoothed channel, whose rms is
\CrNoiseFrac{} of the raw-channel rms for the $0.25/0.5/0.25$ kernel;
converting to that convention accounts for $\times\CrNormTerm$ of the
factor and is the same number for every window in the survey. What
remains is the genuine inefficiency of reading a carrier in one smoothed
channel: the peak channel holds \CrRhoHi{} to \CrRhoLo{} of the carrier
while the noise has fallen by \CrNoiseFrac, so relative to an ideal
single-channel detector on unsmoothed data the loss is
$\times\CrEffLo$--$\times\CrEffHi$, median $\times\CrEffMed$. That is the
number to have in mind when comparing this search with one on
unsmoothed data; $\times\HanFacMed$ is the number to multiply
$P_{\rm trig}$ by, because $P_{\rm trig}$ is defined in smoothed-channel
units.

\noindent\emph{Gaia} distance errors are negligible,
\BudDist{} per cent typically. Two survey classes are reported,
distinguished by the dimensionless drift parameter
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\rm drift} \;=\; |\dot\nu_{\max}|\,\tau_{\rm track}\,/\,\Delta\nu_{\rm ch},
\label{eq:etadrift}
\end{equation}
the channel-width fraction the \emph{maximum} searched drift
$|\dot\nu_{\max}|$ traverses over the full on-source track $\tau_{\rm track}$
at native channel width $\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$. The track is the right
time-scale because the de-drift step sums along it; $\tau_{\rm track}$ is
written throughout, leaving $T$ for the search statistic alone, and
$\eta_{\rm smear}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}) is the same ratio
over one integration. A window is \emph{drift-resolving} when
$\eta_{\rm drift}\gtrsim1$, carrying a carrier across enough
channels for its motion to be measured. The drift ceiling inside
$\eta_{\rm drift}$ is a search-grid boundary, the widest trial drift the
stack evaluates, and not a prior on transmitter drift rates: carriers
drifting faster are missed, and \S\ref{sec:detectable} gives the
resulting selection function (Fig.~\ref{fig:accel}).

\emph{Class~A is the primary experiment}, the fine-channel
drifting-spectral-carrier search: the
\NWinA{} windows of native width
\ChanAMinKHz--\ChanAMaxKHz\,kHz (median \ChanAMedKHz\,kHz;
\ChanAModePct{} per cent at the \ChanAModeKHz-kHz mode), spanning
$\eta_{\rm drift}=\EtaAmin$--$\EtaAmax$ (median \EtaAmed), and the
only class with measured end-to-end recovery of a drifting carrier.
\emph{Class~B} is the coarse-channel spectral-excess search: the \NWinB{}
windows, \PctCoarse{} per cent of the sample, of native width
\ChanBMinMHz--\ChanBMaxMHz\,MHz (\ChanBModeN{} at the
\ChanBModeMHz-MHz mode), spanning
$\eta_{\rm drift}=\EtaBmin$--$\EtaBmax$ (median \EtaBmed), sensitive to
persistent near-stationary excess power but carrying no drift
discrimination. It is the \emph{secondary experiment} throughout, an
unresolved spectral-excess search, and is never described as a carrier
search here.

$\eta_{\rm drift}$ is the physical criterion and the classes are
labelled by the instrument. Channelisation is bimodal, with no window
between \ChanAMaxKHz\,kHz and \ChanBMinMHz\,MHz, an empty gap of factor
\ChanGapFac, so any boundary inside the gap assigns identical membership.
The instrumental split is the one used throughout; $\eta_{\rm drift}$ is
released per window so any reader may reclassify on the physical criterion,
which selects \NEtaResolved{} windows of \NWindows{} against \NWinA{} in
Class~A. Each window's
$\eta_{\rm drift}$, $\eta_{\rm smear}$, trial drift count and maximum
searched acceleration $a_{\max}=\AccelCeilMed$--$\AccelCeilMax$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ are
released catalogue columns, so any reader may reclassify on the physical
criterion alone. Results are given per class first and combined
only where statistically appropriate (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}).

Catalogued molecular transitions are excluded: the search applies a
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame velocity exclusion mask
(\S\ref{sec:linecost}) to any threshold crossing. \textbf{That half-width was set after the $\beta$~Pictoris crossings had
been inspected, and is the one element of the analysis not fixed in
advance.}

Reclassifying all \NHits{} crossings at $\pm20$, $\pm30$, $\pm50$ and
$\pm100$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ admits no new unattributed event at any width, and
CP$-$72~2713 remains the only unmasked one throughout; this is a one-sided
test (\S\ref{sec:linecost}). The physical scale that motivates masking at
all is the Keplerian bound, $\lesssim13$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ where these
molecules survive beyond 10\,au, and it is a stellar-frame quantity.

\emph{The mask is a classification mask, not a detection mask.} It is
applied after the search, to decide how an event that has already been
found should be interpreted, and it removes nothing from the detection
stage: every masked window is searched exactly as an unmasked one is, and
no threshold moves. What it defines is the frequency space
\emph{excluded from the exclusion statement}. The survey covers
\UnionGHz\,GHz of unique sky frequency in \UnionIntervals{} clustered
islands (Fig.~\ref{fig:waterfall}); the effective default quoted
throughout is the ${\approx}\SearchedUnionGHz$\,GHz surviving the mask,
and no constraint is placed on carriers inside the excluded velocity
regions, not because they could not have been detected but because a
detection there could not have been distinguished from the molecular
emission the mask marks.

All flux densities and EIRP thresholds are total intensity,
$I=(XX+YY)/2$, the QA2-weighted mean of the two parallel-hand
cross-correlations of ALMA's linear feeds. No cross-hand extraction was
performed; the consequence for what the survey can and cannot discriminate
is stated once, in the main limitations (\S\ref{sec:excludes}), and is not
repeated here. Incomplete parallactic-angle sampling, unequal flagging of
the two hands and instrumental leakage all break the equivalence between a
strongly polarised and an unpolarised signal of the same total power. The
archive
records both parallel hands for \NEbBothHands{} of the \NEbPolKnown{}
blocks whose polarisation setup it resolves, but not per-correlation
flagging inside QA2; a window that lost a hand would be $\sqrt{2}$ less
sensitive than its threshold implies, and that factor is carried as a
one-sided term in Table~\ref{tab:p90budget}.

Two power scales carry the main text, and
Table~\ref{tab:quantities} defines the rest. The trigger power
$P_{\rm trig}$ is five times the window's per-channel rms at its native
channel width, for a transmitter at the star's position matching the
searched morphology: the level at which the search fires. It is an
internal pipeline quantity, not a sensitivity and not a significance.
$P_{90}^{\rm sel}$, the power the complete automated selection recovers
nine times in ten, is $\times\SelPNinetyFine$ the nominal trigger for
Class~A, and is the survey sensitivity. It comes from \SelNTone{} carriers
injected into calibrated visibilities across \SelNUnit{} block--window
configurations spanning both arrays, four ALMA bands and channelisations
from \ChanLoA\,kHz to 31.25\,MHz (\S\ref{sec:pninetysel},
Appendix~\ref{app:inject}); $P_{95}$ remains \emph{not determined},
because recovery saturates below it. Between them sits the
response-corrected threshold $P_{\rm eff}=\HanFacMed\,P_{\rm trig}$,
which converts the trigger from smoothed-channel units into transmitter
power, and $P_{90}$, the trigger-stage completeness,
$\times\SelPNinetyTrigFine$ the nominal trigger for Class~A; both are
released per window and are defined in Appendix~\ref{app:notation}. Every
headline EIRP is quoted beside its native channel width. Three signal
morphologies are detectable, and \S\ref{sec:detectable} quantifies each
against real windows: a continuous frequency-stationary carrier, a
continuously drifting one, and an intermittent one.

\medskip

The selection logic is four steps, in order. The first generates
crossings; the rest dispose of them.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.9em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt,parsep=0pt]
\item Threshold crossing. A channel$\times$drift cell at the
stellar position reaches $T_\star\geq T_{\rm trig}$. A fixed trigger
power, not a calibrated $p$-value, and not a rare event: a single control
position in a Class~A window reaches the survey's smallest crossing
statistic \EpCtrlRateA{} per cent of the time (\S\ref{sec:vistest}).
\item Astrophysical identification. The crossing is checked against
catalogued molecular transitions in the stellar frame and against
instrumental signatures.
\item Spatial prioritisation. The stellar statistic exceeds all
\NCtrl{} control positions in the same window. A candidate-generation
mechanism: it carries no significance of its own, for the reasons above.
\item Independent recurrence. It repeats at the same tuning in an
\emph{independent epoch}, or acquires independent confirmation; and its
block is compared with the other blocks of the same star.
\end{enumerate}

The drift-following visibility fit runs on every crossing and is reported
beside this chain, not inside it. It asks a genuinely physical question ---
is the emission at the stellar coordinates? --- on recalibrated raw data
with no control ensemble of \NCtrl{} positions; but it is evaluated at the
position and in the cell where the search has already found an excess in
the same visibilities, and its \EpNVisCtrl{} controls resolve a rank of
only \EpVisRankRes. Its place in the argument, and the reference-epoch
defect that once made it look decisive, are in \S\ref{sec:vistest}. Every
crossing is carried through all four steps in Table~\ref{tab:ledger}, and
nothing short of (iv) is called a candidate.

All delivered channels of each spectral window were searched, with no
edge-channel or bandpass-roll-off trimming before extraction, so residual
bandpass structure near a window edge is carried in the
calibration-residual term of the systematic budget rather than removed
by flagging. The distance of every threshold crossing from its window
edges is a released catalogue quantity, reported for the flagged windows
in \S\ref{sec:technosearch}.

The primary statistic is formed on extracted spectra, never on images or
visibilities, from the QA2-calibrated measurement set as delivered, per
integration and per native channel, with the archive's flagging carried
through untouched and no regridding
(Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}, step~3). What the extraction returns at a
propagated sky position $\mathbf{x}$ is the real part of the weighted vector
average of the phase-shifted visibilities, the natural-weighted
dirty-map value there; there is no deconvolution, so a quoted $S_{\rm peak}$
in a field with extended emission is a dirty-map amplitude. The
primary-beam correction enters the quoted thresholds and fluxes
(\S\ref{sec:frames}) and leaves untouched the extracted amplitudes the
statistic ranks. Each per-integration spectrum $I(\mathbf{x},t,\nu)$ is
then baseline-subtracted by a block median along the \emph{frequency}
axis, with no temporal median at any stage (\S\ref{sec:ancillary}). The
residual dynamic spectrum is stacked along each trial linear drift rate
$\dot\nu$ with inverse-variance weights, and the statistic is the maximum
of that stack over the channel$\times$drift grid,
%
\begin{equation}
T(\mathbf{x})=\max_{\dot\nu,\;\nu}\;
\frac{\sum_{t} I\!\left(\mathbf{x},t,\nu+\dot\nu t\right)\big/\sigma^{2}(t,\nu)}
     {\left[\sum_{t}1\big/\sigma^{2}(t,\nu)\right]^{1/2}},
\label{eq:tstar}
\end{equation}
%
a matched filter whose denominator is the noise of the stacked spectrum.
$T(\mathbf{x})$ is neither assumed nor shown to be Gaussian or $\chi$
distributed under the null, which is why $T_{\rm trig}$ denotes a trigger
level and not a tail probability; Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}
quantifies the departure. It reduces to a single-channel amplitude
divided by one noise scale only when $\sigma$ is the same for every
integration and channel; otherwise the weights vary along the track. The
scale
$\sigma(t,\nu)=1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD}_{i=1\ldots\NCtrl}\!\left[I_i(t,\nu)\right]$
is $1.4826\times$ the median absolute deviation taken
\emph{across the control positions} at each integration $t$ and
channel $\nu$, making it global in position, local in frequency and
time, and shared by the star and every control. The across-position
median enters only in forming that deviation and is never subtracted from
the numerator, so $T$ is an absolute stacked signal-to-noise carrying a
cross-position noise scale, and measures no local contrast.

$T$ is evaluated at one position, identically at the star and at each
control, which is what makes the rank interpretable: a statistic taking a
maximum over several stellar-region positions while the controls remain
single positions would not be exchangeable between the two sides. A line
filling the primary beam drives star and annulus up together instead of
cancelling.



The searched drift-rate range is the larger of (a) a generic
frequency-scaled default (12--13\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$), the
\citet{Mason2025} fiducial with a threefold safety margin, and
(b) for confirmed exoplanet hosts, the maximum line-of-sight orbital
acceleration of the innermost known planet, so a transmitter on a
short-period planet cannot drift out of the window. Drift rate maps
onto line-of-sight acceleration through the first-order Doppler
relation
%
\begin{equation}
a_{\rm los} \;=\; c\,\frac{\dot\nu}{\nu},
\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
%
so the ceiling is quoted in cross-band-comparable form $\dot\nu/\nu$:
12--13\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$ is $(1.2$--$1.3)\times10^{-8}$\,s$^{-1}$,
that is $|a_{\rm los}| = 3.6$--$3.9$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ independent of observing
frequency, the acceleration at ${\sim}0.04$\,au around a solar-type star.
Earth-motion contributions lie two orders of magnitude below and are
subsumed by the grid, which is uniform in $\dot\nu$ and fine enough that a
carrier midway between two trials drifts at most \DriftGridFineHalf{} of a
native channel over the longest Class~A track. Two
limits of the model matter more. The rates searched are
\emph{apparent} topocentric ones, so the survey does not separate a
deliberately chirped carrier from an accelerating platform and constrains only
linear apparent drift inside the ceiling; and an emitter need not be tied to a
known planet at all, a free-flying platform or rotating structure accelerating
as it chooses. For circular orbits the ceiling covers a transmitter co-orbiting an
Earth analogue by three orders of magnitude and Proxima~b comfortably, while
TRAPPIST-1\,b ($a_{\rm los}=\OrbAccTrapb$\,m\,s$^{-2}$) exceeds the default
ceiling, \AccelCeilMed\,m\,s$^{-2}$, by \AccelShortPctMed{} per cent, and c
($\OrbAccTrapc$\,m\,s$^{-2}$) reaches it at modest eccentricity, which raises
the periastron value by up to $(1{+}e)/(1{-}e)^{2}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:accel}).
TRAPPIST-1's own windows were searched on a widened grid, to
\AccelCeilMax\,m\,s$^{-2}$, which falls \AccelShortPctMax{} per cent short of
planet b at its maximum line-of-sight acceleration.
TRAPPIST-1 transits, so its inclination is measured and effectively
edge-on, and the edge-on figure is the relevant one: against the default
ceiling TRAPPIST-1\,b lies above it for \TrapPhaseHi{} per cent of its
orbital phase, and against the widened one for \TrapPhaseLo{} per cent;
it is searchable through the rest.

Across the \NWindows{} windows maximum searched drift rates span
\DriftKHzLo--\DriftKHzHi\,kHz\,s$^{-1}$. The number of trial drift rates per
window separates sharply by class and must be quoted that way: Class~A
windows search a median of \TrialsAMed{} trials (interquartile
\TrialsAQLo--\TrialsAQHi, maximum \TrialsAMax, the narrow and finely
channelised $\beta$~Pictoris Band~6 window, \S\ref{sec:bpicval}), while
Class~B windows search a median of \TrialsBMed{} because a coarse channel
needs almost no de-drifting. Residual smear separates in the same direction: all \SmearBN{} Class~B
windows that carry a stored value have $\eta_{\rm smear}$ exactly
\SmearBExact, so every residual smear loss the survey records is in
Class~A, where \SmearANLossy{} of \SmearAN{} windows fall below 0.99 and
the worst retains \SmearAMin{} of the signal. The threefold margin on the
ceiling matters: at $1\times$ the search would have been blind to two of
its threshold-crossing windows, including the AU~Mic crossing, while at
$5\times$ no disposition changes. Transmitters drifting faster than the
\DriftCeilLo--\DriftCeilHi\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$ ceiling remain
unconstrained. Channel widths are effective resolutions from the
measurement sets' spectral window tables
(Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}).



\subsection{Ancillary and validation analyses}
\label{sec:ancillary}

Nothing here generates candidates; it is validation, and the detail is in
the appendices. Two demonstrations carry the weight. Synthetic tones of
known amplitude injected into real visibilities and recovered through
the unmodified pipeline --- \NTrials{} trials across six window
configurations, three bands and both classes
(Appendix~\ref{app:inject}) --- establish that the machinery
recovers a drifting unresolved spectral carrier, the searched morphology; and
$\beta$~Pictoris CO is recovered and localised in two transitions and
\NStageOneBpicEb{} blocks (\S\ref{sec:bpicval}), establishing that
it finds a real feature at a known stellar position. The baseline step is
a per-integration median along \emph{frequency}
(Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}, step~4) and so cannot remove a one- or
two-channel feature. Appendix~\ref{app:provenance} reports the three
quantified checks that bound undetected pipeline faults.

\subsection{The experiment in summary} \label{sec:summary}

The experiment runs in the following steps, with the number surviving
each: \LedProgenitor{} public execution blocks whose fields contain a
catalogued star within 40\,pc; \LedProcessed{} of them delivered with a
usable calibration and put through one frozen pipeline with no per-target
tuning; \LedCatalogue{} in the science sample once \LedHoldout{} are
withheld by a rule fixed before any was looked at, a hash of the block
identifier, and \LedNoWindow{} yield no usable window; \NWindows{}
spectral windows, de-duplicated by identifier; \NWinA{} of them Class~A,
the primary experiment, the remaining \NWinB{} supporting only a search
for unresolved spectral excess; \LgNCross{} threshold crossings, where
$T_\star\geq5$ somewhere on the drift grid; \LgNUnattr{} of those with no
catalogued transition within $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the star's
own frame, the other \LgNAttr{} attributed, most of them the

\subsection{Technosignature search}
\label{sec:technosearch}

The screen used here ranks the stellar position against \NCtrl{} controls
under a single global noise normalisation. \rankstatus{} A radius-corrected
variant, described in Appendix~\ref{app:radius}, changes no conclusion:
both give zero confirmed candidates and differ only in the count of
screened events without an attribution, \NStageOneUnattrib{} against
\PriNUnattrib{} windows.

All \NStarBands{} star/band datasets yielded at least one carrier-lane
result, \NWindows{} spectral-window measurements after the catalogue audit
removed \NDup{} duplicate rows, Bands~6 and~7 dominating
(Table~\ref{tab:perband}). Nominal trigger thresholds span
$\EirpMin$--$\EirpMax$\,W at a median of $\EirpMedian$\,W
(Figs.~\ref{fig:context} and \ref{fig:waterfall}), the deepest reached
toward the nearest systems --- $1.6\times10^{13}$\,W for the UV~Ceti pair
at 2.7\,pc --- though depth tracks integration time and channelisation as
well as distance.

The statistics come strictly before the astrophysics, in the levels of
Table~\ref{tab:vocab}, which do not promote one another. A
\emph{threshold crossing} is one channel$\times$drift cell at the stellar
position reaching $T\geq5$: \NHits{} of the \NWindows{} windows contain
one --- \LgNCross{} crossings in all, counting a window of one block once
--- and \PctCrossClassA{} per cent of those windows are Class~A, a trials
effect rather than a physical one, since a median Class~A window holds
$\CellsMedA$ channel$\times$drift cells against \CellsMedB{} for a median
Class~B window. A \emph{screened event} is a crossing whose window also
ranks the star above all \NCtrl{} controls: \NStageOneWin{} windows do, in
\NStageOneSys{} systems and \NStageOnePairs{} star--band pairs, and those
windows are referred to below as the stage-1 flagged windows where the
distinction between a window and a crossing matters. A local
scramble test, which asks only whether a feature exceeds the noise at its
own position, clears the control-ring maximum too in \LNNCtrlClear{} of
the \LNNWindows{} windows, so screening turns on the star-versus-control
comparison. A \emph{confirmed candidate} is a localised event that recurs
or is independently confirmed. There are \LgNConfirmed.

The screened events fall into two groups. \NStageOneLine{} of the
\NStageOneWin{} stage-1 windows sit within $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of a molecular
transition in the star's own frame and carry independent CO evidence:
\NStageOneBpic{} are $\beta$~Pictoris, in \NStageOneBpicThree{} Band~3 and
\NStageOneBpicSix{} Band~6 windows drawn from \NStageOneBpicEb{} execution
blocks; one is the published foreground emission at HD~48370. That is
\NStageOneSources{} astrophysical sources, not \NStageOneWin{} independent
events, and
the recurrence is the point: a frozen pipeline recovers the same disc in
two transitions and \NStageOneBpicEb{} independent blocks without being
told it is there.

The remaining \NStageOneUnattrib{} stage-1 windows have no astrophysical
attribution, and
what disposes of them is the absence of a second epoch rather than
anything the visibilities say. \emph{This is a change from earlier
versions of this paper}, which reported both as showing no emission at
the stellar position; that finding was produced by a visibility estimator
referred to the wrong epoch and is withdrawn (\S\ref{sec:vistest}). Under
the corrected epoch one of the two, towards \EpGapStar, returns
${\rm Re}/\sigma=\EpGapReCor$ --- \VpScrRatio{} of what a compact source
at the star producing its trigger would give --- and the other has not yet
been re-fitted. Neither recurs, and neither is called a candidate on that
ground alone.

The chance expectation of an unattributed stage-1 event, its conditioning
on the trigger as well as the rank, and the size of the observed count
against it are calibration and are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.

\paragraph*{Stellar flares, excluded by measurement rather than by
argument.} The hosts are not a quiet sample. \HostNActive{} of the
\HostNTot{} carry activity indicators in SIMBAD
(Table~\ref{tab:hosts}, queried \HostQueried) and \HostNMult{} is
catalogued as multiple; CP$-$72~2713 in particular is a K7Ve star flagged
as an emission-line, rotationally variable X-ray source, the profile of a
star that flares. Magnetically active stars do produce coherent bursts at
centimetre wavelengths. Stellar activity is therefore the leading
astrophysical alternative to an instrumental origin for the crossings ---
and, unlike in earlier versions of this paper, it is \emph{not} disfavoured
by the localisation test, because \FlNLocalised{} of the \FlNFitted{}
fitted crossings do localise at the stellar position and stellar emission
is expected to.

It is excluded instead by a broadband prediction with no free parameter. A
millimetre flare raises every channel of a window by the same flux density,
so a flare able to produce a $5\sigma$ single-channel crossing must produce
a $5\sqrt{N}\,\sigma$ continuum event in the same integrations --- $\FlIllusZWide\sigma$
in a $\FlIllusNWide$-channel window, $\FlIllusZNarrow\sigma$ in a
$\FlIllusNNarrow$-channel one. Over the \FlNCont{} crossings with a
recoverable continuum measurement the prediction runs \FlPredLo--\FlPredHi{}
(median $\approx\FlPredMed$), while the observed continuum statistic in the
crossing's own time window has median \FlObsMed{} and $|Z|<3$ in \FlNQuiet{}
of \FlNCont; the largest continuum excursion anywhere in those blocks, at
any duration, corresponds to a single-channel contribution of median
\FlImpliedMed$\sigma$ and at most \FlImpliedMax$\sigma$. The crossings are
also not concentrated in time: the de-drifted track's boxcar maximum minus
the same bank's maximum at the controls has median \FlConcMed, and
\FlConcNGt{} of \FlNCont{} exceeds $+2$.

One block settles it directly. \FlEgStar{} \texttt{\FlEgEb} contains both a
threshold crossing ($T_\star=\FlEgT$) and the published 2015 June 24
millimetre flare of \citet{MacGregor2020}. In the crossing's own time window
the continuum is raised at $Z=\FlEgContZ$ --- the flare --- while the
crossing's de-drifted track reaches only $Z_{\max}=\FlEgTrackZ$, below its
own controls. \emph{A real, bright, independently published millimetre
flare in the very same data contributes \FlEgContrib$\sigma$ to the
single-channel statistic.} The statistic has the power to see temporal
concentration when it is there: $\beta$~Pictoris CO, the survey's strongest
real feature at $T_\star=\FlCtlTLo$--\FlCtlTHi, has a boxcar maximum of
\FlCtlBoxLo--\FlCtlBoxHi{} against \FlCtlCtrlLo--\FlCtlCtrlHi{} at its own
controls, because a steady line is steady.



Both have a persistence test and neither passes it. Each has
\NUnattRepeatLo{} to \NUnattRepeatHi{} further execution blocks of the
same star whose tuning covers the event's frequency, \NUnattRepeatBlocks{}
in total, and the feature recurs in none: the largest stellar statistic
anywhere in them is $T_\star=\UnattRepeatMaxT$, \NUnattRepeatAboveCtrl{}
exceed their own window's control maximum and \NUnattRepeatFlag{} are
stage-1 flagged windows. The baselines are days rather than years:
\NUnattSepResolved{} of the repeats are separated from their event by a
resolvable interval, \UnattSepMinD--\UnattSepMaxD\,d, and the remaining
\NUnattSepSameDay{} fall within the same scheduling block. \emph{These
tests exclude a transmitter persistent over hours to days and bound no duty
cycle measured in months or years.} Repeat coverage is matched on the window
rather than on the crossing frequency, which the release records only where
the pipeline stored a peak. CP$-$72~2713 is examined in detail
(\S\ref{sec:cp72}).
\subsubsection{The crossing ledger, and what the visibility fit is for}
\label{sec:vistest}

\emph{Every threshold crossing has been carried through the same chain, and
the drift-following visibility fit is reported beside that chain rather than
inside it.} Table~\ref{tab:ledger} is the resulting ledger: all
\LgNCross{} crossings, each with its search statistic, its stellar-frame
line attribution, the \NCtrl-position rank flag, the visibility fit under
\emph{both} reference epochs, and its disposition. Table~\ref{tab:ledgersum}
sets the counts against what chance would give. The
fit is no longer conditional on the image-plane rank: a crossing earns the
physical test by crossing the threshold, and the rank is a column beside the
result rather than a gate in front of it, because a screen that decides which
events are examined cannot also be the evidence that they are real.

\emph{The reference epoch, and a defect in the estimator.} The fit follows
the carrier along its recovered drift rate, which means it must decide the
time at which the recovered frequency is the crossing frequency. The
implementation used first took the median of the timestamps it loaded; the
search de-drifts to its own first retained integration. The two differ by
$\dot\nu\,(t_{\rm med}-t_{0})/\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$ channels, which is nothing
for a short window and several channels for a long one, and the question is
not one of convention: the injection campaign of
\S\ref{sec:pninetysel} fitted every triggered tone \emph{both} ways.
Of \EpNTone{} injected carriers in \EpNUnit{} block--window configurations,
the \EpNAbove{} displaced by more than \EpDLo{} channels are recovered at a
median \EpFracCor{} of their injected flux when referred to the search's own
epoch and $\EpFracCom$ when referred to the median, and are localised in
\EpLocPctCor{} per cent of cases against \EpNLocCom{} of \EpNAbove. Below a
quarter of a channel the two are the same experiment, which is why a pilot
on a short window could not have found this. The paper adopts the search's
epoch; the ledger reports both, because the difference between them is one
of the results.

Two consequences follow. The rejections produced by the earlier
implementation are \emph{withdrawn as evidence} --- an estimator evaluating
the wrong channel rejects almost everything, and \LgNMoved{} of the
\LgNFit{} fitted crossings change verdict between the two epochs, every one
at a displacement above a channel and none below \LgDSame. And the molecular
lines offered as the test's positive controls are withdrawn as validation of
the estimator: they are \emph{resolved} emission an order of magnitude wider
than a channel, and a track scanning across a line peaks near its centroid
whichever epoch is used, so they never exercised the failure mode. A
validation has to be sensitive to the error it exists to catch.

\emph{The criterion} was fixed before any fit was run and recorded at commit
\LgCommit: a crossing is localised at the stellar position when
Re/$\sigma\ge\LgReMin$ there, $|{\rm Im}/\sigma|<\LgImMax$, and Re/$\sigma$
exceeds the maximum returned by the same fit at eight annulus control
positions and four off-event control frequencies. \LgNFit{} of the
\LgNCross{} crossings (\LgFitPct{} per cent) have been fitted; the remaining
\LgNUntest{} in \LgNUntestBlocks{} execution blocks have not, and are
recorded as untested rather than as non-detections. Of the fitted crossings
\LgNLocalCom{} are localised under the earlier epoch and \LgNLocalCor{} under
the adopted one, \LgNLocalCorVer{} of them with the residual to the
extraction epoch verified from the retained products and
\LgNLocalCorProv{} with it bounded rather than measured.

\emph{This is a consistency check on position and epoch, not a candidate
filter}, and the reason is measurable rather than rhetorical. Of the
\LgNCorReFour{} crossings reaching Re/$\sigma\ge\LgReMin$ under the adopted
epoch, the imaginary-part and control clauses reject \LgNCorReFourRejected{}
--- all of them \LgVerRejectStar, one grossly displaced source with
$|{\rm Im}/\sigma|$ of tens --- and on the epoch-verified subset
\LgNVerReFourRejected{} of \LgNVerReFour. The control clause never rejects on
its own. That is what should be expected of it: the fit is evaluated at the
position and in the cell where a threshold crossing was, by definition,
already found, in the same visibilities; and its \EpNVisCtrl{} controls
resolve a rank of only \EpVisRankRes, with an expected maximum of
\EpEmaxVis{} for pure noise against \EpEmaxRank{} for the \NCtrl-position
ensemble, so the spatial clause is passed by anything above about $2\sigma$.
It is the weakest screen in the chain, not the strongest. With the epoch
wrong it behaved like a powerful independent test by accident, because it was
looking where nothing was.

What the fit does establish is worth keeping. Phase-rotating every visibility
to the star turns emission centred there into a constant real offset, while
emission elsewhere is suppressed in the real part and in general leaves a
non-zero imaginary part. The two parts do different work: the real part
carries the detection, and the imaginary part discriminates against
\emph{displaced} emission only. A small imaginary part is not itself evidence
that emission is present, because for pure noise it is centred on zero
whether or not a source is there. The average is taken over the measurement
set's own rows, which carry one point of each conjugate pair rather than the
Hermitian completion of the $uv$-plane; with the conjugates included the
imaginary part would vanish identically. \LgVerRejectStar{} is the test
working in that direction, and so is HD~48370, whose large real part comes
with an imaginary part far from zero --- the signature its published
identification as foreground cloud emission predicts.

\paragraph*{\BdStar{} (\BdAlias): a defective execution block, diagnosed.}
\BdNCross{} threshold crossings belong to this star, with the highest
statistics in the survey outside $\beta$~Pictoris and HD~48370
($T_\star$ up to \BdTMax), and they deserve a disposition rather than a
sentence. \emph{All \BdNCrossBlock{} are in one ACA execution block},
\texttt{\BdBlock}, of the \BdNBlocks{} this star contributes; the
\BdNSibling{} sibling executions of the same field, in the same project with
the same array and the same spectral setup, reach a maximum of
$T_\star=\BdSibTMax$ and produce no crossing at all. In the defective block
the \emph{whole field} is above threshold: \BdNCtrlAbove{} of \BdNCtrl{}
control positions exceed $T=5$, with a median of \BdCtrlMed{} and a maximum
of \BdCtrlMax, against a survey median control of \BdSurvCtrlMed{} over
\BdSurvCtrlN{} windows. The single-integration noise is exactly as predicted
(\BdRmsMeas\,Jy measured against \BdRmsPred\,Jy), but the residual does not
integrate down: a jackknife on the channel mean gives \BdJack\,Jy against
\BdThermal\,Jy thermal, a factor \BdJackRatio, a binned-mean test is flat,
and the statistic scatters by \BdScatter{} where it should scatter by unity.
A slow, smooth temporal drift common to every channel and every position
inflates the statistic roughly uniformly, which turns an empty field into
\BdNCtrl{} detections. The alternatives fail separately: line emission,
because off-frequency controls at the star's own position reach
$|{\rm Re}/\sigma|$ up to \BdOffFreqMax; a common gain or atmospheric mode,
because the median channel-to-channel correlation of the star's time series
is \BdChanCorr; a field source, because the statistic is flat in position
angle and radius across the annulus and because a source on the sky cannot
appear in one execution and be absent from \BdNSibling{} others; and a
signal at the star, because the star is outranked by a third to nine tenths
of its own controls in three of the \BdNCrossBlock{} windows and the
visibility fit returns an imaginary part tens of $\sigma$ from zero. Two of
the \BdNCrossBlock{} sit \BdEdgeChan{} channels from a band edge at the
identical fractional position \BdEdgeFrac{} in basebands exactly
\BdBasebandGHz\,GHz apart, which is a fixed intermediate frequency and not a
sky frequency.

\emph{The quality criterion this survey lacked is the median of the control
ensemble, not its maximum.} The survey median is \BdSurvCtrlMed{} with a
99th percentile of \BdSurvCtrlPNN, and this block's windows are the extreme
of the distribution at \BdBlockCtrlMed{} against \BdSibCtrlLo--\BdSibCtrlHi{}
for its siblings. We report it as a \emph{diagnostic} and deliberately do not
adopt it as an exclusion, because a cut strict enough to remove this block
would also remove the HD~48370 CO positive control.

\emph{The fit does have the power to see a compact source, and that is why
it does not settle these crossings.} An event of statistic $T_\star$ in a
window of trigger flux $S_{\rm min}=5\sigma$ is a flux
$T_\star S_{\rm min}/5$, and the fit reports its own flux uncertainty in the
same units, so a compact source at the stellar position producing the trigger
would return ${\rm Re}/\sigma=\VpExpectLo$ at the rank-flagged unattributed
crossing that has been fitted; no free parameter enters that statement. The
value it returns is $\VpScrObsMax$, \VpScrRatio{} of the expectation. The
visibility fit therefore neither excludes nor establishes anything there:
under the corrected epoch the crossing looks exactly like what a threshold
crossing at the stellar position is defined to be. \emph{Earlier versions of
this paper reported the opposite} --- an exclusion at several $\sigma$ ---
and that exclusion was a product of the reference-epoch defect and is
withdrawn.

\emph{The chain that decides} is therefore the one drawn in
Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}: crossing, then stellar-frame line attribution, then the
\NCtrl-position rank, then recurrence and the block-versus-position test. The
frozen $\pm\LgMaskKms$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ mask attributes \LgNAttr{} of the
\LgNCross{} crossings against \LgExpAttr{} expected by chance from its
\LgMaskOccPct{} per cent occupancy of the searched band, so the mask carries
information; \LgNUnattr{} are unattributed, \LgNUnattrScreen{} of those also
outrank all \NCtrl{} controls, and of those \LgNConfirmed{} recur.
\emph{Nothing survives among the crossings tested}, and
\S\ref{sec:chaingap} says what that qualification covers.

\emph{What a threshold crossing is worth on its own.} The trigger is not a
significance level for a single position in a fine-channel window, and the
survey's own control positions say so. Over the \EpCtrlNA{} Class~A windows
whose \NCtrl-element control vectors are retained, a single control position
reaches the smallest crossing statistic in the survey,
$T=\EpTmin$, \EpCtrlRateA{} per cent of the time (median
\EpCtrlRateAMed{} per cent), against \EpCtrlRateB{} per cent in the coarse
windows. Summing each window's own rate predicts \EpCtrlExpA{} Class~A
windows whose star reaches that value by chance, and
\EpCtrlObsA{} are observed; extrapolated to all \EpCtrlNWinA{} Class~A
windows the chance expectation is of order \EpCtrlExpFullA. \emph{A crossing
at the trigger is an expected event, not a rare one}, which is why the chain
does not stop at one and why the rank screen exists.

\subsubsection{The crossings that are unattributed and localised}
\label{sec:etacrv}

Under the adopted epoch \LgNLocalCorUnattr{} crossings are both localised by
the fit and unattributed by the frozen mask, and under the earlier epoch
\LgNLocalComUnattr{} is: \emph{this paper's earlier statement that there were
none was wrong.} The clearest case is also the clearest demonstration of the
preceding paragraphs.

$\eta$~Crv, \EpEtaEb, Band~7, \EpEtaFreq\,GHz localises under \emph{both}
reference epochs, Re/$\sigma=\EpEtaReCom$ then \EpEtaReCor{} with
${\rm Im}/\sigma=\EpEtaImCor$ against control maxima \EpEtaCtrlCom{} and
\EpEtaCtrlCor, the displacement between the conventions being only
\EpEtaD{} channels so that the epoch defect could not reach it; and it is
unattributed, the nearest frozen-mask transition \EpEtaLineFrozen{} lying
\EpEtaDvFrozen\,km\,s$^{-1}$ away in the stellar frame. \emph{It also fails
the rank screen} --- \EpEtaNGe{} of \EpEtaNCtrl{} control positions equal or
exceed the star, an add-one rank of \EpEtaPRank{} where the screen requires
zero --- \emph{with $T_\star=\EpEtaT$ against a trigger of 5, the smallest
search statistic of all \LgNCross{} crossings.} Those two facts belong with
the localisation every time it is quoted. Its one covering repeat block is
\EpEtaRepDepth{} times deeper (\EpEtaRepRms{} against \EpEtaDiscRms\,mJy),
\EpEtaSepD\,d earlier, and returns $T_\star=\EpEtaRepT$ maximised over its
own drift grid at $\EpEtaRepFlux\pm\EpEtaRepFluxErr$\,mJy against the
discovery's \EpEtaDiscFlux\,mJy, excluding a persistent source at the
discovery flux at $\EpEtaExclSig\sigma$; it is \EpEtaEdgeChan{} channels from
the nearer window edge, and \EpEtaRfiCross{} of the \EpEtaRfiBlocks{} other
blocks covering the same sky frequency carry a crossing.

\emph{A caution about line lists, which generalises beyond this survey.}
Against the wide Splatalogue harvest rather than the frozen mask this
crossing does have a near coincidence, \EpEtaWideSpecies{} at
\EpEtaWideDv\,km\,s$^{-1}$, and it means nothing: at \EpWideNu\,GHz that
harvest holds \EpWideLinesGHz{} transitions per GHz, so a
$\pm\LgMaskKms$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ window contains \EpWideNInMask{} on average
and a \emph{uniformly random} frequency in the spectral window falls within
the mask half-width of some harvested transition \EpWideFrac{} per cent of
the time. A hit is the default outcome, so the wide list can neither
attribute a crossing nor be cited to say that nothing lies near one. The
frozen mask is the only line test in this paper that carries information, and every count above is computed against it. Any
survey quoting a line coincidence at these frequencies should quote the
occupancy of the list it used.

\subsubsection{The end of the chain, and what it still rests on}
\label{sec:chaingap}

\LgNUnattrScreen{} of the \LgNCross{} crossings are both unattributed and
rank-flagged, and the terminal count of \LgNConfirmed{} is what is known
about those two. \EpGapStar{} \EpGapEb{} ($T_\star=\EpGapT$) is the
crossing whose visibility verdict the epoch correction moves furthest, from
Re/$\sigma=\EpGapReCom$ to \EpGapReCor{} at a displacement of \EpGapD{}

largest statistic returned anywhere in them is $T_\star=\LgRecurTMax$,
against the trigger of 5.

\emph{The bound on that statement is stated rather than absorbed.}
\LgNUntest{} crossings in \LgNUntestBlocks{} blocks have no visibility fit,

argument of \S\ref{sec:vistest} that bound is weak --- the fit is a
consistency check and not what disposes of anything --- and it cannot
manufacture a detection: fitting them can only produce a result that has to
be reported alongside the chain, as \EpGapStar's was. The counts of untested
crossings and of crossings without a recurrence test are both assertions in

\subsubsection{Dispositions of the remaining outlier windows}

The third stage-1 flagged window, towards HD~48370 in Band~6, has
$T_\star=\HdTsym$ against a control-ring maximum of \HdRingMax{} ---
star and ring rising together, as emission filling the primary beam does.
The velocities agree: \citet{Cataldi2023} report prominent CO($2{\to}1$)

the circumstellar alternative: the same authors report the disc itself

\subsubsection{Line exclusion: definition and cost}
\label{sec:linecost}

The exclusion regions are defined before any crossing is classified, and
those frequencies are \emph{excluded from the experiment} rather than
vetoed within it. Each catalogued transition of \MaskNSpecNew{} species is
masked to $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the stellar frame;
Appendix~\ref{app:linemask} gives the kinematic justification for that
half-width and the species criterion, and Appendix~\ref{app:linecostfull}
the cost species by species. The statistic is computed inside the masked
channels and the mask applied afterwards, at the reporting stage: the
threshold and rank tests run over every delivered channel, and the mask
then removes the masked intervals from the \emph{reported} search domain,
because a crossing coincident with a catalogued transition cannot be
dispositioned as artificial on these data alone.

That ordering makes a robustness check possible, and removing the mask
entirely adds no candidate. Of the \NHits{} threshold crossings,
\MaskCrossIn{} fall inside a tube, of which \MaskCrossInStageOne{} are
stage-1 flagged windows already dispositioned as CO in
Table~\ref{tab:ledger} and the remaining \MaskCrossInNoFlag{} do not
exceed their own control ensembles. The mask costs \MaskUnionPct{} per
cent of the unique-frequency union, \MaskGrossPct{} per cent of gross
bandwidth, and nothing else. One caveat of principle survives: those rest
frequencies are the `magic' channels \citet{CocconiMorrison1959} argued a
communicator might choose, so a transmitter deliberately parked on one
lies in unsearched reported space and this survey would not claim it
either way. Those channels are retained in the release for anyone bringing
independent spatial or time-domain discrimination.

\subsection{What this experiment excludes, and what it does not}
\label{sec:excludes}\label{sec:pninetysel}

\noindent\emph{Excluded.} For a continuously transmitting, unresolved
carrier lying outside the molecular-line mask, within the searched
frequency range and inside the linear-drift grid, and present during one
of the archival epochs, the complete automated selection recovers the
signal at least nine times in ten above
$P_{90}^{\rm sel}=\PromoteLoA$--$\PromoteHiA$\,W of EIRP per window, and
per system, on each system's best window,
$\PromoteSysLoA$--$\PromoteSysHiA$\,W with a median of
$\PromoteSysMedA$\,W. That per-system median is the figure quoted in the
abstract, in Table~\ref{tab:searchspace} and in the conclusions. No such
carrier was found toward any of the \NSystems{} systems searched.

That number is measured, not assumed. Carriers of known amplitude,
convolved with the instrumental response and given a random sub-channel
phase and a random drift rate within each window's own grid, are added to
the \emph{calibrated visibilities} and recovered through the unmodified
pipeline --- extraction, baseline removal, de-drift stack, trigger and the
\NCtrl-control rank --- and scored on one criterion: peak de-drifted
statistic at the injected cell above the trigger \emph{and} above every
control. The campaign is \SelNTone{} tones over \SelNUnit{} block--window
configurations; it recovers \SelPCFrac{} of tones injected at twenty times
the trigger and \emph{none} of the \SelNullN{} cells scored on the
uninjected passes, so the criterion neither fails on strong signals nor
fires on noise. The tones were deposited through a channel model that
over-attenuates a carrier at a channel boundary by \CrAdoptOverPct{} per
cent (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}), so these powers are pessimistic by at
most that factor and are quoted unaltered rather than corrected by a
model. The control rank is what makes the selection expensive: Class~A
reaches ninety per cent \emph{trigger} recovery at
$\times\SelPNinetyTrigFine$ the nominal trigger power against
$\times\SelPNinetyFine$ for the selection, a further factor of
\SelGateCostFine, while Class~B, whose coarse channels leave the control
ensemble far less disturbed, pays only \SelGateCostCoarse.

Two properties of that measurement bear on how far it should be pushed.
Recovery does not reach unity: at the top rung the trigger fires for
\SelTopTrigFrac{} of Class~A tones and \SelFineTopFrac{} are recovered,
because a bright source at the stellar position leaks into the control
ring through the dirty beam and raises the control maximum with it, from
$\SelCtrlClean\sigma$ on the uninjected pass to $\SelCtrlTopRung\sigma$ at
the top rung. There is a ceiling on selection that transmitter power does
not remove, and it is a property of the control geometry rather than of
the sky. And the Class~A ninety-per-cent point lies close to the end of
the measured ladder: in \SelFineUndefPct{} per cent of the unit-level
bootstrap resamples the Class~A curve does not reach ninety per cent
within the range injected, so $\times\SelPNinetyFine$ should be read with
its interval, $\times\SelPNinetyFineLo$--$\times\SelPNinetyFineHi$, and as
the point at which the measurement runs out rather than a sharp threshold. Figure~\ref{fig:dwellsel} shows the
same completeness against duty cycle and Fig.~\ref{fig:classasens} the
per-system spread and the epoch coverage that limits confirmation;
Appendix~\ref{app:notation} defines the chain from $P_{\rm trig}$ to
$P_{90}^{\rm sel}$.

Read every such value as an upper bound on reach rather than a central
estimate. The campaign measures \SelNUnit{} of the \NWinA{} Class~A
windows directly, for a computational reason --- every injected window is
re-extracted and re-searched end to end over its full drift grid, once per
amplitude --- so the ratio measured on those is applied to the nominal
trigger of every window, and the window-to-window spread of that ratio is
carried as an uncertainty rather than removed. The interval quoted above
is a bootstrap over configurations, not over tones, because tones injected
into one window share its noise realisation, its control ensemble and its
beam. Widening the campaign is the recommendation of
\S\ref{app:futurework} that would most directly improve the headline
number.

Table~\ref{tab:p90budget} is the budget, one line per term: $P_{90}$ is
linear in every factor that builds it and in $d^{2}$, so each entry is a
percentage of the answer, the independent terms combine to
$\pm\BudComb$ per cent dominated by \BudDominant, and three terms of a
different kind are carried separately --- the carrier's unknown position
within a channel, the one-sided atmospheric decorrelation the injected
tones do not suffer, and the window-to-window transfer spread, which is
the largest. The second significant figure of $P_{90}$ is therefore
meaningful for a single injected window and not for the survey.

\emph{Not excluded.} A transmitter absent during the archival observation,
or on for less than about a tenth of the time. Emission inside the
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ molecular-line mask, at any strength.
Drift outside the searched grid, or drift curved enough to leave a channel
within the track. Signals broad enough to be removed with the continuum
baseline. Anything below the window's own completeness threshold. And any
emission from the \NGaiaCensus{} stars within 40\,pc that ALMA has not
observed, which is almost all of them.

\paragraph*{The survey's own on-source time, in both directions.}
\label{sec:exposure}
Two defects of opposite sign were found in the bookkeeping of the exposure,
and neither may be quoted without the other. First, the extractor kept a
single \texttt{FIELD} row per execution block. In \TruncNBlocks{} of the
\ExpNBlocks{} released blocks --- \TruncNWin{} windows
(\TruncPctWin{} per cent) toward \TruncNStars{} stars --- the discarded rows
were not calibrators but further in-beam pointings on the same star, so
\TruncHoursLost{} h of real on-source time out of about \TruncHoursAvail{} h
available, \TruncPctSurvey{} per cent of the survey, was thrown away before a
visibility was read. Recovering it would deepen those windows by a median
factor \TruncGainMed{} in flux density (\TruncEirpMed{} in EIRP), and up to
\TruncGainMax. Nothing published is wrong as a result --- every rms and every
$S_{\rm min}$ was measured from the data actually used --- but the
\emph{achievable} sensitivity in those windows is understated, and a
re-extraction of the affected blocks is in progress. Second, and in the
opposite direction, the released \texttt{on\_source\_s} is
$n_{\rm int}\times\mathrm{median}(\Delta t)$ rather than a sum of integration
times, so sub-scan boundary dumps shorter than the median are over-counted:
\OcNWinMoved{} of \OcNWinReleased{} released windows fall by more than one per
cent under the exact computation and \OcNWinRose{} rise, taking the survey
total from \OcOldMax{} to \OcNewMax\,h on the aggregation the published
figure uses (\ExpTotalMax\,h) and from \OcOldMed{} to \OcNewMed\,h on the
alternative one, that is $-\OcDeltaLo$ to $-\OcDeltaHi$\,h
($\OcPctLo$--$\OcPctHi$ per cent). \emph{The first defect costs the survey
about \TruncHoursLost\,h it had; the second credits it with \OcDeltaLo--\OcDeltaHi\,h
it never had.} The published exposure is therefore approximately right by
accident, to within \ExpNetLo--\ExpNetHi\,h, and both statements belong in the
same paragraph because either alone misrepresents the experiment in the
opposite direction. The reason the loss survived seven rounds of review is
that nothing in the products recorded it: a selection that discards data must
say how much, and this one did not.

\paragraph*{The omitted annual parallax.}
\label{sec:parallax}
The extraction phase-rotates the visibilities to each star's
\emph{barycentric} direction, while the visibilities see the apparent one, so
the annual parallax is omitted from the assumed phase centre. The resulting
amplitude loss is the normalised dirty beam evaluated at (parallax
displacement)/(synthesised beam) --- parameter-free, multiplicative and
shape-preserving --- and it was identified by the external flux-scale check
of Appendix~\ref{sec:absscale}. Measured over the \PxNWin{} retained windows
that carry the astrometric keys, the loss is \PxLossMedPct{} per cent in the
median window, \PxLossNinetyPct{} per cent at the 90th percentile,
\PxLossNNPct{} per cent at the 99th and \PxLossMaxPct{} per cent at worst;
\PxNGtTen{} windows lose more than 10 per cent. It is negligible throughout
the ACA data that dominate this survey and matters only for nearby stars
observed in long-baseline 12\,m configurations. \emph{We state it and do not
apply it.} Correcting it would move the median per-system
$P_{90}^{\rm sel}$ from $\PxSysMedBase$ to $\PxSysMedCorr\times10^{15}$\,W
(\PxSysMedShiftPct{} per cent) and the median Class~A window from
$\PxWinMedBase$ to $\PxWinMedCorr\times10^{15}$\,W, and would leave the
number of systems reaching $10^{15}$\,W unchanged at \PxNSysCorr, because a
long-baseline block is never a system's most sensitive window. Two things
about the direction must be said explicitly. The sign is
\emph{optimistic}: every limit quoted here is too deep by $1/(1-{\rm loss})$
in its own window. And the completeness campaign cannot see the term at all,
because its tones are deposited at the same assumed position the estimator
evaluates at --- the third instance in this work of a validation being
insensitive to the error it exists to catch. The defect is repaired in the
extraction code and the repair is carried by the re-extraction described
above; the released catalogue is not retrospectively corrected by a model.

Two blind spots remain. The search is Stokes~$I$ only: a fully polarised
carrier is not missed, but the discrimination polarisation would offer
against unpolarised astrophysical emission is unavailable. ALMA's feeds
are linear, so a per-hand search would help only a carrier aligned with
one feed, and a circularly polarised one --- the electron-cyclotron maser
case of \S\ref{sec:bpicval} --- divides equally between XX and YY and
gains nothing; its circularity is in the cross-hands, which this analysis
does not extract. And the frequency coverage is the
archive's, not a choice: none of the ``magic frequency'' arguments that
have guided targeted searches --- the 1420\,MHz line, its multiples, the
water hole, or $\pi$ and $e$ times them --- falls in an ALMA band, so a
transmitter deliberately placed on one is outside this experiment by
construction and its absence here is not evidence against it.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Everything below is conditional on what the archive chose to observe.
\PctDiscProposal{} per cent of the searched stars were observed for
debris-disc or planet-formation science, which skews the sample young,
dusty and dynamically active and puts circumstellar CO --- the source of
\NStageOneLine{} of the \NStageOneWin{} stage-1 events --- in the field of
a large fraction of the windows (\S\ref{sec:bias}). The quantities that
follow describe \emph{these} stars and not the 40\,pc population.

\subsection{What signals could ALMA archival data actually detect?}
\label{sec:detectable}

Three morphologies set the reach of the archive, each illustrated with a
real window and with the completeness entries of
Table~\ref{tab:classcomp} and Fig.~\ref{fig:sens2d} behind it.
Denominators count the \NSystems{} systems whose deepest threshold in the
class lies at or below the case power.

\emph{(i) A continuous, frequency-stationary carrier} in a coarse channel
is the best case. The UV~Ceti pair at 2.7\,pc has a deepest window whose
trigger is $1.6\times10^{13}$\,W and whose effective threshold is
$\EirpEffDeepest$\,W, and a channel-confined carrier there is recovered at
near-ideal $\sqrt{N}$ gain (\S\ref{sec:dwell}). Read on the effective
threshold, the comparison a transmitter power calls for,
\CaseDetEffArec{} systems of \NSystems{} reach the numerical EIRP of the
Arecibo planetary radar and \CaseDetEffArecTwo{} twice it; on the trigger
the figures are \CaseDetCoarseNow{} and \CaseDetCoarseTwoNow. The
survey's best case is a few times that numerical EIRP.

\emph{(ii) A continuously drifting carrier} in a fine 488-kHz channel is
the only morphology with a measured end-to-end recovery curve
(Appendix~\ref{app:inject}): above ${\sim}8\times10^{13}$\,W nominal it is
recovered in \RecTwice{} per cent of trials with its drift inside the
searched ceiling, a depth \CaseDetFineNow{} systems reach in Class~A.

\emph{(iii) An intermittent carrier, on for a quarter of the track,} above
${\sim}2\times10^{14}$\,W is detected toward \CaseDetDwellNow{}
mid-distance systems, and recovery falls rapidly at smaller dwell
fractions (Table~\ref{tab:dwell}): below $f_{\rm dwell}\sim0.1$ only the
nearest windows carry information.



\subsection{What does the null result mean?}
\label{sec:null}

No persistent, independently confirmed unresolved spectral-carrier
technosignature has been found within the domain searched
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). \emph{The null result primarily constrains
persistent spectral carriers.} For \EpNoConfirm{} of the \EpTot{} systems
the archive provides no independent epoch capable of testing recurrence at
all --- \EpOne{} were observed in a single block and \EpSameDay{} have all
their blocks within one day --- so for close to half the sample the
experiment places little constraint on a low-duty-cycle emitter, whatever
its power. Only \EpYears{} systems can be tested for recurrence over
years. The EIRP$_{\rm trig}$ values are nominal trigger powers
(\S\ref{sec:method}), and completeness-calibrated exclusion holds only in
Class~A, the \NWinA{} fine-channel windows where the measured recovery
curve applies; the \NWinB{} coarse Class~B windows provide
spectral-excess coverage only and cannot discriminate drift.

The drift ceiling cuts the other way. It is derived from line-of-sight
Doppler acceleration --- of the \NExoPlanetsTab{} planets known around the
\NExoHostsTab{} hosts only TRAPPIST-1\,b reaches it in its edge-on worst
case --- and does not model transmitter-side oscillator drift, so a
transmitter whose own reference drifts faster than the kinematic budget
falls outside the searched grid (Fig.~\ref{fig:accel}), as does one parked
inside the line mask (\S\ref{sec:linecost}).

Set against \citet{Wright2018}'s haystack, only Band~3 falls inside that
axis's span, which their \S3.2.2 sets at 10\,MHz--115\,GHz --- the
ionospheric cutoff to the top of the Green Bank Telescope's instrumentation
--- (\UnionBandThree{} of 115\,GHz); the
remaining \UnionBandsFourEight\,GHz in \UnionHiBandLabel is new territory, though
the median threshold detects a $10^{13}$\,W transmitter only within
${\sim}0.9$\,pc. The sole prior ALMA-archival search
\citep[][\MasonNStar{} stars in \MasonNCal{} calibrator fields at
$\geq\MasonNearKpc$\,kpc]{Mason2025} is four orders of magnitude more
distant, so an EIRP ratio between the two would be almost entirely the
distance term. The quantity that does not depend on how far the target
happens to lie is the minimum detectable \emph{received} flux,
$F_{\rm min}=S_{\rm min}\,\delta\nu$, and it must be quoted at a matched
channel width, because $S_{\rm min}$ falls as $\delta\nu^{-1/2}$ while
$F_{\rm min}$ rises as $\delta\nu^{1/2}$. Mason et~al.\ reach
\MasonFluxLo--\MasonFluxHi\,W\,m$^{-2}$ across their four fields, on a
\MasonChanKHz\,kHz channel at a bare $5\sigma$ threshold. Rescaled to that
channel width and on that same threshold convention --- this paper's
$P_{\rm trig}$, not $P_{90}^{\rm sel}$ --- the Class~A windows here reach
\FluxMatchBest\,W\,m$^{-2}$ at best, a factor \FluxBestRatio{} deeper, and
\FluxMatchMed\,W\,m$^{-2}$ at the median, a factor \FluxMedRatio{}
shallower. Carried through to this survey's own completeness convention,
the median becomes \FluxSelMatchMed\,W\,m$^{-2}$, a factor
\FluxSelMedRatio{} shallower than their threshold; Mason et~al.\ quote no
recovery-based completeness, so that last comparison is not like for like
and is given only to show the size of the convention.
Comparison is in general better made
axis by axis than on a single figure of merit: this survey differs from
centimetre-wave searches in frequency, spectral resolution, number of
systems, EIRP completeness, drift range and temporal coverage at once, and
those axes are coupled, so a product of marginals would misstate the
overlap by orders of magnitude. Figure~\ref{fig:context} makes the central
point, that the novelty is the frequency regime and not depth; on the
conventional Continuous Waveform Transmitter Figure of Merit
(Appendix~\ref{app:cwtfm}) this survey is about four orders of magnitude
less competitive than major centimetre-wave programmes.

How far the null reaches is set by recurrence rather than by the rank
statistic. No single event in this design can clear the survey-wide
threshold (Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}), so a genuine but weak
transmitter seen once is not separable from the \ClustStageMean{} chance
events the survey expects over its Class~A windows --- exactly the position
CP$-$72~2713 leaves us in --- and what separates them is a second epoch or an independent
astrophysical identification. A repeat block is not necessarily a second epoch:
\textbf{only \EpIndep{} of the \EpTot{} systems, \LgxIndepPct{} per cent,
have \emph{searched} epochs separated by more than \EpSep{} day}
(Table~\ref{tab:epochclassa}), and only \EpYears{} systems, \LgxYearsPct{} per
cent, have baselines extending over years --- so recurrence on a
year time-scale can be tested for fewer than a third of the sample. Three
figures are easily confused here and each is quoted with its criterion:
\LgxMultiSearched{} systems (\LgxMultiSearchedPct{} per cent) have more than
one \emph{searched} execution block; \LgxMultiArch{} (\LgxMultiArchPct{} per
cent) have more than one \emph{archived} block, searched or not, the extra
\NSysRecArchOnly{} holding a second block that was never processed; and
\EpIndep{} (\LgxIndepPct{} per cent) have searched epochs more than a day
apart. Extending the search to every archived block therefore cannot deliver
\LgxMultiArchPct{} per cent of the sample as a recurrence test: only
\LgxDayFracPct{} per cent of multi-block systems clear the one-day bar on the
blocks already searched, so the realistic projection is about \LgxProjN{}
systems, \LgxProjPct{} per cent, with no new observing time.

\subsection{The two array configurations}
\label{sec:acalimit}

The \NWinSevenM{} ACA windows are \PctWinSevenM{} per cent of the survey and
are screened on the same footing as the 12\,m windows, each with a control
annulus scaled to its own primary and synthesised beams
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}). The array configuration does not enter the
sensitivity at all: the control geometry enters the \emph{screen}, while
$P_{\rm trig}$, $P_{\rm eff}$, $P_{90}$ and $P_{90}^{\rm sel}$ are set by
the noise in each window and by the injection campaign. The deepest trigger
and the deepest $P_{\rm eff}$ in the survey are both 12\,m windows. What
the rank is worth in each array, and why no rank against \NCtrl{} controls
can carry survey-level significance, is in
Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.

\subsection{The null result has not been checked against an independent
pipeline}
\label{sec:nocross}

Every validation reported here tests this pipeline against itself, and the
only prior ALMA-archival search \citep{Mason2025} targeted stars beyond
1\,kpc, disjoint from this sample, so no external comparison is available.
\textbf{The null result therefore rests on internal validation alone.} The
release includes \KatNCases{} known-answer test vectors generated from first
principles --- Gaussian noise plus a closed-form drifting sinusoid, with no
import from the search or injection modules --- and a re-implementation of
Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} run on them meets all \KatNPass{} stated expectations:
the null reaches only $T=\KatNull$ anywhere on the grid, the drifting
$5\sigma$ carrier returns $T=\KatDrift$ at its own drift trial against
$\KatDriftZero$ at zero drift, and the off-grid drift returns
$\KatOffGrid$, \KatOffGridLoss{} per cent lower, which is the grid-loss
term the completeness curve has to carry. The stationary case returns
$\KatStat$ rather than 5: the injected cell carries one unit-variance noise
draw and this realisation's is low, which is the scatter a single vector can
show and not a defect.

\subsection{What this sample can and cannot speak for} \label{sec:bias}


Selection is on archival coverage, and ALMA's archive was not built to
sample stars. \NDiskCatStars{} of the \NStars{} searched stars were
observed under proposals categorised \emph{Disks and planet formation}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:selbias}), and \NDebrisKwStars{} carry the \emph{Debris
disks} science keyword. Among the \NTeffSample{} searched stars with a
Gaia temperature, \MNSample{} are M~dwarfs, \MPctSample{} per cent,
against \MPctCensus{} per cent of the reference census
(Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}), so the sample is F- and G-enriched and M-dwarf
deficient; and window counts are concentrated, three systems ---
\ConcThreeNames{} --- carrying \ConcThreePct{} per cent of the searched
windows.

Two consequences follow, and they run in opposite directions. A
debris-disc-dominated sample is the worst case for astrophysical false
positives at millimetre wavelengths, which is where the stage-1 flagged
windows of \S\ref{sec:technosearch} concentrate, so the vetting burden
here is heavier than a blind sample would carry. And M~dwarfs host most of
the habitable-zone planets in the solar neighbourhood while only
\NMSearched{} of the \NMCensus{} within 40\,pc carry a searched window,
so this result carries very little demographic weight for the local
habitable-zone planet population. Both counts are restricted to the
temperature-classified sample of Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}, and illustrate
the selection rather than measure it.

The point is sharper in targets than in percentages. Six of the searched
M~dwarfs host a planet that some published analysis places in the
habitable zone --- Proxima~Centauri, TRAPPIST-1, LHS~1140, GJ~581, HN~Lib
and GJ~273 (\BdStar) with GJ~273\,b \citep{AstudilloDefru2017}, which
earlier versions of this list omitted --- and since habitable-zone
membership is model-dependent and two of these are contested, six is an
order of magnitude rather than a census. That is this survey's coverage of the class of target most current
technosignature programmes prioritise, against \NMCensus{} M~dwarfs within
40\,pc. Sun-like stars are sampled relatively heavily by comparison, but
still through a highly non-random, disc-selected archive: the survey
constrains transmitters around the stars ALMA happened to point at, not
the local stellar population.

Useful subsamples still fall out of archival selection. \NExoHostsTab{} of
the \NStars{} processed stars (\NExoPlanetsTab{} known planets) are
confirmed exoplanet hosts, among them Proxima~Centauri --- the subject of
the most closely scrutinised radio technosignature candidate to date, the
Breakthrough Listen signal of interest blc1, which was ultimately
attributed to local interference \citep{Sheikh2021} --- $\tau$~Ceti,
$\epsilon$~Eridani, $\beta$~Pictoris and the seven-planet TRAPPIST-1
system, while HN~Lib and LHS~1140, each with a habitable-zone planet, now
carry an ALMA technosignature non-detection. Four are white dwarfs \citep{GentileFusillo2021}, among the few
mm/submm SETI constraints for that class \citep{PollutedWD2026}.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=4pt,topsep=4pt,parsep=0pt]

\item \textbf{Search performed.} \NWinA{} fine-channel spectral windows
toward \NSysClassA{} independent stellar systems, covering
$\Delta\nu_A=\DnuA$\,GHz of unique sky frequency, searched for an
unresolved spectral carrier whose frequency drifts. A secondary
coarse-channel spectral-excess search covered a further
$\Delta\nu_{B\rm -only}=\DnuBonly$\,GHz in \NWinB{} windows but cannot
discriminate drift. Both come from a uniform reduction of \LedProcessed{}
public execution blocks, \TotalDataTB\,TB of raw data.

\item \textbf{Sensitivity.} Measured by injection and recovery through the
complete automated selection, not inferred from a nominal threshold: per
system, on each system's best window,
$P_{90}^{\rm sel}=\PromoteSysLoA$--$\PromoteSysHiA$\,W of equivalent
isotropic radiated power with a median of $\PromoteSysMedA$\,W. This is an
upper bound on reach rather than a central estimate, the calibration
being uncertain by
$\times\SelTransFineLo$--$\times\SelTransFineHi$, and a one-sided
$+\BudDecorLo$ to $+\BudDecorHi$ per cent
decorrelation bias can only make the figure optimistic.

\item \textbf{Result.} All \LgNCross{} threshold crossings are reported
with their dispositions (Table~\ref{tab:ledger}). \LgNAttr{} are
attributable to circumstellar or foreground carbon monoxide, among them
the $\beta$~Pictoris debris disc recovered blind in two transitions;
\LgNUnattr{} are unattributed and \LgNUnattrScreen{} of those also outrank
all \NCtrl{} spatial controls. \textbf{Neither of those two is
independently confirmed: neither recurs in the available repeat
observations, and \LgNConfirmed{} crossing survives the chain among those
tested.} The bound on that statement is stated rather than absorbed:
\LgNUntest{} crossings in \LgNUntestBlocks{} blocks are not yet fitted in
the visibilities (\S\ref{sec:chaingap}), a test which in any case disposes
of nothing.
Non-recurrence is the load-bearing evidence, and it is weak in time: the
repeats are separated from the event by no more than \UnattSepMaxD\,d, so
they constrain intermittent emission poorly. The screen assigns no
calibrated false-alarm probability: no rank against \NCtrl{} controls can
carry survey-level significance, whatever its null (\S\ref{sec:floor}).
\emph{The visibility-domain test does not carry this conclusion, and in
earlier versions of this paper it was reported as doing so
(\S\ref{sec:vistest}).}

\item \textbf{Interpretation.} No persistent, independently confirmed
unresolved spectral carrier was found within the frequency, drift-rate,
duty-cycle and sensitivity domain searched. \textbf{This cannot be
converted into an occurrence constraint on technological civilisations
within 40\,pc.} The sample is archive-selected and strongly incomplete:
\PctDiscProposal{} per cent of the searched stars come from disc and
planet-formation programmes, only \NMSearched{} of \NMCensus{} catalogued
M~dwarfs within 40\,pc are covered, and the median system is searched over
\SysUnionAMed\,GHz rather than the survey's \DnuA\,GHz union. Occurrence
figures, hosting fractions and figures of merit require a denominator this
sample does not supply, and are confined to Appendix~\ref{app:cwtfm},
conditional on these stars, frequencies and epochs and on nothing wider.

\emph{These EIRP limits apply specifically to carriers unresolved by
ALMA's \ChanLoA--\ChanHiA\,kHz Class~A channels, and should not be read
as equivalent in signal class or spectral resolution to Hz-resolution
narrowband SETI limits.}

\end{enumerate}

\noindent
We find no independently confirmed unresolved spectral carrier among the
\NSysClassA{} systems searched with drift-resolving Class~A data. The result
constrains transmitters only within the observed frequency intervals, the
searched acceleration and drift range, the measured completeness limits of
\S\ref{sec:pninetysel} and the archival temporal sampling; and because the
targets are strongly archive-selected --- chosen by other astronomers for
disc and planet-formation science, and deficient in M~dwarfs --- it does
\emph{not} constitute an occurrence-rate constraint on technological
transmitters in the 40\,pc stellar population.

\section*{What this paper did, and where the details are}

In plain terms, this paper asks whether any nearby star is transmitting a
narrow radio signal that ALMA happened to record while observing something
else. ALMA's public archive holds thousands of hours of millimetre and
submillimetre observations taken for quite different reasons --- mostly
studies of dust discs and planet formation --- and every one of those
observations also contains a spectrum of the star at the centre of the
field. A transmitter would show up in such a spectrum as a spike confined
to one or two channels, sitting at the star's position and not anywhere
else in the field, and reappearing if the star were observed again. We
searched the archive for exactly that, with a single pipeline applied
without human intervention. Of the \NSystems{} systems
covered, \NSysClassA{} have channels fine enough to resolve the drift a
transmitter on a moving planet would show; those \NWinA{} windows are the
primary experiment, and the remainder can register an unexplained spike
but cannot tell a drifting signal from a stationary one.

Nothing survived. \LgNCross{} threshold crossings were found;
\LgNAttr{} of them fall within $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of a
catalogued transition, most of them carbon monoxide from the
$\beta$~Pictoris debris disc, which the pipeline recovered without being
told it was there. \NStageOneWin{} windows were also picked out by the
automatic spatial screen; \NStageOneLine{} of those carry a line
attribution and the remaining \NStageOneUnattrib{} do not, and neither of
those two reappears in a later observation, though the repeats available
span days rather than years and so test intermittent emission only weakly.
An earlier version of this paper added that neither is a compact source at
the stellar position in the raw interferometric data; that finding came
from a visibility estimator referred to the wrong epoch and is withdrawn
(\S\ref{sec:vistest}). The result is therefore a non-detection, and
the useful part of the paper is the calibration: how deep the search
actually reached, for what kind of signal, and over what fraction of the
frequencies and epochs a real transmitter would have had to use.

Two cautions matter more than the limit itself. The targets were chosen by
other astronomers for other purposes, so the sample is not representative
of the solar neighbourhood and cannot speak for M~dwarfs, which dominate
that population but are barely represented here. And the star-versus-control ranking used to pick candidates is a
prioritisation tool, not a significance test: it cannot assign a
probability to any individual window, which is why every disposition here
rests on physical evidence instead.

(\ref{app:table}); the line mask (\ref{app:linemask}); the continuum lane
false-alarm accounting (\ref{app:falsealarm});
provenance, extraction geometry and robustness checks (\ref{app:repro});

\subsection{Extraction, control geometry and position-correlated
systematics}
\label{app:extraction}

The baseline filter takes the median in contiguous blocks of \MedWin{}
channels and interpolates linearly between block centres, using
$\max(2,n/\MedWin)$ blocks over $n$ usable channels. Its effective width is
\MedWin{} channels in the \NWinFullWidth{} windows long enough to hold more
than two blocks and $n/2$ in the short coarse windows, capping the linewidth
it passes at ${\approx}\MedWinCeilFineMHz$\,MHz for the fine class and
${\approx}\MedWinCeilCoarseGHz$\,GHz for the coarse one
(Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). The crossing channel is \emph{not} excluded
from the scale. The median runs across positions at a fixed channel, so what
matters is how many of the \NCtrl{} controls carry the feature: a compact
feature at the star appears in none and the 50-per-cent breakdown point
holds, while emission filling the primary beam appears in all of them, as for
HD~48370 (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}).

$T_\star \equiv T(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ is evaluated at the
proper-motion-propagated stellar position, and the same operation, drift
grid, channelisation and noise map give $\{T_i\}$ at all \NCtrl{} control
positions. \texttt{probe\_positions()} in the released extraction code adds
its offsets to the star's own direction cosines, so the annulus is centred on
the \emph{star} wherever the star sits in the field. It holds \RingNProbe{}
positions drawn uniform in area, $r=\sqrt{U(r_{\rm in}^{2},r_{\rm out}^{2})}$,
and uniform in azimuth, from the fixed seed \RingSeed{} re-applied per window,
so all windows share one pattern in units of $\theta_{\rm PB}$. Radii run
$\RingInFrac$--$\RingOutFrac\,\theta_{\rm PB}$ with $\theta_{\rm PB}$
evaluated at each window's own median frequency, and an inner check-ring of
\RingNSrc{} positions serves the point-source test. The geometry ships as
catalogue columns but is absent from the frozen products, so it is
reconstructed from the code; the reconstruction recovers the check-ring's
\RingNSrc{} positions, and the control statistics fall with reconstructed
radius where extended CO fills the beam (Spearman $\rho=\RingRhoCO$,
HD~48370 Band~6).

Placing controls off-centre costs sensitivity. A star near the pointing
centre sits at primary-beam response ${\approx}1$ while the annulus samples
\RingGainIn{} at $\RingInFrac\,\theta_{\rm PB}$ falling to \RingGainOut{} at
$\RingOutFrac\,\theta_{\rm PB}$, area-weighted mean \RingGainMean. Star and
controls are therefore not exchangeable in flux density. Forming
$T(\mathbf{x})$ on uncorrected amplitudes against one shared noise scale
removes that particular asymmetry from the ranked quantity, but it does not
establish exchangeability and earlier versions of this paper wrongly said it
did: the out-of-sample test of Appendix~\ref{sec:heldout} shows the stellar
ranks are displaced even on windows the pipeline had never seen. \rankstatus{} Extended sky brightness does not cancel: smooth emission
lives on short baselines whose phases barely change under a rotation of a few
arcsec, so star and annulus rise \emph{together}, as for HD~48370, while
emission offset from the star raises particular controls above it, as in the
same star's $^{13}$CO window, whose ring maximum sits \HdThirteenOffset\,arcsec
from the star (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). The two
residual asymmetries run in opposite directions: a suppressed control makes a
star-exceeds-ring outcome more likely under the null, while a multiplicative
calibration error scales with the continuum flux at the extracted position
and so favours the star.

Neighbouring controls are correlated below the synthesised-beam scale, so a
window's annulus carries $N_{\rm eff}\simeq30$--$43$ independent spatial
trials. That is an analytic beam-geometry estimate and a worst case, the
annulus containing far more resolution elements than the \NCtrlCap{}
positions drawn. The rank test needs exchangeability rather than
independence, and correlation among controls costs resolution without
touching the $1/\RankFloor$ floor. Radii are quoted in arcsec and in
$\theta_{\rm PB}$ because the frozen products carry neither the synthesised
beam nor the array configuration; the \PctWinSevenM{} per cent of windows
taken with the ACA 7\,m array are treated in Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.

Residual continuum, self-calibration residuals and position-correlated
calibration error all concentrate at or near the phase centre, where the
pointed science target sits, and $\sigma$ is built from the annulus and
carries none of it. These stars are not all blank there --- an ancillary continuum lane detects
17 of 57 target/bands --- and a
residual bandpass ripple of fractional amplitude $\epsilon$ leaves a
channel-confined artefact of order $\epsilon\,S_{\rm cont}$ at the extracted
position and essentially nothing on the annulus. Three measurements bound the
effect. The star's own residual variance equals the controls' to better than
a per cent in the flagged windows (\S\ref{sec:rsigma}). Recomputing the noise
scale locally, in a band around each feature and excluding the feature
itself, gives local-to-global ratios of 0.97 (CP$-$72~2713), \HdLocalRatio{}
(HD~48370), 0.93 ($\beta$~Pic~B3) and 1.02 ($\beta$~Pic~B6); the global scale
is therefore good to a few per cent except towards HD~48370, where the field
is filled with CO, and rescaling both the statistic and its ensemble there
leaves the window flagged ($T_\star=\HdCorrectedT$ against \HdCorrectedRing).
A badly misstated $\sigma$ would also displace the control distribution,
which the uniform pseudo-star ranks exclude. A per-crossing local scale
inside the search remains an open item (\S\ref{app:futurework}).

The statistic evaluates one position at the star and one at each control,
because a rank means something only when the identical operation runs
everywhere; \S\ref{sec:aumic} scores every window under the asymmetric
region-maximum form as well and audits the difference.

\subsection{Statistical units and independence} \label{sec:units}

Six units appear in this paper and they are not interchangeable. A
\emph{catalogue entry} is one star as SIMBAD designates it, \NStars{}
searched. A \emph{physical system} is the set of entries gravitationally
bound together, \NSystems{} of them, the difference being seven
designation-linked pairs. A \emph{target/band} dataset is one entry in one
ALMA band, \NStarBands{} in all. An \emph{execution block} is one archival
observation, \NEB{} searched, and is the unit that shares weather,
calibration and correlator setup. A \emph{spectral window} is one window of
one execution block: \NWindows{} catalogue rows, reducing to
\NDistinctDatasets{} distinct (execution block, window) datasets once
repeated archive products are collapsed. An \emph{independent frequency
trial} is none of these, the channel$\times$drift grid being oversampled on
both axes, so a window's \CellsWinMin--\CellsWinMax{} cells carry about a
factor \FaOverCount{} fewer independent trials than cells
(Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}). An \emph{independent epoch} is a separate
execution block at the same tuning; blocks of one scheduling block executed
on one night are not independent epochs in any useful sense.

The survey-level flag expectation uses \NWindows{} windows, which is
conservative because \NDistinctDatasets{} is the smaller number and because
windows within a block are correlated. Statements about how common
transmitters are use \NSystems{} systems, never \NStars{} entries and never
\NWindows{} windows; statements about persistence use epochs, of which most
stars here have one.


\subsection{Data-quality exclusions and open items}
\label{sec:exclusions}

\NStarBands{} target/bands are searched to completion and reported here. Four
classes of entry are absent from the released catalogue and are itemised in
Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}.

A further \NAudit{} candidate target/bands were selected but never delivered
a searchable product, and the cause lies in the selection step.
\NNotPointed{} of them were never observed at all, sitting a median
\SepMedianDeg{} degrees from the nearest field centre: those observations
were pointed at solar-system targets whose quoted field of view is enormous,
and a crossmatch that trusts that quantity admits stars far outside any real
primary beam. The remaining \NPointed{} entries were pointed at but yielded
no search product. The per-star pointing ledger is released so that the
selection can be audited. \NNeverObsStars{} of the \NCoveredCand{} candidate
stars fail on pointing in every band, so the sample is \NStars{} of at most
about \NGenuinelyCovered{} genuinely covered stars rather than \NStars{} of
\NCoveredCand, and the coverage completeness of Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}
should be read against the smaller denominator.

One automated quality check precedes any sensitivity number. The radiometer
relation makes
$q\equiv\sigma\sqrt{t_{\rm on}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}}\propto{\rm SEFD}/\sqrt{N_{\rm bl}}$
a quantity physics predicts, so a window whose $q$ falls far below the sample
median cannot be a noise fluctuation. \NDefect{} coarse time-division windows
fail by about three decades, all of them channel-averaged auxiliary windows
riding on a science baseband and carrying a constant WEIGHT column, so they
never received that baseband's calibration. The deflation is common mode in
data and weights alike, so it misstates a threshold but cannot manufacture a
flag: those windows are excluded from the sensitivity accounting only, and
their statistics and ranks were carried through candidate generation
unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}).

\NDup{} of the \NExtracted{} extracted rows are duplicate entries for one
physical window, the same (star, execution block, frequency range, channel
width) key appearing twice in the archive query, so one row per key is the
right statistical unit. The retention rule --- the key's first-listed row in
the query's own identifier order --- consults no measured value and cannot
prefer a crossing or a deeper threshold. Repeats need not agree: two
reductions of the same $\tau$~Ceti Band~6 window differ by 44 per cent in
threshold, which exceeds the flux-scale systematic of \S\ref{sec:frames} and
is the most direct empirical bound here on threshold reproducibility, and the
two HR~1010 Band~6 rows straddle the trigger at $T_\star=5.10$ and $4.95$, so
the crossing count is rule-dependent at the level of one window. The four
flagged windows are not, no discarded row being both a crossing and a stage-1
flagged window. The line-excluded continuum lane is exploratory, bears on no
technosignature limit, and is reported separately.


\subsubsection{CP$-$72~2713: an unconfirmed event, absent in the repeat
block}
\label{sec:cp72}

CP$-$72~2713 is a worked example of the full vetting chain rather than a
result: at the survey's one chance expectation (Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm})
\ClustStageMean{} such events are expected over the Class~A windows and
\NStageOneUnattrib{} are observed. In Band~7 it reached
$T_\star=5.81$ against a ring maximum of 5.68, with no transition
\emph{of the frozen mask} within $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the
stellar frame. A wider catalogue does put one nearby --- SO
$N_J=8_8$--$7_7$ at $-12.3$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in this block's own stellar frame
--- and by the calibration of \S\ref{sec:etacrv} that is the default
outcome rather than evidence: over this window the harvest holds 39
transitions per GHz and a uniformly random frequency falls within
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of one of them 91 per cent of the time, the
nearest here being an unidentified line and not SO. Two measurements close
it independently of any line list: the feature is 3 channels wide at the
recovered drift against 2 for the instrumental response, where a warm
circumstellar SO line would be resolved; and CO($3{\to}2$) lies in this same
window and is absent, below a 5$\sigma$ limit fainter than the crossing
itself. Sulphur-bearing species are not in the frozen mask and were not in
it before the search. It is one of the
\LgNUntest{} crossings whose visibility fit is outstanding
(\S\ref{sec:chaingap}); the value earlier versions of this paper quoted for
it came from an estimator referred to the wrong epoch and is withdrawn. It
acquired no astrophysical attribution and was recorded as an unattributed
stage-1 flagged window under promote and retire criteria committed
\CpCritLeadHours{} hours before the second block was searched (\CpCritDate,
\texttt{\CpCritHash}).

That second block matches the first in tuning, channel width, channel count,
integration count and on-source time, registers to \CpRecOffChan{} of a
channel, and is the deeper of the two, so a persistent emitter at the first
block's flux would have returned $T_\star=\CpRecExpT$. Pinned to the first
block's own frequency and drift it returns \CpRecT, and read as two
measurements of one constant amplitude the pair is inconsistent at
$\CpRecExclPair\sigma$. The blocks are \CpSepH\,h apart, so this is a
within-night test: it excludes a persistent emitter of that strength on that
baseline and excludes nothing about an intermittent one. The disposition
rests on that recurrence test and not on the rank; the frequency, drift,
control and continuum diagnostics are in Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}.


\subsection{Sample bookkeeping: entry counts, coverage criteria and
pointed versus serendipitous}
\label{app:bookkeeping}

The \NCensus{} stars are complete against, and conditional only on, the
40-parsec reference catalogue intersected with the public-archive snapshot of
2026 September~8. Against that Gaia~DR3 catalogue of \NGaiaCensus{} stars
they are $\lesssim$1 per cent, and the \NSystems{} systems searched are
\PctSysOfCensus{} per cent, fewer still once \S\ref{sec:exclusions} removes
those never observed (Fig.~\ref{fig:funnel}). The two parallax cuts are not
interchangeable, the sample's binding condition being
$\sigma_\varpi/\varpi<\PlxAdqlPct$ per cent against the census's 10 per cent;
the realised sample reaches \PlxWorstPct{} per cent at worst.

Stellar counts throughout are catalogue-entry counts. The \NStars{} searched
entries comprise \NSystems{} systems, differing by seven designation-linked
component pairs, so the \NStarBands{} star/band datasets, \NWindows{} windows
and \NEB{} execution blocks all derive from \NSystems{} systems. The
\NWindows{} rows comprise \NDistinctDatasets{} distinct (execution block,
spectral window) datasets; the larger number is the trials denominator
throughout, which is conservative and makes no practical difference,
predicting \ExpDistinctDen{} chance outliers against \BootPoisson{} for the
smaller.

Admission to the sample required the following.

\begin{enumerate}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt,parsep=0pt,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item At least one public execution block whose visibilities are the
  archive's delivered QA2 products, taken as delivered and not
  re-calibrated, covering the star's epoch-propagated position. Calibration
  vintage therefore varies across the 2013--2025 span; the flux-scale term
  this contributes is small beside the $\times\HanFacMed$ spectral-response
  correction. There is no minimum integration, sensitivity, antenna count,
  mosaic-tile count or array configuration beyond QA2.
\item That position inside the field's primary beam. Extraction aborts
  beyond $2\,\theta_{\rm PB}$ and the largest retained offset is
  $\PbMaxRoverTheta\,\theta_{\rm PB}$.
\item Each spectral window admitted individually, on QA2 vetting alone.
\item No programme-category restriction: science, calibration and
  observatory projects enter on identical terms.
\item A fixed archive snapshot date, 2026 September~8.
\end{enumerate}

Completeness with respect to the work list holds in targets and tunings, not
in epochs or integration time: the survey takes at most \MaxSearchedEBStar{}
blocks per target and de-duplicates spectral windows by identifier, so \NEB{}
of the \NProgenitorEBTotal{} public execution blocks those targets hold are
in this release, \PctEbSearched{} per cent, with \CampBlocks{} further blocks
reserved as an external calibration sample. ALMA project metadata quantify
pointed against serendipitous coverage: almost every spectral-window-covered
star is the pointed science target in at least one execution block, leaving
Wolf~219 the sole serendipitous star, so the residual selection function of a
work-list-complete sample is the union of ALMA target lists.

\section{The conventional figure of merit}
\label{app:cwtfm}

These are the standard occurrence metrics, computed so the survey can be
placed on the axes the literature uses. Following
\citet{Enriquez2017}, the Transmitter Rate is
$(N_\star\nu_{\rm rel})^{-1}$ and the Continuous Waveform Transmitter
Figure of Merit is
%
\begin{equation}
{\rm CWTFM}=\zeta_{\rm AO}\,\frac{\rm EIRP}{N_\star\,\nu_{\rm rel}},
\qquad \nu_{\rm rel}=\frac{\Delta\nu_{\rm tot}}{\nu_{\rm mid}},
\label{eq:cwtfm}
\end{equation}
%
with $\zeta_{\rm AO}=\OccZeta$ fixed by their normalisation
(${\rm CWTFM}=1$ at ${\rm EIRP}=10^{13}$\,W, $\nu_{\rm rel}=1/2$,
$N_\star=1000$) and lower values better. On the Class~A experiment,
$N_\star=\OccNSysA$ systems and $\Delta\nu_{\rm tot}=\OccDnuSurvey$\,GHz
about $\nu_{\rm mid}=\OccNuMid$\,GHz give
$\nu_{\rm rel}=\OccNuRelSurvey$, a Transmitter Rate of \OccTRSurvey{} and,
at the median per-system $P_{90}^{\rm sel}=\OccEirpMed$\,W,
${\rm CWTFM}=\OccCwtfmMed$ ($\OccCwtfmWorst$ at the worst-case
system).
That figure is reported for comparison only. It uses the survey-wide
frequency union, which no single star was observed over; the quantity that
answers whether a transmitter's frequency was actually observed is the
per-system version below. For scale on the conventional axis, \citet{Enriquez2017} reach
\OccLitEnriquez{} and \citet{Price2020} \OccLitPriceGBT{} at the GBT. This survey is
therefore some four orders of magnitude worse than cm-wave SETI on the
standard continuous-carrier metric, and that is the correct conclusion:
\OccNSysA{} systems at a $10^{15}$\,W floor cannot compete with hundreds
of stars at $10^{13}$\,W. What the metric does not score is the frequency axis, the one on which
this survey is first, since Eq.~\ref{eq:cwtfm} credits fractional
bandwidth and not location, and \OccDnuSurvey\,GHz at
\OccNuMid\,GHz is a small fraction of a large number.

Two caveats are not presentational. First, the transmitter-hosting
fraction. Zero confirmed detections in \OccNSysA{} Class~A systems bounds
the fraction hosting a continuously transmitting carrier above each
system's own $P_{90}^{\rm sel}$ at $f<\OccFracPct$ per cent, the 95 per
cent Poisson limit; the $1/N$ convention common in the literature would
say \OccFracNaivePct{} per cent, and is optimistic. That bound holds only
for carriers inside the searched frequency islands, drift grid, duty-cycle
domain and archival epochs, on a sample ALMA selected for disc science,
and it is not a statement about the 40\,pc population.

Second, and more seriously, $\nu_{\rm rel}$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:cwtfm} is a
survey-level quantity, and \emph{a civilisation around one star does not
have access to the union of frequencies observed toward all \NSystems{}
systems.} System by system the median Class~A coverage is
\SysUnionAMed\,GHz, from \SysUnionAMin{} to \SysUnionAMax\,GHz over the
\NSysUnionA{} systems that have any --- \SysUnionAFracMed{} per cent of
the survey's own union. Recomputing on that median gives
$\nu_{\rm rel}=\OccNuRelSys$ and ${\rm CWTFM}=\OccCwtfmSys$, worse by
$\times\OccSysSurveyRatio$. We therefore take the per-system
value as the scientifically meaningful one and the survey-union figure as
a literature convention: it measures aggregate bandwidth, not the
probability that a given transmitter frequency was observed. Under any
frequency prior broader than the searched islands the occurrence bound
vanishes, which is a property of the archive and not of the method.


\section{The spatial normalisation: evidence and validation}
\label{app:radius}

This appendix collects the three measurements behind
\S\ref{sec:radval} and the prospective test of the correction.

\subsection{Prospective validation of the radius correction}
\label{app:radvalfull}

The radius-corrected normalisation was derived from the calibration data,
so on its own it establishes nothing about data it has not seen. We
therefore froze it --- the probe radii, the bin edges and the
standardisation are those of Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}, with no free
parameter to tune --- and applied it unmodified to \RadValStarN{} windows
that played no part in its construction: the hold-out and the extension of
\S\ref{sec:extension}. The level for consistency with uniformity,
$p\geq\RadValAlpha$, was written down before that test was run
(Table~\ref{tab:decisions}).

Two rank distributions can be tested and they answer different questions.
The \emph{pseudo-star} distribution ranks an inner-annulus probe against
the other probes; it passes both before and after correction
(uncorrected $D=\RadValRawD$ at $\pv{\RadValRawP}$, corrected
$D=\RadValCorD$ at $\pv{\RadValCorP}$, over \RadValNProbe{} probes). That
is expected and is not evidence for the correction: every probe is drawn
from the same radial distribution, so probe-against-probe ranks are
near-uniform however they are normalised. The discriminating test is the
\emph{stellar} distribution, because the star lies at the phase centre,
inside the probe annulus, and that asymmetry is what the correction exists
to repair.

On that test the correction fails. Out of sample the
uncorrected stellar rank has median \RadValStarRawMed{} against the
expected 0.5, with $D=\RadValStarRawD$ at $\pv{\RadValStarRawP}$; after
correction the median is \RadValStarCorMed{} with
$D=\RadValStarCorD$ at $\pv{\RadValStarCorP}$. The departure from
uniformity is essentially undiminished, and the median moves
\RadValStarToward{} the exchangeable value. The result holds separately in
both samples (hold-out \RadValStarHoRawMed$\to$\RadValStarHoCorMed,
extension \RadValStarExtRawMed$\to$\RadValStarExtCorMed).

The interpretation we draw is narrow and, we think, unavoidable. The
radial gradient is real and is one mechanism behind the displacement, but
removing it does not leave an exchangeable statistic, so the residual is
not radial. Neither normalisation yields a calibrated tail probability,
and the spatial screen is therefore a candidate-generation device under
both. \textbf{We consequently retain the frozen screen as primary --- it
at least has the virtue of having been fixed in advance --- and rest every
disposition on the physical evidence rather than on either rank.}


\subsubsection{The external calibration sample} \label{sec:heldout}

\emph{Two distinct out-of-sample sets are used in this paper and they must
not be conflated.} The \textbf{hold-out} is \LedHoldout{} execution blocks
(\HoldWinN{} windows toward \HoldStarN{} stars) reserved by a hash of the
block identifier, committed \HoldoutCommit{} on \HoldoutDate, and it sits
inside the block ledger of \S\ref{sec:sample} as the
$\LedHoldoutInScope$-block term of the second identity there. The
\textbf{external calibration sample} described in this subsection is a
different object: \CampBlocks{} execution blocks toward \CampStars{} stars,
assembled in processing order from the host's own campaign log rather than
by the hash rule, and reserved by a rule fixed and committed before the
archival search finished and before any reserved block was searched.
\emph{It is not a subset of the \LedProgenitor{} in-scope blocks of
\S\ref{sec:sample}} --- it includes blocks toward stars outside the 40\,pc
work list, which is why its block count cannot be placed inside that ledger
and must not be added to the \LedProcessed{} processed there. It enters no
count of the science sample; it is used only to calibrate the rank
statistic. Appendix~\ref{app:heldoutfull} gives both reservation rules, their
commitment dates and the full result, and Table~\ref{tab:partitions} gives
the provenance of each. Three findings carry into the main line of the
paper.

First, the pipeline behaves on unseen data: over \CampWindows{} windows it
returns \CampStageOne{} stage-1 flagged windows, of which \CampStageOneCO{} are
$\beta$~Pictoris CO and \CampUnattrib{} are unattributed, against
\CampExpUn{} expected at the measured tail rate. Second, and adversely,
the stellar rank is \emph{not} uniform out of sample: its median is
\HoRankMed{} where 0.5 is expected, with a block-clustered 95 per cent
interval of \HoRankMedLo--\HoRankMedHi{} and $D=\HoRankKsD$ at
$\pv{\HoRankKsP}$ over \HoWin{} windows. A displacement that survives a
hold-out chosen before the data is a property of the statistic and not of
this sample, which is why the spatial rank is used as a screen and never as
a significance (\S\ref{sec:statistic}). It reproduces a third time, at
\ExtRankMed{} over \ExtNRanked{} prospectively searched windows
(\S\ref{sec:extension}). Third, the false-alarm tail factor
--- the ratio of the measured rate at which a no-signal position ranks
first to the rate exchangeability would give --- is \HoTailFactor{} on
these blocks, with a bootstrap over the \HoTailBlocks{} reserved blocks
giving \HoTailLo--\HoTailHi. That factor and its width set the accuracy of
every chance expectation in this paper.


\subsubsection{Where the displacement comes from: radius, not stars}
\label{sec:radial}

The displacement has a mechanism, and it is instrumental. Images are
corrected for the primary-beam response, so the true noise rises with
distance from the pointing centre, while the search divides every position
by one scale estimated over the whole window. The control probes therefore
sit at systematically different effective noise from the star, which is at
the phase centre. Appendix~\ref{app:radialfull} sets out the evidence:
the control level varies with radius across the annulus, pseudo-stars
carrying no star at all are displaced as far as real ones, and rescoring
the survey with the gradient removed changes which windows are flagged but
not what the paper concludes.

Two numbers matter here. Standardising each control probe within its own
radius bin --- the probes come from one fixed seed, so their radii are
known, and the correction has no free parameter --- takes the stage-1 list
from \LocStageGlobal{} windows to \LocStageLocal, losing
\LocStageLost{} and gaining \LocStageGained{}, all of them marginal and
all already dispositioned on other grounds. And the median add-one rank
over the \LocAllWin{} windows that retain a control vector does \emph{not}
move toward 0.5 under the correction: it goes from \LocAllMedGlobal{} to
\LocAllMedLocal, slightly further away. The
radial term is one demonstrated mechanism for the displacement, not the
whole of it, so we do not adopt the corrected statistic as primary and we
do not present it as a validated null.

\subsection{An end-to-end check of the absolute flux scale}
\label{sec:absscale}

One external check is available, and it is the one that found the
positional defect of \S\ref{sec:parallax}. Two stars in this sample have
millimetre photometry published by other groups from the \emph{same}
archival execution blocks, which tests our extraction end to end rather
than against itself. For AU~Mic, \citet{MacGregor2020} measured the two
2015 June 24 flares by fitting point-source models to the visibilities with
CASA's \texttt{uvmodelfit}, an estimator that solves for source position as
well as flux and is therefore immune to the term below; for Ross~154,
\citet{Burton2024} measured two 2022 September flares from the same ACA
visibilities used here. AU~Mic's impulsive millimetre flaring at 106\,GHz is
described in \citet{White2026}.

Our extractor returns \PxRossObsLo--\PxRossObsHi{} of the published
Ross~154 amplitudes and \PxAuMicObsLo--\PxAuMicObsHi{} of the published
AU~Mic ones. The two comparisons differ in exactly the way a
\emph{positional} error predicts and no other candidate does. The
extraction phase-rotates to the star's barycentric direction and so omits
the annual parallax; the resulting amplitude loss is the normalised dirty
beam at (parallax displacement)/(synthesised beam). For the Ross~154 blocks
that ratio is \PxRossBeamsLo--\PxRossBeamsHi{} of a 7\,m-array beam and the
predicted loss is \PxRossPredLossPct{} per cent; for the AU~Mic block it is
\PxAuMicBeams{} of a $0.255''$ beam and the predicted retained amplitude is
\PxAuMicPred{} (\PxAuMicPredLo--\PxAuMicPredHi{} over the
\PxNTwelveConfig{} real 12\,m configurations whose dirty beam we measured
directly). A calibration scale error, a polarisation convention, a
channel-weighting error or a time-averaging loss would all apply to the
Ross~154 comparison equally, and none does.

\emph{The residual is stated, not explained away.} At the measured-beam
prediction the parallax term accounts for about \PxAuMicExplainedPct{} per
cent of the AU~Mic deficit, leaving
\PxAuMicResidLoPct--\PxAuMicResidHiPct{} per cent unaccounted for. That is
comparable to the combined quoted uncertainties (ours ${\approx}5$ per cent,
theirs ${\approx}2.5$ per cent), and the block's own measurement set has
since been deleted, so its dirty beam cannot be computed. The honest
statement is therefore that this check bounds any \emph{further} systematic
in our absolute flux scale at roughly the \PxScaleBoundPct{} per cent level
--- not that no second term exists. The identification of the mechanism
rests on the \emph{pattern} across the two comparisons, not on the size of
either.


\subsubsection{A prospective epoch extension} \label{sec:extension}

The work list was built against an earlier archive map, which had not
exposed \LedNotProcessed{} public in-scope execution blocks. \FateDone{} of

\subsubsection{An astrophysical positive control: $\beta$~Pictoris CO}
\label{sec:bpicval}

A search that finds nothing must show that it could have found something,
and two demonstrations are needed. The injection campaign of
Appendix~\ref{app:inject} establishes that the pipeline recovers a drifting
unresolved spectral carrier, which is the searched morphology. $\beta$~Pictoris CO,
resolved, several km\,s$^{-1}$ wide and effectively undrifting, establishes
the complementary property: a real, spatially localised, narrow spectral
feature at a stellar position, recovered blind by the frozen pipeline, in
two transitions, in \NStageOneBpicEb{} execution blocks, over
\BpBaselineYr\,yr from \BpSpanFirst{} to \BpSpanLast, and at a drift
consistent with none --- the Band~3 crossings agree across blocks to
\BpRecThreeTuneChanMax{} channel once each block's own tuning is removed,
which bounds any apparent drift far below the grid the window searches. It
is therefore a positive control for localisation and recurrence, not for the
carrier morphology. HD~48370 is the complementary case, an extended
foreground line that fills the annulus and so is rejected by the same
spatial statistic that promotes $\beta$~Pictoris; CP$-$72~2713 and HD~23484
(Table~\ref{tab:heldout}) are the third regime, marginal single cells with
no recurrence in the repeats available.

$\beta$~Pictoris is also the site of a radio detection of a very different
kind. \citet{BpicbRadio2026} report recurring, highly circularly polarised
bursts together with persistent emission from the planet $\beta$~Pic~b over
\BpicbFlo--\BpicbFhi\,GHz with MeerKAT, which they attribute to the
electron-cyclotron maser instability and use to infer a field of
${\gtrsim}\BpicbFieldkG$\,kG at the planet. That emission is inaccessible to
this survey through the physics of the mechanism rather than through our
depth. Cyclotron maser emission cannot exceed the electron cyclotron
frequency at its source, $2.8\,{\rm MHz\,G^{-1}}\times B$; the quoted field
reproduces their own \BpicbCeiling\,GHz upper edge, and reaching the lowest
frequency searched here, \EcmiSurveyFlo\,GHz, would require
\EcmiFieldLo\,kG, about \EcmiRatioLo{} times the $\beta$~Pic~b field, with
Band~7 requiring \EcmiRatioBSeven{} times it. Their detection sits
\EcmiDecades{} decades below our coverage, so nothing in this paper bears on
it and our null is not evidence against it. It does settle one argument of
\S\ref{sec:method} in both directions. Strong circular polarisation is
measurable from a planet-mass companion in one of the very systems searched
here, so the polarisation discrimination we forgo is real rather than
hypothetical; and it is not by itself a mark of artificiality, since a
natural magnetosphere produces it. Polarisation would separate compact
coherent emission from the thermal dust and line emission that fills this
archive, not engineering from magnetospheres.

The working behind the attribution is as follows. The Band~6 window is a
line tuning, \BpSixBwMHz\,MHz wide at \BpSixChanKHz\,kHz, so it holds
\BpSixNChan{} channels like a wide window and searches \BpSixTrials{} trial
drift rates against a survey median of \TrialsMed, the largest trials load
in the survey at $\eta_{\rm drift}=\BpSixEta$: this rank is harder to earn
than a typical one, not easier. Carried through the frame chain of
\S\ref{sec:frames}, the Band~3 crossing lies \StelBpicThree{} and the
Band~6 crossing \StelBpicSix\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from the stellar systemic
velocity, in the two lowest CO rotational transitions of a star with mapped
circumstellar CO \citep{Matra2017,Dent2014}, and a third, unflagged window
six years earlier shows the same component at \StelBpicCoarse\,km\,s$^{-1}$;
all three lie within \BpicMaxAbsStel\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of systemic
(Table~\ref{tab:ledger}). The full frame audit, the flux ratio and the
excitation argument are in Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}, with the
per-window numbers in Table~\ref{tab:bpicaudit}.

Attributing the pair to CO does not diminish it. Against the survey
background the Band~3 peak $T_\star=\BpicBThreeTsym$ is matched or exceeded
by \NCtrlGeBpicBThree{} of the \NWindows{} per-window control maxima, and
the Band~6 peak $T_\star=\BpicBSixTsym$ by \NCtrlGeBpicBSix{} of them.
Those are ranks in a reference set this paper shows is not exchangeable with
the stellar position, so they are quoted as ranks and not as $p$-values; the
attribution rests on the velocity agreement and the recurrence, neither of
which needs them, and the alternative reading needs a transmitter placed by
coincidence at matched offsets from two CO transitions in one star.

A non-recurrence elsewhere means nothing unless the same machinery recovers
a repeat when one exists, so the recurrence test was run on emission already
believed in. Every $\beta$~Pictoris CO feature this survey
flagged is recovered in an independent execution block: the Band~3 unit is
exhaustive at \BpUnitThreeDone{} of \BpUnitThreeAvail{} blocks with the line
present in every one, the Band~6 recurrence unit likewise, and the stage-1
Band~6 window recurs in its unit's second block at $T_\star=\BpRecTTwo$.
Three things make this a recurrence and not a coincidence: the peaks agree
to about one channel once each block's own tuning is removed, the
barycentric velocities agree to \BpRecVsysSpread\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of systemic
across the full baseline, and the lines are
\BpWidthThreeLo--\BpWidthThreeHi\,km\,s$^{-1}$ wide, an order of magnitude
broader than the single channel a carrier occupies.
Appendix~\ref{app:provenance} gives the block-by-block numbers, including
the warning that the spatial screen loses power when the CO belt fills the
control annulus.

Every frequency axis here --- searched spectra, tabulated ranges and
crossing frequencies alike --- is \emph{topocentric} as delivered by the
ALMA correlator, with no velocity-frame shift applied at any stage.
Velocities enter only in the vetting stage's line mask. The stacking is
drift-aligned and inverse-variance weighted: coherent in frequency
trajectory, not in electric-field phase.

The native channel width is also the linewidth convention for every EIRP
threshold. Channel dilution spreads a sub-channel carrier's total flux
across the channel, so EIRP$_{\rm trig}$ is the minimum \emph{total} power
of a channel-confined carrier, independent of the transmitter's own width
below the channel, and never a spectral power density. The Hz-resolution surveys'
limits are total powers in the same sense, each the best case of its own
channelisation, which makes the axes of Fig.~\ref{fig:context}
commensurable. One number fixes the scale, illustratively and not achievably:
the median Class~A channel is \SqrtScaleChanKHz\,kHz, so rechannelising the
same data to the \SETIChanHz\,Hz of a dedicated narrowband search would, on
$P_{\min}\propto\sqrt{\Delta\nu}$, divide the threshold by $\SqrtScaleFac$
and put the median Class~A selection power at
${\sim}\SqrtScalePsel$\,W. ALMA cannot do this: the correlator delivered the
channelisation the archive holds, and no reprocessing recovers resolution
that was never recorded.

Every spectral window of an observation enters the spectral-carrier search,
capped at 8 per target to bound compute cost; the cap bound one target/band,
61~Vir Band~7, and three further targets are represented by their deepest
single window and marked in the released catalogue. Each searched window
yields its own EIRP$_{\rm trig}$ row, and Figure~\ref{fig:context} plots
only the deepest per star.

\emph{Primary-beam response.} Carrier-lane thresholds and continuum fluxes
are corrected for ALMA's primary-beam response at the star's offset from the
phase centre, negligibly for most targets (median factor \PbMedian, 90th
percentile \PbPninety), though \NPbAboveTwoPct{} of the \NWindows{} retained
windows exceed 2 per cent and \NPbAboveTenPct{} exceed 10 per cent. A window
is kept only where that response is at least \PbFloorResp, a correction of
at most $\times\PbFloorCorr$, since at low response a small error in the
beam model becomes a large error in inferred flux. The offsets are bimodal
and the floor sits in the gap: the last retained window has response
\PbGapKeptResp{} at $\PbGapKeptU\,\theta_{\rm PB}$ (\PbMaxStar{}
Band~\PbMaxBand, $\PbMax\times$), the first window beyond it
\PbGapHeldResp{} at $\PbGapHeldU\,\theta_{\rm PB}$
($\PbGapHeldCorr\times$), and nothing lies between, so the rule binds
prospectively and excludes none of the \NWindows{} released windows. Beyond
it are the \EpsEriNWin{} $\epsilon$\,Eri Band~6 windows at
$\EpsEriPbLo$--$\EpsEriPbHi\times$, also past the first sidelobe transition
where the Gaussian form fails; the two criteria are independent and select
the same windows. They are \emph{withheld}, enter no number here and are
absent from the released catalogue, although under the nominal rule they
would have covered $\EpsEriEirpLo$--$\EpsEriEirpHi$\,W at
\EpsEriDistPc\,pc, among the deepest thresholds in the survey but resting on
a beam model that cannot be defended. The annulus geometry keeps the
pipeline's own $\theta_{\rm PB}=\RingPbCoefCode\,\lambda/D$, an Airy
first-null coefficient; recomputing it on ALMA's FWHM
$\RingPbCoef\,\lambda/D$ at each window's own dish diameter moves
\NPbFixOnePct{} of \NWindows{} windows by more than 1 per cent, extremes
$\PbFixLoPct$ to $+\PbFixHiPct$ per cent, and no headline threshold moves.

\subsubsection{Transmitter power benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

The benchmark is frequency-matched and purely geometric. For a filled
circular aperture of diameter $D$ radiating $P_{\rm tx}$ at wavelength
$\lambda$,
\begin{equation}
G=\frac{4\pi A_{\rm eff}}{\lambda^{2}},\qquad
\mathrm{EIRP}=P_{\rm tx}G,
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
so a \BenchDiam-m aperture radiating 1\,MW at 230\,GHz with a realistic
aperture efficiency of 0.7 has $G=\BenchGainSeven$ and an EIRP of
$\BenchEirpSeven$\,W; a perfect aperture, $\eta=1$, would give
$\BenchGainSci$ and $\BenchEirpSci$\,W, and the realistic case is the one
quoted. The median Class~A effective threshold, $\PeffWinMedA$\,W, misses
such a transmitter by a factor \BenchEffRatio.

The Arecibo-like planetary radar used for comparison
\citep[][EIRP $\AreciboW$\,W]{Drake1974,EarthDetectingEarth2025} is a
\ArecGHz-GHz \emph{power} scale carrying no transmitter model. Gain scales
as $\nu^{2}$, the same equation at fixed $A_{\rm eff}$, so the same aperture
and transmitter power at 230\,GHz would radiate
$\AreciboW\times(230/\ArecGHz)^{2}\simeq\ArecScaledW\times10^{17}$\,W. The
comparison is between physical transmitter powers, so it uses $P_{\rm eff}$
and not the trigger: that difference carries the deepest window from below
the numerical Arecibo figure to $\EirpEffDeepest$\,W above it
(\S\ref{sec:detectable}). \textbf{The frequency-scaled figure,
${\sim}\ArecScaledW\times10^{17}$\,W at 230\,GHz, is the physically
appropriate comparison for this survey rather than the \ArecGHz-GHz
number}, because it is the same aperture and the same transmitter power
radiating in our band; on that scale every window here is far below the
benchmark. \emph{And this paper's own rule is that sensitivity means}
$P_{90}^{\rm sel}$, so a benchmark statement expressed in $P_{\rm eff}$ or
$P_{\rm trig}$ is an instrument-level quantity and is labelled as such:
the corresponding completeness figure is a further
$\times\SelPNinetyFine$ ($\times\SelPNinetyFineLo$--$\times\SelPNinetyFineHi$)
on the nominal Class~A trigger (\S\ref{sec:pninetysel}), and every
comparison in this appendix should be read with that factor applied.

\emph{Reference frames.} Two things must be kept apart. \emph{The
released line-offset columns, and the attribution the search applied, are
topocentric}: all \MaskNOffsetsMHz{} of them reproduce from the observed
sky frequency against the frozen rest frequency, and none reproduces from a
barycentric or stellar-frame evaluation
(Appendix~\ref{app:linemask}). The \emph{audit} chain of
Table~\ref{tab:bpicaudit}, applied by hand to the attributed windows, runs
topocentric $\rightarrow$ barycentric (Earth ephemeris at the
execution-block mid-time) $\rightarrow$ stellar rest frame (catalogued
systemic radial velocity), with
JPL/CDMS rest frequencies \citep{Pickett1998,CDMS2005}; in the LSR frame it
stops at the standard solar motion, not the systemic velocity
(\S\ref{sec:linecost}). The residual error is the catalogue
radial-velocity uncertainty alone, a few km\,s$^{-1}$ within 40\,pc;
second-order relativistic terms at the \MaskVWidth\,km\,s$^{-1}$ scale are
$\lesssim5$\,kHz at 350\,GHz, below the finest native channel searched
(15.3\,kHz). The $\beta$~Pictoris attributions use this chain, so their offsets are
comparable to the exclusion tolerance.

\emph{Channel widths, and the instrumental response.} Channel widths come
from the measurement sets' own spectral window tables (CHAN\_WIDTH). A
missing ``EFFECTIVE\_BANDWIDTH'' column is not evidence of an absence of
online spectral averaging, MS~v2 defining no such column, so the quoted
channelisations are not claimed to be noise bandwidths.

ALMA applies online Hanning smoothing by default, so the spectral response
is ${\sim}2$ channels wide and neighbouring channels are correlated. For the
$0.25/0.5/0.25$ kernel the coefficients are
$\rho_1=\HanRhoOneFrac=\HanRhoOne$ and $\rho_2=\HanRhoTwo$, with
$\sigma_{\rm smoothed}/\sigma_{\rm raw}=\HanSigRatio$: the correlation is
not confined to adjacent channels, the lag-two term being a sixth. The
peak-channel fractions \HanWorst--\HanBest{} that follow, median \HanMed,
and the resulting $\times\HanFacBest$--$\times\HanFacWorst$ optimism of the
nominal thresholds are given in \S\ref{sec:method}; the released catalogue
carries the nominal threshold and both corrected values per window, and the
sensitivity summary quotes the worst case. The injections deposit an
unsmoothed delta-in-channel tone, so the recovery curves of
\S\ref{sec:dwell} and Appendix~\ref{app:inject} carry the same bias and the
end-to-end check (Eq.~\ref{eq:stacked}) cannot see it.

Two measurements fix the smoothing state. The retained per-integration
residuals of the second-stage local null (\S\ref{sec:results}) have a
measured lag-one spectral autocorrelation of \LNLagOne{} against the Hanning
prediction \HanRhoOne, so that correlation is instrumental and the residual
spectra are smoothed and un-averaged. And the archive's reported effective
spectral resolution per window, against each measurement set's own channel
separation, is exactly 2.00 in \NHanTwo{} of the \NHanKnown{} windows, in
both correlator modes and including \NHanTwoCoarse{} of the
\NCoarseHanKnown{} coarse time-division-mode (TDM) ones; the other
\NHanOther{} report a smaller ratio, the signature of online channel
averaging, where the peak-channel loss is smaller, and the \NNoArchRes{}
Band~9 and~10 windows, absent from the metadata snapshot, take ALMA's
default. The blanket factor is right for the great majority of both classes.
Thresholds are not rescaled: each is nominal and up to $\times\HanFacWorst$
optimistic. The correlation model is truncated at lag two, and the baseline
step --- a per-integration median in blocks of up to 65 channels --- could
imprint correlation on longer scales, which the released products retain no
residual spectra to measure. Such a term would make the independent-trials
count of Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm} an over-estimate and the achievable
per-window resolution coarser still.

\emph{Linewidth convention.} The statistic is the single-channel flux
density after drift correction, so the quoted EIRP$_{\rm trig}$ applies to
any transmitter narrower than the native channel and is flat in the assumed
linewidth. Two sub-channel qualifications attach: flatness is exact only for
a centred tone, a boundary-straddling one splitting its power between two
channels at worst by a factor of two; and the channel-to-channel transition
follows the correlator's passband shape, which the frozen products do not
retain. Thresholds therefore carry a sub-channel-position systematic of tens
of per cent, and EIRP values are quoted to two significant figures.

\emph{Transmitters wider than one channel.} The baseline filter
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}) is flat for features narrower than roughly half its
effective width $W$ and falls to zero above $W$, so the survey's sensitivity
is to channel-confined morphologies and $W$ is the linewidth ceiling:
\MedWinFineLo--\MedWinFineHi{} channels over the fine class and
\MedWinCoarseLo--\MedWinCoarseHi{} over the coarse one, that is
${\approx}\MedWinCeilFineMHz$\,MHz at \ChanFineKHz\,kHz and
${\approx}\MedWinCeilCoarseGHz$\,GHz at \ChanCoarseMHz\,MHz. Within that
ceiling, a top-hat transmitter spanning $N_{\rm ch}$ native channels has
per-channel flux the total divided by $N_{\rm ch}$ at unchanged noise, so to
first order
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EIRP}_{\rm trig}(N_{\rm ch}) \;\simeq\; N_{\rm ch}\times
\mathrm{EIRP}_{\rm trig}(1), \qquad N_{\rm ch} \lesssim W/2,
\end{equation}
the rescaling the main text applies to signals broader than one channel.

\emph{Worked example: from flux to EIRP.} Take UV~Ceti (G~272-61B) Band~3,
the deepest of its \NUVCetiWin{} windows in that band (execution block
\UVCetiEB, lower edge \UVCetiFlo\,GHz). Its per-channel rms is
\UVCetiRms\,mJy, so $S_{\rm min} = 5\times\UVCetiRms = \UVCetiSmin$\,mJy in
one 15.625-MHz native channel, or
$1.21\times10^{-29}$\,W\,m$^{-2}$\,Hz$^{-1}$ at 2.67\,pc, and
$\mathrm{EIRP}_{\rm trig} = 4\pi d^{2} S_{\rm min} \Delta\nu =
1.6\times10^{13}$\,W.

\emph{Intra-integration smearing.} The frequency excursion at the maximum
searched drift rate across one integration, $\dot\nu_{\max} t_{\rm int}$, is
at most 1.10 channels across the \NWindows{} windows (median 0.001, 90th
percentile 0.06). A linear drift smeared by $\Delta$ channels within an
integration keeps a fraction
$\eta_{\rm smear} = \mathrm{sinc}(\pi\Delta/2)$ of the peak-channel
amplitude, so the effective threshold is
$\mathrm{EIRP}_{\rm trig}/\eta_{\rm smear}$; a pure occupancy argument would
retain $\min(1,1/\Delta)$ instead, giving 1.0 rather than 0.64 at
$\Delta=1$, so the sinc form is the conservative reading. The \NSmearLo{}
windows with $\eta_{\rm smear}<0.99$, as star, band,
($\eta_{\rm smear}$, threshold multiplier), are \SmearList; the remaining
\NSmearOk{} agree with their nominal thresholds to better than 1 per cent.
Compounding the worst smearing penalty with the worst Hanning factor gives
$\times\CompoundWorst$ on the nominal threshold for those five windows, and
no released column carries that product.

The drift ceiling carries one caveat. The searched value is
\DriftCeilLo--\DriftCeilHi\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$, line-of-sight

\emph{Interference rejection} is an operative gate. The highest frequencies
allocated to any satellite constellation are the E-band feeder links of the

exclusion claim rests on one; the operative defences are the control

\subsection{Amplitude completeness from visibility-domain injections}
\label{sec:ampinject}

Synthetic tones of known amplitude were injected into real visibilities,
using the extraction code's own mechanism as far upstream of the statistic
as the retained products allow, and the unmodified pipeline's return
measured. Six configurations span three bands and both classes: three coarse
Band~7 windows of AT~Mic, one coarse window each in Bands~3 and~6 of AU~Mic,
and AU~Mic's 488-kHz flagband window with its 49-trial drift grid. Each
receives a complex tone at the star position at $4$--$10\times$ the
statistic's own noise, over drift fractions $f_{\rm drift}=0$--$1.0$ of the
ceiling, on- and half-grid rates and channel-centred and boundary
placements: 200 trials per configuration, \NTrials{} in all. The recovery
criterion was frozen in advance: $T\geq5$, peak channel within 3 of the
injection, peak drift within one grid step.

Coarse windows recover 0 of 500 injections from $T_\star=4$ to $10$ under
that criterion, which they cannot satisfy, every in-grid drift being
sub-channel over the track so that the reported peak drift is arbitrary; the
fine window fails it the same way. Scored on detection alone the same
windows recover essentially everything
(Appendix~\ref{app:dwelldetail}). Class~B limits therefore bound carriers of
any dwell fraction, with the completeness of \S\ref{sec:dwell}; what those
windows lack is drift discrimination.

On the fine window, drifting carriers ($f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$, pooled over
grid phase and sub-channel placement) are recovered 17, \RecThresh, 58, 75
and \RecTwice{} per cent at $T_\star=4$, 5, 6, 8 and 10, saturating by twice
threshold power (Fig.~\ref{fig:completeness}). With the tone injected after
the baseline step the machinery alone is amplitude-faithful to within
$-9$/$+14$ per cent for channel-centred tones. Boundary-straddling placement
costs $0.6$--$0.8$ in the statistic and recovers 0 per cent at the trigger
against 83 per cent for centred tones, crossing 50 per cent near
$1.4\times$ threshold amplitude. Survey completeness uses the
placement-pooled curve, conservative for the boundary-straddling case:
EIRP$_{50}=1.10\,\mathrm{EIRP}_{\rm trig}$ and
EIRP$_{90}>2.0\,\mathrm{EIRP}_{\rm trig}$, a bound only.

That curve is measured on one window and is not a survey completeness
function. Of the \CfgNA{} Class~A windows, \CfgAxesZeroOne{}
(\CfgAxesZeroOnePct{} per cent) differ from the calibration configuration on
at most one axis, the mismatch dominated by band (\CfgBandOut{} windows) and
drift-trial count (\CfgDriftOut) rather than channel width (\CfgChanOut),
integration time (\CfgOnSrcOut) or beam offset (\CfgOffOut). Those are the
axes the stratified campaign spans; other target environments and
edge-phased drift grids are not spanned at all.

\emph{The stratified campaign.} A drifting carrier was injected into the
retained per-integration spectra of \StratWindows{} further windows, one per
star, spanning Bands~\StratBandLo--\StratBandHi,
\StratChanLo--\StratChanHi\,kHz channels, \StratIntLo--\StratIntHi{}
integrations, \StratDriftLo--\StratDriftHi{} drift trials and both the 12\,m
array and the ACA, and pushed through the pipeline's own baseline removal
and de-drift stack on the search's drift grid: nine amplitudes,
\StratTrials{} trials. Recovery is a threshold crossing at the injected
frequency and not the global maximum of the grid, since requiring an
injection to beat a window's brightest cell measures that window's worst
artefact. Pooled recovery is \StratRecFour, \StratRecFive, \StratRecSix{}
and \StratRecEight{} per cent at $T_\star=4$, 5, 6 and 8, with
$P_{50}=\StratPFifty\sigma$ and $P_{90}=\StratPNinety\sigma$

\emph{Drift rate, resolved.} All \DriftStratTrials{} trials carry a non-zero

worst; at a fixed $\DriftStratFixAmp\sigma$ injection they recover

\emph{The spectral response, computed from the correlator's window and
validated against the delivered data.} The response correction is the
largest single factor between the level at which the search fires and the
power a transmitter must radiate, so it is computed from the instrument
rather than assumed. ALMA applies its window in the lag domain, which is
equivalent to the $0.25/0.5/0.25$ smoothing of the delivered spectrum that
\texttt{hanningsmooth} performs, so the operation is not in doubt; what
matters is the channel's response to a monochromatic carrier. We build the
lag function of a continuum source --- a delta at zero lag, where the Hann
taper is unity --- and of an unresolved carrier at sub-channel position
$\phi$, transform both under one normalisation, and take the ratio. The
continuum reading fixes the flux scale, the same scale in which
$S_{\rm min}$ is measured, and the carrier's peak delivered channel is what
the single-channel trigger sees. That channel retains a fraction
\CrRhoHi{} of the carrier at a channel centre, falling to \CrRhoLo{} at a
boundary, so $C_{\rm response}=\times\CrLo$--$\times\CrHi$, median
$\times\CrMed$.

Five quantities, none of them used to construct the model, test it. The
continuum flux scale is unchanged by the window. One channel's frequency
response has a full width at half maximum of \CrFwhmUnif{} channel spacings
unsmoothed and \CrFwhmHann{} with the default smoothing, against ALMA's
stated $1.2$ and $2.0$. The worst-case scalloping loss is
\CrScallopDb\,dB, the textbook figure for a Hann window. The effective
noise bandwidth of a smoothed channel is \CrEnbw{} times its width, against
the documented $8/3$. Strongest of the five, the archive reports an
effective spectral resolution for every window in this survey, and all but
one of those reports take two values: \HanArchHi{} for \HanArchNHi{}
windows and \HanArchLo{} for the \HanArchNLo{} delivered with online
channel averaging by \CrNAvg. The model reproduces both --- the first from the taper alone,
the second from the same taper followed by that averaging --- so it is
tested on the survey's own data and not only against textbook values. The
averaged windows carry a smaller correction,
$\times\CrAvgLo$--$\times\CrAvgHi$, median $\times\CrAvgMed$, because their
delivered channels are wider.

Two earlier estimates are superseded, in opposite directions. Modelling a
channel as a top-hat, so that a carrier divides linearly between the two
channels it straddles before the kernel mixes them, gives
$\times\CrOldAdoptLo$--$\times\CrOldAdoptHi$: a correlator channel is the
transform of its lag window, not a two-point interpolation, and the linear
split over-attenuates the channel edge by \CrAdoptOverPct{} per cent.
Taking instead the peak \emph{power fraction} of a Hann-windowed transform
gives $\times\CrOldPowLo$--$\times\CrOldPowHi$, which is \CrPowUnderPct{}
per cent too permissive at the edge, because dividing by the total power of
the windowed series divides out the window's coherent loss --- the dominant
part of the effect. The scalloping loss cannot distinguish the two. It is a
ratio of the channel-centre to the channel-edge response, and an error in
the absolute normalisation cancels in that ratio exactly, so a model can
reproduce $1.42$\,dB and still be \CrPowUnderPct{} per cent wrong in the
number the paper needs. Adopting the validated value lowers the median
correction by \CrMedShiftPct{} per cent relative to the top-hat model, and
every power quoted here follows it.

\subsection{Dwell campaign: end-to-end check, scope and representativeness}
\label{app:dwelldetail}

A zero-drift, always-on tone injected into the calibrated visibilities of a
coarse Band~7 window at 1, 3 and 10 times the per-integration rms returns
$T_\star = 20.5$, $65.3$ and $217.7$, each within one channel of the
injection. The ideal stacking gain over its 639 integrations is
$\sqrt{639}=25$, and the measured 20.5 is that value less weighting and
flagging losses. This is both the end-to-end check through the unmodified
pipeline and what a coarse window returns when detection alone is scored:
Class~B detects excess power, and cannot tell whether the excess drifted.

Two distinctions apply. The dwell campaign injects into retained
per-integration spectra, after the baseline step, while the end-to-end check
injects into visibilities before it; and one cell, the through-pipeline
recovery of near-static carriers on Class~A windows, is measured only in
part.

How representative the injected windows are of the survey is set out in
\S\ref{sec:ampinject}: the end-to-end demonstration is made on one
configuration, about half the Class~A windows differ from it on at most one
axis, and the stratified campaign measures the transfer to the rest and
states which windows that bracket interpolates and which it extrapolates.

The injected carriers do not drift, so what is measured across \CompWin{}
configurations is the persistent and partial-dwell non-drifting class. On
Class~B windows that is the searched class; on Class~A windows the searched
class is the drifting carrier, whose curve rests on the single calibration
configuration of \S\ref{sec:ampinject}. The stacked amplitude $a$ is a
derived combination, justified by the form of the statistic and by the
collapse of \CompWin{} configurations onto one curve, not measured
independently at every $(A,f_{\rm dwell})$ pair.

\section{False-alarm accounting}
\label{app:falsealarm}

This survey quotes \emph{one} false-alarm expectation. It is built from the
control statistics themselves, which are the only empirical null the data
provide, and it imposes the condition a stage-1 event must actually
satisfy: the position must reach the trigger \emph{and} rank first.
Drawing one control index per execution block --- so that windows sharing
weather, calibration and correlator setup are not treated as independent
trials --- and resampling blocks, the expectation over the \NWinA{}
Class~A windows is \ClustStageMean{} unattributed stage-1 events against
the \ClustStageNObs{} observed, and the probability of that count or more
is $P(\geq\ClustStageNObs)=\ClustStagePobs$. Clustering changes the
number little, because the \emph{position} of a block's control maximum is
not shared across its windows. Expectations that condition only on rank,
without the trigger, predict a different quantity --- the number of
windows in which a no-signal position would rank first --- and are
pipeline diagnostics rather than a statement about candidates; they are
not quoted as chance expectations for candidates.

The remainder of this appendix sets out what that expectation rests on:
the conditioning, a second treatment of the same quantity that carries the
measured non-exchangeability as a tail factor, the exchangeability of the
ensemble itself, what its resolution is worth, and the trials budget.

\subsection{What the expectation is conditioned on}
\label{sec:radval}

A stage-1 event requires
the stellar
position both to reach the trigger and to outrank every control, so the
expectation must be conditioned on both: exactly one probe per window is
the ensemble maximum, and the probability that the stellar position would
have been that maximum, treated as exchangeable with the probes, is
$1/(\NCtrl+1)$ in windows whose control maximum reaches
$T_{\rm trig}=\StageNullTrig$ and zero in the rest. The condition is
restrictive and informative: only \StageNullEligible{} of the
\StageNullAllWin{} windows that retain a control vector --- the subset of
the \NWindows{} searched for which the per-window ensemble was stored ---
have a control maximum reaching the trigger at all, and \StageNullEligibleA{} of those are
Class~A. \emph{This is why every stage-1 event in the survey is Class~A},
and the choice of reference class largely dissolves once the trigger
condition is imposed. An expectation built on the rank alone predicts a
different quantity, the number of windows in which the star would
\emph{rank first}, which is why rank-only figures are larger.

The same quantity admits a second treatment, which is quoted here for
comparison and not as a second expectation. Resampling execution blocks
with replacement, and additionally applying the measured
non-exchangeability of the stellar position as a factor $\times\PriTail$,
gives \PriMean{} expected over the \PriNWin{} windows with stored
ensembles, 95 per cent interval \PriQLo--\PriQHi, against
\ClustStageMean{} without the tail factor. The difference is the tail
factor and nothing else, and that factor is itself known only to
$\PriTailLo$--$\PriTailHi$; the probability of the observed count or more
under it is \RobP{} to one significant figure, and no inference is drawn
from either. The count cannot be dismissed as an artefact of the screen's
position-dependent rank bias: that bias places the star \emph{low} in its
control distribution, and a bias in that direction suppresses star-first
outcomes rather than manufacturing them. Neither number is evidence about any individual
event.

An excess this size needs only a small unmodelled tail in the control
ensemble, and \S\ref{sec:radial} shows that one exists. The same noise
model sets the recovery fractions of \S\ref{sec:dwell}, however, so a
tail large enough to remove the excess would make the quoted completeness
optimistic by roughly the same factor; the two cannot be traded
independently, and neither correction has been applied.


\subsection{The control ensemble: its geometry, its calibration and its
resolution}
\label{sec:acarepair}

The screen's geometry is set in \S\ref{sec:statistic}. Its
calibration, its resolution and what both cost are collected here.

Under that geometry --- the repaired one, carried by \FixNCatAcaFixed{} of
the \FixNCatAca{} ACA windows in the released catalogue --- the uniform
distribution exchangeability implies is not rejected in either stratum: $D=\VerTwmAfterD$
($\pv{\VerTwmAfterP}$) over \VerTwmAfterN{} 12\,m windows and
$D=\VerAcaAfterD$ ($\pv{\VerAcaAfterP}$) over \VerAcaAfterN{} ACA
windows, whose median rank is \VerAcaAfterMed{} against the expected 0.5.
Both tests run on the released catalogue: \VerNTested{} of the \NWindows{}
windows, the remaining \VerNUnmatched{} having no stored control vector.

Two cautions keep that from being a proof of exchangeability. At these
sample sizes the Kolmogorov--Smirnov test resolves only
$D\gtrsim\VerKsPowerAca$, so a value below it is consistent with uniform
rather than demonstrably uniform. And the diagnostic most directly
sensitive to contamination of the inner controls --- the excess of the
innermost control bin over the outer annulus --- is \VerAcaAfterExc{} with
a bootstrap interval of \VerAcaAfterExcLo{} to \VerAcaAfterExcHi, which
spans zero and therefore neither confirms nor excludes a residual effect.
The screen is used to decide which windows are examined and never as a
significance.

Among the ACA windows the screen returns \FixNStageOne{} stage-1 events.
\FixNAttrib{} are molecular --- foreground carbon monoxide toward HD~48370
($T_\star=\FixTHd$, \FixDvHd\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from CO($2\to1$)) and the
$\beta$~Pictoris disc ($T_\star=\FixTBp$, \FixDvBp\,km\,s$^{-1}$), the
survey's positive control --- and \FixNUnattrib{} is unattributed, 61~Vir
($T_\star=\FixTVir$, \FixDvVir\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from the nearest catalogued
transition). Every window retains the frequency and drift rate at which its
statistic peaked, so each event can be re-tested at its own cell.

Neither form is a significance test. Ranking the star against \NCtrl{}
control positions drawn from the same field is a powerful way to decide
which windows deserve examination, but $1/(\NCtrl+1)=\RankFloorVal$ is
the \emph{resolution} of the ensemble, the smallest rank it can express,
and not the probability that noise produced the window. Against a
survey-wide Bonferroni scale
%
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\rm survey}=\frac{0.05}{\NWindows}=\BonfScaleCalc,
\qquad
\frac{\RankFloorVal}{\alpha_{\rm survey}}=\BonfRankRatio,
\label{eq:bonf}
\end{equation}
%
that floor is \BonfRankRatio{} times too coarse
(Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}), so a technosignature claim here requires
independent recurrence or independent astrophysical confirmation and
never rank alone.


\subsection{Why no single window in this design can be significant}
\label{sec:floor}

The rank floor of Eq.~\ref{eq:bonf} is a structural limit on what any
survey built this way can claim, and enlarging the control ensemble does
not rescue it: the primary beam bounds how many independent control
positions a field contains. That bound can be made explicit. The
\NCtrl{} controls are drawn uniformly in the annulus, so many of them land
within one synthesised beam of a neighbour and measure the same noise.
Requiring every control--control and control--star pair to be at least
\SepBeams{} synthesised beams apart --- selecting greedily in the order the
positions were drawn, which is fixed before any statistic is seen ---
leaves a median of \SepNTwelveMed{} independent controls in the 12\,m
windows but only \SepNSevenMed{} in the ACA windows, whose beams are an
order of magnitude wider (Table~\ref{tab:ctrlsep}). The rank
resolution these support is $1/(N_{\rm ctrl}+1)=\SepFloorTwelve$ and
\SepFloorSeven{} respectively, and the ACA figure is \SepFloorRatio{}
times coarser than $1/(\NCtrl+1)$ suggests.

Two things follow, and the second is the more useful. The reduced ensemble
is exchangeable: the add-one rank over the \SepNWin{} windows it can be
formed for has median \SepRankMed{} against the expected 0.5, with
$D=\SepKsD$ ($\pv{\SepKsP}$) once the rank is dithered within its own
cell, which it must be --- with a dozen controls the rank takes a dozen
values, and comparing that to a continuous uniform manufactures
$D=\SepKsDRaw$ out of the quantisation alone. And the separated screen is
\emph{more} permissive, not less: \SepNFlagSep{} windows outrank all of
their separated controls where \SepNFlagFull{} outranked all \NCtrl,
\SepNGained{} gained and \SepNLost{} lost. Fewer controls resolve less, so
a window clears them more easily: the ensemble that looks most significant
is the one with the least power.

The survey's own data show what a visibility-domain statistic buys
instead. In the image plane the \VgNFlag{} stage-1 windows are
indistinguishable: every one has zero of the \NCtrl{} control positions at
or above the star, so every one sits at rank 1 of $\NCtrl+1$, equivalent
to \VgRankFloorZ$\sigma$, with no dynamic range left to separate them. The
visibility fit separates the same windows by \VgSep$\sigma$, and its
strongest reaches \VgVisMax$\sigma$, a factor of \VgGain{} beyond
anything the rank statistic can express whatever the data contain.

Hence the design adopted here, in which a technosignature claim rests on
independent recurrence or independent astrophysical confirmation and the
screen decides only which windows are examined. It also sets a target for
successor surveys: raising the resolution floor requires either many more
independent control positions per field than a single pointing affords, or
a visibility-domain statistic, which does not inherit the floor at all.


Table~\ref{tab:excess} gives the trials budget in cells and decomposes the
on-star excess. A window holds \CellsWinMin--\CellsWinMax{}
channel$\times$drift cells (median \CellsWinMed), the survey \CellsTotal{} in
total. Correlation cannot move the expected \emph{number} above the trigger,
which is $Np$ whatever the correlation, only its clumping, so the excess over
an iid-Gaussian budget decomposes into two multiplicative parts: cell
multiplicity within a crossing window, a factor \CellsPerCrossRatio{} which
is what an over-count of \FaFreqOver{} on the frequency axis predicts, one
real threshold excursion being counted about three times; and the count of
crossing windows, a factor \CrossWinRatio{} whose one-sided Poisson
probability is \CrossWinPoisson. Once the \LNNWindows{} windows carried through the
scramble validation below are set aside, the residual excess is consistent
with chance at that level.

The control ensembles calibrate the budget without a Gaussian assumption. The
same arithmetic predicts \NCtrlWinExp{} of the \NWindows{} windows carrying a
control above $T_{\rm trig}$ and \NCtrlWinObs{} do, agreeing to
\CtrlWinAgreePct{} per cent, with per-window control maxima tracking the
iid-Gaussian prediction at a median ratio \CtrlMaxRatioMed{} (5th--95th
percentile \CtrlMaxRatioLo--\CtrlMaxRatioHi). Grid cells are not independent
trials: Hanning smoothing correlates neighbouring channels at
$\rho_1=\HanRhoOneFrac$ and $\rho_2=\HanRhoTwoFrac$, and the drift grid is
oversampled two to one, giving the combined over-count \FaOverCount{} of
Table~\ref{tab:excess}. Correcting for it leaves the observed control maxima
$+\FaExcessFine$ and $+\FaExcessCoarse$ per cent above prediction in the fine
and coarse classes independently. Non-Gaussianity of that size is why the
operative statement is the empirical rank and not the Gaussian budget. The
residual on-star excess is not zero, runs one way and carries the same sign
at the controls, so it is reported as an open systematic: the crossing list
is dominated by CO($2{\to}1$) at 230.5\,GHz toward disc hosts, and a residual
excess of chance crossings at the tens-of-per-cent level cannot be excluded.

\subsection{Rank calibration and exchangeability}

The drift grid and the thousands of channels apply identically at the star
and at every control, so the per-window trials factor is absorbed
empirically: under exchangeability one designated position of \RankFloor{}
ranks first with probability exactly $1/\RankFloor$ whatever the noise
distribution, and a heavy tail cannot by itself produce an excess of
rank-first windows. The survey expectation is \ExpFlags{} and \FaNFirst{} are
observed; the execution-block clustered permutation returns
$P(\geq\ClustNObs)=\ClustPobs$, against $\ClustPobsPois$ for a plain Poisson
of the same mean. \emph{Those two are identical because they agree, not
because one was copied}: at this mean the clustered convolution and the
Poisson tail coincide to three significant figures, and the regeneration
script asserts it.

\emph{The arithmetic that follows was wrong in earlier versions of this
paper, and correcting it reverses what this paragraph concluded.} Those
versions stated that identified line emission accounted for 3 of the
\FaNFirst{} rank-first windows and that the remaining 2 were ``far too many
for chance'', which neither subtracts correctly nor compares in the right
direction. Recomputed from the released catalogue: \FaNFirstTrig{} of the
\FaNFirst{} rank-first windows also reach the trigger, and \FaNFirstAttr{}
of them carry an identified CO attribution at the stellar position
(Table~\ref{tab:ledger}). Removing those leaves \FaNFirstResid{} against
\ExpFlags{} expected, $P(\geq\FaNFirstResid)=\FaPResid$ --- \emph{entirely
consistent with chance}. \textbf{The rank-first excess is therefore
explained by astrophysics, and is not by itself evidence that the rank is
non-exchangeable.}

The claim that the rank is not exchangeable stands, but on evidence
independent of the crossings and out of sample: on windows the frozen
pipeline had never seen, the stellar add-one rank has median
\FaOutRankMed{} where 0.5 is expected, and the displacement survives block
clustering (Appendix~\ref{sec:heldout}). \rankstatus{}

Ranking against \NCtrl{} controls cannot resolve a probability below
$1/\RankFloor$, so a local scramble test carries \LNNWindows{} of the
\NStageOneWin{} stage-1 windows past that floor. It scrambles each integration independently in channel and
re-runs the identical search with the same noise map, weights and drift grid,
destroying anything coherent along a drift track while preserving each
integration's own spectral autocorrelation and trials factor. \LNNCtrlClear{} of the \LNNWindows{} pass validation. The two $\beta$~Pictoris windows behave as positive
controls, no draw of $\LNDrawsMax$ reaching $T_\star$ in either
($p<\LNpBpicThree$ and $p<\LNpBpicSix$); for CP$-$72~2713 the null resolves
the value itself, \LNnExceedCP{} draws of $\LNDrawsMax$ equalling or
exceeding $T_\star$, with an extreme-value fit to that window's own control
maxima agreeing to a factor \LNCPAgreeFac. HD~48370 Band~6 fails outright,
\LNHDPctAbove{} per cent of its real controls exceeding the maximum of
$\LNDrawsMax$ null draws, because the field carries extended emission and
imaging residuals that are coherent across integrations and that the scramble
destroys by design, so no scrambled-noise probability is quoted there. That
failure follows from retaining full dynamic spectra for only \LNNRetained{}
of the \LNNFirstStage{} control positions. No number quoted here rests on a
parametric extrapolation of a null tail, and the diagnostic has not been
re-run on the windows the completed sweep added (\S\ref{app:futurework}).

Exchangeability is the statistic's central assumption. For a debris-disc
archive its dominant failure is resolved astrophysical emission filling the
annulus, seen in
HD~48370's CO ring and in $\beta$~Pictoris' recurring Band~6 window, where
the ring outshines the star by $\BpRecSixRingRatio\times$. That suppresses a
star-exceeds-ring outcome instead of manufacturing one, so it costs
sensitivity and nothing else, and the repeat block of the \emph{flagged}
Band~6 window measures the cost (\S\ref{sec:bpicval}). The primary beam is
settled by construction, the statistic being formed on \emph{uncorrected}
amplitudes whose thermal noise does not depend on position with
$\sigma(t,\nu)$ one scale shared by the star and every control
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}). The \RankFloor{} positions are therefore
approximately exchangeable under a noise-only null, a statement that covers
the thermal noise and the beam but not residual calibration or continuum
systematics, which can occur preferentially where the pointed science target
sits.

Four measurements bound what is left, and all four are null. Ranking each
control against the other \NCtrl-1 by the rule used for the star gives
\NPseudo{} pseudo-star ranks, uniform to the resolution the ensemble can
express ($p=\PseudoKSp$) in every band and at both channelisations. That
geometry cannot by itself detect a centre-versus-annulus asymmetry, so the
test that can is the two-sample comparison of the \NWindows{} real stellar
ranks against the pooled pseudo-ranks, which returns $\pv{\StarPseudoP}$: on
these data the stellar position behaves like one more position in its own
annulus, though out of sample it does not (\S\ref{sec:heldout}). The Spearman
correlation of control statistic against reconstructed radius has median
\RingRhoNull{} over the \RingRhoNullN{} crossing-free windows, bounding any
systematic radial suppression of the controls below \RingRhoBoundPct{} per
cent in the mean. And the star's own residual variance is measured directly
against the controls' in \S\ref{sec:rsigma}. Per-control noise records and
gain are absent from the frozen products, so those two stratifications are
bounded only through these rank distributions.

One stratum departs. The fine-channel windows fail the $U(0,1)$ rank test at
$\pv{\KsFineP}$ ($n=\NFine$), because \NStageOneFine{} of them contain an on-star
peak above their own control maximum against \ExpStageOneFine{} expected, and
those are precisely the flagged windows of Table~\ref{tab:ledger}. The
coarse stratum, three times larger and predicting \ExpStageOneCoarse{} such
windows, contains \NStageOneCoarse: every stage-1 flagged window in the
survey is drift-resolving. A 15.6--31.25\,MHz channel dilutes a narrow
circumstellar line, so the class that can resolve one is the class that finds
it, and the same reasoning says a genuine unresolved spectral carrier would appear in
Class~A first. The departure is nonetheless genuine, and it survives removal
of every fine window carrying a stellar crossing (Fig.~\ref{fig:ctrldiag}),
so fine-window rank probabilities are optimistic for the class as a whole and
not only where a crossing sits. This is the quantitative reason the spatial
comparison is used as a prioritisation and never as a test of the null
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}).

\subsubsection{The survey rescored under a repaired screen}
\label{sec:repaired}

Two corrections were built from the stored control vectors and rescored over
the \RmScopeWin{} windows of the \NWindows{} searched that retain a control
vector, and the \RmScopeReleased{} stage-1 windows inside them.
\emph{Radius matching} ranks the star against only the \RmInnerN{}
probes with $u\leq\RmInnerU$, at a cost in resolution; \emph{detrending} keeps
all \NCtrl{} probes and subtracts the pooled radial profile $m(u)$ measured on
the calibration windows, at unchanged resolution, its one free choice being
the stellar debit $m(0)$ between \RmDebitCons{} and \RmDebitCal$\,\sigma$.
Table~\ref{tab:repaired} therefore reports the debit each flagged window
survives rather than adopting a value.

Neither correction is adopted, for two reasons. Neither promotes a window:
under detrending the flagged set stands at \RmBFlag{} windows
(Table~\ref{tab:repaired}), and the unattributed count is one for every repair
and every debit between zero and \RmSurvCp$\,\sigma$. And neither makes the
rank distribution uniform: detrending moves the calibration-sample median from
\RmHORawMed{} to \RmHODetMed{} ($D=\RmHORawD$ to $D=\RmHODetD$) and the median
over the windows it can be applied to from
\RmSvRawMed{} to \RmSvDetMed{} ($\pv{\RmSvRawP}$ to
$\pv{\RmSvDetP}$),
while a leave-one-star-out fit over the \LooNStars{} calibration stars returns
median \LooMedian{} with $D=\LooD$ against \HoDetMedian{} and \HoDetD{} in
sample, so the profile is neither over-fitted nor sufficient to restore
exchangeability. The survey's false-alarm budget is therefore taken from the
measured tail rate below.

\subsubsection{Noise at the stellar position}
\label{sec:rsigma}

The noise scale of Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} is built across the annulus, so it
carries no variance peculiar to the stellar position, where residual
continuum, deconvolution or self-calibration residuals and position-correlated
calibration error would concentrate. That is the method's largest untested
assumption, and the retained products bound it. $R_\sigma$, the star's robust
residual scale over the \NRsigWin{} stage-1 windows that retain full control
vectors, measured against the median of the \RsigNCtrl{} retained control
positions, runs \RsigLo{} to \RsigHi: the star agrees with its ring to better
than \RsigBoundPct{} per cent at 95 per cent confidence. That matters most for
CP$-$72~2713, where an unmodelled broadband excess of \RsigNeedCPPct{} per
cent at the star would push $T_\star$ below the ring maximum; the bound is a
factor of three smaller, so that explanation is disfavoured without being
excluded. On a seeded random sample of \RsigSampN{} \emph{unflagged} windows
the same test returns \RsigSampMed{} (\RsigSampLo--\RsigSampHi), every one
within \RsigSampPct{} per cent of unity. It tests variances and not means, and
is blind to the one- or two-channel artefact the search is built to find,
which the local null and recurrence address instead.

\subsubsection{The measured false-alarm rate}
\label{sec:empiricalfa}

Because the body of the rank distribution is displaced, $1/(\NCtrl+1)$ is the
rank \emph{resolution} of the ensemble and not a per-window null probability,
and the survey's false-alarm budget is quoted from measurement. Two
independent measurements agree: of the \RankCalN{} calibration windows
carrying no attributed line, \RankCalK{} exceed all \NCtrl{} of their
controls, one in \RankCalOneIn{} or \RankCalRatio{} times exchangeable, and
the pseudo-stars, which cannot contain a signal at all, rank first in
\PseudoFAPct{} per cent of out-of-sample trials against \ExchFAPct{} per cent
exchangeable, a factor \PseudoFirstRatio.

The uncertainty on that factor is not binomial, because the 16 inner probes of
one window share its noise realisation and its execution block. Over all
\TailNWinBoot{} calibration windows the stored control vectors give
\TailNExceed{} first ranks in \TailNTrials{} trials, a factor \TailRatioBoot,
and resampling whole windows, or whole execution blocks (\TailNBlockBoot{} of
them), widens the 95 per cent interval barely at all,
\TailWinLo--\TailWinHi{} and \TailBlockLo--\TailBlockHi{} against
\TailBinomLo--\TailBinomHi{} binomial. The factor lies between \TailBlockLo{}
and \TailBlockHi, with the released-product value \PseudoFirstRatio{} inside
that interval, and no disposition changes anywhere in it.

Propagated over the survey's \NWindows{} windows, the pseudo-star rate
predicts \PseudoExpFlags{} \emph{rank-first} windows,
$P(\geq1)=\PseudoPgeOne$ per cent, against \RankCalExpIdeal{} under
exchangeability. That is a rank-only rate: it does not impose the trigger,
so it predicts rank-first windows and \emph{not} candidates, and it is not
this survey's chance expectation for an unattributed stage-1 event, which is
\ClustStageMean{} (Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}). Every per-window probability in this paper
should be read as multiplied by \PseudoFirstRatio, and none was claimed
significant after the survey-wide correction of Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.
The tail is mildly anti-conservative where the body is strongly displaced, and
it is the tail the candidate screen uses. The displacement is not one
configuration's doing: \RankCalStarsBelow{} of \RankCalStarsN{} stars have a
median rank below 0.5 (Fig.~\ref{fig:rankcdf}). The calibration sample covers
\CampStars{} stars and windows within a block are not independent epochs, so
the sizes above carry that caveat; at \HONoiseWin{} null windows the test
resolves a shift of this size ($D\geq\HOKsCrit$ against \HOKsD{} observed).

\subsection{Audit against a region-maximum statistic} \label{sec:aumic}

The statistic of Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} evaluates star and controls identically. A
region maximum over $n_{\rm src}=\RingNSrc$ positions at the star, ranked
against single-position controls, is not exchangeable with them and ranks
first with probability $n_{\rm src}/(n_{\rm src}+\NCtrl)=\AsymExpectPct$ per
cent rather than $1/(\NCtrl+1)$, a property of the estimator class. All
\NWindows{} windows are scored under both forms and both columns are released
per window. The symmetric criterion flags a strict \emph{subset}, \NSpatial{}
windows against \NRegionMaxFlag{} in \NRegionMaxPairs{} star--band pairs, with
no window flagged by the symmetric form alone (Table~\ref{tab:bothstats});
those that drop out do so because the stellar position never exceeded its own
control ensemble. AU~Mic is the clearest case: the excess lies in the field
around the star rather than at it.

\NStarMoreEB{} stars hold further public blocks this survey did not take
(\S\ref{sec:sample}), which \S\ref{sec:discussion} turns into an

\subsection{Validation checks in detail}

\subsubsection{The hold-out reservation}
\label{app:heldoutfull}

The star-versus-control rank prioritises candidates and provides no detection
$p$-value, because exact star/control exchangeability fails in the prospective
hold-out sample. Every disposition in \S\ref{sec:technosearch} therefore rests
on astrophysical evidence or on recurrence, never on a rank alone.

The statistic and the mask were settled on the \NWindows{} windows above and
cannot also calibrate themselves. A hold-out was therefore reserved by a rule
committed on \HoRuleDate{} (\texttt{\HoRuleHash}), before the archival search
finished and before any reserved block was searched: a block is held out if
and only if $\mathrm{sha256}(\textsc{uid})_{[:8]} \bmod 5 = 0$, subject to two
guards that use identifiers alone --- blocks in the previous release stay in
the survey, and no system loses every block --- so that the
hold-out assignment can be recomputed from the UIDs alone. The
three choices that can manufacture a candidate were fixed first: the detection
statistic of Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} on \StatFreezeDate{}
(\texttt{\StatFreezeHash}), the promote/retire criteria on \PreregDate{}
(\texttt{\PreregHash}), and the hold-out rule \HoLeadDays{} days after the
statistic, with the first reserved block searched \HoLeadBlockDays{} days
later. Because local commit timestamps can be rewritten, the dates are
corroborated by GitHub server-side push times and by the Zenodo deposit's
minted date. The diagnostics built afterwards --- the radius-corrected
normalisation, the visibility-domain test, the drift-resolved injection
analysis and the $P_{90}$ budget --- cannot promote a window.

Of \NEbProcessedAll{} blocks processed, \NEbHeldOut{} are reserved
(\HoPctHeld{} per cent), giving \HoWin{} windows toward \HoStars{} stars with
no star and no system lost from the headline sample. Four quantities are
calibrated on those blocks and on nothing else: the rank displacement, the
false-alarm tail factor, the radial behaviour of the control ensemble, and the
claim that stage-1 status alone carries no candidate significance.

The ranks are displaced out of sample by very nearly the amount measured in
sample: median add-one rank \HoRankMed{} against 0.5 (block-clustered 95 per
cent \HoRankMedLo--\HoRankMedHi), $D=\HoRankKsD$ at $\pv{\HoRankKsP}$ over
\HoWin{} windows. The false-alarm tail factor, the measured pseudo-star
first-rank rate divided by the exchangeable rate $\RankFloorVal$, is
\HoTailFactor{} on \HoPseudoTrials{} trials, with a bootstrap clustered over
the \HoTailBlocks{} reserved blocks giving \HoTailLo--\HoTailHi, and the
reserved blocks contain \HoStageOne{} stage-1 outliers against
\HoStageOneExp{} expected. The standardised control level runs \HoRadBins{}
from the inner to the outer edge of the annulus, a mean per-window slope of
$\HoRadSlope\pm\HoRadSlopeSe$: the displacement reproduces out of sample, but
the steep inner-edge excess seen in sample does not, so the radial correction
of \S\ref{sec:radial} is one demonstrated mechanism for the displacement
rather than the whole of it.

The older, processing-order calibration set is retained because it carries
repeat observations the hold-out does not, and it must be read with one
caveat stated plainly. \textbf{It was scored on \CampAsOf, by the pipeline as
it then stood --- that is, before the ACA control-annulus repair of
\S\ref{sec:acarepair}.} Its \CampUnattrib{} unattributed stage-1 outliers are
therefore pre-repair outliers, and its measured tail rate, \CampExpUn{}
unattributed events over \CampWindows{} windows, is a pre-repair tail rate.
Because the repair \emph{removes} outliers rather than creating them (below),
that rate is an upper bound on the post-repair one, and it is used here only
in that direction. The positive control recovers ($\beta$~Pictoris CO at the
stellar position in \CampStageOneCO{} of the \CampStageOne{} stage-1
windows), and Table~\ref{tab:cprepeat} gives the CP$-$72~2713 repeat test in
full.

\paragraph*{Which extraction change removed HD~14055 and HD~23484.} Earlier
versions of this paper listed \CampUnattrib{} unattributed outliers here
beside \NStageOneUnattrib{} in the Results, and the two lists disagreed
because they are different samples scored at different times, not because
either count was mistyped. Table~\ref{tab:heldout}(b) resolves it by
carrying both scorings of the same three windows. The repair re-extracted
\RcNReExtracted{} released windows in \RcNReExtractedBlocks{} blocks on the

\emph{across} the control positions, moving the controls moves the star's own
statistic as well as the ring's. Over the \RcNCompared{} windows scored both
ways the repair is unbiased --- median change \RcMedDStar{} at the star and
\RcMedDCtrl{} at the ring --- but it is not small: the typical move is

change a rank decision in a window where the two were within a tenth of each
other, as all three of these were. Under the repaired geometry \RcTwoStar{} and \RcThreeStar{} fall
behind their own rings --- \RcTwoPostNge{} and \RcThreePostNge{} control

Recurrence rather than rank settles a marginal event in either scoring: none
of the three recurs at its own tuning in a further block.

\subsubsection{The radial gradient}
\label{app:radialfull}

The cause was the control geometry, not the primary beam. Earlier versions
of this paper argued that primary-beam-corrected noise rising with radius
left the probes at a different effective noise from the star. That
explanation cannot be right: the statistic is formed on uncorrected
amplitudes, whose thermal noise does not depend on position, and a
primary-beam effect would act on both arrays, whereas the departure was
confined to the ACA stratum. The original extraction code took the primary
beam from a fixed 12\,m dish for every window, so an ACA 7\,m window had
its control annulus placed at \AcaDishRatioPct{} per cent of the correct
radius; at \AcaRefFreq\,GHz the inner edge landed at
\AcaInnerImpl\,arcsec instead of \AcaInnerCorr\,arcsec, inside a typical
ACA synthesised main lobe of \AcaSynBeam\,arcsec. Those controls carried the
star's own flux, stood high, and pushed the star low --- the direction
observed. The diagnosis below is of that original extraction; the repair,
and what the released catalogue carries, are in \S\ref{sec:acarepair}.

Three tests establish it, all from the released products. The control
probes are drawn once from a fixed seed, so fractional radii are identical
in every window and each stored control statistic pairs with its radius.
\emph{First}, the excess is confined to the affected stratum and sits at
the inner edge of the annulus: pooling by fractional radius, the innermost
bin stands \AcaExcessSeven{} above the outer half in the
\AcaNWinProfSeven{} ACA windows with stored control vectors, against
\AcaExcessTwelve{} in the \AcaNWinProfTwelve{} 12\,m windows. Pooled over
both arrays the profile runs \RadProfIn{} at $u=\RadProfUIn$, falls to
\RadProfMin{} near mid-annulus and returns to \RadProfOut{} at the outer
edge --- the U shape that a monotonic radial noise gradient does not
predict and that contamination of the innermost probes does. \emph{Second}, matching on
radius removes most of the departure: ranking the star against only the
\RadInnerN{} probes with $u\leq\RadInnerUHi$ moves the median over that
restricted ensemble from \RadRankAllMed{} to \RadRankInMed{} and $D=\RadRankAllD$
($\pv{\RadRankAllP}$) to \RadRankInD{} ($\pv{\RadRankInP}$). \emph{Third}, a
position with no star in it behaves the same way: an innermost probe
treated as a \emph{pseudo-star} and ranked against the other \NCtrl$-1$
probes is displaced as far as the real ones out of sample, median
\HOPseudoMed{} against \HORadRankAllMed{} ($\pv{\HOPseudoP}$). The
displacement is a property of \emph{position in the field}, not of stars.

The repair belongs at the measurement stage: standardising each probe
within its own radius bin, and the star on the innermost bin, removes the
gradient by construction, with no free parameter. Run over all \LocAllWin{}
windows it changes the list --- \LocStageLost{} windows lose the flag and
\LocStageGained{} gains it, so the count falls from \LocStageGlobal{} to
\LocStageLocal. We do not adopt the radius-corrected statistic as
primary, and the reason is empirical: applied prospectively to \RadValStarN{} windows it played no part in
building, it leaves the stellar rank inconsistent with uniformity and moves
the median \RadValStarToward{} the exchangeable value
(\S\ref{sec:radval}). Since the correction removes the radial component by
construction, what remains is larger than what it removed and is not
radial. The frozen statistic therefore defines every count reported here;
under the corrected one the survey would have \LocCorrStage{} stage-1
windows, \LocCorrAttrib{} attributed to CO and \LocCorrUnattrib{}
unattributed --- different windows, the same conclusion --- and the
released catalogue carries both.

The same mechanism touches completeness, which the injections alone would
not reveal because they are placed at the stellar position only.
Extrapolating the out-of-sample radial profile to the stellar radius gives
an offset of \HoRadAtStar$\,\sigma$, so an injected signal is divided by
too much and the quoted recovery fractions are \HoRadTrendSign{} by of
order \HoRadBiasPct{} per cent of the threshold: small, and in the safe
direction, but not zero.

The general point is that spatial control positions prioritise candidates
soundly in targeted interferometric data but cannot establish significance
in them, because the field around a chosen target is not blank sky. The
remedy is physical rather than statistical --- a test for a point source at
the stellar position, which the visibilities answer directly
(\S\ref{app:futurework}).

\subsubsection{The line mask}
\label{app:linecostfull}

The searches as executed matched crossings to catalogued rest frequencies
within one spectral channel at zero drift. The $\beta$~Pictoris windows
showed that inadequate: both fell outside that tolerance, by 10.6 and
21.9\,MHz, while lying within 3.6\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of the systemic velocity of
a resolved, mapped disc. Every crossing was therefore reclassified with a
velocity-space mask, $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ around each catalogued
transition --- a post-hoc classification criterion, never a detection
threshold, whose half-width was adopted after the $\beta$~Pictoris
crossings had been seen. Reclassifying all \NHits{} crossings at $\pm20$ to
$\pm100$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ costs \MaskBWTwenty{} to \MaskBWHundred{} per cent
of gross bandwidth, and every crossing that narrowing the mask releases as
unattributed is an identified carbon monoxide line of this survey's own:
the two rank-flagged unattributed crossings are the same pair at every
half-width in that range. Widening it cannot suppress anything further, so
the choice of $\pm\MaskVWidth$ cannot have been tuned to remove a
candidate.

The mask holds \MaskNTransNew{} transitions of \MaskNSpecNew{} species at
full JPL/CDMS precision \citep{Pickett1998,CDMS2005}. \emph{The search
itself ran a shorter list}: \MaskNTransOld{} transitions of
\MaskNSpecOld{} species, rounded to 1\,MHz, identical value for value to
the veto list compiled into the search code; [C\,\textsc{i}]$(1{-}0)$ and
H30$\alpha$ were added to the paper's mask at a later revision. Neither
addition lies near a threshold crossing and no nearest-transition
assignment changes between the two lists, so the difference is a
documentation matter and not a correctness one; it is stated because one
number was being used for two objects. The species criterion
is the one objective rule statable in advance: the transitions ALMA's band
definitions are built around --- the CO isotopologues, HCN, HCO$^{+}$, CS,
silicon monoxide SiO, CN, H$_2$CO and [C\,\textsc{i}] --- which dominate
the emission of
disc-selected fields. The choice is consequential --- the full Splatalogue
selection of \S\ref{app:linemask} masks \MaskDbPctUnion{} per cent of the
union against \MaskUnionPct{} per cent here --- so ``coincident with a
known molecular line'' is a statement about a catalogue rather than a
binary property. Since no masked crossing exceeds its own control ensemble,
no plausible widening or narrowing of the species list changes a
disposition.

The cost is \MaskUnionPct{} per cent of the unique-frequency union and
\MaskGrossPct{} per cent of window-summed gross bandwidth, CN and CO
dominating. It is not uniform, so Table~\ref{tab:maskband} gives it band by
band: it reaches \MaskWorstPct{} per cent in Band~\MaskWorstBand{} and
stays below 1.5 per cent in Bands~3--5. Nor is it shared equally between
the two experiments --- \MaskPctA{} per cent of the Class~A union against
\MaskPctB{} per cent in Class~B --- so the fine-channel experiment pays
\MaskPctRatioAB{} times the fractional price, its coverage being
concentrated in the line-tuned windows a molecular mask is designed to
remove. The two do not sum to the total because the classes overlap in
frequency.

Masking is a design selection effect separating \emph{discovery} from
\emph{classification}. A carrier at a line frequency is as detectable as
anywhere else, but it cannot be classified, because the astrophysical prior
at a catalogued transition is overwhelming. Removing those frequencies from
the searched domain is the conservative choice, at the price that \emph{we
place no constraint at all on artificial emission lying inside the masked
regions, at any signal strength}. A better design searches every frequency
and attaches a \emph{lower prior} to a line-coincident event instead, since
these are exactly the frequencies a communicator wanting to be found near a
natural reference might choose. The released channels support that second
search, for which the $\beta$~Pictoris crossings are the test case.

\section{Recommendations for a next-generation ALMA technosignature search}
\label{app:futurework}
\label{app:instrument}

ALMA's advantages here are its mm-wave sensitivity, an essentially
unsearched tuning range, the spatial discrimination an interferometer
supplies, and a public archive that can be reprocessed without new
allocations. Six changes would enlarge what this experiment can say,
given in priority order. The first two would change a conclusion; the
rest widen a domain.

\emph{(1) Use the visibilities.} A celestial point source at the stellar
direction $\mathbf{x}_\star$ contributes
\begin{equation}
V_{ij}(\nu,t)=A(\nu,t)\,
\exp\!\left(-2\pi i\,\mathbf{u}_{ij}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{x}_\star\right),
\label{eq:visphase}
\end{equation}
so rotating the phase centre to the star leaves a coherent signal entirely
in the real part, with an imaginary component consistent with zero on every
baseline. That asks whether the data contain a point source \emph{where the
star is}. The control ranking asks only whether one extraction is unusually
large compared with its neighbours, which is a weaker question and one
whose null depends on where in the field those neighbours sit. Applied to
every window it would demote the control ensemble to a calibration layer
and give each candidate a physically specific test rather than a rank. It
requires retaining the visibilities, which this survey did not do.

\emph{(2) Search the remaining public blocks these stars already hold.}
\S\ref{sec:extension} reports \ExtBlocks{} of the \LedNotProcessed{}
searched with the frozen pipeline, at no telescope cost. The gain is
narrower than it sounds: every block is repeat coverage of a star and
tuning already searched, so they add no star, no system and no frequency,
and they extend only the time axis. They are also concentrated, attaching
to \NStarUnsearchedEB{} of the \NStars{} stars. Of the \NSysOneEB{} systems
with a single searched epoch only \NSysOneEBGain{} would gain a second
block, and the extension moved \ExtSysGained{} system out of the
single-epoch class. A second block often supplies a baseline of hours
rather than years, which one archive query settles before any download.

\emph{(3) Retain more control spectra.} Full dynamic spectra survive for
only \LNNRetained{} of the \NCtrl{} control positions, which is what breaks
the second-stage null in a structured field. Retaining all of them costs
storage and nothing else.

\emph{(4) Drift discrimination on coarse windows.} Every in-grid drift on a
15.625-MHz window is sub-channel over a track, so \NCoarse{} of
\NWindows{} windows measure amplitude but cannot distinguish a drifting
carrier from a stationary one. Finer channelisation, or a drift model
spanning several blocks, would recover that axis.

\emph{(5) Extend the injection campaign and add polarisation.} The
stratified campaign of Appendix~\ref{app:inject} spans band, channel width,
integration time and array; what remains unspanned is other target
environments and edge-phased drift grids. A per-hand search buys partial discrimination against weakly polarised
backgrounds at no sensitivity cost, but ALMA's linear feeds mean it is
blind to circular polarisation; a cross-hand extraction is what a
circularly polarised carrier would require.

\emph{(6) A boundary on drift linearity.} The search assumes linear drift
over a track, and curvature matters once $\tfrac{1}{2}\ddot\nu T^{2}$
exceeds half a native channel, which only the shortest-period orbits reach:
$P\lesssim1.6$\,d for a typical Class~A window. Among the \NExoPlanetsTab{}
known planets of the \NExoHostsTab{} hosts in the sample, \PlanetInsideN{}
lies inside that boundary, \PlanetInsideName{} at \PlanetInsideP\,d. The
grid is built at constant acceleration, so the boundary is one number
across the band, \AccelCeilMed\,m\,s$^{-2}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:accel}); it
clears the accelerations a transmitter on a rotating, orbiting planet
cannot avoid by a wide margin, exceeding Earth's equatorial rotational
acceleration, \AccelEarthRot\,m\,s$^{-2}$, by a factor of
\AccelRotMargin{} ($\AccelCeilMed/\AccelEarthRot$).

Finally, disc-CO false positives should be budgeted for, but the targets
should not be pre-judged. \DiscFpWin{} of the \DiscFpOfFlags{} stage-1
flagged windows are molecular emission toward disc hosts, arising in
\DiscFpSys{} of the \NDebrisKwSys{} debris-disc systems searched: one
flagged system in \DiscFpOneIn. Any future search on ALMA, NOEMA or the
JVLA inherits that rate. The remedy is procedural --- measure the continuum at each target
\emph{before} promoting a window, which would have caught HD~48370 and both
$\beta$~Pictoris windows at the first gate. We do \emph{not} recommend lowering the threshold toward disc hosts:
a detection statistic should not know whether its target is
astrophysically interesting, or a real transmitter would be penalised for
the company it keeps.

\section{Radio-frequency interference}
\label{app:rfi}

Millimetre interferometry is an unusually poor environment for terrestrial
interference, though not an empty one, and an earlier version of this
statement overreached. The searched range is not above all allocated
services: ITU Radio Regulations Article~5 carries active allocations
throughout 90--275\,GHz, and WRC-19 identified parts of 275--450\,GHz for
land-mobile and fixed use. The specific hazard in this band is the
\RadarLo--\RadarHi\,GHz Earth exploration-satellite (active) allocation
used by spaceborne cloud radar, CloudSat and, since 2024, EarthCARE, which
is a documented contaminant of ALMA Band~3. \RadarNWin{} of the
\RadarNTot{} searched windows overlaps that band, and it carries
\RadarNCross{} threshold crossings and \RadarNStage{} stage-1 events, so
no result here rests on it and no overpass timing needs to be checked.
What does favour this band is that terrestrial transmitters are sparse
compared with centimetre wavelengths, that the array sits at 5000\,m on a
site chosen partly for radio quietness, and, decisively, that an
interferometer is not a total-power detector. A signal that is not in the far field at the phase
centre does not fringe-track: it acquires a rapidly varying geometric
phase across the array and is suppressed in the correlation rather than
imaged.

\emph{What interference screening this survey actually has, stated with its
coverage, because the coverage is uneven.} Two screens are computed for
every searched window from the released catalogue and are reproducible from
it: the \RadarLo--\RadarHi\,GHz allocation test above, and the
sky-frequency and intermediate-frequency clustering tests below. The
\NCtrl-position rank screen asks whether an excess is at the star or
everywhere in the field, which is an interference test as well as a
prioritisation, and covers every window. Recurrence at the same tuning in an
independent epoch covers the crossings for which a covering repeat block
exists. The visibility fit of \S\ref{sec:vistest} asks whether an excess has
the phase signature of a source at the phase centre, which a near-field
emitter would not have, but it is a consistency check rather than a filter
and it is not what disposes of anything here. Finally, the correlator
flagging is the observatory's own, replayed verbatim from each block's
delivered calibration script; it is not recorded per window in the release
and cannot be reconstructed from it. \emph{No per-channel interference flag
of our own exists, and none can be recovered.}

Three measurements support that reasoning. First, interference is a
property of the observatory and not of the star, so a terrestrial carrier
should appear at the same \emph{sky} frequency in every window that covers
it. Of the \NStageOneUnattrib{} unattributed events, \RfiNTestable{}
\RfiTestableVerb{} a released crossing frequency; \RfiOtherStars{} windows
toward \RfiOtherStarsMin--\RfiOtherStarsMax{} other stars in
\RfiOtherBlocks{} other blocks cover those frequencies, and \RfiSameChan{}
of them carries a crossing in the same channel.\RfiNotTestableClause{}
Second, over all
\RfiNCross{} released crossing frequencies in the survey there is no
clustering in sky frequency: binned at \RfiBinMHz\,MHz the largest
multiplicity is \RfiMaxMult, against \RfiNullMaxMean{} for a null that keeps each crossing in its own window and randomises its position
within it ($\pv{\RfiNullMaxP}$). Third, an artefact of the signal chain would sit at
a fixed intermediate frequency rather than a fixed sky frequency, and the
crossings do not: their fractional positions within their own windows have
median \RfiFracMed{} and are uniform to $D=\RfiFracKsD$ at
$\pv{\RfiFracKsP}$ over \RfiNFrac{} crossings. CP$-$72~2713's crossing,
the one with a released frequency, lies \RfiEdgeChanMin{} channels from
its nearest window edge.

None of this is a substitute for the ON/OFF cadence a dedicated search
uses, and we do not present it as one. Accordingly we claim only that
\emph{no identified interference mechanism accounts for these events};
allocation tables cannot exclude harmonics, intermodulation products,
unconventional emitters or near-field sources, and an archival dataset
without a simultaneous off-source strategy cannot do better than that. What it establishes is that the crossing population as a whole carries none
of the three signatures interference leaves --- fixed sky frequency, fixed
intermediate frequency, or presence in unrelated targets --- which is a
statement about the population and not a clearance of any individual
crossing. One crossing \emph{is} rejected as instrumental on these grounds:
\LgVerRejectStar, which appears in the same coarse channel toward unrelated
stars and whose visibility fit places it far off the phase centre.

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.955\columnwidth}{\footnotesize

Appendix~\ref{app:external}: a prospectively searched extension of
\ExtBlocks{} public blocks, which recovers the $\beta$~Pictoris disc CO in
three previously unsearched epochs and adds no unattributed event; the
$\beta$~Pictoris recovery itself, which is the survey's astrophysical
positive control for both the spatial and the spectral machinery; and the
absence of any shared data with the only prior ALMA-archival search, which
leaves the null result resting on internal validation alone.
