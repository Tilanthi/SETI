A narrowband carrier search of archival ALMA spectra toward nearby stars

The manuscript, its appendices, tables and figures in one PDF, with the
programs that write every number in it.

The search covers 405 fine-channel windows toward 61 systems, where a drift
can be resolved, and 1258 coarser windows where it cannot, over 47.7 GHz of
union bandwidth in disjoint intervals above 90 GHz. Windows are filtered for
an unresolved carrier drifting linearly in the stellar frame; threshold
crossings are tested against catalogued transitions at the star's velocity and
then sought again in later execution blocks. Nine injections in ten are
recovered at 1.5e15 W for the median system. No confirmed carrier is found:
of 56 crossings 18 are attributed and 38 are not, none of the unattributed
ones recurs, and the remainder is consistent with chance. Line emission, not
interference, produces every astrophysical false positive. What limits the
search is the coverage the archive supplies rather than depth.

Provenance is the ALMA Science Archive and the project codes listed in the
paper; nothing here is redistributed data.

No number in the paper is typed. Each is written by the program that computes
it from the catalogue and re-derived whenever the paper is built; the whole
product tree is deleted and regenerated and compared byte for byte; an audit
re-derives the identities that must hold among the published values and stops
the build where one does not; and every assertion in the analysis was first
made to fail on deliberately wrong input. The abstract is held to the
1920-character limit of the arXiv metadata field in three independent places:
on the typeset page, in the source with the macros expanded, and in the
plain-ASCII text prepared for the submission form.
