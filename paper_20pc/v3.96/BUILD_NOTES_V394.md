## Glenn's item

**G-1 — β Pictoris b radio detection.** Ortiz Ceballos et al. (2026),
arXiv:2609.16720, report MeerKAT detection of auroral radio emission from
the planet β Pic b: recurring, highly circularly polarised bursts plus
persistent emission over 0.85–3.5 GHz, attributed to the electron-cyclotron
maser instability, implying ≳1.25 kG at the planet.

Written into §"An astrophysical positive control … β Pictoris CO", because
β Pic is this paper's positive control and a genuine planet-localised radio
source in the same system belongs there. Three points, all generated by
`ecmi_v396.py` (round 57) rather than hand-typed:

1. **It is out of our bands by physics, not by depth.** ECMI cannot exceed
   the cyclotron frequency at its source, 2.8 MHz/G × B. Their 1.25 kG
   reproduces their own 3.5 GHz upper edge — the generator *asserts* this,
   so if either number moves the build fails. Reaching our lowest
   frequency, 89.6 GHz, needs 32 kG = 26× the β Pic b field; Band 7 needs
   99×. Their detection sits 1.4 decades below our coverage. Our null is
   therefore not evidence against it, and the paper says so.
2. **It cuts both ways on polarisation.** It shows strong circular
   polarisation *is* measurable from a planet-mass companion in one of our
   own targets, so the discrimination we forgo is real rather than
   hypothetical. It also shows high circular polarisation is *not* a mark
   of artificiality, because a natural magnetosphere produces it. The text
   now says polarisation would separate compact coherent emission from
   thermal dust and line emission, and would **not** separate engineering
   from magnetospheres.
3. Bibliography entry added in alphabetical position; verified in the
   rendered PDF, not in source.

## Two corrections found while doing referee work

**1. The visibility-extension partition was built on a false premise.**
The previous `visextend` generator partitioned the 75 crossings by whether
their block had been *searched*, assuming a searched block is one we still
hold. It is not: the pipeline deletes each measurement set once its search
completes. Checking the host directly, **two** execution blocks retain
visibilities and **neither contains a threshold crossing** — so no crossing
can be tested from data in hand. Counts corrected 60 → 62 windows,
50 → 52 blocks, 0.31 → 0.40 TB, and the partition is now asserted to close.
`OPEN_TASKS.md` corrected to match. Same failure mode as the recurring
frozen-input bugs: a number that looked right, derived from the wrong
predicate.

**2. The line-mask half-width comparison was frame-ambiguous.** The
manuscript claimed a ±13 km/s Keplerian-only mask "would have suppressed
and released exactly the same windows". The mask is applied to
`line_offset_kms`, an **uncorrected sky-frequency offset** (β Pic reads
27.7 km/s there but −0.39 km/s in the stellar frame), while the ±13 km/s
Keplerian bound is a **stellar-frame** velocity. The two are not
comparable, and in the frame the mask actually uses, ±13 km/s would have
attributed *none* of the nine β Pic windows. Corrected: the Keplerian bound
is now quoted as the physical scale motivating masking, not as an
alternative that was tested, and the robustness claim rests on measurement
instead (below).
