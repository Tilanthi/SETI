\begin{abstract}
We ask whether any nearby star operates a narrowband millimetre or
submillimetre transmitter bright enough for ALMA to have recorded it
incidentally. Technosignature searches have barely touched
\SurvFreqLo--\SurvFreqHi\,GHz; published work lies almost entirely below
30\,GHz.

We searched the ALMA archive for an unresolved, drifting carrier
at a stellar position. The sample is \NWindows{} windows toward
\NSystems{} systems within 40\,pc, all observed for other science,
mostly debris discs. Of these, \NWinA{} windows toward
\NSysClassA{} systems have channels fine enough to follow a drifting
carrier. These cover \UnionClassA\,GHz of sky frequency, in disconnected
intervals, not one continuous band.

Sensitivity was measured, not modelled, by injecting artificial carriers
into the calibrated visibilities and recovering them through the same
pipeline. The median system's best window reaches
$\mathrm{EIRP}_{90} = \EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W. A \BenchDiam\,m dish
radiating \BenchPowerMW\,MW at \BenchFreqGHz\,GHz appears at
$\BenchEirp$\,W, reached for \BenchNSysA{} of the \NSysClassA{} systems.

The search returns \NCross{} threshold crossings, and the unexplained
ones are as numerous as chance alone predicts. Carbon monoxide accounts
for \NAttributed{}, including the $\beta$~Pictoris disc, recovered with
no prior information. Only \NUnattrRankFlagged{} crossings outrank all
\NCtrl{} spatial controls, and neither recurs at a second epoch.
Stacking epochs for \StkNStar{} stars in the stellar rest frame reaches
\StkProxEirpCalMath\,W for Proxima~Centauri, for carriers whose reflex drift
stays within \StkProxTolKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

We find no persistent narrowband transmitter. ALMA chose these targets,
so the result constrains these \NSystems{} systems, not the solar
neighbourhood.
\end{abstract}
