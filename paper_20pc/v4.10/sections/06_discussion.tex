\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The search space this archive opens}
\label{sec:newspace}

The frequencies are what is new here. The windows searched span
\UnionGHz\,GHz of unique sky frequency, of which \DnuA\,GHz carries channels
fine enough to resolve a drifting carrier. Only \UnionBandThree\,GHz of that lies inside the 10\,MHz--115\,GHz
interval \citet{Wright2018} take as the frequency axis of the search
haystack, the ionospheric cutoff to the top of the Green Bank Telescope's
instrumentation. The remaining \UnionBandsFourEight\,GHz in
\UnionHiBandLabel{} has not been searched toward nearby stars before. The
targets of the one prior search of ALMA archival data \citep{Mason2025} lie
four orders of magnitude further away, so the comparison is best made in
minimum detectable \emph{received} flux at a matched channel width. On that
footing the windows here reach \FluxMatchBest\,W\,m$^{-2}$ at best, a
factor \FluxBestRatio{} deeper, and \FluxMatchMed\,W\,m$^{-2}$ at the
median, a factor \FluxMedRatio{} shallower.

There are physical reasons to look at millimetre wavelengths rather than
merely to accept them as an archival accident. A transmitter's directive gain rises as the square of frequency at fixed
aperture, $G=\eta(\pi D\nu/c)^{2}$. A dish of modest size is therefore an
extremely narrow, high-gain link in this regime: the \BenchDiam\,m aperture
used as this paper's benchmark has $G=\BenchGain$ at \BenchFreqGHz\,GHz.
The absolute bandwidth available also scales with carrier frequency, which
is what a high-rate data link or a wideband radar needs. Hardware that beams
energy, for power transmission or propulsion or ranging, radiates in the
same regime. Propagation is favourable as well. Interstellar dispersion and
scattering both fall steeply with frequency, so a millimetre carrier arrives
with far less temporal smearing and frequency-dependent delay than a
centimetre one. The narrow beams that come free at high frequency also waste
little power on empty sky. None of this makes millimetre
wavelengths the likeliest place to transmit. It makes them a plausible one
that had not been examined.

\subsection{What this experiment excludes, and what it does not}
\label{sec:excludes}

\noindent\emph{Excluded.} Consider a continuously transmitting,
unresolved carrier that lies outside the molecular-line mask, inside the
searched frequency range and inside the linear-drift grid, and that is
present during one of the archival epochs. Such a carrier would have been
found nine times in ten above the $\mathrm{EIRP}_{90}$ of
\S\ref{sec:pninetysel}, a median of $\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W per system on
its best window. None is present toward any of the \NSystems{} systems
searched.

One benchmark carries the physical reading of that figure. A \BenchDiam\,m
dish radiating \BenchPowerMW\,MW at \BenchFreqGHz\,GHz, at an aperture
efficiency of \BenchEta, reaches an EIRP of $\BenchEirp$\,W, and the survey
is sensitive to that depth for \BenchNSysA{} of the \NSysClassA{} Class~A
systems. \emph{EIRP is not transmitter power.} The same megawatt radiated
isotropically would be $10^{6}$\,W of EIRP, and it is the directivity, a
factor $\BenchGain$ here, that lifts a modest transmitter into the range
this survey probes. The exclusion therefore applies to beamed transmitters
whose beam contains the Solar System, and says almost nothing about
omnidirectional beacons.

Four further axes bound the excluded population as sharply as power does.
\emph{Frequency:} the coverage is sparse and inherited, \DnuA\,GHz of unique
Class~A frequency in a window function set by other astronomers' science,
and the median system is covered over only \SysUnionAMed\,GHz. \emph{Drift and acceleration:} the searched grid spans
\DriftCeilLo--\DriftCeilHi\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$, a line-of-sight
acceleration of \AccelCeilMed--\AccelCeilMax\,m\,s$^{-2}$. That contains
the rotational and orbital accelerations of the \NExoPlanetsTab{} known
planets of the \NExoHostsTab{} hosts in the sample, only TRAPPIST-1\,b
reaching it in its edge-on worst case. A transmitter whose own oscillator
drifts faster than that kinematic budget lies outside the grid.
\emph{Persistence:} the carrier must be radiating during one of the archival
observations, so these are limits on persistent emission.
\emph{Spectral width:} the statistic is built for emission confined to one
\ChanLoA--\ChanHiA\,kHz channel. A carrier broadened beyond a channel
loses signal to its own width, and a signal broad enough to resemble
continuum is removed with the spectral baseline.

\noindent\emph{Not excluded.} A transmitter absent during the archival
observation, or on for less than about a tenth of the time. At a duty cycle
of one tenth, recovery falls to \DwellFineT{} per cent for the fine class
and \DwellCoarseT{} per cent for the coarse, measured on the same injection
ladder; those trials are undrifted, so the joint
drifting-and-intermittent case is not measured at all. Emission inside
the $\pm\MaskHalfKms$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ molecular-line mask, at any strength,
which costs \MaskLostGHz\,GHz of coverage. Drift outside the searched grid,
or drift curved enough to leave a channel within the track. Signals broad
enough to be removed with the continuum baseline. Anything below a window's
own completeness. And any emission from the \NGaiaCensus{} stars within
40\,pc that ALMA has not observed, which is almost all of them.

\subsubsection{The survey's on-source time, in both directions}
\label{sec:exposure}

Two bookkeeping biases of opposite sign affect the exposure, and neither may
be quoted without the other. The extractor retained a single \texttt{FIELD} row per execution block.
In \TruncNBlocks{} of the \ExpNBlocks{} released blocks --- \TruncNWin{}
windows toward \TruncNStars{} stars --- further in-beam pointings on the
same star were discarded, \TruncHoursLost\,h of real on-source time and
\TruncPctSurvey{} per cent of the survey. Nothing published is thereby
wrong, because every noise level was measured from the data actually used.
But the \emph{achievable} sensitivity in those windows is understated by a
median factor \TruncGainMed{} in flux density, and those blocks were
re-extracted and re-searched. In the opposite direction, the released
on-source time is the number of integrations times the median dump length
rather than a sum of dump lengths, so short sub-scan boundary dumps are
over-counted. The exact computation moves the survey total from \OcOldMax{}
to \OcNewMax\,h. \emph{The first bias costs the survey time it
had and the second credits it with time it never had}, and they offset to
within \ExpNetLo--\ExpNetHi\,h.

\subsubsection{The omitted annual parallax} \label{sec:parallax}

The extraction phase-rotates the visibilities to each star's
\emph{barycentric} direction while the visibilities see the apparent one, so
annual parallax is omitted from the assumed phase centre. The resulting
amplitude loss is the normalised dirty beam evaluated at the parallax
displacement in units of the synthesised beam: parameter-free,
multiplicative and shape-preserving. Over the \PxNWin{} windows carrying the
astrometric keys it is \PxLossMedPct{} per cent in the median window and
\PxLossMaxPct{} per cent at worst, with \PxNGtTen{} windows losing more than
ten per cent. It is negligible throughout the ACA data that dominate this
survey and matters only for nearby stars observed in long-baseline 12\,m
configurations. \emph{We state it and do not apply it.} The sign is optimistic: every
limit quoted here is too deep by $1/(1-{\rm loss})$ in its own window. The
completeness campaign cannot see the term either, because its carriers are
deposited at the same assumed position the estimator evaluates. Correcting
it would leave the number of systems
reaching $10^{15}$\,W unchanged at \PxNSysCorr, because a long-baseline
block is never a system's most sensitive window.

\subsubsection{Two blind spots}

The search is Stokes~$I$ only. A fully polarised carrier is not missed, but
the discrimination that polarisation would offer against unpolarised
astrophysical emission is unavailable. ALMA's feeds are linear, so a per-hand search would help only a carrier
aligned with one feed. A circularly polarised signal divides equally between
XX and YY, and its circularity is in the cross-hands, which this analysis
does not extract. The frequency coverage, finally, is the archive's and not
a choice. None of the ``magic frequency'' arguments that have guided
centimetre-band searches --- the 1420\,MHz line, its multiples, the water
hole, or $\pi$ and $e$ times them --- falls in an ALMA band. A transmitter
deliberately placed on one lies outside this experiment by construction, and
its absence here is not evidence against it.

\subsection{What the archival experiment taught us}
\label{sec:lessons}

\emph{Molecular-line confusion, and not interference or instrumental
artefacts, is the dominant obstacle to a millimetre technosignature
search.} The interference screens are in Appendix~\ref{app:rfi}, and no
crossing in this survey is attributed to them. Catalogued molecular
transitions, almost all of them carbon monoxide,
account for \NAttributed{} of the \NCross{} threshold crossings, and \DiscFpWin{} of the \DiscFpOfFlags{} windows that
reached the spatial stage are disc or foreground CO, arising in
\DiscFpSys{} of the \NDebrisKwSys{} debris-disc systems searched --- one
flagged system in \DiscFpOneIn. The remedy that works is a velocity-space
mask evaluated in the stellar frame, and it is not free: it removes
\MaskLostPct{} per cent of otherwise usable coverage. The spatial screen is weakest in the same place. A line that fills the
primary beam raises the star and the control annulus together instead of
cancelling, so the screen loses power exactly where circumstellar gas is
brightest. A future millimetre search inherits that confusion rate. It
should measure the continuum at each target before promoting a window,
rather than lowering a threshold toward astrophysically interesting
targets.

\emph{Repeat epochs are what turn a crossing into a candidate, and an
archive supplies them only incidentally.} Temporal sampling is the weakest
axis of this experiment, and for close to half the sample it constrains an
intermittent emitter hardly at all, whatever its power. Archival coverage is also incomplete at the level of whole observations.
\FateDone{} of the \FateScope{} execution blocks in scope were searched.
\FateNoCal{} carry no ALMA pipeline calibration at all, \FateCalFail{}
failed calibration, \FateInfeas{} is infeasible at this data volume and
\FateUnattempted{} were not attempted. For one of them the observatory
delivered raw data it had never calibrated, so part of that gap lies in the
delivery rather than in the processing.

\emph{Completeness has to be measured rather than inferred from a threshold,
and measuring it is expensive.} A nominal $5\sigma$ level is a property of the noise. It is not a
statement about what a transmitter of a given power would do to a pipeline,
and the two differ here by the factor $\EirpNinetyMultA$ in Class~A and
$\EirpNinetyMultB$ in Class~B. The cost is that each injected window must be re-extracted and re-searched
over its full drift grid once per amplitude. That is why the campaign covers
a sample of windows and transfers its ratio to the others. Two limitations
of a different kind should be read with every limit. The null result rests on
internal validation alone: every check reported here tests this pipeline
against itself. Because the only prior ALMA-archival search targets a
disjoint sample, there is no external pipeline to compare against. And the
threshold crossings are not concentrated toward window edges, and the one
measurement that appeared to say otherwise is accounted for. Pooled over the
searched windows the near-edge exceedance rate is
$\times\VnEdgeRatio$ the interior rate, but \VnEdgeDomPct{} per cent of those
exceedances are in a single window carrying resolved CO close to the band
edge: drop that window and the ratio is $\times\VnEdgeNoDomRatio$
($\pv{\VnEdgeNoDomP}$), mask the line and it is $\times\VnEdgeMaskRatio$
($\pv{\VnEdgeMaskP}$), and on the half of the windows held out from that
window's own stratum it is $\times\VnEdgeHalfCleanRatio$
($\pv{\VnEdgeHalfCleanP}$). The excess is a property of one resolved disc and
not of the band edges, so no edge correction is applied to any limit.

\emph{Archival SETI buys sky and frequency cheaply and buys confirmation not
at all.} This experiment reached \NSystems{} systems and \UnionGHz\,GHz without
an allocation, at a sensitivity no targeted programme at these frequencies
has matched. It did so on data selected by others. \PctDiscProposal{} per
cent of the searched stars were observed for disc or planet-formation
science, which puts circumstellar CO in a large fraction of the fields, and
only \NMSearched{} of the \NMCensus{} catalogued M~dwarfs within 40\,pc
have any coverage at all. The quantities above therefore describe
\emph{these} stars, frequencies and epochs and nothing wider. The
non-detection cannot be converted into an occurrence rate for transmitters
within 40\,pc, which would need a denominator an archive-selected sample
does not supply. On the conventional Continuous Waveform Transmitter Figure
of Merit this survey is some four orders of magnitude less competitive than
major centimetre-wave programmes. That figure measures the frequency regime
and the archival temporal sampling rather than the design. Comparison is in
general better made axis by axis, because this survey differs from
centimetre-wave searches in frequency, spectral resolution, number of
systems, completeness, drift range and temporal coverage at once, and those
axes are coupled.

\subsection{What a dedicated ALMA technosignature experiment should do
differently}
\label{sec:future}

A purpose-built experiment on the same telescope would be a far stronger
one, and the changes it needs follow from the limitations above.
\emph{Channelise finely everywhere:} \NCoarse{} of the \NWindows{}
windows searched here have channels so coarse that every in-grid drift is
sub-channel over a track. They measure amplitude but cannot distinguish a
drifting carrier from a stationary one. \emph{Choose the targets:} an
archive weighted toward dusty discs is the worst case for molecular-line
confusion, and the M~dwarfs that dominate the nearby habitable-zone planet
census are the least covered. Both are decisions a proposal can simply
reverse. \emph{Choose the frequencies:} windows placed deliberately away
from the bright molecular transitions would recover most of the
\MaskLostGHz\,GHz the mask costs. \emph{Repeat the epochs on purpose:} two
or three visits separated by days and by years turn a crossing into a
testable claim, and the separation is a scheduling constraint rather than
extra sensitivity. \emph{Keep contemporaneous off-source controls:} full dynamic spectra
survive for only \LNNRetained{} of the \NCtrl{} control positions here,
which is what limits the second-stage null in a structured field. Retaining
them all costs storage and nothing else. \emph{Record
polarisation, including the cross-hands:} a per-hand search costs no
sensitivity and buys discrimination against weakly polarised astrophysical
emission, and only a cross-hand extraction can see a circularly polarised
carrier. \emph{Retain the visibilities:} rotating the phase centre to the star
leaves a genuine point source entirely in the real part of the visibility,
with an imaginary part consistent with zero on every baseline. That asks
whether the data contain a source \emph{where the star is}. It is a
physically specific question, and a stronger one than any rank against
neighbouring positions in an image.
