\subsection{A stellar-frame multi-epoch stacked search}
\label{sec:stack}

Combining the epochs of a star in that star's own rest frame deepens the
limit. \StkNStar{} of the surveyed stars hold more than one searched
execution block. Over \StkNGroup{} stacked groups, \StkNEpochComb{}
epoch-windows and \StkHours\,h, the flux limit improves by a median factor
\StkLimitGainMed{} over each star's best single epoch
(\StkLimitGainPNinety{} at the 90th percentile, \StkLimitGainMax{} at most).
Nothing survives the criterion except \StkNSurvive{} known molecular lines.
The limit a stack reaches is quoted on the same completeness convention as
every other sensitivity in this paper, measured by injection into the stack
itself, and not as a threshold on a stacked spectrum.

\emph{A fixed stellar-frame frequency is not a fixed observed frequency, so
the epochs must be registered before they are added.} The observer's motion
and the star's systemic velocity move the observed frequency of a
stellar-frame carrier by tens of km\,s$^{-1}$ between epochs. The
barycentric term alone swings by a median \StkVcorrSwingMed\,km\,s$^{-1}$
between the epochs of one star, and by as much as
\StkVcorrSwingMax\,km\,s$^{-1}$, which is many channels at the
\StkRefChanKHz\,kHz Class~A channel width. Added as observed, the epochs of
a real carrier would land in different channels and dilute rather than
reinforce. Each epoch's frequency axis is therefore transformed to the
stellar rest frame before stacking. The transform is applied per execution
block rather than per star, because within a block the correction moves only
\StkIntraBlockKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and a single mid-time suffices. Epochs are
then divided by their own primary-beam response and combined by inverse
variance over the channels of common stellar-frame coverage, with a minimum
of \StkMinBlocks{} blocks and \StkMinChan{} common channels. The combination
is thus a matched filter across epochs rather than a rescaling of one of
them. Groups are keyed on the star, resolved externally, and not on the
archival directory the search ran in: \StkNStarPre{} directories resolve to
\StkNStar{} stars and \StkNGroupPre{} groups to \StkNGroup, with no star lost
and none gained. Merging identities does not merge windows. A group is still
the intersection of the windows covering one stellar-frame frequency, so two
tunings sharing no channel cannot combine. \StkBadWeight{} of the
\StkNProd{} retained products (\StkBadWeightPct{} per cent) carry weights up
to $10^{11}$ times the median and are excluded.

The criterion was fixed before any stellar position was examined. A group is a candidate if its stacked $Z\ge\StkZmin$ at a common channel
and above every stacked control maximum. Two further clauses guard against
one epoch carrying the result: no single epoch may supply more than
\StkDomMax{} of the weighted sum, and at least \StkJkMin{} of the signal
must survive removal of the strongest epoch. Controls are stacked
identically and every clause is evaluated at them as well. That measures the
false-alarm rate of the whole test at \StkCtrlFap{} per cent, against the
\StkFapCeil{} per cent ceiling fixed in advance.

\emph{Registration is verified on real lines before it is used on anything
else.} The per-block transform collapses HD~285968's molecular line from a
\StkRegTopoChan-channel topocentric spread to \StkRegStellarChan{}
channels, an rms of \StkRegRmsChan{} channels $=\StkRegRmsKms$\,km\,s$^{-1}$
and a factor \StkRegFactor{} improvement, across \StkRegNEpoch{} epochs
spanning \StkRegSpanD\,d in \StkRegChanKHz\,kHz channels. It also places
$\beta$~Pictoris' CO(2--1) at \StkRegBpVsf\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in its
\StkRegBpChanA\,kHz windows against \StkBpCoTwoOff\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in its
\StkRegBpChanB\,kHz ones. The two agree to \StkRegCrossKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$
across a factor \StkRegBpChanRatio{} in channel width.

\emph{The gain is $\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}$ and not $\sqrt{N}$}, where
$N_{\rm eff}=\sigma_{\min}^{2}\sum_e\sigma_e^{-2}$ is the equivalent number
of equal-depth epochs. The median $N_{\rm eff}/N$ is \StkNeffFrac{} and the
tenth percentile \StkNeffFracPTen. Measured against $\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}$ the
realised improvement is \StkGainVsNeff, so the combination is ideal to about
one per cent. Against $\sqrt{N}$ it is \StkGainVsN, and the shortfall is
unequal epoch depth rather than registration error. An epoch count alone is
therefore misleading, and $N_{\rm eff}$ is quoted with it throughout:
\StkWorstStar{} stacks \StkWorstN{} epochs for a gain of only
\StkWorstGain, because its $N_{\rm eff}$ is \StkWorstNeff.
Carrier-equivalent, the median gain is \StkCarrierGainMed{} after the
measured sub-channel registration loss of $1-\StkRegLoss$. Every stacked
limit also carries a realisation term, because the noise scale is a median
absolute deviation over a median of \StkNChanMed{} common channels. That
scatter is $\pm$\StkMadZScatterPct{} per cent across the \StkNGroup{}
groups, and it is irreducible at this epoch count.

\emph{The deepest stacked limit is Proxima Centauri's.} Proxima holds
\StkProxN{} blocks over \StkProxHours\,h spanning \StkProxSpanYr\,yr, but
its $N_{\rm eff}$ is only \StkProxNeff, and it is the second number that
sets the realised gain of \StkProxGain$\times$. Carriers injected into every
epoch of that stack and recovered through the same path give a 90 per cent
recovery point at \StkCalMult$\times$ the stack's nominal threshold. The
limit is therefore
$\mathrm{EIRP}_{90}=\StkProxEirpCal$\,W for an emitter steady in the
stellar frame to \StkProxTolKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ across epochs spanning
\StkProxSpanYr\,yr, with no inter-epoch drift. That is about
\StkProxBelowMed{} times below this survey's per-system median, and as far as
we are aware it is the deepest millimetre technosignature limit measured on
any star through a recovery campaign. The calibration factor is a property of
the stacking chain rather than of this star: a second group returns
\StkCalMultTwo$\times$, and the factor is the ratio of the two thresholds,
times the channel response of a carrier at a random sub-channel position,
times the margin a ninety-per-cent point needs over a fifty-per-cent one.
Proxima's stack is built from coarse \ChanBModeMHz\,MHz windows, which is
why its registration tolerance is so wide: it is robust to planetary reflex
motion and dilute in bandwidth, the opposite trade from the fine-channel
stacks.

\emph{\StkNSurvive{} groups pass the full criterion and both are known lines.} They are
$\beta$~Pictoris' CO $J=1\!-\!0$ at \StkBpCoOneOff\,km\,s$^{-1}$ over
\StkBpCoOneN{} epochs ($Z=\StkBpCoOneZ$) and its $J=2\!-\!1$ at
\StkBpCoTwoOff\,km\,s$^{-1}$ ($Z=\StkBpCoTwoZ$). The two bands agree to
\StkBpCrossKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$, or \StkBpCrossChan{} of a CO(1--0) channel.
That is the astrophysical positive control passing in the stack. Attributing
every survivor against the survey's own $\pm\StkAttrKms$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ mask
leaves \StkNUnattributedComputed{} unattributed. The stack is also an
independent test of the threshold crossings of \S\ref{sec:etacrv}: the
\StkNPersistStar{} stars carrying them stack to
$Z=\StkPersistZLo$--\StkPersistZHi{} in \StkNPersistBelow{} cases,
\StkNPersistAboveCtrl{} exceed their own control ensembles and
\StkNPersistSurvive{} passes the criterion. The exception is
\StkPersistLineStar, which reaches $Z=\StkPersistLineZ$ on the same
frame-locked foreground line used above as the registration standard. It is
rejected because its own controls reach \StkPersistLineCtrl: the emission is
extended, which is what a foreground cloud does and a transmitter does not.

\emph{The restriction travels with every number above, and its width is set
by the channel.} The stack assumes an emitter whose velocity is steady in the
stellar frame, and a channel is finite. At CO(2--1) a \StkRefChanKHz\,kHz
channel is
\StkProxTolKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$, so a reflex motion of
$\pm\StkReflexAmpKms$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ --- a transmitter on a short-period
giant planet --- is $\StkReflexMHz$\,MHz, about \StkReflexChan{} channels.
Such an emitter de-registers itself completely between epochs. The same
applies to any intrinsic drift above roughly \StkDriftTolHzS\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$
over the median inter-epoch span of \StkSpanMedD\,d, or
\StkDriftTolTightHzS\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$ over the longest (\StkSpanMaxD\,d). Each stacked limit therefore constrains persistent emitters that are stable
in the stellar frame to within its own channel, and nothing else. The
single-epoch limits of \S\ref{sec:excludes} are the ones that apply
generally.
