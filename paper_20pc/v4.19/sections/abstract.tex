\begin{abstract}
Above 30\,GHz, narrowband searches of nearby stars have reached one spectral
line.
We uniformly search \NWinA{} fine-channel ALMA archive windows toward
\NSysClassA{} systems, spanning \CvUnionA\,GHz of union bandwidth, for an
unresolved carrier drifting linearly in the
stellar frame, plus \NWinB{} coarser ones without drift discrimination.
Crossings are tested against catalogued transitions at the star's velocity,
then in later blocks.
The median system recovers nine injections in ten at an estimated
$\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W, for a carrier narrower than the instrumental channel
drifting below \DriftCeilLo\,nHz, \AccelSheikhPct{} per cent of the
recommended ceiling. They carry no atmospheric decorrelation, so these limits
are optimistic by it.
No confirmed carrier is found. Of \EvNCrossRaw{} crossings, \MkAttr{} are
attributed and \MkUnattr{} are not, the \PrFlagXrossWord{} coarse-window ones
from one block failing a quality criterion. Attribution is not identification:
\LnAttrCoincWord{} rest on frequency coincidence alone.
None of the unattributed crossings returned to its predicted
cell, including \PrRankClause. That excludes a carrier whose stellar-frame frequency returns
to within $\pm\RqDvMed$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and radiates through both observations,
not a persistent transmitter of any kind. For \RcNPowerLoTrig{} the repeats
are too shallow for that test at the trigger amplitude.
The remainder is consistent with chance at the control positions, and does not
exclude a smaller added population. A calibration residual could mimic a
carrier in \CxNAdmit{} windows and is measured to act in none.
Line emission, not interference, explains the strongest quality-passing
crossings. What limits the search is inherited coverage, not depth:
\ZcNoRepSys{} systems have no confirmation epoch.
\end{abstract}
