\begin{abstract}
We have searched \NWindows{} archival ALMA spectral windows spanning
\UnionLo--\UnionHi\,GHz (Bands~3--8) toward \NStars{} stars in
\NSystems{} systems within 40\,pc for unresolved spectral carriers,
comparing each stellar position with \NCtrl{} control positions in an
annulus centred on the star. \textbf{No credible technosignature is
found.} The sample is every star that usable public archival
observations cover, so it is ALMA-archive-complete within 40\,pc rather
than interest-selected, but it is not a volume-complete stellar sample:
it holds ${\sim}1$ per cent of the stars in that volume, over-representing
F and G types ($\times3.7$, $\times3.1$) and under-representing M dwarfs
($\times0.6$). Everything above ${\sim}115$\,GHz is essentially
unsearched elsewhere, and these are the first millimetre limits for
Barnard's Star and Wolf~359. Four windows raise a statistical screening
flag rather than a candidate detection: two toward $\beta$~Pic and one
toward HD~48370 are consistent with known circumstellar or foreground
CO, and one, toward CP$-$72~2713, is unclassified. The screening test resolves no
probability below $1/\RankFloor$, far above the survey-wide Bonferroni
scale $\BonferroniThresh$, so no flag is by itself significant. A
second-stage local null resolves past that floor: for CP$-$72~2713 it
gives $p=\LNpCP$, confirmed at $p=\LNpCPgev$ by an independent
extreme-value fit to the measured controls, about \LNCPRatio{} times
above the Bonferroni scale. Trigger thresholds, the powers at which a signal
first exceeds the screening test, span $\EirpMinA$--$\EirpMaxA$\,W EIRP
at \ChanLoA--\ChanHiA\,kHz native resolution over the \NWinA{}
drift-resolved Class~A windows, and $\EirpMinB$--$\EirpMaxB$\,W at
\ChanLoB--\ChanHiB\,MHz over the \NWinB{} unresolved-spectral-excess
Class~B windows; the frequency-matched benchmark is a 12\,m aperture
radiating 1\,MW at 230\,GHz, EIRP ${\approx}8\times10^{14}$\,W.
\NCoarse{} of the \NWindows{} windows (\PctCoarse{} per cent) carry no
drift discrimination. Two limits qualify every threshold quoted here:
ALMA's default Hanning response leaves \HanWorst--\HanBest{} of a
sub-channel carrier's power in the peak channel, so the thresholds are
optimistic by $\times\HanFacBest$ to $\times\HanFacWorst$; and
completeness for the drift-resolved class is measured on a single
configuration and transferred by assumption, with unbounded transfer
error. The detection statistic and the line mask were defined using
these data, so the rank probabilities and false-alarm figures are
descriptive of this dataset rather than an independent validation of the
pipeline's false-positive rate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A technological civilisation, signalling deliberately or going about
its affairs, may leave a detectable imprint on the electromagnetic
spectrum: a \emph{technosignature} \citep{Sheikh2020,SETIreview2025}.
Six decades of radio searches cover many thousands of stars yet, as
\citet{Wright2018} quantified, sample only \HaystackFrac{} of the
multi-dimensional haystack of transmitter frequency, bandwidth, power
and duty cycle. Three gaps persist: the millimetre and submillimetre
regime above ${\sim}50$\,GHz is essentially unsearched; most surveys
target interest-selected classes of star, not the local stellar
population; and few reprocess \emph{existing} interferometric archives.

The symmetric detection statistic and the
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ molecular-line mask were both defined,
and the statistic once revised, using these data (\S\ref{sec:aumic}).
The rank probabilities and false-alarm figures below are therefore
descriptive of this dataset, not an independent validation of the
pipeline's false-positive rate; this is restated where it bears on a
number.

This paper addresses all three, asking how strongly existing ALMA
observations constrain unresolved artificial spectral emission from
nearby stellar systems. What follows characterises the stars ALMA
has observed, not the 40\,pc population: the searched subsample
over-represents F and G stars and under-represents M dwarfs (\S\ref{sec:sample}), and
every limit below inherits that bias. The sample is ALMA-archive-complete within 40\,pc: every star in that
volume with public ALMA archival coverage, whatever its spectral type,
disc-hosting status or known planets. We mine it, correcting for
proper motion and Doppler drift, for unresolved spectral carriers
(channel-confined excess at native ALMA resolution) and anomalous
millimetre continuum. Throughout,
``narrowband'' describes the assumed intrinsically narrow transmitter
and never the resolving power of the data (\S\ref{sec:method}).
The processing sets the search class, measured in \S\ref{sec:dwell}: a
continuous carrier is the best case, short-duty fast-drifting emitters
the worst. Coarse-channel windows retain sensitivity to
persistent unresolved excess power only (\S\ref{sec:method}).
Three contributions carry the paper: systematic exploitation of
archival ALMA stellar fields, \NStarBands{} target/band datasets mined
from public data with no new observing time; spatial-control null
calibration for interferometric SETI, the same statistic evaluated at
the star and at \NCtrl{} control positions so the false-alarm rate is
measured, not assumed; and a quantitative accounting of the
frequency, EIRP and drift domain actually searched, which turns a
non-detection into a statement with edges. Of the ancillary analyses
(closure-phase vetting, a continuum anomaly screen, a chirp and
periodicity re-analysis, a cross-target frequency-occupancy check),
only the continuum screen enters a disposition; the rest are capability
demonstrations (\S\ref{sec:ancillary}). One worked case runs through
the paper: a crossing towards $\beta$~Pictoris, the largest
star-versus-control contrast here, attributed to the disc's known
circumstellar CO across two transitions and three epochs
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). Per-target results are in
Appendix~\ref{app:table}; dispositions, false-alarm accounting and
injection completeness consolidate in Tables~\ref{tab:searchspace}
and~\ref{tab:flagged} with Appendices~\ref{app:falsealarm}
and~\ref{app:inject}.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Six decades of searching, from \citet{CocconiMorrison1959} and
\citet{Drake1960} through the programmes reviewed by
\citet{Tarter2001} and Project Phoenix \citep{Backus2002}, divide into
wide-field commensal and targeted surveys
\citep{Zhang2020,Czech2021,Morrison2023,FAST3IATLAS2025,LOFARdualsite2023,SRT2025,Tremblay2024},
with machine-learning interference rejection now central
\citep{Ma2023,Choza2024,GLOBULAR2025,AnomalyParkesGBT2025,BRaTs2026}, a
capability our classical threshold search lacks. Essentially all of it
lies below ${\sim}10$\,GHz. Above it the mm/submm regime has been
searched once, also with ALMA \citep{Mason2024}, despite physical
reasons to go higher: interstellar scattering and dispersion fall
steeply with frequency, a given aperture delivers a tighter beam per
watt of EIRP, and directional communication, radar and beamed power
transmission are already engineered coherent mm-wave emission, so
plausibility needs no assumption of Arecibo-scale transmitters at
hundreds of gigahertz. The countervailing costs,
atmospheric transmission, receiver noise, photons per watt per hertz and
pointing and phase stability, price the search, not forbid it.

\citet{Mason2024} searched archival Band~3 windows toward 28 ``stellar
bycatch'' stars, observed for other science and usable for free,
placing EIRP$_{\rm min} > 7\times10^{17}$\,W for the nearest and
identifying the challenges of high-frequency work: drift rates scale
with frequency, smearing erodes sensitivity without a drift search, and
the molecular line forest confuses. Our pilot
\citep[][citation provisional]{White2026} extended that methodology to
TRAPPIST-1 across Bands 3, 6 and 7 (EIRP$_{\rm
min}\sim5$--$10\times10^{13}$\,W, no detection) and built the ranked
100-star list this survey grew from. Its pipeline is that design applied
to one target. Nothing in this paper depends on that pilot: every claim attributed to
it below is restated from the products released with this work. New here
are the archive-complete sample selection (\S\ref{sec:sample}), the
\NCtrl-position control annulus and its local false-alarm estimation,
closure-phase vetting, the spectral-type-normalised continuum screen,
the chirp and periodicity extension and the cross-target
frequency-occupancy check. Each is new, to our knowledge, \emph{in an
ALMA archival technosignature survey}; we claim no wider priority over
their centimetre-wave counterparts.

A non-detection must become a quantitative exclusion statement:
\citet{Wright2018} formalised the ``Cosmic Haystack'' search volume,
\citet{Margot2023} its conversion into an upper bound on the
transmitter-hosting fraction, and \citet{EarthDetectingEarth2025} asked
where Earth's technosignatures would be detectable. That bound is valid
only if the sample represents the underlying population, which
interest-selected samples do not. Most surveys select by proximity, spectral type, planets or Earth
Transit Zone membership; the ALMA-specific form, disc selection, skews
the sample young and dynamically active. We instead take every star
within 40\,pc in the usable field of at least one public ALMA
observation (\S\ref{sec:sample}): archive-complete for the observed subset of the
local population in a fixed volume, not for that population itself. This removes technosignature-specific but not
archival astrophysical selection; the \NCensus{} sample entries are
${\sim}1$ per cent of the catalogued population within 40\,pc (the
\NStars{} actually searched here are ${\sim}0.5$ per cent), and the
residual coverage selection is characterised as a completeness function
(\S\ref{sec:sample}, Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}).

\section{Sample selection}
\label{sec:sample}

The sample is ALMA-archive-complete within 40\,pc, which is not a
volume-complete stellar sample: it is every star within 40\,pc of the
Sun (Gaia~DR3 parallaxes, distances direct inversions $d = 1/\pi$; every
entry $\pi \ge 25$\,mas with $\sigma_\pi/\pi \le 0.8$ per cent) whose
proper-motion-corrected position at the observation epoch falls in the
field of view of at least one public ALMA execution block, not
necessarily at the pointing centre: mosaics and wide pointings
serendipitously cover more than one catalogued star. Disc, planet and
spectral type are descriptive metadata here, not selection criteria.
Throughout, a \emph{star} is one catalogue entry and a \emph{system}
the set of entries bound to it; a \emph{target/band} dataset is one
star in one ALMA band; an \emph{execution block} (EB) one archival
observation; and a \emph{window}
is one spectral window of one execution block. This gives \NCensus{}
unique target stars, processed nearest-first. Because trigger thresholds
scale with $d^{2}$ at fixed noise, that order is sensitivity-ranked, not
an unbiased draw, and the prevalence statements of
\S\ref{sec:discussion} are conditional on it.

Composition follows from the archival selection, tabulated against the
reference census in Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}. It rests on
\texttt{teff\_gspphot} as a spectral-class proxy, available for 52 of
\NStars{} sample stars and 49 per cent of the census and missing
preferentially for the coolest objects, so the M fraction is a lower
limit and the earlier-type ratios upper limits: of the searched stars
carrying a Gaia temperature, 41 per cent are M~dwarfs against 69 per
cent of the reference census, while F and G stars are over-represented
(Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}). The archive prefers earlier types and nearer
systems, so the non-detection bears on the ALMA-observed, F/G-enriched
mix of Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}, which under-represents the M dwarfs
dominating the local volume: a mismatch of astrobiological priority, not
of statistical validity. Eighteen of the \NStars{} processed stars are confirmed exoplanet hosts
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}); the debris-disc hosts ($\beta$~Pic, AU~Mic, $\epsilon$~Eri, $\tau$~Cet)
contribute dusty continua, kept from masquerading as anomalies by the
continuum lane's spectral-type-normalised screen
(\S\ref{sec:method}). Window counts are also concentrated:
$\beta$~Pic (\ConcBpicWin{} windows), AU~Mic (\ConcAumicWin{}) and
TRAPPIST-1 (\ConcTrapWin{}) alone carry \ConcThreeWin{} of the
\NWindows{} searched windows (\ConcThreePct{} per cent) from
\ConcThreeSys{} systems, so any window-weighted statement leans on
a few heavily observed young, active or disc-hosting systems.

The \NCensus{} stars are complete with respect to, and conditional only
on, the 40-parsec reference catalogue intersected with the
public-archive snapshot of 2026 September~8. Against that Gaia~DR3
solar-neighbourhood catalogue (17\,566 stars within 40\,pc) they are
${\sim}1$ per cent. The two parallax cuts are not interchangeable: the
sample requires $\sigma_\varpi/\varpi\le0.8$ per cent, the reference
census $\le$10 per cent (Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}), so the ratio
compares two differently selected populations; the census is also
incomplete at both ends, and the fraction says only that coverage is
small. Table~\ref{tab:selfunc} leaves the selection function
inspectable; age and disc-hosting flags are not uniformly available and
are documented qualitatively.

Stellar counts throughout are catalogue-entry counts. The \NStars{}
searched entries comprise \NSystems{} independent systems, differing by
seven designation-linked component pairs (SIMBAD designations;
per-entry rows, distances and unresolved-pair notes in the
machine-readable catalogue). The \NStarBands{} star/band datasets,
\NWindows{} windows and \NEB{} execution blocks therefore derive from
\NSystems{} systems, the paired rows being bookkeeping, not
independent trials.

``Usable public ALMA coverage'' is a five-criterion decision tree,
frozen absent an erratum: (i) at least one public
execution block (EB) whose QA2-calibrated visibilities cover the star's
epoch-propagated position in the primary beam, with no minimum
integration, sensitivity, antenna count, mosaic-tile count or array
configuration beyond QA2; (ii) that position within one primary-beam FWHM of the field centre
(largest accepted offset ${\sim}0.7$ FWHMs, Sirius~B, retained with
bounded 3.6--16 per cent primary-beam corrections whose residual
systematic a holography-beam recomputation would settle,
\S\ref{sec:frames}); (iii) each spectral window admitted
individually, on QA2 vetting alone; (iv) no
programme-category restriction, science, calibration and observatory
projects entering on identical terms; (v) a fixed archive snapshot
date (2026 September~8). No star
or window was excluded by an unlisted criterion, none for short
integration (the shortest retained are 1.0 and 1.5\,min).

ALMA project metadata quantify pointed versus serendipitous coverage:
almost every spectral-window-covered star is the pointed science target
in at least one execution block, the exceptions a few blocks on another
programme's field or calibrator-only, leaving Wolf~219 the sole serendipitous star.
An archive-complete sample's residual selection function is the union
of ALMA target lists, differing from an interest-selected sample in
degree, but the degree is \emph{quantified}: a bycatch fraction of
1 star in 44, 1 EB in 57.

\section{Data and methodology}
\label{sec:method}

The design is one shared archive-ingestion stage feeding two
independent search lanes over the 40\,pc archive-complete sample;
Table~\ref{tab:algorithm} gives the data path of the primary lane in
order. For each target we mine, within a bounded budget, all calibrated
(or locally recalibrated) archival execution blocks
covering the star's Gaia-propagated position, and search their
visibilities in (i) the \emph{primary} lane, a Doppler-drift-corrected
spectral-carrier search following \citet{Mason2024} and
\citet{Enriquez2017}, converting each non-detection into an equivalent
isotropic radiated power limit
$\mathrm{EIRP_{5\sigma}} = 4\pi d^{2} S_{\rm min}\Delta\nu$, with
$S_{\rm min}$ five times the searched window's per-channel rms,
primary-beam corrected, $\Delta\nu$ its native channel width and $d$ the
target distance (worked numerically in
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). The quoted thresholds are nominal.
Their systematic budget is the ALMA absolute flux scale (5--10 per cent,
band- and epoch-dependent), the primary-beam model at the stellar
offset, the instrumental spectral response (ALMA's default online
Hanning smoothing leaves \HanBest{} of a channel-centred sub-channel
carrier's power in the peak channel and \HanWorst{} for one on a channel
boundary, so every threshold quoted here is optimistic by
$\times\HanFacBest$ to $\times\HanFacWorst$, median $\times\HanFacMed$;
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}) and visibility calibration. Each
multiplies $S_{\rm min}$ and hence EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ directly; \emph{Gaia}
distance errors are negligible here. The same
visibilities feed (ii) an \emph{ancillary} line-excluded continuum lane
(Appendix~\ref{app:continuum}). ``5$\sigma$'' throughout is the local
instrumental \emph{trigger} threshold of Table~\ref{tab:nomenclature},
estimated empirically (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). In the majority of windows an intrinsically
Hz-wide carrier is unresolved within a 15.625\,MHz channel, and the
data could not demonstrate that a one-channel excess is intrinsically
narrow. The search is reported as two formal survey
classes, distinguished by the dimensionless drift parameter
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\rm drift} \;=\; |\dot\nu_{\max}|\,T\,/\,\Delta\nu_{\rm ch},
\label{eq:etadrift}
\end{equation}
the channel-width fraction the \emph{maximum} searched drift
$|\dot\nu_{\max}|$ traverses over an integration $T$ at native channel
width $\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$. A window is \emph{drift-resolving} when
$\eta_{\rm drift}\gtrsim1$, only then carrying a carrier across enough
channels for its motion to be measured, not assumed. The drift
ceiling inside $\eta_{\rm drift}$ is a search-grid boundary, the widest
trial drift the stack evaluates, not a physical prior on transmitter
drift rates: carriers drifting faster are missed, and the resulting
selection function is explicit in \S\ref{sec:detectable} (Table~\ref{tab:searchspace},
Fig.~\ref{fig:accel}).

\emph{Class~A} is the drift-resolved spectral-carrier search: the
\NWinA{} windows of fine channelisation, native widths
\ChanAMinKHz--\ChanAMaxKHz\,kHz (median \ChanAMedKHz\,kHz;
\ChanAModePct{} per cent at the \ChanAModeKHz-kHz mode), spanning
$\eta_{\rm drift}=\EtaAmin$--$\EtaAmax$ (median \EtaAmed). It is the
only class with measured end-to-end recovery of a drifting carrier.
\emph{Class~B} is the unresolved spectral-excess search: the \NWinB{}
coarse windows, \PctCoarse{} per cent of the sample, native widths
\ChanBMinMHz--\ChanBMaxMHz\,MHz (\ChanBModeN{} windows at the
\ChanBModeMHz-MHz mode), spanning
$\eta_{\rm drift}=\EtaBmin$--$\EtaBmax$ (median \EtaBmed). It is
sensitive to persistent near-stationary excess power, carries no drift
discrimination, and is never described as a narrowband search anywhere
in this paper.

$\eta_{\rm drift}$ is the physical criterion and the classes are
labelled by the instrument. Channelisation is bimodal, with no window
between \ChanAMaxKHz\,kHz and \ChanBMinMHz\,MHz, an empty gap of factor
\ChanGapFac, so any boundary inside the gap assigns identical membership
and figures label the classes by name. The two criteria do not coincide
exactly, and the overlap is quantified:
\EtaBgeOneNow{} long-integration Class~B windows reach
$\eta_{\rm drift}\geq1$, and only marginally (maximum \EtaBgeOneMax),
where a one-to-two channel traverse supports no discriminative drift fit
after baseline removal; \EtaAltOneNow{} short Class~A windows fall below
unity and are conservatively kept in Class~A. Each window's
$\eta_{\rm drift}$, $\eta_{\rm smear}$, trial drift count and maximum
searched acceleration $a_{\max}=\AmaxMin$--$\AmaxMax$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ are
columns of the released catalogue, so any reader may reclassify on the
physical criterion alone. Results are given per class first and combined
only where statistically appropriate (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}).

Both lanes exclude catalogued molecular transitions at tolerances suited
to their statistics. The continuum lane masks the common species at
native resolution before integrating; the carrier lane applies a
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame velocity exclusion mask
(\S\ref{sec:linecost}) to any threshold crossing. The mask is
\emph{excluded search space}: the unmasked survey covers
\UnionGHz\,GHz of unique sky frequency, the effective default quoted
throughout is the ${\approx}\EffBandGHz$\,GHz surviving it, and no
constraint is placed on carriers inside the excluded velocity regions.
Masking reduces the frequency space a candidate can occupy, not the
per-channel noise, so no threshold moves. All flux densities and EIRP
thresholds are total intensity (Stokes~$I$), formed from the two
parallel-hand cross-correlations the archive delivers; no cross-hand
($V$/$Q$/$U$) extraction or circularity test was performed. The
construction is polarisation-blind in amplitude, $I$ being the summed
parallel-hand power, so at fixed total transmitter power a strongly
polarised artificial signal responds like an unpolarised one to within
cross-polarisation leakage. That holds for the estimator, not the data:
polarisation-dependent flagging in the archival QA2 calibration could
have reduced sensitivity for some datasets, and the retained products do not record per-correlation
flagging (the extraction sums over the correlation axis with QA2
weights), so we cannot state what fraction of the processed datasets
retained both correlation hands unflagged through QA2. A
dataset that lost one hand would be $\sqrt{2}$ less sensitive than its
quoted threshold implies, and nothing here would catch it. Full Stokes
analysis would add discrimination against weakly polarised backgrounds,
not depth.

\noindent\textbf{How to read every limit in this paper.}
\emph{Every EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ value here is a nominal, local
instrumental detection threshold, the \emph{trigger power}
$P_{\rm trig}$: five times the per-channel rms of
the searched window, at that window's native channel width, for a transmitter at the star's position matching the searched
spectral morphology, with a measured recovery rising with the fraction
of the track the carrier occupies its channel, highest for a continuous
one (\S\ref{sec:ancillary},
Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). It is not a calibrated completeness
limit: completeness is the power $P_x$ at which recovery reaches $x$
per cent. $P_{50}=1.10\,P_{\rm trig}$ and $P_{90}>2\,P_{\rm trig}$ for the
calibration configuration's drifting class, both carrying an
\emph{unbounded} transfer systematic to any other configuration
(bracketed as $0.5$--$2\times$, Appendix~\ref{app:population}), where
they are \emph{not determined}; $P_{\rm trig}$ is quoted throughout. It
is a total-power threshold set by ALMA's native channelisation, not a
Hz-resolution sensitivity, optimistic by $\times\HanFacBest$ to $\times\HanFacWorst$ under the
instrument's Hanning response (Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). Every
headline EIRP is quoted beside its native channel width, and the
machine-readable catalogue carries the nominal threshold and both
Hanning-corrected values per window. The same reading applies to
every subsequent ``5$\sigma$''.}

\noindent The three detectable morphologies, quantified with real

\subsection{The search statistic, and the words used for its output}
\label{sec:statistic}

The primary statistic is formed on extracted spectra, not images or
visibilities. The extraction operates on the QA2-calibrated
measurement set as delivered: per integration and per native channel,
with the archive's QA2 flagging carried through untouched and no
spectral regridding or interpolation (Table~\ref{tab:algorithm},
step~3); it returns the Stokes~$I$
intensity at the propagated sky position $\mathbf{x}$
(\S\ref{sec:method}). Baseline
removal is applied \emph{after} extraction (step~4), and the
primary-beam correction enters the quoted thresholds and fluxes
(\S\ref{sec:frames}), not the extracted amplitudes the
statistic ranks. The estimator's visibility-domain weighting is
fixed in the released code (Data Availability), which itemises the
quantities consumed and emitted per window and is the reproducibility
reference for steps 1--3. For a sky position $\mathbf{x}$, each per-integration
spectrum is baseline-subtracted by a running median along the
\emph{frequency} axis. No temporal median is applied at any stage
(\S\ref{sec:ancillary}). The residual dynamic
spectrum is stacked along each trial linear drift rate $\dot\nu$, and
the statistic is the largest single-channel amplitude in units of the
window's noise,
%
\begin{equation}
T(\mathbf{x})=\max_{\dot\nu,\;\nu}\;
\frac{S(\mathbf{x},\nu,\dot\nu)}{\sigma(\mathbf{x})},
\label{eq:tstar}
\end{equation}
%
where $S$ is the drift-stacked flux density in one native channel and
$\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ a single robust scale per position and window,
estimated as $1.4826\times$ the median absolute deviation of that
position's baseline-subtracted spectrum over all channels: local in
position, global in frequency. The baseline median's width $W$ is
likewise a single fixed channel count for all \NWindows{} windows,
independent of native channel width, so the linewidth ceiling it
imposes scales in frequency with each window's channelisation
(Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). The crossing channel is \emph{not}
excluded from the scale, standard for a median-based estimator, whose
50-per-cent breakdown point makes a single-channel feature immaterial
over a window's hundreds of channels; the per-window local
recomputation below excludes it explicitly.
$T_\star \equiv T(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ at the proper-motion-propagated
stellar position, and the same operation, drift grid, channelisation and
noise estimator give $\{T_i\}$ at all \NCtrl{} control positions.

\emph{The control geometry, in full.} The controls are placed by
\texttt{probe\_positions()} in the released extraction code, which adds
its offsets to the star's own direction cosines, so the construction is
concentric with the \emph{star}, not the field phase centre. It is an annulus rather than a ring: \RingNProbe{}
positions drawn uniform in area, $r=\sqrt{U(r_{\rm in}^{2},r_{\rm
out}^{2})}$, and uniform in azimuth, from the fixed seed \RingSeed, so
the layout is reproducible. Radii run
$\RingInFrac$--$\RingOutFrac\,\theta_{\rm PB}$ with $\theta_{\rm
PB}=\RingPbCoefCode\,\lambda/D$ evaluated at each window's own median
frequency, scaling the annulus window by window: across the survey
$\theta_{\rm PB}$ spans \RingThetaMin--\RingThetaMax\,arcsec, $r_{\rm
in}$ \RingRinMin--\RingRinMax\,arcsec and $r_{\rm out}$
\RingRoutMin--\RingRoutMax\,arcsec (Table~\ref{tab:ring}). A separate
inner check-ring of \RingNSrc{} positions, reaching
$\RingCheckFrac\,\theta_{\rm PB}$, serves the point-source test. Ring
centre, $\theta_{\rm PB}$, $r_{\rm in}$, $r_{\rm out}$, the control
count and the seed are columns of the released per-window catalogue.

The geometry is reconstructed from the code, not the frozen products,
so two checks establish that it is the layout that ran. The
inner check-ring it generates has exactly the \RingNSrc{} positions the
retired region-max statistic maximised over (\S\ref{sec:aumic}). And
where extended CO emission fills the beam, the released control
statistics fall with the reconstructed radius: Spearman
$\rho=\RingRhoCO$ at $p\sim10^{\RingRhoCOexp}$ in the HD~48370 Band~6
window, against a median $\rho=\RingRhoNull$ over the \RingRhoNullN{}
windows containing no crossing at all. A wrong position-to-index mapping
could not produce that.

\emph{What the geometry costs.} A star
near the pointing centre sits at primary-beam response ${\approx}1$,
while the annulus samples \RingGainIn{} at $\RingInFrac\,\theta_{\rm
PB}$ falling to \RingGainOut{} at $\RingOutFrac\,\theta_{\rm PB}$,
area-weighted mean \RingGainMean{} (on ALMA's actual
$\RingPbCoef\,\lambda/D$ beam the same radii give \RingGainInTrue{} to
\RingGainOutTrue). Star and controls are therefore not exchangeable in
flux density. They are exchangeable in signal-to-noise, what the
statistic ranks: primary-beam correction divides an extracted
amplitude and its noise by the same $G(r)$, so $T(\mathbf{x})$ in
Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} is invariant under it. What does not cancel is sky brightness, which is why a beam-filling line
raises the ring faster than the star (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). The
residual asymmetry is one-sided: a suppressed control can only make a
star-exceeds-ring outcome more likely under the null.

Neighbouring controls are correlated below the synthesised-beam scale,
so a window's annulus carries $N_{\rm eff}\simeq30$--$43$ independent
spatial trials, as the symmetric reprocessing runs measure
(Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}). The synthesised beam is not
carried by the frozen products, so radii are quoted in arcsec and in
$\theta_{\rm PB}$, and $N_{\rm eff}$ is the operative trial count.

Being global in frequency, that scale can misstate the noise
\emph{local} to a crossing wherever a window carries bandpass-edge
structure, atmospheric features or a line forest. The retained products
keep the per-window scale but not the per-channel spectra, so no local
estimate can be recomputed survey-wide; for the four flags we have
measured it. Re-extracting their per-integration spectra and recomputing the scale
locally, within a band around the feature and
excluding the feature itself, gives local-to-global ratios of 0.97
(CP$-$72~2713), 1.29 (HD~48370), 0.93 ($\beta$~Pic~B3) and 1.02
($\beta$~Pic~B6). The global scale is accurate to a few per cent except
towards HD~48370, where the local value sits 29 per cent higher because
that field is filled with CO; rescaling both the statistic and its
ensemble by the local value leaves it flagged ($T_\star=\HdCorrectedT$
against a corrected ring maximum of \HdCorrectedRing), the disposition
already assigned to it. For CP$-$72~2713 the local scale is slightly
\emph{lower}, so its excess is marginally understated, not
inflated, and no disposition moves. The retained products keep no
drift-stacked cube, so this tests the noise scale and not $T_\star$
itself, and a per-crossing local scale inside the search is an open item
(\S\ref{sec:futurework}). The same estimator runs at the star and at all
\NCtrl{} control positions, so a badly misstated $\sigma$ would displace
the control distribution, and the pseudo-star ranks are uniform in every
band and at both channelisations (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). The one
departing stratum is the fine-channel one, and that departure is the
four flagged windows themselves.

\emph{Why the statistic is symmetric.} The exchangeability that gives a
rank its meaning requires the identical operation at the star and at
every control; maximising over an $n_{\rm src}$-position region at the
star while comparing against single-position controls breaks that.
Under a pure-noise null the region maximum exceeds all $n_{\rm ctrl}$
single controls with probability $n_{\rm src}/(n_{\rm src}+n_{\rm
ctrl})$, which for the $n_{\rm src}=\RingNSrc$ check-ring and
$n_{\rm ctrl}=\NCtrl$ controls is \AsymExpectPct{} per cent, not
$1/\RankFloor$. The inflation is a property of the estimator, holding
for any window and any noise distribution, so the released statistic
evaluates one position at the star and one at each control, the form of
Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar}. An earlier version of this analysis
used the asymmetric region maximum, and \S\ref{sec:aumic} states the
chronology and audits every window the change affected.

The searched drift-rate range is the larger of (a) a generic
frequency-scaled default (12--13\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$), the
\citet{Mason2024} fiducial with a threefold safety margin, and
(b) for confirmed exoplanet hosts, the maximum line-of-sight orbital
acceleration of the innermost known planet, so a transmitter on a
short-period planet cannot drift out of the window. Drift rate maps
onto line-of-sight acceleration through the first-order Doppler
relation
%
\begin{equation}
a_{\rm los} \;=\; c\,\frac{\dot\nu}{\nu},
\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
%
so the ceiling is quoted in cross-band-comparable form
$\dot\nu/\nu$: 12--13\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$ is
$\dot\nu/\nu = (1.2$--$1.3)\times10^{-8}$\,s$^{-1}$, i.e.\
$|a_{\rm los}| = 3.6$--$3.9$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:drift}),
independent of observing
frequency. That is the acceleration at ${\sim}0.04$\,au around a
solar-type star. Earth-motion contributions
($\lesssim0.13$\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$) lie two orders of magnitude
below, subsumed by the grid. The grid itself is uniform in $\dot\nu$,
spanning the searched range in $n_{\rm drift}$ steps (2--1944 across
windows, recorded per window in the released file), so the
worst-case mismatch, a carrier midway between trial rates, leaves a
residual drift that traverses at most \DriftGridFineHalf{} of one
native channel over the longest fine-class track
(\DriftGridCoarseHalf{} over the coarse class), and the
stacked-amplitude loss is bounded by that sub-channel smearing alone,
an order below the recovery slope measured between $5\sigma$ and
$10\sigma$. The injection campaign injected on grid
points; continuous randomisation between them belongs with the
stratified campaign of \S\ref{sec:futurework}.
Very short-period systems are the ceiling's sacrificial edge. An
artificial emitter need not be tied to a known planet at all (a free-flying platform, rotating
structure or deliberately chirped carrier can accelerate as it
chooses), and such emitters are unconstrained, not excluded.
These are \emph{apparent} topocentric drift rates, including deliberate
transmitted chirp and acceleration-induced drift, which the survey does
not distinguish, so the constraints concern linear apparent drift within
the searched range only. For circular orbits the ceiling covers a
transmitter co-orbiting an Earth analogue by three orders of magnitude
and Proxima~b comfortably, while TRAPPIST-1\,d, c and b
($a_{\rm los}=\OrbAccTrapd$, \OrbAccTrapc{} and \OrbAccTrapb\,m\,s$^{-2}$)
sit at or beyond it. Eccentricity raises the periastron value by up to
$(1{+}e)/(1{-}e)^{2}$, $1.9\times$ at $e=0.2$, so borderline cases move
outside the searched domain at modest eccentricity
(Fig.~\ref{fig:accel}).

Across the \NWindows{} windows maximum searched drift rates span
1.1--5.9\,kHz\,s$^{-1}$, native channel widths 15.3\,kHz--31.25\,MHz
(median \ChanMedianMHz\,MHz), trial drift rates per window 2--1944
(median 4). The threefold margin matters: at $1\times$ the search
would have been blind to two of its threshold-crossing windows,
including the AU~Mic crossing; at $5\times$ no disposition changes,
transmitters drifting faster than
${\sim}13$\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$ remaining unconstrained.
Intra-integration smearing is negligible almost everywhere: at most
1.10 channels, median 0.001; only three windows have $\eta_{\rm
smear} < 0.99$ (Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}), the worst the $\beta$~Pic Band~6 fine
window, whose effective threshold is 1.74$\times$ nominal. Channel widths are
effective resolutions, verified against the measurement sets' spectral
window tables (Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}).

\subsection{Frequency and velocity reference frames}
\label{sec:frames}

Every frequency axis here, searched spectra, tabulated ranges and
crossing frequencies alike, is \emph{topocentric} as delivered by the
ALMA correlator, with no velocity-frame shift applied at any stage. Velocities enter only in the vetting stage's line
mask, whose conversion chain is in Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}. The
stacking is coherent in frequency and in the drift-rate trial but
preserves no electric-field phase coherence between integrations.

The native channel width is also the linewidth convention for every EIRP
threshold, the commonest confusion when technosignature limits are
compared across surveys. For a transmitter intrinsically narrower than
the channel ($\delta\nu_{\rm tx}\ll\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$),
channel dilution spreads its total line flux $F_{\rm line}$ across the
channel, $S_{\nu,\rm ch}=F_{\rm line}/\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$, and
triggering requires $S_{\nu,\rm ch}\geq S_{\min}$, so the minimum
detectable total power is $4\pi d^{2}S_{\nu,\rm ch}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$,
independent of $\delta\nu_{\rm tx}$ below the channel width.
EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ is thus the minimum \emph{total} power of a
channel-confined carrier and not a spectral power density; dividing by
$\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$ converts to the minimum detectable spectral power
for wider transmitters, so a $10^{15}$\,W threshold at 15.625-MHz
channelisation is $6.4\times10^{7}$\,W\,Hz$^{-1}$. The 1-Hz and 3-Hz
limits of Hz-resolution surveys are total powers in the same sense, each
the best case of its own channelisation, which makes the axes of
Fig.~\ref{fig:context} commensurable. Channelisation is the
dominant price the archive exacts, but we do not project an
Hz-resolution sensitivity: a radiometer-equation scaling
$P_{\min}\propto\sqrt{\Delta\nu}$ across six orders of magnitude in
spectral resolution is a thought experiment, not an achievable ALMA
sensitivity.

The spectral-carrier search covers every spectral window of an
observation, capped at 8 per target to bound compute cost. The cap
bound one target/band, 61~Vir Band~7 (four of eight windows searched),
whose frequency-space selection function is tabulated. Of the
\NStarBands{} star/band datasets, 53 have every configured window
searched; the remaining 3 ($\tau$~Cet, Kapteyn's Star, Van~Maanen's
Star) are represented by their deepest single window and are marked in
the released catalogue. Each searched window yields its own
EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ row, and Figure~\ref{fig:context} plots only the
deepest per star.

Velocity coincidence, not drift, is the operative discriminant when a
crossing is checked against the masked species: circumstellar emission
sits at rest offset by the gas's kinematics, whereas a technosignature
may appear at any frequency and drift rate. The searches as executed
used a narrower one-channel-width implementation, which agrees with the
velocity-space form on every disposition here (\S\ref{sec:linecost}).

Both carrier-lane thresholds and continuum fluxes are corrected for
ALMA's primary-beam response at the star's offset from the phase centre,
negligibly for most targets (median factor 1.0001, 90th percentile
1.012, none above 2 per cent). Two sit at large offset.
$\epsilon$\,Eri Band~6 lies at ${\sim}0.7$ primary-beam FWHM, where the
correction reaches $2.9$--$3.4\times$ and the Gaussian beam form fails;
rather than publish provisional values we \emph{withhold} those four
windows, which enter no number here but are released, flagged, in the
catalogue and return once a holography beam model is applied. Sirius~B
is retained and quoted as such, with corrections of 3.6--16 per cent
across bands, a bounded systematic, not an invalid model.
Excluding it too would change neither endpoint of the quoted EIRP range,
nor the median ($1.3\to1.4\times10^{15}$\,W), nor any conclusion.
\label{sec:benchmarks}

The EIRP thresholds are compared throughout to an Arecibo-like planetary
radar \citep[][EIRP $2\times10^{13}$\,W]{Drake1974,EarthDetectingEarth2025},
which is a technological \emph{power} scale and not a matched
transmitter model. It is an S-band number, and aperture gain scales as
$\nu^{2}$, so the same aperture and transmitter power at 230\,GHz would
radiate ${\sim}4\times10^{17}$\,W. The frequency-matched benchmark is a
12-m aperture radiating 1\,MW at 230\,GHz, EIRP
${\approx}8\times10^{14}$\,W, within a factor of two of the median
Class~A threshold $\EirpMedA$\,W. Neither is a model.

\subsection{Ancillary and validation analyses}
\label{sec:ancillary}

Four analyses beyond the spectral-carrier search are executed here,
each summarised only as far as the main text depends on it.

\emph{Injection-recovery validation} (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}).
Synthetic tones of known amplitude were injected into real visibilities
and recovered with the unmodified pipeline: \NTrials{} trials across six
window configurations, three bands and both resolution classes. The
pipeline's baseline step is a per-integration running median along
\emph{frequency}
(Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}, step~4), which by construction cannot remove
a feature confined to one or two channels, and its de-drift step is an
inverse-variance-weighted sum over integrations, which accumulates a
persistent carrier coherently. One validation-history item, told in
full in Appendix~\ref{app:inject}: the campaign's original report
of 0 of 500 coarse-window recoveries was an artefact of its recovery
\emph{criterion}, not of the search pipeline. The corrected
criterion has not been re-derived by an independently written
implementation, and that cross-check is an open item
(\S\ref{sec:futurework}). The independent evidence is the dwell campaign
of \S\ref{sec:dwell}, which measures persistent near-static carriers as
\emph{recovered} (Table~\ref{tab:dwell}), the reading the artefact had
reversed.

The primary statistic has no automated injection test on every pipeline
change; three quantified checks bound undetected pipeline faults
instead: the machinery-only lane (fidelity $-9$/$+14$ per cent, the
bound that caught this artefact), the AU~Mic re-analyses of
\S\ref{sec:aumic} under three treatments, whose recomputation agrees
with the released products, and the pseudo-star rank uniformity
(\NPseudo{} pooled ranks uniform, mean \PseudoMean{} against 0.5, KS
$p=\PseudoKSp$), which any defect of the statistic itself would
displace. For the drifting class on the fine window, recovery is 17,
\RecThresh, 58, 75 and \RecTwice{} per cent at $4$, $5$, $6$, $8$ and
$10\sigma$, measured on the single calibration configuration of one
star and one Band~6 window and transferred by assumption to all \NWinA{}
Class~A windows, an error the bracketing of
Appendix~\ref{app:population} treats as a 0.5--2$\times$ rescaling. That
gives EIRP$_{50}=1.10\,$EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ and, as a bound,
EIRP$_{90}>2.0\,$EIRP$_{5\sigma}$, each with an unbounded transfer
systematic bracketed as $0.5$--$2\times$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:completeness}, Table~\ref{tab:classcomp}). Tables here
stay in nominal EIRP$_{5\sigma}$.

\subsection{Dwell-fraction completeness}
\label{sec:dwell}

The searched class is defined by how long a carrier occupies one native
channel, so we measure recovery against that directly. A carrier was
injected into the retained per-integration spectra of \NDwellWin{} real
windows, six coarse and six fine, at amplitudes of 2--20 times the
per-integration noise and dwell fractions
$f_{\rm dwell}=0.10$--1.00, six realisations each: \NDwellTrials{}
trials, released with one row per injection (Table~\ref{tab:dwell}). Its
detection criterion, recovered when the logged per-integration S/N
reaches five, was independently re-derived from the released rows: over
\XcheckN{} trials and \XcheckWin{} windows the rule and the logged flag
agree \XcheckAgree{} times, with \XcheckMismatch{} mismatches, highest
unrecovered S/N \XcheckFalseMax{} and lowest recovered
S/N \XcheckTrueMin, a clean separating gap. Recovery \emph{rises}
monotonically with dwell. Pooling amplitudes at or above $4\sigma$,
coarse windows recover \DwellCoarseOne{} per cent of continuous
carriers, \DwellCoarseQ{} per cent at $f_{\rm dwell}=0.25$ and
\DwellCoarseT{} per cent at 0.10; fine windows give \DwellFineOne,
\DwellFineQ{} and \DwellFineT{} per cent.

An end-to-end check confirms this through the unmodified pipeline, not
a re-implementation. Injecting a zero-drift, always-on tone into the
calibrated visibilities of a coarse Band~7 window at 1, 3 and 10 times
the per-integration rms and running the released extraction and search
returns $T_\star = 20.5$, $65.3$ and $217.7$, each recovered within one channel of the
injection and flagged as a detection. For 639
integrations the ideal coherent gain is $\sqrt{639}=25$, so a carrier at
the per-integration noise level should reach ${\sim}25\sigma$: the
measured 20.5 is that, less weighting and flagging losses.

Three distinctions discipline how these numbers are read.
\emph{Injection point}: the dwell campaign injects into retained
per-integration spectra, after the baseline step of
Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}, while the end-to-end check injects into
visibilities, before it. \emph{Drift discrimination}: on Class~B windows
every in-grid drift is sub-channel over the track, so the search recovers
the carrier's amplitude but carries no information on its true drift
rate, and no drift-rate claim is made for the \NWinB{} of \NWindows{}
windows in that class. \emph{The one cell measured only in part}:
through-pipeline recovery of near-static carriers on Class~A windows,
never yet constrained by an end-to-end zero-drift injection
(Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). The earlier claim that the pipeline
suppresses persistent carriers is withdrawn.

\emph{The campaign measures completeness at the survey threshold, across
configurations.} The trigger acts on the stacked statistic and the
de-drift step is an inverse-variance-weighted sum over integrations, so
a carrier of per-integration amplitude $A$ present for a dwell fraction
$f_{\rm dwell}$ of a track of $N_{\rm int}$ integrations accumulates to
%
\begin{equation}
a \;=\; A\,f_{\rm dwell}\sqrt{N_{\rm int}}
\label{eq:stacked}
\end{equation}
%
in units of the per-integration noise. Recovery is a function of $a$, not
of $A$ and $f_{\rm dwell}$ separately, so binning the
released trials in $a$ unifies the two axes and places all \CompWin{}
injected windows on one axis. Those windows span
$N_{\rm int}=\CompNintLo$--\CompNintHi, a factor of \CompNintFactor,
over \CompTgt{} targets in Bands \CompBands{} and both resolution
classes, so configuration-independence is measurable here, not
assumed. In the \CompFiftyN{} windows whose amplitude grid straddles the
trigger, the 50 per cent recovery point lies at
$a=\CompFiftyLo$--\CompFiftyHi{} (median \CompFiftyMed), which is the
$5\sigma$ threshold itself, not a value set by channelisation,
integration count or band. In the other \CompSatN{} the weakest
injection already stacks to $a=\CompSatMin$ and at least \CompSatRec{}
per cent of every injection is recovered, consistent but uninformative
about the curve's position. The pooled curves
(Table~\ref{tab:compcurve}) agree between classes within the sampling
noise.

\emph{What this does and does not license.} The injected carriers do not
drift, so what is measured across \CompWin{} configurations is the
persistent and partial-dwell, non-drifting class. On Class~B windows
that is the searched class, which therefore carries a survey-threshold
completeness curve measured on six configurations where it had none. On
Class~A windows the searched class is the drifting
carrier, whose curve is still the single calibration configuration of
Appendix~\ref{app:inject}; the fine column of
Table~\ref{tab:compcurve} constrains the near-static cell at the search
stage only, and neither replaces the drifting-class curve nor bounds its
transfer error. The campaign injects after the baseline step, so it
measures the search stage, the visibility-domain injection above
confirming the stage before it on one coarse window. And $a$ is a
derived combination, justified by the form of the statistic and by the
collapse of \CompWin{} configurations onto one curve, not
measured independently at every $(A,f_{\rm dwell})$ pair.

\emph{Drift-ceiling caveat.} The fixed $12$\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$
searched ceiling, line-of-sight $|a| = 3.6$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, is uniform

\emph{Closure-phase vetting} (Appendix~\ref{app:closure}). A one-sided
point-source test, unavailable to single-dish and beamformed surveys
because it needs multiple independent baselines. It is implemented and
simulation-validated, and at this survey's flux densities it has
\emph{no discriminating power}: per-baseline S/N never exceeds 5.1 in
the positive-control fields, so no flagged window could have failed it
whatever its nature. It is a proof-of-principle diagnostic, not an
operative filter, and no window is described as having ``survived''
it.

\emph{Extended and cross-target screens}
(Appendices~\ref{app:extended} and \ref{app:freqoccupancy}). Satellite constellations grew across the 2013--2025 archival
baseline. No known ordinary downlink
allocation maps into the searched frequencies, but that excludes neither
harmonics, intermodulation products nor unconventional emitters, and no
exclusion claim rests on allocation alone. The primary defences are the
control ensemble and the time-domain tests. A satellite enters star and
control extractions alike within an integration, the channel-width
mismatch and the drift-stacked statistic dilute transient passes, and a
pass confined to one block and one target would evade the cross-target
occupancy check by construction. A near-field emitter need not share a
celestial source's far-field interferometric response, one reason a
dedicated per-integration RFI screen and visibility-domain checks
belong in any successor analysis. A chirped (quadratic-drift)
extension and a per-channel periodicity search ran on the 122 retained
spectra as a demonstration, not a survey-grade screen; null
behaviour is as expected (star-exceeds-ensemble rates 10.7 and 13.1 per
cent against an 11-per-cent chance rate), no instance is a candidate and
no limit derives from them. Separately, crossings towards different
targets were cross-matched for recurrence at the same sky frequency, the
archival substitute for the ON/OFF cadence a single-pointing survey
lacks. Of the 366 star pairs with overlapping searched ranges, none
falls within the matching kernel, subject to one bookkeeping caveat in
the appendix.

\subsection{Data-quality exclusions and open items}
\label{sec:exclusions}

\NStarBands{} target/bands are searched to completion and reported here.
Four classes of entry are absent from the released catalogue and

\subsubsection*{The coarse-noise defect, and the bound on its reach}

A reproducible defect produces implausibly small noise in a minority of
coarse-channel windows. The radiometer relation
$\sigma\simeq{\rm SEFD}/\sqrt{N_{\rm bl}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}t_{\rm on}}$
makes $q\equiv\sigma\sqrt{t_{\rm on}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}}\simeq{\rm
SEFD}/\sqrt{N_{\rm bl}}$ a prediction, not a convention, so a window far below the sample median is a bookkeeping defect, not a
fluctuation; we exclude such windows at $100\times$ below the median.
The failure mode itself is not identified. Six of \NWindows{} windows
fail, across five stars, and their nominal thresholds of
$2.0$--$7.8\times10^{12}$\,W would otherwise have set this paper's
headline depth; excluding them, the best is $1.6\times10^{13}$\,W.

The exclusion does not rest on the retained population being narrow,
which it is not: recomputed from the released products, $q$ runs from
$0.33\times$ to $96\times$ the sample median, a factor of ${\sim}290$,
with a secondary mode from the Band~8 windows (band median $56\times$)
and a factor-52 spread within Band~6 alone. The same relation predicts
that band dependence through SEFD, and
normalising $q$ per band would turn this screen from an outlier cut into
a physical comparison. The exclusion survives because the six defective
windows sit a factor $1.5\times10^{3}$ below the lowest retained one
across an empty gap of 3.2 decades (Fig.~\ref{fig:noiseqa}), and any
threshold between $1.5\times10^{1}$ and $2.2\times10^{4}$ selects the
same six. The load-bearing argument is one-directional: a window-level deflation
of $\sigma$ acts at stellar and control positions alike, so it can only
over-state sensitivity, never manufacture a star-exceeds-ring window,
and the same holds for a weaker recurrence hidden in the retained
population. What is not known, while the mechanism is unknown, is whether the defect
is window-level or position-level, and the check that would settle it,
whether the \NCtrl{} control $\sigma$ values in a defective window show
the stellar one's deflation, needs per-position noise records the frozen
products do not carry.

\NDup{} of the \NExtracted{} extracted rows are duplicate catalogue
entries for one physical window, the same (star, execution block,
frequency range, channel width) key appearing twice in the archive
query. All are repeated archive products or alternative calibrations of one
execution block, not independent observations, so one row per key is the
right statistical unit. The retention rule is deterministic and quantity-independent, the key's
first-listed row in the archive query's own identifier order, so it
consults no measured value and cannot prefer a crossing or a deeper
threshold. Two repeats are not
identical: two reductions of the same $\tau$~Ceti Band~6 window differ
by 44 per cent in threshold, and the two HR~1010 Band~6 rows straddle
the $5\sigma$ threshold at 5.10 and 4.95, so the crossing count is
rule-dependent at the level of one window. The four flagged windows are
not, no discarded row being both a crossing and star-exceeds-ring, and
every row ships with its status. The audit is otherwise clean apart from four windows of elevated system
temperature, and every sample identifier re-resolves in SIMBAD and
\emph{Gaia}. Item (iv) is a cautionary example for archival work: the
catalogue entry ``$\gamma$~Lupi'' resolved to the F4V debris-disc host
HD~139664, holder of the historical identifier ``*~g~Lup'', a name-lookup substitution caught by inverting the pipeline distance to
recover the \emph{Gaia}~DR3 parallax (Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}).

volume-ordered survey the deepest are the nearest systems'
($1.6\times10^{13}$\,W for the UV~Ceti pair at 2.7\,pc) and the

benchmarks of \S\ref{sec:benchmarks}, only the two resolved components

EIRP.

The statistics come strictly before the astrophysics, in three named
stages that do not promote one another. A \emph{crossing} is one
channel$\times$drift cell at the stellar position reaching $T\geq5$:
\NHits{} of the \NWindows{} windows contain one. A \emph{stage-1 statistical flag} is a window whose stellar statistic
also exceeds all \NCtrl{} controls: four do. That is a screening device
and nothing more, selecting a window for examination. The smallest
probability it can express is $1/\RankFloor$, two orders of magnitude
above the survey-wide Bonferroni scale $\BonferroniThresh$, so a stage-1
flag is not a statistically significant detection. Each of the four is
then carried past that floor by a second-stage local null ((vi) below),
which measures one thing: whether a feature exceeds the noise at its own
position. It is not a spatial discriminator, and in \LNNCtrlClear{} of
the \LNNWindows{} windows the control-ring maximum clears the local null
as well, so the first-stage star-versus-control comparison remains the
binding test for candidacy. A \emph{candidate} is a stage-1 flag
surviving every veto, and there are none. Three of the four flags have
independent CO evidence, matched transitions, velocities and epochs at
$\beta$~Pictoris and published foreground emission at HD~48370; the
fourth, towards CP$-$72~2713, is unclassified. The
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ mask is not the instrument of rejection

\subsubsection*{Survey-level false-alarm calibration}

Each window-level test compares the peak signal-to-noise ratio at the
star's position with the same statistic at \NCtrl{} controls. The drift grid and thousands of channels apply identically to both, so
the per-window trials factor is absorbed empirically. The statistic is
symmetric: the on-source quantity is one propagated stellar position,
each control one position. Every number here derives from that frozen statistic; the retired
asymmetric variant is development history
(Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}). The
false-alarm probability has five components, in the order the procedure
applies them.

\emph{(i) The empirical spatial rank probability.} The per-window
significance quantity is an empirical spatial rank probability under the
control-position null, not a Gaussian-tail $p$-value: under
exchangeability one designated position of \RankFloor{} ranks first with
probability exactly $1/\RankFloor$, independent of whether the noise is
Gaussian or heavy-tailed, so heavy tails cannot explain an excess. The
survey expectation is $\NWindows/\RankFloor=\ExpFlags$ windows, and
$P(\geq\SbrN\,|\,\ExpFlags)=\SbrPobs$ under an independent-Poisson
approximation. That excess over the noise null is real, and
astrophysical: in \SbrAstro{} of the \SbrN{} windows genuine celestial
line emission sits at the stellar position (Table~\ref{tab:flagged}),
making the star rank first by construction, so those \SbrAstro{} are not
draws from the noise null. Removing them leaves \SbrResid{} rank-first
windows against \ExpFlags{} expected,
$P(\geq\SbrResid\,|\,\ExpFlags)=\SbrResidP$: the CP$-$72~2713 window,
and one towards Barnard's Star whose stellar peak (4.40 against a ring
maximum of 4.38) does not reach the trigger and so is not a flagged
window. Three levels stay distinct throughout: an
instrument and noise null, calibrated by the control ensemble; an astrophysical-source null, addressed by line identification and
kinematics; and only then a technosignature reading.

\emph{(ii) The $\geq5\sigma$ amplitude requirement.} Only \NHits{} of
\NWindows{} windows contain any $\geq5\sigma$ crossing at the stellar
position, each contributing at most the exchangeable rate
$1/\RankFloor$. The rank, not the trial count, is the calibrated
quantity: a window's channel$\times$drift grid holds up to $1.1\times10^{5}$
effective trials (Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}), more than \NCtrl{}
controls can resolve, so the control maximum absorbs whatever effective
trial count the noise carries. Four flagged windows exceed the budget
($P(\geq4\,|\,\ExpFlags)=\PgeFourPct$ per cent) with no tension:
excluding the three astrophysical ones, it need explain one unattributed
crossing against \ExpNonBP{} expected across the \NNonBP{}
non-$\beta$~Pictoris windows.

\emph{(iii) The line mask.} The velocity-space mask
(Appendix~\ref{app:linemask}) removes the velocity space of known
molecular emission from every crossing before it can count as
unattributed. It is an analysis choice, not a measurement: it never
lowers the noise-null budget above, and no disposition rests on it
alone, the three CO attributions being kinematic
(Table~\ref{tab:flagged}).

\emph{(iv) The family-level pseudo-target Monte Carlo.} Each trial draws
one control per window uniformly from its annulus as the pseudo-star and
passes it through the operative gates, $T\geq5\sigma$ then the velocity
mask. The expected count of pseudo-target windows flagged by the full
procedure is \PsFlagMu{} (\PsFlagPzero{} per cent of trials flag none,
\PsFlagPgeOne{} per cent at least one). This is the prespecified family-level procedure, one expectation
carrying threshold, mask and ring geometry together on real data; the
Poisson expectation of (i) and the Bonferroni reference of (v) are
checks against it, not independent claims. Worked through: \ExpFlags{}
expected flags from the rank test alone, the amplitude gate and mask
removing about three-quarters of that budget, and the Monte Carlo
landing at \PsFlagMu.

\emph{(v) The control-ensemble floor.} The controls can show a crossing
unremarkable but cannot certify one as significant survey-wide: the
smallest expressible per-window probability is $1/\RankFloor$, two
orders above the Bonferroni reference $\BonferroniThresh$ over
\NWindows{} windows, itself conservative because windows share execution
blocks. Authentication therefore falls to localisation, recurrence and
astrophysical attribution, and we pre-commit that no crossing will be
advanced as a candidate on rank statistics alone. No flagged window is
promoted by any number in this account.

\emph{(vi) The second-stage local null.} That floor belongs to the first
stage, and the four flagged windows are carried past it. For each we take a retained control position, standardise its residual
by the pipeline's per-integration noise map, shift every integration
independently and at random in channel, and re-run the identical search: same noise map, weights, drift grid and
channel count. A rigid shift preserves each integration's spectral autocorrelation,
\LNLagOne{} at lag one, and the maximum runs over the same grid, so the trials factor is the
star's.
Independence across integrations leaves nothing coherent along a drift
track, so each realisation is signal-free by construction. Between $\LNDrawsMin$ and $\LNDrawsMax$ realisations per window resolve
probabilities well below $1/\RankFloor$. The statistic was reimplemented
from the pipeline source and reproduced the published $T_\star$ of all
four windows bit for bit before any null was drawn.

Each null has first to reproduce the distribution of the
\LNNFirstStage{} real control maxima, genuine draws of the same
statistic at exchangeable positions. The criteria were fixed before inspection: \LNValidLo{} to \LNValidHi{}
per cent of real controls above the null's 99th percentile, under 1 per
cent above its maximum. Three windows pass. HD~48370 Band~6 fails badly: \LNHDnAbove{} of its
\LNNFirstStage{} real controls, \LNHDPctAbove{} per cent, exceed the
maximum of $\LNDrawsMax$ null draws, and those controls sit at median
\LNHDRealMed{} against the null's \LNHDNullMed. The cause is physical,
the field carrying extended emission and its imaging residuals, coherent
across integrations, which the scramble destroys by design. A
scrambled-noise probability there would be manufactured significance, so
we quote none for that window.

The two $\beta$~Pictoris windows act as positive controls: no draw
reaches $T_\star$ in either, giving $p<\LNpBpicThree$ in Band~3 and
$p<\LNpBpicSix$ in Band~6. That is what a working statistic should
return where a genuine localised astrophysical signal is present, here
on independently known CO. For
CP$-$72~2713 the value is resolved rather than bounded:
\LNnExceedCP{} of $\LNDrawsMax$ draws equal or exceed $T_\star$, so
$p=\LNpCP$. An extreme-value fit to the \LNNFirstStage{} measured
control maxima gives $p=\LNpCPgev$ independently (95 per cent confidence
$\LNpCPgevLo$ to $\LNpCPgevHi$), agreeing to a factor \LNCPAgreeFac.
Both sit about \LNCPRatio{} times above the survey-wide Bonferroni scale
$\BonferroniThresh$, and a \LNNBootGEV-fold bootstrap puts the chance of
the true value falling below that scale at \LNCPBootPct{} per cent. The
same fit places HD~48370 at $p=\LNpHDgev$ ($\LNpHDgevLo$ to
$\LNpHDgevHi$), unremarkable and consistent with the CO attribution.

Three limits belong with these numbers. A small local-null probability means only that a feature exceeds the
noise at its own position, and in \LNNCtrlClear{} of the \LNNWindows{}
windows the control-ring maximum clears it too. The retained products
keep full dynamic spectra for \LNNRetained{} of the \LNNFirstStage{}
control positions, so position-to-position heterogeneity rests on
\LNNRetained{} draws; that is what breaks the HD~48370 null, a limit of the retained products,
not of the method, and removable by retaining more control spectra. Parametric extrapolation of a null tail
is not used for any number quoted here: for $\beta$~Pictoris Band~3 the
natural fits disagree by \LNTailDex{} orders of magnitude and one
implies a bounded tail and returns exactly zero.

\emph{Is the ensemble exchangeable with the star?} Exchangeability is the central assumption of the statistic: the
$1/\RankFloor$ expectation holds only if the star and its \NCtrl{}
controls are exchangeable under the null, which spatially varying noise,
sidelobes, correlated pixels or phase-centre-localised calibration
residuals could spoil. The primary-beam term is settled by construction
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}): the annulus is concentric with the star and the
correction divides signal and noise alike, leaving the ranked statistic
invariant. The rest we measure by stratum
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ctrldiag}). Median per-window control maxima are flat
across bands, 4.46, 4.59, 4.42, 4.52, 4.63 and 4.69 in Bands 3 to 8, and
a Kolmogorov--Smirnov test of the star's rank among its controls against
$U(0,1)$ is consistent in every band ($p=\KsBandLo{}$--$\KsBandHi$) and
in the coarse stratum ($p=\KsCoarse$). Fine-channel windows sit higher
(control maximum 5.76 against 4.42), as their far larger channel$\times$drift trial count predicts; the test
is conditional on the window, so that is no violation.

For the stronger question, exchangeability of the ensemble itself, we
ranked each of a window's \NCtrl{} controls against the other
\NCtrl-1 by the statistic and add-one rule used for the star, pooled over the
survey. The \NPseudo{} pseudo-star ranks are uniform to the
resolution the ensemble can express (mean \PseudoMean{} against 0.5,
$p=\PseudoKSp$) in every band and at both channelisations. Two caveats belong with it: the construction compares a control against
the other 511 plus add-one, a different geometry from star-against-ring,
so it cannot detect a centre-versus-annulus asymmetry; and the \NPseudo{} pooled ranks are not independent, 512 coming from each window's single
noise realisation with $N_{\rm eff}\simeq30$--$43$, so the nominal
pooled $p$-values are optimistic. The relevant check is the two-sample comparison of the \NWindows{} real
stellar ranks against the pooled pseudo-ranks, $p=\StarPseudoP$: the
stellar position behaves like one more position in its own annulus, and
the medians coincide (\SymMedStar{} against \SymMedCtrl, $D=\SymKSd$). In
\emph{independent} spatial trials the ensemble delivers only
${\sim}30$--43, so the achievable resolution on a genuinely independent
null is nearer a few$\times10^{-2}$ than $1/\RankFloor$.

One stratum departs, and is reported, not pooled away. The fine-channel
windows fail the same $U(0,1)$ test at $p=\KsFine$ ($n=\NFine$): four
contain an on-star peak above their own control maximum, against 0.22
expected, precisely the four flagged windows of Table~\ref{tab:flagged}.
That fits the astrophysical line features identified below, not a
misbehaving ensemble, but the departure is genuine, so fine-window rank
probabilities are slightly optimistic, and localised: excluding the 20
fine windows with any $\geq5\sigma$ stellar crossing restores uniformity
($p=\KsFineNoCross$, $n=98$). Per-control noise records and gain are two
stratifications the frozen products cannot support, so the empirical
rank distributions bound the net effect: expected flags \PsFlagMu{}
against a rank-only budget of \ExpFlags, with no excess in any stratum.
Nor are the windows nearest the beam edge silently affected: Sirius~B
sits at $r\simeq\PbSirRoverTheta\,\theta_{\rm PB}$, inside the annulus's
own radial span, and the four $\epsilon$\,Eri Band~6 windows are
withheld outright (\S\ref{sec:frames}). Controls at equal
holography-model gain would remove the sky-brightness term too, the
natural next step. Remaining robustness checks, trials-count
reconciliation under channel adjacency above all, are in
Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.

\subsubsection*{The fully symmetric region-max check}

The SR statistic applies identical source-region, drift-rate and
frequency maximisation at the star and at each of the \NCtrl{} controls
under one search rule. The frozen release products
cannot meet that, so its domain is the seven locally reprocessed
windows: six validation windows plus the AU~Mic window's first-epoch
block. All give $T_\star=3.7$--$4.9$ against control maxima of 6.7--7.7,
$p=0.81$--1.00, so the star is exchangeable with its ring wherever the
test runs at full symmetry. The single-position (SP) calibration above
is therefore the operative release-wide criterion over all \NWindows{}
windows, the SR statistic a validation subset; the two are never
interchanged. The machinery ships with the release, and the full
statement is in Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.

\subsubsection*{Astrophysical end-to-end validation: $\beta$~Pictoris CO}

Two of the four flagged windows are towards $\beta$~Pictoris, a debris
disc with resolved CO emission \citep{Matra2017,Dent2014}: one in Band~3
at \FobsBpicThree\,GHz, \DnuBpicThree\,MHz from the CO($J{=}1{\to}0$)
rest frequency (\LabCOoneGHz\,GHz, \citealt{Pickett1998}), the other in
Band~6 at \FobsBpicSix\,GHz, \DnuBpicSix\,MHz from CO($J{=}2{\to}1$).
Neither coincidence is exact, so the automated one-channel check missed
both. Through the frame chain (Table~\ref{tab:bpicaudit}) the offsets are one
kinematic component, \StelBpicThree{} and \StelBpicSix\,km\,s$^{-1}$
from stellar systemic and \BpicPairSepKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ apart, in the
two lowest CO rotational transitions of a system with detected
circumstellar CO. The unflagged lower-resolution Band~6 window, a third
epoch six years earlier, independently shows CO at
\StelBpicCoarse\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the same frame: all three epochs and
both transitions lie within \BpicSpreadKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$, deep inside
the $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ mask. The Band~6 window carries 114
formal $\geq5\sigma$ crossings where a channel-confined carrier admits
one, and the retained products do not record whether those channels are
contiguous, so we quote the count, not a line width. Peak flux densities of \FluxBpicThree{} and \FluxBpicSix\,mJy per
channel, ratio \FluxRatioBpic, are the right order for this belt's
spatially integrated CO but well below the optically thin LTE ratio
($\simeq4$), as resolved-out flux in the finer configuration would
predict; the comparison with calibrated imaging is indicative, the two
weighting the emission differently.

We attribute both crossings to this kinematically offset CO. The pair is
the survey's astrophysical end-to-end validation, a known signal run
through the same search as the null results, and it recovers the whole
check list: the frequencies to the frame chain's stated tolerance, the
velocity concordance across epochs and transitions, the localisation
(the Band~3 stellar peak exceeding all \NWindows{} control maxima and
the Band~6 peak all but its own CO-filled ensemble), and the drift
class, zero to within the grid. Nothing about it was tuned. A
three-panel figure of both features and their velocity alignment would
complete the record, but the frozen release does not carry the
channel-level spectral products of those execution blocks, so the
validation rests on the velocity chain of Table~\ref{tab:bpicaudit} and
the numbers above.

The attribution is no dismissal, their empirical significance against
the survey background being extreme: the Band~3 region peak of 20.3
exceeds all \NWindows{} per-window control maxima (add-one $p<0.0044$)
and the Band~6 peak of 12.8 all but one, that window's own CO-filled
ensemble reaching 15.2, the largest here ($p\approx0.0088$). In robust units, the stellar statistic minus the
ring median over $1.4826\times$ the ring median absolute deviation, the standardised excesses are $\zeta=\ZetaBpicThree$ and \ZetaBpicSix,
the yardsticks for the remaining flagged windows.
The alternative needs a transmitter coincidentally placed at matched
offsets from two CO transitions in the same star.

\subsubsection*{Dispositions of the remaining flagged windows}

The third, towards HD~48370 in Band~6, is the largest excess here in
absolute and the smallest in relative terms: $T_\star=27.10$ against a
control-ring maximum of 26.83, the star exceeding its ring by one per
cent of the statistic. Its standardised excess, $\zeta=\ZetaHd$, is near the CP$-$72 value
below, though the amplitude and velocity evidence differ. Star and ring rising together is what emission filling the
primary beam looks like, and the window sits $-34.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$
topocentric from CO($J{=}2{\to}1$). The attribution is independently
published: \citet{Cataldi2023} report prominent CO($2{\to}1$) and
$^{13}$CO($2{\to}1$) emission towards HD~48370 at $v_{\rm
bary}\approx41$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, well separated from its
$23.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ systemic velocity and attributed by them to
foreground cloud contamination; our window recovers it, and the crossing is consistent with, and very
likely caused by, it. Two internal corroborations make this the most
secure, not the most fragile, disposition. The peak is $\FluxHdFour$\,Jy
in one 122-kHz channel with the ring at the same level, a bright line
filling the beam and not a marginal statistical excess. And the $^{13}$CO($2{\to}1$) window of the same execution block has a
control maximum of \HdThirteenRingMax{} against only \HdThirteenStar{}
at the star, so there is bright $^{13}$CO in the field but not at the
star, as extended foreground emission predicts. The reconstructed annulus geometry (\S\ref{sec:statistic}) locates that
peak: the control carrying the maximum lies \HdThirteenOffset\,arcsec
from the star, $\HdThirteenOffsetPB\,\theta_{\rm PB}$, and the ten
strongest controls all fall between \HdThirteenTopLo{} and
\HdThirteenTopHi\,arcsec against a median control radius of
\HdThirteenRingMed\,arcsec: emission offset and clustered, not centred
on the star. A third window is consistent: all twelve Band~8 windows here belong to
$\eta$~Crv, HD~48370 and HD~61005 and are [C\,\textsc{i}]
$^{3}P_{1}$--$^{3}P_{0}$ (\MaskCIGHz\,GHz) tunings, and HD~48370's
carries a $5.05\sigma$ on-star crossing against a ring maximum of 6.23.
Its crossing channel sits \CIcrossKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from
[C\,\textsc{i}], too far for the repaired mask to exclude it (\S\ref{sec:linecost}), so
its control ensemble dispositions it, not line identification. The coincidence is with
\citeauthor{Cataldi2023}'s quoted $v_{\rm bary}$, not an independent
frame-chain re-derivation on our side, and this window is identified by
that velocity coincidence and the near-equality of star and ring, not by
the rank statistic: a stated limitation of the spatial test.

\subsubsection*{The unidentified CP$-$72~2713 feature}

The fourth flag, towards CP$-$72~2713 in Band~7, is the only one with no
astrophysical attribution, and it is marginal. The star is not
anonymous, and the prior for an astrophysical explanation at it is
higher than at a random sample member: a K7/M0 member of the
${\sim}24$\,Myr $\beta$~Pictoris moving group hosting a cold, dust-rich
debris disc detected in ALMA 1.33-mm continuum \citep{Moor2020}, the
same population and class of magnetically active star as AU~Mic.

\emph{What its own data show.} The feature is at \FobsCpSeven\,GHz, not
the window centre \CpTwoFreqGHz\,GHz quoted in earlier versions of this
paper: the crossing sits ${\sim}0.85$\,GHz below it, 35 channels above
the window's lower edge, where a global noise scale is least reliable
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}). The local recomputation gives 0.97 here, so the
excess is if anything understated. Peak flux density at the crossing is \FluxCpSeven\,mJy in one 488-kHz
channel of a \CpTwoBwGHz-GHz window, with two adjacent channels crossing
threshold. On the numbers,
$T_\star=\CpTwoTstar$ against a ring maximum of \CpTwoRingMax, a margin
of \CpTwoMargin: the stellar position ranks first among the \RankFloor{}
tested positions, but \CpTwoMargin{} is the smallest margin \NCtrl{}
controls can express, a resolution floor and not a measurement of
significance. Its standardised excess is $\zeta=\ZetaCp$ in robust units, two orders
below the $\beta$~Pictoris controls, and the margin is smaller than the
scatter across this star's own windows, whose other fine-channel control
maxima are 5.16, 5.41, 5.67 and 5.85: its ensembles reach 5.81 routinely
where the star does not cross threshold.
Figure~\ref{fig:cp72ctrl} shows the crossing inside its window's full
\NCtrl-position control distribution, at the upper edge of an unremarkable ensemble, not beyond it, one draw of \RankFloor{}
against expected ring maxima of median \BootMaxMed{} and 90th percentile
\BootPctNinety; a ring maximum at or above \CpTwoRingMax{}
occurs in \BootPGeRing{} per cent of windows and at or above
\CpTwoTstar{} in \BootPGeStar{} per cent.

\emph{Two of its own windows bear on the astrophysics.} The flagged
Band~7 window covers CO($3{\to}2$) at the stellar velocity and the
Band~6 fine window CO($2{\to}1$); neither crosses threshold, so we
report a non-detection of circumstellar CO in the CP$-$72~2713 belt.
That interests the debris-disc community and removes the obvious
circumstellar reading of the flag. The nearest transition of the masked species lies
$\DvCpSeven$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ away (radio convention; relativistic,
$-1326$), and because that mask is an automated contamination cut, not a complete
line catalogue, we re-queried a wider one, whose closest entry is
H$^{13}$CN($J{=}4{\to}3$) at $-195$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, still far outside any
circumstellar tolerance. The repaired mask of \S\ref{sec:linecost}
changes nothing here. The defensible reading is ``no plausible known
transition identified'', a statement about the catalogues consulted and
not a proof that none exists. Atmospheric or calibration structure near \FobsCpSeven\,GHz is
controlled as everything here is: it enters star and control positions
alike within the one execution block, so it widens the ensemble rather
than mimicking a stellar excess; isolating any telluric contribution
needs the calibration products beneath the frozen release. With a single execution block at this
frequency no recurrence test is available.

\emph{What is not done, and why.} Table~\ref{tab:cp72props} lists every
quantity the retained products carry. Polarisation hands, time and baseline splits, visibility-domain
localisation, calibrator comparison and synthesised-beam localisation
all need re-extraction beneath the frozen products. We do not claim tests we have not run. A Gaia/2MASS/WISE cross-match for an unresolved background source is
likewise open: its catalogue half could list sources in the synthesised
beam, but without visibility-domain localisation it could not say which,
if any, the excess belongs to, so a null match would not retire the flag
and a populated match could not promote it.

We record the window as an unclassified stage-1 flag, a statistical or
astrophysical feature and not candidate evidence, with explicit promote
and retire criteria. The second-stage local null replaces the $1/\RankFloor$ rank resolution
with a measured probability: $p=\LNpCP$ from $\LNDrawsMax$ draws on this
window's retained dynamic spectrum, and $p=\LNpCPgev$ from an
independent extreme-value fit to its \NCtrl{} control maxima, both about
\LNCPRatio{} times above the survey-wide Bonferroni scale. This window's
conservative disposition is therefore measured, not asserted. \emph{Promote} only on recurrence at an independent epoch, which a
targeted ALMA re-observation at \FobsCpSeven\,GHz would test directly.
No such observation is scheduled or proposed. An enlarged control
ensemble is no longer the route it was: the extreme-value fit already
estimates where a larger ensemble would land, above $\BonferroniThresh$,
so the excess would have to grow, not just be resolved more finely. \emph{Retire} on a clean
second epoch or on identification with catalogued emission. Until
either, it stays released and reported for what it is: unclassified, and
not statistically significant at the survey level.

\subsubsection*{Line exclusion: definition, repair, and cost}
\label{sec:linecost}

The searches as executed used an automated line check matching crossings
to catalogued rest frequencies within one spectral channel at zero
drift. The $\beta$~Pictoris windows showed that inadequate: both fell
outside its one-channel tolerance, by 10.6 and 21.9\,MHz, while lying,
after the frame chain, within 3.6\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of the stellar systemic
of a resolved, mapped disc, the regime a channel-width tolerance is
blind to. Every crossing was therefore reclassified with a
velocity-space mask, $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the stellar frame
around each catalogued transition. Its kinematic justification, empirical conservatism and the
counter-argument that a communicator might deliberately choose these
natural reference frequencies are in Appendix~\ref{app:linemask}. The mask is applied \emph{retrospectively}, as a post-hoc classification
criterion, not a detection threshold: the half-width was adopted
after the $\beta$~Pictoris crossings had been seen. A sensitivity test
reclassifying all \NHits{} crossings at $\pm20$, $\pm30$, $\pm50$ and
$\pm100$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ masks \MaskBWTwenty, \MaskBWThirty,
\MaskBWFifty{} and \MaskBWHundred{} per cent of gross bandwidth and
admits no new unattributed flag; at every width the only unmasked
unattributed flag is CP$-$72~2713. Masked regions are reported
throughout as \emph{excluded search space} and never as
searched-and-empty.

\emph{The mask as executed had four defects; all four are repaired
here.} It carried rest frequencies rounded to \MaskRestRoundMHz\,MHz, worth up
to \MaskRoundWorstKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$; no atomic carbon, although every
Band~8 window here is a [C\,\textsc{i}] $^{3}P_{1}$--$^{3}P_{0}$ tuning;
no hydrogen recombination line, although H30$\alpha$ falls in the
standard Band~6 tuning of much of the sample; and it was applied in the
stellar frame only, the wrong frame for the Galactic foreground emission
behind one of the three line attributions. The repaired catalogue holds
\MaskNTransNew{} transitions of \MaskNSpecNew{} species at full JPL/CDMS
precision \citep{Pickett1998,CDMS2005}, including [C\,\textsc{i}] at
\MaskCIGHz\,GHz and H30$\alpha$ at \MaskHthirtyGHz\,GHz, against
\MaskNTransOld{} of \MaskNSpecOld{} before, and it is applied in the
LSR frame as well as the stellar frame. The species count also
reconciles the text with the products, which always used
\MaskNSpecOld{} species: earlier versions of this paper said eight,
omitting H$_2$CO.

Repairing a mask is cheap; re-running the survey is not. The repaired mask is therefore re-applied to the frozen crossing list as
a post-hoc filter, using each crossing channel's own frequency as the
pipeline recorded it. \textbf{No disposition changes.} The two
$\beta$~Pictoris crossings remain inside the stellar-frame tube, at
\StelBpicThree{} and \StelBpicSix\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from CO($1{\to}0$) and
CO($2{\to}1$). HD~48370's crossing remains inside it at
$-17.8$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and is now caught by the LSR-frame tube as well,
at $-23.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, which is the frame its foreground
attribution always implied. CP$-$72~2713's crossing lies $\DvCpSeven$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from the nearest
transition in the topocentric frame and $-1300$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the LSR
frame, so no tube in any frame excludes it and it stays unclassified. Across all \NCrossFreq{}
crossing windows the repaired catalogue moves the nearest transition in
exactly \MaskNewCoinc{} case, \CIcrossStar's Band~8 crossing at
\CIcrossGHz\,GHz, whose nearest entry changes from CO($4{\to}3$)
\CIcrossOldKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ away to [C\,\textsc{i}] at
\CIcrossKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$. That is still outside the tube, and the frame corrections are at most a
few tens of km\,s$^{-1}$, so no frame masks it either; it was never a
flag, its own control ensemble exceeding it. The two new transitions cost a further
\MaskExtraGHz\,GHz of unique searched frequency,
\MaskExtraPct{} per cent.

The masking cost uses one definition: every JPL catalogued transition of
the \MaskNSpecOld{} frozen species (276), displaced by each system's radial velocity,
$\pm50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame half-width, intersected with each
window and merged. On the unique-frequency union, the survey holds \UnionGHz\,GHz of
archival spectral coverage, of which \SearchedUnionGHz\,GHz enters the
primary technosignature test and \MaskUnionLostGHz\,GHz (\MaskUnionPct{} per cent) is deliberately
excluded because of molecular-line confusion
(Figure~\ref{fig:waterfall}). On the window-summed gross bandwidth the same mask removes
\MaskGrossLostGHz\,GHz of \GrossBwGHz\,GHz (\MaskGrossPct{} per cent),
the per-window usable fraction having median 0.960 (10th percentile
0.790). CN and CO dominate the loss, and the per-species, per-band breakdown
ships in the released veto-mask export. The exposure accounting is as
follows: gross bandwidth \GrossBwGHz\,GHz (Class~A \ExpFineBwPct{} per cent, Class~B
\ExpCoarseBwPct{} per cent), unique sky-frequency union \UnionGHz\,GHz (Class~A \ExpUnFine\,GHz),
summed per-star union 371.9\,GHz, and on-source$\times$bandwidth
$\ExposureSGHz$\,s\,GHz, or \ExpoTotalSHG{} star-hour-GHz. The frequency-exposure curve
$C(\nu)=\sum_i t_i(\nu)$ has covered-bin median \ExpoMedSH{} and peak
\ExpoPeakSH{} star-hours in 2-GHz bins, and the archive's tuning concentrations put \ExpoTopPct{} per cent of
all exposure in the five best-populated bins, which any frequency-prior
calculation must weight. The mask governs dispositions, not the noise-derived thresholds, so the
quoted EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ values are unchanged; no signal was found
outside the excluded velocity regions in any window.

The mask is a design selection effect: a signal coincident with a
molecular line is not thereby ``false'' but \emph{excluded from the
technosignature search domain}, the astrophysical prior there being
overwhelming and this pipeline unable to separate the two. We place no
constraint on transmitters in the excluded regions, \MaskUnionPct{} per
cent of the unique-frequency union. Masked channels are retained, not discarded, for a line-coincident
search with independent discrimination. Nor is the exposure confined to
the flagged windows: 131 of \NWindows{} windows (30 per cent) contain an
in-band catalogued transition of the masked species, as do all four
flagged windows, yet in none does a crossing fall within one native channel of a rest
frequency, the regime the velocity-space criterion covers.

\subsection{Changing the detection statistic, and the AU~Mic crossing}
\label{sec:aumic}

The estimator is justified on its own terms in \S\ref{sec:statistic},
from the exchangeability the rank test needs and independently of any
dataset. The chronology is this. An earlier region-max statistic
maximised over an $n_{\rm src}=\RingNSrc$-position region centred on the
star against \NCtrl{} single-position controls, and under it a crossing
towards AU~Mic in Band~6 at 230.83\,GHz was carried as a candidate; the asymmetry was then identified algebraically, the symmetric form of
Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} substituted, the whole analysis re-run and the
criterion frozen; only afterwards was any flagged window, AU~Mic's
included, inspected under the new criterion. We state that plainly
because the revision removed the one candidate it was not designed to
remove, and a reader is entitled to weigh the sequence.

The sequence can be checked against Table~\ref{tab:bothstats}, which
audits every window the original statistic flagged, not the AU~Mic
window alone. The symmetric criterion flags a strict \emph{subset},
seven before and four after. Both $\beta$~Pic windows and HD~48370 keep
their flags under either statistic; AU~Mic, ALMA~J1537$-$3319 and
HD~14055 drop out, in all three cases because the stellar position never
exceeded its own control ensemble, the one configuration a localised
transmitter cannot produce. Under a
decision rule fixed without reference to these data, the $1/\RankFloor$
rank test, AU~Mic fails on the same evidence: 10 of its \NCtrl{}
controls reach or exceed the stellar statistic. Because the statistic
was settled on these data, the false-alarm calibration here is
descriptive of them.

(Appendix~\ref{app:continuum}). The line-exclusion step identifies every $\geq5\sigma$ outlier channel

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What signals could ALMA archival data actually detect?}
\label{sec:detectable}

Three cases, one per measured morphology, with real windows and the
completeness entries of Table~\ref{tab:classcomp}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sens2d} maps every window). Denominators count the
\NSystems{} systems whose deepest threshold in that class lies at or
below the case power.

\emph{(i) A continuous, frequency-stationary carrier} in a coarse
channel is the best case, not the worst. Toward the UV~Ceti pair
(2.7\,pc, deepest window EIRP$_{5\sigma}=1.6\times10^{13}$\,W) such a
carrier confined to its channel is recovered at near-ideal
$\sqrt{N}$ gain (\S\ref{sec:dwell}), so a carrier matching the
\emph{total} EIRP of an Arecibo-like planetary radar
(\S\ref{sec:benchmarks}) is detectable toward the nearest systems at
15.625-MHz resolution. \CaseDetCoarseNow{} system of \NSystems{}
qualifies, \CaseDetCoarseTwoNow{} at twice the power.

\emph{(ii) A continuously drifting carrier} in a fine 488-kHz channel
toward $\tau$~Ceti (3.65\,pc, threshold $3.8\times10^{13}$\,W) is
recovered \RecThresh{} per cent of the time at threshold for
$f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$ and $75$--$83$ per cent at $1.6$--$2\times$
threshold (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). A drifting transmitter above
${\sim}8\times10^{13}$\,W is recovered in \RecTwice{} per cent of trials
if its drift stays inside the searched ceiling; \CaseDetFineNow{}
systems reach that depth in Class~A.

\emph{(iii) An intermittent carrier, on for a quarter of the track},
toward HD~10647 (17.4\,pc, $1.5\times10^{14}$\,W) is recovered
\DwellFineQ{} per cent of the time in fine channels, \DwellCoarseQ{}
per cent in coarse ones, so a duty-$1/4$ transmitter above
${\sim}2\times10^{14}$\,W is detected toward \CaseDetDwellNow{} such
mid-distance systems. Recovery falls rapidly at smaller dwell fractions
(Table~\ref{tab:dwell}), and below $f_{\rm dwell}\sim0.1$ only the
nearest windows carry information.

\subsection{What does the null result mean?}
\label{sec:null}

No credible technosignature has been found (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}).
The EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ values
of the released catalogue are nominal trigger powers (boxed rule,
\S\ref{sec:method}); completeness-calibrated exclusion holds only where
the measured recovery curve applies, in Class~A. The sample is
ALMA-archive-complete within 40\,pc and sensitivity-ranked, not an
unbiased draw.

One exclusion deserves the converse emphasis. The line mask removes
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame tubes around the
catalogued transitions, whose rest frequencies are the
`magic' channels \citet{CocconiMorrison1959} argued a communicator might
choose. This survey is blind by construction to a transmitter parked
there in the stellar frame, a false-positive control purchased with
\MaskGrossPct{} per cent of gross bandwidth and one physically motivated
class of signal. Of the \NExoPlanets{} planets known around the
\NExoHosts{} hosts, only TRAPPIST-1\,b reaches the
drift ceiling in its edge-on worst case, on star--planet relative
accelerations $GM_\star/r^{2}$ from NASA Exoplanet Archive parameters
accessed 2026 September~9, maximised over orbital phase and
inclination. Figure~\ref{fig:accel} shows the ceiling
as a selection function of planet period and host mass; the host-mass
dependence is weak ($a\propto M_\star^{1/3}$ at fixed period), so it is
effectively a function of period alone. Tables~\ref{tab:searchspace},
\ref{tab:perband} and \ref{tab:selfunc} give the detectability axes, the
per-band decomposition and the selection function. Coverage eligibility
and search completeness differ: the public usable ALMA archive
(\S\ref{sec:sample}) sets eligibility, and every held execution block in
scope was searched, so no EB-level selection lies behind the \NEB{}. A
proper-motion-matched archive audit (2026 September~9) measures
availability directly, counting 107 unique public on-star execution
blocks and re-finding all but one searched block.

The survey sits against \citet{Wright2018}'s haystack axis by axis. On
frequency (10\,MHz--115\,GHz) only Band~3 falls inside, 20.6 of
115\,GHz, 0.18 of the axis, where Hz-resolution surveys reach far more
finely; the remaining 66.6\,GHz (Bands~4--8, 125--495\,GHz) lies wholly
outside and is the new territory. On transmitted bandwidth (0--20\,MHz)
a native-channel search probes a fraction set by channel width
(window-mean 0.63, median 0.78). On sensitivity the median
threshold (\SminMedian\,mJy across a 15.625-MHz channel,
$\phi\approx1.0\times10^{-21}$\,W\,m$^{-2}$) detects a $10^{13}$\,W
transmitter only within ${\sim}0.9$\,pc. Against the sole prior
ALMA-archival search \citep[][28 bycatch targets at $\geq$1.01\,kpc,
deepest EIRP$_{\rm min}$ $6.91\times10^{17}$\,W]{Mason2024}, the
\NSystems{} systems here, \NSysInTwentyNow{} inside 20\,pc, reach median
per-target thresholds ${\sim}\MasonRatio\times$ deeper. These fractions
are not combined into one coverage: the axes are strongly coupled,
distance and sensitivity above all, so a product of marginals would
misstate the overlap by orders of magnitude.

What the zero detection bounds is conditioned on each target's searched
frequencies, the epochs observed and the searched morphology class.
Turning it into a fraction of systems requires a per-system
detection probability, which is not a demographic occurrence rate. We
call it the \emph{conditional searched-domain transmitter fraction}
throughout, never ``occurrence'' or ``transmitter fraction''
unqualified, and keep its evaluation out of the interpretive argument:
the framework, the demonstration and its sensitivity analysis sit in
Appendix~\ref{app:population}, labelled \emph{illustrative
calculation only} and among no headline quantity
(Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}). The result of this paper is the search
sensitivity, the exposure and the null. The demotion is measured, not
rhetorical: completeness for the drift-resolved class rests on one
injected configuration, and the error of transferring it to the other
\NWinA{} Class~A windows is unbounded, so a factor-of-two systematic
would rescale every fraction derived from it.

In plain terms: as a by-product of other science, ALMA has stared at
\NStars{} of the nearest stars across parts of the millimetre and
submillimetre band that no technosignature search had examined. We
looked for a narrow, artificial-looking spectral line at each star, and
found none the surrounding sky could not also produce. The powers we
could have seen are enormous by human standards, and we could look only
in the few gigahertz and the few hours each star happened to be
observed. A non-detection on those terms says
little about how common transmitters are, and a good deal about how to
search an archive for them.

Because selection is on archival coverage alone, useful subsamples
fall out without compromising the archive-complete framing, subject to
the unresolved-pair caveat of \S\ref{sec:sample}. \NExoHosts{} of the
\NStars{} processed stars (\NExoPlanets{} known planets) are confirmed
exoplanet hosts, among them Proxima~Centauri, $\tau$~Ceti,
$\epsilon$~Eridani, $\beta$~Pictoris and the seven-planet TRAPPIST-1
system; HN~Lib and LHS~1140, each with a habitable-zone planet, now
carry an ALMA technosignature non-detection. Four searched stars are
white dwarfs \citep{GentileFusillo2021}: Sirius~B, Van~Maanen's Star,
Wolf~219 and
CD$-$38~10980, all continuum non-detections with nominal
EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ trigger thresholds of
$2.3\times10^{13}$--$8.4\times10^{14}$\,W, among the few mm/submm SETI
constraints for white dwarfs \citep{PollutedWD2026}.

\subsection{ALMA as a technosignature instrument: strengths and limits}
\label{sec:instrument}

ALMA offers four advantages. Aperture and receiver temperature give
mm-wave sensitivities no single dish reaches. The tuning range,
125--495\,GHz here, is essentially unsearched. Interferometric spatial
discrimination, the control statistic of \S\ref{sec:statistic}, tests a
feature's position against \NCtrl{} alternatives, which single-dish and
beamformed searches cannot. And the archive supplies \NEB{} public
execution blocks re-processable without new allocations. Each strength
has its mirror. Native channelisation reaches 15.3\,kHz at best, orders
coarser than the Hz resolution of dedicated searches, so the search
tests unresolved spectral excess, not fine structure; coverage is
fragmented into \UnionIntervals{} frequency islands weighted by other
science; the line mask excludes \MaskUnionPct{} per cent of the union;
configurations are heterogeneous, so no completeness transfers between
them; and targeting is interest-driven rather than uniform
(\S\ref{sec:sample}).

The largest unused advantage is the visibilities themselves. ALMA can
test whether a spectral feature carries the interferometric phase
signature of a celestial point source at a known position. For each
surviving candidate one would compare competing likelihoods:
$H_{\star}$, a source at the propagated stellar position,
$V_{ij}=A(t,\nu)\,e^{-2\pi i(u_{ij}l_\star+v_{ij}m_\star)}$; $H_{\rm
sky}$, a source elsewhere; $H_{\rm common}$, correlated or common-mode
interference; and $H_{\rm noise}$, thermal and calibration residual, and
evaluate $\Lambda=\mathcal L(V|H_\star)/\mathcal L(V|H_{\rm
noise}{+}H_{\rm common})$. This uses information single-dish and
beamformed searches lack, is strictly more powerful than ranking a peak
against a control ensemble, and would make that ensemble a calibration
layer, not the primary authentication statistic. The search here is
its amplitude-only prologue.

\subsection{A next-generation ALMA technosignature search}
\label{sec:futurework}

Four changes would enlarge what this experiment can say, ranked by the
signal space they buy.

\emph{Drift discrimination on coarse windows.} Within the searched
ceiling every in-grid drift on a 15.625-MHz window is sub-channel over a
track, so \NCoarse{} of \NWindows{} windows measure amplitude but cannot
distinguish a drifting carrier from a stationary one
(\S\ref{sec:dwell}). Finer channelisation on re-observation, or a drift
model spanning multiple execution blocks, would recover that axis.

\emph{Retain more control spectra.} The two-stage scheme of
\S\ref{sec:technosearch}, \NCtrl{} controls routinely and a local null
for any window that passes, fails wherever that null cannot be
validated, because full dynamic spectra survive for only \LNNRetained{}
control positions. Retaining all of them, at modest storage cost, would
make the second stage usable in structured fields too.

\emph{Extend the injection campaign and the polarisation axis.} The
completeness curve should be measured per band, channel width,
integration time, antenna count and primary-beam position rather than
transferred by construction, and pushed past $10\sigma$ until it
asymptotes, with EIRP$_{90}$ unlocated. The design is a stratified grid
over those axes, injections at randomised drift rates and channel phases
rather than preferentially on grid points, morphologies extended to
sinusoidal acceleration tracks from representative sample orbits,
trial-level logging from the first run, and an independent
re-implementation cross-check of the injection code. The
polarisation axis buys discrimination against weakly polarised
astrophysical backgrounds at no sensitivity cost.

\emph{A boundary on drift linearity.} The search assumes drift linear
over a track. Curvature matters when $\tfrac{1}{2}\ddot\nu T^{2}$
exceeds half a native channel, reached only by the shortest-period
orbits: $P\lesssim1.6$\,d for a typical Class~A window (230\,GHz,
488\,kHz, $T\approx2000$\,s), about an hour for Class~B.
TRAPPIST-1\,b, at $1.51$\,d, sits just inside the boundary and is also
the case exceeding the drift ceiling; elsewhere the constant-drift model
is adequate, and the chirp-search extension
(Appendix~\ref{app:extended}) covers the remainder.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This ALMA-archival technosignature survey searched, within 40\,pc, every
star with public usable coverage: \NStarBands{} target/bands, \NStars{}
stars in \NSystems{} independent systems, ALMA-archive-complete and
audited for position and identity (\S\ref{sec:exclusions}), but covering
${\sim}1$ per cent of the catalogued stars in that volume and inheriting the
archive's non-uniform targeting. The archive's channelisation splits the
search: \NWinA{} Class~A windows cover drift-resolved spectral carriers,
\NWinB{} Class~B windows only unresolved spectral excess, leaving no
drift discrimination over \PctCoarse{} per cent of the survey
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}). Four windows raised a stage-1 statistical
screening flag. Three are consistent with, and very likely caused by, CO
emission: two circumstellar components towards $\beta$~Pictoris across
two transitions and three epochs, and one previously reported foreground
cloud towards HD~48370
\citep{Cataldi2023}. The fourth, towards CP$-$72~2713 at
\FobsCpSeven\,GHz, is unclassified and marginal: a second-stage local
null on its own dynamic spectrum gives $p=\LNpCP$, about \LNCPRatio{}
times above the survey-wide Bonferroni scale, so it is not significant
at the survey level, and it is held under pinned promote and retire
criteria (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). None survives vetting, so we report
\NCandidates{} credible technosignatures. Barnard's Star and
Wolf~359 carry their first published mm/submm technosignature limits
(\S\ref{sec:barnardswolf}), and forty ancillary continuum measurements
(median $5\sigma$ limit 0.52\,mJy) are non-detections of unquantified
statistical power.

The EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ values are nominal trigger thresholds (boxed rule,
\S\ref{sec:method}): $\EirpMinA$--$\EirpMaxA$\,W over Class~A at
\ChanLoA--\ChanHiA\,kHz native resolution and
$\EirpMinB$--$\EirpMaxB$\,W over Class~B at
\ChanLoB--\ChanHiB\,MHz, deep enough toward the nearest system for an
unresolved carrier matching the total power of an Arecibo-like planetary
radar, and optimistic by $\times\HanFacBest$ to $\times\HanFacWorst$
under the Hanning response. The conditional searched-domain transmitter
fraction stays illustrative (Appendix~\ref{app:population}). Searched is
not the same as constrained: the \emph{Scope of the constraints} box of
\S\ref{sec:method} fixes what the non-detection binds; outside it
nothing is bounded. The first-priority extensions (\S\ref{sec:futurework})
are drift discrimination on coarse windows, a visibility-domain likelihood
ratio, control ensembles past the $1/\RankFloor$ rank floor, and a
stratified injection campaign over band, channel width, integration time
and beam offset. By-products ship with the data: a serendipitous
molecular-line catalogue, exoplanet-host and white-dwarf subsample
limits, and a closure-phase diagnostic, demonstrated but without
discriminating power at these flux densities.

\section{Frequency conventions, linewidth reading, and smearing}
\label{app:conventions}

\emph{Reference frames.} The line mask's chain runs topocentric
$\rightarrow$ barycentric (Earth ephemeris at the execution-block
mid-time) $\rightarrow$ stellar rest frame (catalogued systemic radial
velocity), with JPL/CDMS rest frequencies
\citep{Pickett1998,CDMS2005}; in the LSR frame it stops at the standard
solar motion, not the systemic velocity (\S\ref{sec:linecost}).
The residual error is the catalogue radial-velocity uncertainty alone, a
few km\,s$^{-1}$ within 40\,pc; second-order relativistic terms
at the \MaskVWidth\,km\,s$^{-1}$ scale are $\lesssim5$\,kHz at 350\,GHz,
below the finest native channel searched (15.3\,kHz). The
$\beta$~Pictoris and AU~Mic attributions use the same chain, so their
offsets are comparable to the exclusion tolerance.

\emph{Channel widths, and the instrumental response.} These come from
the measurement sets' own spectral window tables (CHAN\_WIDTH). Earlier
versions read a missing ``EFFECTIVE\_BANDWIDTH'' column as evidence of
no online spectral averaging, hence that CHAN\_WIDTH is also the
effective resolution. MS~v2 defines no such column; the required
EFFECTIVE\_BW and RESOLUTION are always present. The check is withdrawn,
and with it the claim that the quoted channelisations are noise
bandwidths.

ALMA applies online Hanning smoothing by default, so the spectral
response is ${\sim}2$ channels wide and adjacent-channel noise is
correlated at $\rho\simeq0.5$. Under the $0.25/0.5/0.25$ kernel a
sub-channel tone keeps \HanBest{} of its power in the peak channel at
the channel centre and \HanWorst{} on a boundary, median \HanMed{} over
uniform placement. Every EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ here is therefore optimistic
by $\times\HanFacBest$ to $\times\HanFacWorst$, median
$\times\HanFacMed$; the released catalogue carries the nominal threshold
and both corrected values per window, and the sensitivity summary quotes
the worst case. The injections deposit an unsmoothed delta-in-channel
tone, so the recovery curves of \S\ref{sec:dwell} and
Appendix~\ref{app:inject} carry the same bias and the end-to-end check
(Eq.~\ref{eq:stacked}) cannot see it. Part of the lag-one correlation
attributed to the drift-tracked statistic is instrumental on the same
grounds, a decomposition not made here. We do not rescale: the
per-window smoothing state sits in archive metadata not yet harvested,
so each threshold here is nominal, and up to $\times\HanFacWorst$
optimistic if the window is Hanning-smoothed.

\emph{Linewidth convention.} The statistic is the single-channel
flux density after drift correction, so the quoted EIRP$_{5\sigma}$
applies to any transmitter narrower than the native channel, flat in the
assumed linewidth, with two sub-channel qualifications. Flatness is
exact only for a centred tone, a boundary-straddling one splitting its
power between two channels at worst by a factor of two; and the
channel-to-channel transition follows the correlator's passband shape,
not retained in the frozen products, so flatness is a channel-centred
idealisation good to tens of per cent. Thresholds therefore carry a
sub-channel-position systematic of that size, a randomly placed tone
keeping a mean 0.75 of its power in the stronger channel, and EIRP
values are quoted to two significant figures.

\emph{Transmitters wider than one channel.} The baseline filter is a
running median along frequency, flat for features narrower than roughly
half the median width $W$ and falling to zero above $W$, so the survey's
effective sensitivity is to channel-confined morphologies and $W$ is the
linewidth ceiling. $W$ is not carried in the frozen per-window products,
so it cannot be printed here; it ships with the tagged release. Within
that ceiling, a top-hat transmitter spanning
$N_{\rm ch}$ native channels has per-channel flux the total divided by
$N_{\rm ch}$ at unchanged noise, so to first order
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}(N_{\rm ch}) \;\simeq\; N_{\rm ch}\times
\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}(1), \qquad N_{\rm ch} \lesssim W/2,
\end{equation}
the rescaling the main text applies to signals broader than one channel.

\emph{Worked example: from flux to EIRP.} Take UV~Ceti (G~272-61B)
Band~3. The searched window's per-channel rms is 0.244\,mJy, so
$S_{\rm min} = 5\times0.244 = 1.22$\,mJy in one 15.625-MHz native
channel, or $1.22\times10^{-29}$\,W\,m$^{-2}$\,Hz$^{-1}$ at 2.67\,pc,
and $\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma} = 4\pi d^{2} S_{\rm min} \Delta\nu =
1.6\times10^{13}$\,W.

\emph{Intra-integration smearing.} The frequency excursion at the
maximum searched drift rate across one integration,
$\dot\nu_{\max} t_{\rm int}$, is at most 1.10 channels across the
\NWindows{} windows (median 0.001, 90th percentile 0.06). A linear
drift smeared by $\Delta$ channels within an integration keeps a
fraction $\eta_{\rm smear} = \mathrm{sinc}(\pi\Delta/2)$ of the
peak-channel amplitude, so the effective threshold is
$\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}/\eta_{\rm smear}$. Three windows have
$\eta_{\rm smear}<0.99$: $\beta$~Pic Band~6 ($\Delta=1.10$,
$\eta_{\rm smear}=0.574$), $\eta$~Crv Band~8 (0.585, 0.865) and
$\eta$~Crv Band~7 (0.411, 0.932). Every other window agrees with its
nominal threshold to better than 1 per cent. A signal drifting midway between
adjacent trial rates accumulates at most 0.03 channels of residual drift
across the grid as realised, so no significant sensitivity is lost
between them.

\section{Injection-recovery sensitivity validation}
\label{app:inject}

To test whether a $5\sigma$ threshold behaves as idealised Gaussian
noise predicts, we inject synthetic tones of known amplitude into real
visibilities, through the extraction code's own mechanism and as far
upstream of the statistic as retained products allow, and measure what
the unmodified pipeline returns. Three results inherit its
single-configuration limitation: the drifting-class recovery of
Appendix~\ref{app:population}, the machinery and boundary-placement
amplitude factors of \S\ref{sec:validation}, and the coarse-window dwell
reading of \S\ref{sec:dwell}.

Six configurations span three bands and both classes: three
coarse Band~7 spws of AT~Mic, one coarse spw each in Bands~3 and~6 of
AU~Mic, and AU~Mic's 488-kHz flagband window with its 49-trial drift
grid. Each receives a complex tone at the star position, at amplitudes
$4$--$10\times$ the statistic's own noise, drift fractions
$f_{\rm drift}=0$, $0.25$, $0.5$, $0.75$, $1.0$ of the ceiling, on- and
half-grid rates, and channel-centred and boundary placements:
200 trials per configuration, \NTrials{} in all, under a frozen
criterion ($T\geq5$, peak channel within 3 of the injection, peak drift
within one grid step).

The coarse-window variant recovered 0 of 500 injections at every
amplitude from $4\sigma$ to $10\sigma$ under that criterion. This is a
recovery-criterion artefact, not a pipeline suppression, and it is the
one withdrawn result here: the drift-matching clause cannot be satisfied
on coarse windows, where every in-grid drift is sub-channel over the
track, so the reported peak drift is arbitrary. Scored on detection
alone the same windows recover essentially everything, and an end-to-end
injection through the released pipeline
returns $T_\star$ of 20.5, 65.3 and 217.7 at 1, 3 and 10 times the
per-integration noise, against an ideal coherent gain of
$\sqrt{639}=25$. Class~B limits therefore bound carriers of any dwell
fraction, with the completeness of \S\ref{sec:dwell}; what those windows
lack is drift discrimination. The fine window fails the same criterion
the same way, and that cell is unmeasured rather than lost: carriers
with $f_{\rm drift}\leq0.25$ dwell ${\geq}40$ per cent of the track per
channel and fail it, those with $f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$ surviving as the
searched class.

The second result is the completeness curve. On the fine window,
drifting carriers ($f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$, pooled over grid phase and
sub-channel placement) are recovered 17, \RecThresh, 58, 75 and
\RecTwice{} per cent at $4$, $5$, $6$, $8$ and $10\sigma$, saturating by
twice threshold power (Fig.~\ref{fig:completeness}). The machinery
alone, with the tone injected after the baseline step, is
amplitude-faithful to within $-9$/$+14$ per cent for channel-centred
tones at every drift fraction. Boundary-straddling placement costs a
factor $0.6$--$0.8$ in the statistic and recovers 0 per cent at
$5\sigma$ against 83 per cent for centred ones (67 and 100 per cent at
$10\sigma$), crossing 50 per cent near $1.4\times$ threshold amplitude.
Survey completeness uses the placement-pooled curve, conservative for
the boundary-straddling case: EIRP$_{50}=1.10\,\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}$
and EIRP$_{90}>2.0\,\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}$, a bound only, both
carrying an unbounded transfer systematic to every configuration but
this one, bracketed as $0.5$--$2\times$ in
Appendix~\ref{app:population}. Not spanned: other target environments,
primary-beam offsets, integration durations and edge-phased drift
grids; bounding that transfer error, propagated in
Appendix~\ref{app:population}, needs the multi-configuration campaign
of \S\ref{sec:futurework}.

\section{Demonstrated diagnostics with no operative role here}
\label{app:closure}

\emph{Closure phase.} The closure phase
$\arg(V_{ij})+\arg(V_{jk})-\arg(V_{ik})$ vanishes for an unresolved
point source anywhere on sky and is immune to per-station calibration
errors, so it tests only point-source coherence; a coherent
far-sidelobe interferer would pass. We score consistency with zero by a
circular (unit-phasor) z-score of the mean drift-tracked closure phase,
nulled on each observation's own integrations, validated on synthetic
visibilities and run on continuum detections in four local reprocessing
fields, including the resolved AT~Mic Band~7 binary as a resolving
control. At these flux densities it is useless: per-baseline S/N is
0.5--0.7 at the median and never exceeds 5.1, only 0.02 per cent of
triangles have all three baselines above S/N${\,=\,}5$, and every
field's closure phases, the binary included, are indistinguishable from
noise. It discriminates only at greater depth.

\emph{Chirped drift and periodicity.}
\label{app:extended}
Stacking at constant linear drift rate is blind by design to a drift
rate that changes during the observation, and to pulsed or periodically
modulated carriers. We implemented a chirped (quadratic-drift)
extension on a coarser grid of 21 linear-drift $\times$ 5 chirp-rate
trials, and a per-channel periodicity search, a per-position FFT along
each channel's time axis. Both vet the star against 8 retained control
positions, a strict subset of that window's \NCtrl-position ensemble,
not an independent one. On the 122 retained spectra this is a
demonstration: flag rates of 10.7 per cent for chirp and 13.1 per cent
for periodicity against a ${\sim}11$ per cent chance rate show only
that the extension runs correctly under the null. No instance is
treated as a candidate and no limit derives from it; extending both to
the full ensemble would make the screen survey grade.

\section{Cross-target frequency-occupancy check}
\label{app:freqoccupancy}

With no ON/OFF cadence against radio-frequency interference, this
single-pointing archival survey substitutes the many unrelated stars
sharing similar correlator setups: a channel-confined feature recurring
at the same observed \emph{sky frequency} towards different targets is
almost certainly interference or instrumental. Over every target/spw
result the check pairs the best-fit-channel frequency and the formal
$\geq5\sigma$ crossings, matching $\nu_1,\nu_2$ towards different
targets when $|\nu_1-\nu_2| < \max(k\,\Delta\nu_1,\; k\,\Delta\nu_2)$
with $k=3$ (${\sim}46$\,kHz). Across the \NWindows{} windows and 107
tabulated crossings, no pair falls within the kernel, or within
0.5\,MHz. The kernel is narrower than the $\beta$~Pictoris line offsets
of 10.6 and 21.9\,MHz, so those windows were dispositioned by
line-catalogue inspection.

The null's power is the count of target pairs sharing frequency
coverage: on the $\le$20\,pc subset, 366 of 946 star pairs (39 per cent)
overlap by at least 0.01\,GHz, deepest 14.25\,GHz
($\gamma$~Lep--$\gamma$~Vir), and 109 pairs involve one of the five
stars carrying formal crossings, so every crossing frequency was tested
against overlapping independent targets. The UV~Ceti and AT~Mic shared
execution blocks are excluded, their intra-pair coincidences being
identities by construction. The interim product's
per-window crossing lists are capped at 50 frequencies, so the true
count is 171 crossings against 107 tabulated. The difference is one
window, $\beta$~Pic Band~6 ultra-fine, whose 114 formal crossings enter
as the 50 strongest; its untabulated 64 could not be tested, so the null
is demonstrated for the tabulated set and unresolved for the rest. The
public release ships uncapped crossing lists.

\section{The velocity-space line mask: justification and retrospective
application} \label{app:linemask}

The one-channel line check failed the $\beta$~Pictoris windows
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}): a native channel spans a fraction of a
km\,s$^{-1}$, while circumstellar kinematics shift real emission by
many channels. The replacement mask takes its tolerance from the
kinematic budget of circumstellar gas: Keplerian motion where these
molecules survive ($\gtrsim$10\,au), $\lesssim$13\,km\,s$^{-1}$;
cold-gas linewidths $\lesssim$1\,km\,s$^{-1}$; cold dense-gas tracers
with no bulk-wind term for the dwarfs dominating the sample; and an
uncorrected barycentric term bounded by
$\pm$30\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The result is empirically conservative: the largest
attributed circumstellar component ($\beta$~Pic, $-2.7$ to
$-3.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) sits at 7 per cent of it, and \emph{zero}
crossing frequencies lie 13--50\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from their nearest
transition, so a $\pm13$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ Keplerian-only mask would have
suppressed and released the same windows: the wider tolerance costs no
yield and buys robustness against radial-velocity catalogue error.

\emph{The repaired mask.} The mask's four defects as executed, and the
retrospective re-application that repairs them without re-running the
survey, are in \S\ref{sec:linecost}; no disposition changes. The
rounding defect is also why the released CO($1{\to}0$) offset column,
referred to the frozen mask's rounded \CatCOoneGHz\,GHz entry, reads
\CatCOerrMHz\,MHz (\CatCOerrKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$) smaller than the
laboratory-referred value of Table~\ref{tab:bpicaudit}. A cheaper, better-founded rule than enumerating species: the archive
records the rest frequency each execution block was tuned to, so masking
each window's own tuned line would catch the $\beta$~Pictoris, HD~48370
and [C\,\textsc{i}] windows automatically, alongside an LSR-frame tube of
a few $\times10$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

Two of the six remaining crossing windows fall inside the mask (the
unflagged $\beta$~Pic coarse window at $-3.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$; HD~285968
at $+8.7$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, already rejected by its control ring, maximum
10.9 against a star peak of 6.5); the rest lie far outside any
circumstellar tolerance (AU~Mic $+379$, LHS~1140 $+115$\,km\,s$^{-1}$).
Of the \NHits{} windows with an on-star crossing, \NSpatial{} also beat
their control ring, three accounted for by the mask. The other
\NonExcRingGe{} are ordinary noise crossings: in each, the window's own
control ring reaches or exceeds the stellar value (ring-maximum median
\NonExcRingMed{} against a crossing median \NonExcHitMed), the expected
Gaussian-tailed population of moderate $5$--$5.5\sigma$ crossings with
comparably strong peaks among \NCtrl{} controls. But the transitions
that make astrophysical false positives cheap are also natural reference
frequencies for a deliberate communicator \citep{CocconiMorrison1959},
so the released products keep a known-line-coincident list for a future
search with independent discrimination: every on-source $5\sigma$
crossing inside the masked intervals, with its offset from each nearby
transition.

\section{AU~Mic: the three tests in full}
\label{app:aumic}

\emph{Localisation.} Reprocessed from the raw science data model with
the observatory pipeline's own calibration recipe (310 integrations
against the release pipeline's 249, on a finer drift grid) and re-run
through the symmetric statistic of \S\ref{sec:statistic}, the crossing
window's star does not beat its control ensemble. The frozen products
agree only asymmetrically: the source-region peak exceeds the
\NCtrl-control maximum by 0.12, below that statistic's window-to-window
scatter, and the crossing fails the single-position variant too.

\emph{Recurrence.} The one further public Band~6 block covering
230.83\,GHz, the same programme executed 2014 June~5, was searched
identically: no channel within $\pm$10 of the first-epoch flagged
channel exceeds 3.1, and nothing is localised to the star. Its band rms
(1.48\,mJy per 488-kHz channel) matches the first epoch's 1.9\,mJy, so a
comparable excess would have been recovered, and the frequency recurs at
no other target. Intermittent or precisely-scheduled transmissions
outside the sampled windows stay unconstrained.

\emph{Statistical scale.} The on-source peak (5.97) sits at the
\AuPct{}th percentile of the control-maxima distribution over all
\NWindows{} windows (median \CtrlMed, 90th percentile \CtrlPninety):
\AuNge{} windows (\AuFrac{} per cent) match or exceed it, add-one
empirical $p=\AuP$ before any multiple-testing correction: a survey-wide
window-level rate, distinct from the within-window rank of
\S\ref{sec:aumic}. The crossing matches no catalogued molecular
transition (287\,MHz from CO $J{=}2{\to}1$), and closure-phase vetting
finds it point-source consistent, one-sided evidence only
(Appendix~\ref{app:closure}).

Two astrophysical contexts are recorded for re-examination;
neither grounds the rejection. AU~Mic is an exceptionally active flare
star, but the block's own quiescent continuum (0.15\,mJy) rules out the
later-epoch GHz-wide flares of \citet{MacGregor2020} (16.8 and
6.1\,mJy): an 11\,mJy channel-confined excess would sit ${\sim}70\times$
above a continuum that shows nothing. The controls share the primary-beam
footprint, so smooth structure such as the debris belt raises star and
controls alike and cannot alone produce a localised excess.

\section{The ancillary continuum lane}
\label{app:continuum}
\label{app:contsearch}

The continuum lane is an exploratory by-product, not a co-equal search:
57 measurements, 17 detections and 40 limits, a millimetre excess being
at best an indirect, model-dependent observable of waste-heat
technology. An ``upper limit'' is $5\times$ the
primary-beam-corrected map rms at the star's position, and the deepest
non-detection is towards TRAPPIST-1 in Band~3. Under the combined error
model sixteen of seventeen detections stay above $5\sigma$; the
exception ($\eta$~Corvi Band~7, $4.7\sigma$ combined) is marginal.
Bright far-from-target peaks ($\epsilon$\,Eri, GJ~674, CD$-$23~14742)
fall outside the 3-arcsec tolerance and count as non-detections.

Every detection is astrophysically unsurprising: the unresolved UV~Ceti
binary; six systems whose millimetre flux is disc or chromospheric
emission, among them the young, active $\beta$~Pic, AU~Mic and AT~Mic,
and the old, inactive $\tau$~Cet, a ${\sim}5$--8\,Gyr G8V star whose
flux is photospheric and/or its debris disc; six stars at photospheric
flux, among them $\gamma$~Lep, $\gamma$~Vir, $\chi^{1}$~Ori and
$\eta$~Cru; and two late-M dwarfs of documented magnetic activity,
LP~349$-$25 and the auroral emitter LSR~J1835$+$3259
\citep{Hallinan2015}.
Closure phase cannot discriminate structure for any of the seventeen at
these flux densities.

\emph{The anomaly screen.} Each star's flux is tested against its own
photospheric expectation (Planck function with Gaia DR3 parameters,
Pecaut \& Mamajek fallback where GSP-Phot is unavailable) and against
same-$T_{\rm eff}$ stars in coarse bins of at least five, by a robust
median/MAD statistic. The error model, evaluated before any flag,
is $\sigma_{\rm tot}^{2} = \sigma_{\rm map}^{2} + (f_{\rm cal}\,S_\nu)^{2}
+ \sigma_{\rm model}^{2}$, with $f_{\rm cal}$ 5 per cent in Bands~3--6
and 10 per cent in Bands~7--8 and $\sigma_{\rm model}$ propagated from
Gaia temperature and radius errors and the fallback relation's scatter.
The model has \emph{no intrinsic-variability term}: adequate for a
photosphere, wrong for an active M dwarf whose millimetre flux can vary
by factors of several, so the screen is uninformative for
exactly the stars a radio-stellar reader cares about. At the five-star
minimum the bin median rests on two or three values, so it flags for
inspection rather than testing per-star hypotheses. One outlier results:
$\chi^1$~Ori at Band~3 (excess ratio 2.52, largest of the seventeen;
resampled tail probability 0.53 in its six-star bin, so a ranked outlier
of a small sample, not a significant departure), consistent with a
spectroscopic G0V binary's unresolved companion or a chromosphere.

\section{False-alarm accounting under the symmetric criterion}
\label{app:falsealarm}

The per-window rate is $1/\RankFloor$, the survey expectation
$\NWindows/\RankFloor=\ExpFlags$ flags. The floor
falls only if the control ensemble grows, which the primary beam bounds;
and windows are not fully independent, grouping into \NEB{} execution
blocks that share weather, calibration and correlator setup, so
$N/\RankFloor$ is an expectation over a mildly correlated sample, not
$N$ independent trials. Both limits are conservative for the conclusion
drawn, that no window warrants candidate status: they widen rather than
narrow the null range.

Over $\TrialCells$ channel$\times$drift cells, a one-sided Gaussian
$5\sigma$ tail ($2.87\times10^{-7}$) predicts \GaussCross{} chance
crossings, against the \NHits{} windows with an on-star crossing. Raw
counts run higher because the
drift-tracked statistic is strongly correlated between adjacent channels
($\rho_{\rm lag1}=0.989$--$0.991$, i.e.\ $8.5\times10^{4}$--$1.1
\times10^{5}$ effectively independent cells), so crossings arrive in
contiguous runs; collapsed to clusters on a kernel of 3 native channels
they leave far fewer independent trials, in agreement with the trials
expectation. Beyond the shared primary-beam gain, neighbouring
control positions are correlated below the synthesised-beam scale, so
independent spatial trials fall from \NCtrl{} to $N_{\rm
eff}\simeq30$--$43$, a floor to which the empirical \RateSbr{} per cent
rate, measured on the correlated ensembles themselves, is immune by
construction.

No single stratum drives the star-exceeds-ring rate. The stratification
was run on the $\le$20\,pc subset under the withdrawn asymmetric
statistic, whose counts it quotes, the operative symmetric rate being
too small (\NSbr{} windows) to stratify. Across band, channelisation and
primary-beam offset that rate moved only between 11 and 20 per cent, and
the disc-hosting caveat inverts empirically: disc- or planet-hosting
stars contribute every crossing window and every stage-1 flag but a
\emph{lower} star-exceeds-ring rate, 9.8 against 18.3 per cent for
non-hosts, so circumstellar emission shared by star and ensemble does
not drive the statistic. But that rate test is not the relevant one.
Every flagged window, and the crossing the statistic revision removed,
belongs to one population: $\beta$~Pic, AU~Mic and CP$-$72~2713 are
$\beta$~Pictoris moving-group members with debris discs and HD~48370 is
a disc host behind a molecular cloud. The flags concentrate where the
astrophysical foreground is richest, which is what the archive contains:
the searched sample is, to a good approximation, the ALMA debris-disc
and young-moving-group archive, the worst case for astrophysical false
positives at millimetre wavelengths, and the flag statistics should be
read accordingly. Excluding the \NBPWindows{} $\beta$~Pictoris windows
leaves one unattributed crossing among \NNonBP, against \ExpNonBP{}
expected ($P(\geq1)=\PgeOneNonBPPct$ per cent).

\section{Excluded entries, and the retired statistic}
\label{app:exclusions}

Five pre-freeze changes are logged with their effect on released
products in the repository change-log, the auditable record; the other
four are the threshold convention, the withdrawn baseline-operator
description, the line-exclusion width and the framing of the
conditional searched-domain transmitter fraction, each treated where it
belongs above. Only the replacement of the asymmetric region-max
statistic by the symmetric single-position form of
\S\ref{sec:statistic} (release re-run, criterion frozen) changes a
released number: flagged windows
\NSbrOld/\NWindows{} (\RateSbrOld{} per cent) become \NSbr/\NWindows{}
(\RateSbr{} per cent), and the AU~Mic crossing becomes unflagged. Under
a pure-noise null the retired region maximum over $n_{\rm src}$
positions beats single controls with probability
$n_{\rm src}/(n_{\rm src}+n_{\rm ctrl})=\AsymExpectPct$ per cent, the

orders of magnitude and sits a factor \DefectSibFacLo--\DefectSibFacHi{}
below the shallowest sibling of the same block and channel width, a

relabelled and no measured value changes. The supporting quality audit

(\ChiOriQaLo--\ChiOriQaHi$\times$ the Band~3 median, weakening their

\section{The conditional searched-domain transmitter fraction}
\label{app:population}

\emph{Illustrative calculation only.} Nothing here is a survey result,
and no number in it appears among the headline quantities of
Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}. It is kept for the framework, not for the
number it returns.

A zero detection constrains a fraction of systems only through a
per-system detection probability, factorised as
%
\begin{equation}
C_i(P) \;=\; F_i\;R_i(P)\;D_i\;C_{{\rm morph},i},
\label{eq:conditioning}
\end{equation}
%
with $F_i$ the chance the transmitter's sky frequency lies in system
$i$'s searched intervals, $R_i(P)$ the chance a transmitter of EIRP $P$
would have crossed that system's threshold, $D_i$ the chance it emitted
during the analysed archival interval, and $C_{{\rm morph},i}$ the
measured recovery of a carrier of the searched drift and temporal
morphology (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). The probability of zero credible
detections if a fraction $f$ of systems hosts one is
\begin{equation}
P(N_{\rm det}=0\,|\,f)=\prod_{i=1}^{N_{\rm sys}}\left(1-f\,C_i\right),
\label{eq:prevalence}
\end{equation}
and the one-sided 95 per cent upper limit $f_{95}$ solves
$P(N_{\rm det}=0\,|\,f_{95})=0.05$ over the \NSystems{} systems.

Everything below sets $F_i=D_i=1$ \emph{by conditioning, not by
measurement}, so $f$ is a conditional searched-domain transmitter
fraction and not a demographic occurrence rate. At EIRP
$\geq10^{16}$\,W the measured form gives
$f_{95}\approx\OccMeasuredRound$ per cent over the \NOccSystems{}
systems carrying a Class~A window, with idealised brackets \OccUnit{}
and \OccUniform{} per cent. Folding in the dwell-measured Class~B curve,
so each window carries the completeness measured for the morphology it
searches, extends the calculation to \NOccAll{} systems and moves the
number to \OccAll{} per cent
(\OccAllFifteen{} per cent over \NOccAllFifteen{} systems at
$10^{15}$\,W, \OccAllSeventeen{} per cent over \NOccAllSeventeen{} at
$10^{17}$\,W). Table~\ref{tab:occurrence} carries the ladder.

Three properties bound how far this travels. The Class~A recovery curve
is measured on one configuration (AU~Mic, 488-kHz Band~6) and applied to
all \NWinA{} Class~A windows, spanning Bands~3--8 and on-source times
\OnsrcMin--\OnsrcMax\,s; the transfer error is not bounded by the
$^{+\RecThreshHi}_{-\RecThreshLo}$ percentage-point binomial interval,
which is sampling noise within that one run. Scaling the curve by
0.5--2$\times$ moves the number from \OccMeasured{} to
\OccBracketLo--\OccBracketHi{} per cent, the honest width. The $C_i$ are
not independent: \NWindows{} windows group into \NEB{} execution blocks
with an intraclass correlation of \IccRank{} in the control-normalised
stellar rank, and the design effect moves \OccMeasuredRound{} to
\EffFIccPct{} per cent, an order below the transfer term. Under any
transmitter-frequency prior spread across ALMA's tuning range the bound
vanishes: the per-system Class~A union has median 1.73\,GHz against a
${\sim}75$\,GHz range, so the conditioning supplies the entire content
of the number.

Writing $p_{\rm epoch}$ for the chance a transmitter is on in any one
epoch, a system observed in $n_i$ independent execution blocks is
sampled $n_i$ times, so $D_i=1-(1-p_{\rm epoch})^{n_i}$; \NMultiEpoch{}
of the \NSystems{} systems have $n_i>1$. With at most three epochs at
year-scale separations this is a phase-averaged bound, and an
intermittent transmitter is at least as plausible \emph{a priori}, so
the $p_{\rm epoch}=1$ entries should never be quoted without it.
