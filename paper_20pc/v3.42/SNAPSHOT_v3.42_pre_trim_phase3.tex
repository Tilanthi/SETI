\begin{abstract}
We have searched \NWindows{} archival ALMA spectral windows spanning
\UnionLo--\UnionHi\,GHz (Bands~3--8) toward \NStars{} stars in
\NSystems{} systems within 40\,pc for unresolved spectral carriers,
comparing each stellar position with \NCtrl{} control positions in an
annulus centred on the star. \textbf{No credible technosignature is
found.} The sample is every star that usable public archival
observations cover, so it is ALMA-archive-complete within 40\,pc rather
than interest-selected, but it is not a volume-complete stellar sample:
it holds ${\sim}1$ per cent of the stars in that volume, over-representing
F and G types ($\times3.7$, $\times3.1$) and under-representing M dwarfs
($\times0.6$). Everything above ${\sim}115$\,GHz is essentially
unsearched elsewhere, and these are the first millimetre limits for
Barnard's Star and Wolf~359. Four windows raise a statistical screening
flag rather than a candidate detection: two toward $\beta$~Pic and one
toward HD~48370 are consistent with known circumstellar or foreground
CO, and one, toward CP$-$72~2713, is unclassified. The screening test resolves no
probability below $1/\RankFloor$, far above the survey-wide Bonferroni
scale $\BonferroniThresh$, so no flag is by itself significant. A
second-stage local null resolves past that floor: for CP$-$72~2713 it
gives $p=\LNpCP$, confirmed at $p=\LNpCPgev$ by an independent
extreme-value fit to the measured controls, about \LNCPRatio{} times
above the Bonferroni scale. Trigger thresholds, the powers at which a signal
first exceeds the screening test, span $\EirpMinA$--$\EirpMaxA$\,W EIRP
at \ChanLoA--\ChanHiA\,kHz native resolution over the \NWinA{}
drift-resolved Class~A windows, and $\EirpMinB$--$\EirpMaxB$\,W at
\ChanLoB--\ChanHiB\,MHz over the \NWinB{} unresolved-spectral-excess
Class~B windows; the frequency-matched benchmark is a 12\,m aperture
radiating 1\,MW at 230\,GHz, EIRP ${\approx}8\times10^{14}$\,W.
\NCoarse{} of the \NWindows{} windows (\PctCoarse{} per cent) carry no
drift discrimination. Two limits qualify every threshold quoted here:
ALMA's default Hanning response leaves \HanWorst--\HanBest{} of a
sub-channel carrier's power in the peak channel, so the thresholds are
optimistic by $\times\HanFacBest$ to $\times\HanFacWorst$; and
completeness for the drift-resolved class is measured on a single
configuration and transferred by assumption, with unbounded transfer
error. The detection statistic and the line mask were defined using
these data, so the rank probabilities and false-alarm figures are
descriptive of this dataset rather than an independent validation of the
pipeline's false-positive rate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A technological civilisation, signalling deliberately or simply going
about its affairs, may leave a detectable imprint on the electromagnetic
spectrum: a \emph{technosignature} \citep{Sheikh2020,SETIreview2025}.
Six decades of radio searches now cover many thousands of stars yet, as
\citet{Wright2018} quantified, sample only \HaystackFrac{} of the
multi-dimensional haystack of transmitter frequency, bandwidth, power
and duty cycle. Three gaps persist: the millimetre and submillimetre
regime above ${\sim}50$\,GHz is essentially unsearched; most surveys
target interest-selected classes of star, not the local stellar
population; and few systematically reprocess \emph{existing}
interferometric archives.

One methodological point belongs at the outset. The symmetric detection
statistic and the $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ molecular-line mask
were both defined, and the statistic once revised, using these data
(\S\ref{sec:aumic}). The rank probabilities and false-alarm figures
below are therefore descriptive of this dataset rather than an
independent validation of the pipeline's false-positive rate. That is a
property of this analysis and is stated again where it bears on a
number.

This paper addresses all three, asking how strongly existing ALMA
observations can constrain unresolved artificial spectral emission
from nearby stellar systems. In plain terms, what follows
characterises the stars ALMA has happened to observe, not the
40\,pc population as a whole: the searched subsample over-represents
F and G stars and under-represents M dwarfs (\S\ref{sec:sample}), and
every limit below inherits that bias. We take a sample that is ALMA-archive-complete within 40\,pc: every
star in that volume with public ALMA archival coverage, irrespective of
spectral type, disc-hosting status or known planets. We mine it for two
things, correcting for proper motion and Doppler drift: unresolved
spectral carriers, meaning channel-confined excess at native ALMA
resolution, and anomalous millimetre continuum. Throughout,
``narrowband'' describes the assumed intrinsically narrow transmitter
and never the resolving power of the data (\S\ref{sec:method}).
The processing sets the search class, and \S\ref{sec:dwell} measures
it. A continuous carrier is the best case and short-duty, fast-drifting
emitters the worst. Coarse-channel windows retain sensitivity to
persistent unresolved excess power only (\S\ref{sec:method}).
Three contributions carry the paper. The first is the systematic
exploitation of archival ALMA stellar fields: \NStarBands{} target/band
datasets mined from public data with no new observing time. The second
is the spatial-control null calibration for interferometric SETI, in
which the same statistic is evaluated at the star and at \NCtrl{}
control positions so that the false-alarm rate is measured rather than
assumed. The third is a quantitative accounting of the frequency, EIRP
and drift domain actually searched, which is what turns a non-detection
into a statement with edges. Ancillary analyses accompany the search:
closure-phase vetting, a continuum anomaly screen, a chirp and
periodicity re-analysis and a cross-target frequency-occupancy check. Of
these only the continuum screen enters a disposition; the rest are
capability demonstrations (\S\ref{sec:ancillary}). One worked case runs
through the paper. A crossing towards $\beta$~Pictoris, the largest
star-versus-control contrast here, is attributed to the disc's known
circumstellar CO across two transitions and three epochs
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). Per-target results are in
Appendix~\ref{app:table}; dispositions, false-alarm accounting and
injection completeness consolidate in Tables~\ref{tab:searchspace}
and~\ref{tab:flagged} with Appendices~\ref{app:falsealarm}
and~\ref{app:inject}.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Six decades of searching, from \citet{CocconiMorrison1959} and
\citet{Drake1960} through the targeted programmes reviewed by
\citet{Tarter2001} and Project Phoenix \citep{Backus2002}, divide into
wide-field commensal surveys and targeted programmes
\citep{Zhang2020,Czech2021,Morrison2023,FAST3IATLAS2025,LOFARdualsite2023,SRT2025,Tremblay2024},
with machine-learning interference rejection now central
\citep{Ma2023,Choza2024,GLOBULAR2025,AnomalyParkesGBT2025,BRaTs2026}, a
capability our classical threshold search lacks. Essentially all of it
lies below ${\sim}10$\,GHz. Above it the mm/submm regime has been
searched once, also with ALMA \citep{Mason2024}, despite physical
reasons to go higher: interstellar scattering and dispersion fall
steeply with frequency, a given aperture delivers a tighter beam per
watt of EIRP, and directional communication, radar and beamed power
transmission are already engineered coherent mm-wave emission, so
plausibility needs no assumption that anyone operates Arecibo-scale
transmitters at hundreds of gigahertz. The countervailing costs,
atmospheric transmission, receiver noise, photons per watt per hertz and
pointing and phase stability, price the search rather than forbid it.

\citet{Mason2024} searched archival Band~3 windows toward 28 ``stellar
bycatch'' stars, observed for other science and usable by a search for
free, placing EIRP$_{\rm min} > 7\times10^{17}$\,W for the nearest and
identifying the challenges of high-frequency work: drift rates scale
with frequency, smearing erodes sensitivity without a drift search, and
the molecular line forest confuses. Our pilot
\citep[][citation provisional]{White2026} extended that methodology to
TRAPPIST-1 across Bands 3, 6 and 7 (EIRP$_{\rm
min}\sim5$--$10\times10^{13}$\,W, no detection) and built the ranked
100-star list this survey grew from. Its pipeline is that design applied
to one target, without the archive-complete sample selection, the
control-ring calibration or the completeness machinery introduced here.
Nothing in this paper depends on that pilot: every claim attributed to
it below is restated from the products released with this work. New here
are the archive-complete sample selection (\S\ref{sec:sample}), the
\NCtrl-position control annulus and its local false-alarm estimation,
closure-phase vetting, the spectral-type-normalised continuum screen,
the chirp and periodicity extension and the cross-target
frequency-occupancy check. Each is new, to our knowledge, \emph{in an
ALMA archival technosignature survey}; we claim no wider priority over
their centimetre-wave counterparts.

A non-detection must become a quantitative exclusion statement:
\citet{Wright2018} formalised the ``Cosmic Haystack'' search volume,
\citet{Margot2023} its conversion into an upper bound on the
transmitter-hosting fraction, and \citet{EarthDetectingEarth2025} asked
where Earth's technosignatures would be detectable. That bound is valid
only if the sample represents the underlying population, which
interest-selected samples do not. Hence the case for an archive-complete sample.
Most surveys select by proximity, spectral type, planets or Earth
Transit Zone membership; the ALMA-specific form, disc selection, skews
the sample young and dynamically active. We instead take every star
within 40\,pc in the usable field of at least one public ALMA
observation (\S\ref{sec:sample}): an ALMA-archive-complete sample of
the observed subset of the local population in a fixed volume, not of
that population itself. This removes technosignature-specific but not
archival astrophysical selection; the \NCensus{} sample entries are
${\sim}1$ per cent of the catalogued population within 40\,pc (the
\NStars{} actually searched here are ${\sim}0.5$ per cent), and the
residual coverage selection is characterised as a completeness function
(\S\ref{sec:sample}, Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}).

\section{Sample selection}
\label{sec:sample}

The sample is ALMA-archive-complete within 40\,pc, which is not a
volume-complete stellar sample: it is every star within 40\,pc of the
Sun (Gaia~DR3 parallaxes, distances direct inversions $d = 1/\pi$; every
entry $\pi \ge 25$\,mas with $\sigma_\pi/\pi \le 0.8$ per cent) whose
proper-motion-corrected position at the observation epoch falls in the
field of view of at least one public ALMA execution block, not
necessarily at the pointing centre: mosaics and wide pointings
serendipitously cover more than one catalogued star. Disc, planet and
spectral type are descriptive metadata here, not selection criteria.
Throughout, a \emph{star} is one catalogue entry and a \emph{system}
the set of entries bound to it; a \emph{target/band} dataset is one
star in one ALMA band; an \emph{execution block} (EB) one archival
observation; and a \emph{window}
is one spectral window of one execution block. This gives \NCensus{}
unique target stars, processed nearest-first. Because trigger thresholds
scale with $d^{2}$ at fixed noise, that order is sensitivity-ranked
rather than an unbiased draw, and the prevalence statements of
\S\ref{sec:discussion} are conditional on it.

Composition follows from the archival selection and is tabulated
against the reference census in Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}. It rests on
\texttt{teff\_gspphot} as a spectral-class proxy, available for 52 of
\NStars{} sample stars and 49 per cent of the census and missing
preferentially for the coolest objects, so the M fraction is a lower
limit and the earlier-type ratios upper limits: of the searched stars
carrying a Gaia temperature, 41 per cent are M~dwarfs against 69 per
cent of the reference census,
while F and G stars are over-represented (Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}).
The archive prefers earlier types and nearby systems. The non-detection therefore bears on the ALMA-observed, F/G-enriched
mix of Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}, which under-represents the M dwarfs
dominating the local volume. That is a mismatch of astrobiological
priority rather than of statistical validity. Eighteen of the \NStars{} processed stars are confirmed exoplanet hosts
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}); the debris-disc hosts ($\beta$~Pic, AU~Mic, $\epsilon$~Eri, $\tau$~Cet)
contribute dusty continua, kept from masquerading as anomalies by the
continuum lane's spectral-type-normalised screen
(\S\ref{sec:method}). Window counts are likewise concentrated:
$\beta$~Pic (\ConcBpicWin{} windows), AU~Mic (\ConcAumicWin{}) and
TRAPPIST-1 (\ConcTrapWin{}) alone carry \ConcThreeWin{} of the
\NWindows{} searched windows (\ConcThreePct{} per cent) from
\ConcThreeSys{} systems, so any window-weighted statement leans on a
few heavily observed young, active or disc-hosting systems.

The \NCensus{} stars are complete with respect to, and conditional only
on, the 40-parsec reference catalogue intersected with the
public-archive snapshot of 2026 September~8.
Against that Gaia~DR3 solar-neighbourhood catalogue (17\,566 stars
within 40\,pc) they are ${\sim}1$ per cent. The two parallax cuts differ and
are not interchangeable: the sample requires
$\sigma_\varpi/\varpi\le0.8$ per cent, the reference census
$\le$10 per cent (Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}), so the ratio compares two
differently selected populations; the census is also incomplete at both
ends, and the fraction says only that coverage is small. Table~\ref{tab:selfunc} leaves the selection
function inspectable. Age and disc-hosting flags are not uniformly
available and are documented qualitatively.

Stellar counts throughout are catalogue-entry counts. The \NStars{}
searched entries comprise \NSystems{} independent systems, the
difference being seven designation-linked component pairs (SIMBAD
designations; per-entry rows, distances and unresolved-pair notes in the
machine-readable catalogue). The \NStarBands{} star/band datasets,
\NWindows{} windows and \NEB{} execution blocks therefore derive from
\NSystems{} systems, the paired rows being bookkeeping rather than
independent trials.

``Usable public ALMA coverage'' is a five-criterion decision tree,
frozen absent an erratum: (i) at least one public
execution block (EB) whose QA2-calibrated visibilities (QA2 being the
observatory pipeline's calibrated, quality-scored data products) cover the
star's epoch-propagated position in the primary beam, with no minimum
integration, sensitivity, antenna count, mosaic-tile count or array
configuration beyond QA2 itself; (ii) that position within one primary-beam FWHM of the field centre
(largest accepted offset ${\sim}0.7$ FWHMs, Sirius~B, retained with
bounded 3.6--16 per cent primary-beam corrections whose residual
systematic a holography-beam recomputation would settle,
\S\ref{sec:frames}); (iii) each spectral window admitted
individually, on the pipeline's quality vetting alone; (iv) no
programme-category restriction, science, calibration and observatory
projects entering on identical terms; (v) a fixed archive snapshot
date (2026 September~8). No
star or window was excluded by an unlisted criterion, none for short
integration (the shortest retained are 1.0 and 1.5\,min).

ALMA project metadata quantify pointed versus serendipitous coverage:
almost every spectral-window-covered star is the pointed science target in
at least one execution block, the exceptions a few blocks on another programme's field or
calibrator-only, leaving Wolf~219 the sole genuinely serendipitous star.
An archive-complete sample's residual selection function is the union of
ALMA target lists. That differs from an interest-selected sample in
degree, but the degree is \emph{quantified}: a bycatch fraction of
1 star in 44, 1 EB in 57.

\section{Data and methodology}
\label{sec:method}

The design is one shared archive-ingestion stage feeding two independent
search lanes; Table~\ref{tab:algorithm} gives the data path of the
primary lane in order. The sample is ALMA-archive-complete within 40\,pc, so what follows
characterises the archive's coverage of that volume and not the volume
itself. For each target we mine, within a bounded budget, all calibrated
(or locally recalibrated) archival execution blocks
covering the star's Gaia-propagated position, and search their
visibilities in (i) the \emph{primary} lane, a Doppler-drift-corrected
spectral-carrier search following \citet{Mason2024} and
\citet{Enriquez2017}, converting each non-detection into an equivalent
isotropic radiated power limit
$\mathrm{EIRP_{5\sigma}} = 4\pi d^{2} S_{\rm min}\Delta\nu$, with
$S_{\rm min}$ five times the searched window's per-channel rms,
primary-beam corrected, $\Delta\nu$ its native channel width and $d$ the
target distance (worked numerically in
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). The quoted thresholds are nominal.
Their systematic budget is the ALMA absolute flux scale (5--10 per cent,
band- and epoch-dependent), the primary-beam model at the stellar
offset, the instrumental spectral response (ALMA's default online
Hanning smoothing leaves \HanBest{} of a channel-centred sub-channel
carrier's power in the peak channel and \HanWorst{} for one on a channel
boundary, so every threshold quoted here is optimistic by
$\times\HanFacBest$ to $\times\HanFacWorst$, median $\times\HanFacMed$;
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}) and visibility calibration. Each
multiplies $S_{\rm min}$ and hence EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ directly, while
\emph{Gaia} distances contribute negligibly at these distances. The same
visibilities feed (ii) an \emph{ancillary} line-excluded continuum lane
(Appendix~\ref{app:continuum}). ``5$\sigma$'' throughout is the local
instrumental \emph{trigger} threshold of
Table~\ref{tab:nomenclature}, separately and empirically estimated
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). ``Narrowband'' is reserved for the assumed
intrinsic signal morphology and never for the spectral resolving power
of the archival observations: in the majority of windows an
intrinsically Hz-wide carrier is unresolved within a 15.625\,MHz
channel, and the data could not demonstrate that a one-channel excess is
intrinsically narrow. The search is accordingly reported as two formal
survey classes, distinguished by the dimensionless drift parameter
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\rm drift} \;=\; |\dot\nu_{\max}|\,T\,/\,\Delta\nu_{\rm ch},
\label{eq:etadrift}
\end{equation}
the channel-width fraction the \emph{maximum} searched drift
$|\dot\nu_{\max}|$ traverses over an integration $T$ at native channel
width $\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$. A window is \emph{drift-resolving} when
$\eta_{\rm drift}\gtrsim1$, only then carrying a carrier across enough
channels for its motion to be measured rather than assumed. The drift
ceiling inside $\eta_{\rm drift}$ is a boundary of the search grid, the
widest trial drift the stack evaluates, and not a physical prior on
transmitter drift rates: carriers drifting faster are simply missed, and
the resulting selection function is made explicit in
\S\ref{sec:detectable} (Table~\ref{tab:searchspace},
Fig.~\ref{fig:accel}).

\emph{Class~A} is the drift-resolved spectral-carrier search: the
\NWinA{} windows of fine channelisation, native widths
\ChanAMinKHz--\ChanAMaxKHz\,kHz (median \ChanAMedKHz\,kHz;
\ChanAModePct{} per cent at the \ChanAModeKHz-kHz mode), spanning
$\eta_{\rm drift}=\EtaAmin$--$\EtaAmax$ (median \EtaAmed). It is the
only class with measured end-to-end recovery of a drifting carrier.
\emph{Class~B} is the unresolved spectral-excess search: the \NWinB{}
coarse windows, \PctCoarse{} per cent of the sample, native widths
\ChanBMinMHz--\ChanBMaxMHz\,MHz (\ChanBModeN{} windows at the
\ChanBModeMHz-MHz mode), spanning
$\eta_{\rm drift}=\EtaBmin$--$\EtaBmax$ (median \EtaBmed). It is
sensitive to persistent near-stationary excess power and carries no
drift discrimination. Class~B is never described as a narrowband search
anywhere in this paper: the data cannot show that a one-channel excess
is intrinsically narrow.

$\eta_{\rm drift}$ is the physical criterion and the classes are
labelled by the instrument. Channelisation is bimodal, with no window
between \ChanAMaxKHz\,kHz and \ChanBMinMHz\,MHz, an empty gap of factor
\ChanGapFac, so any boundary inside the gap assigns identical membership
and figures label the classes by name. The two criteria do not coincide
exactly, and the overlap is quantified rather than smoothed over:
\EtaBgeOneNow{} long-integration Class~B windows reach
$\eta_{\rm drift}\geq1$, and only marginally (maximum \EtaBgeOneMax),
where a one-to-two channel traverse supports no discriminative drift fit
after baseline removal; \EtaAltOneNow{} short Class~A windows fall below
unity and are conservatively kept in Class~A. Each window's
$\eta_{\rm drift}$, $\eta_{\rm smear}$, trial drift count and maximum
searched acceleration $a_{\max}=\AmaxMin$--$\AmaxMax$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ are
columns of the released catalogue, so any reader may reclassify on the
physical criterion alone. Results are given per class first and combined
only where statistically appropriate (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}).

Both lanes exclude catalogued molecular transitions at tolerances suited
to their statistics. The continuum lane masks the common species at
native resolution before integrating; the carrier lane applies a
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame velocity exclusion mask
(\S\ref{sec:linecost}) to any threshold crossing. The mask is
\emph{excluded search space}: the unmasked survey covers
\UnionGHz\,GHz of unique sky frequency, the effective default quoted
throughout is the ${\approx}\EffBandGHz$\,GHz surviving the mask, and no
constraint is placed on carriers inside the excluded velocity regions.
Masking reduces the frequency space a candidate can occupy, not the
per-channel noise, so no threshold changes. All flux densities and EIRP
thresholds are total intensity (Stokes~$I$), formed from the two
parallel-hand cross-correlations the archive delivers; no cross-hand
($V$/$Q$/$U$) extraction or circularity test was performed. The
construction is polarisation-blind in amplitude, $I$ being the summed
parallel-hand power, so at fixed total transmitter power a strongly
polarised artificial signal gives the same response as an unpolarised
one to within cross-polarisation leakage. That is a statement about the
estimator and not about the data: polarisation-dependent flagging in the
archival QA2 calibration could have reduced sensitivity for a subset of
datasets, and the retained products do not record per-correlation
flagging, the extraction summing over the correlation axis with QA2
weights. We therefore cannot state what fraction of the processed
datasets retained both correlation hands unflagged through QA2. A
dataset that lost one hand would be $\sqrt{2}$ less sensitive than its
quoted threshold implies, and nothing here would catch it. Full Stokes
analysis would add discrimination against weakly polarised backgrounds,
not depth.

\noindent\textbf{How to read every limit in this paper.}
\emph{Every EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ value here is a nominal, local
instrumental detection threshold, the \emph{trigger power}
$P_{\rm trig}$: five times the per-channel rms of
the searched window, at that window's native channel width, for a transmitter at the star's position matching the searched
spectral morphology, with a measured recovery that rises with the
fraction of the track the carrier occupies its channel and is highest
for a continuous one (\S\ref{sec:ancillary},
Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). It is not a calibrated completeness
limit: completeness is the power $P_x$ at which recovery reaches $x$
per cent. $P_{50}=1.10\,P_{\rm trig}$ and $P_{90}>2\,P_{\rm trig}$ for the
calibration configuration's drifting class, both carrying an
\emph{unbounded} transfer systematic to any other configuration
(bracketed as $0.5$--$2\times$, Appendix~\ref{app:population}); for

\subsection{The search statistic, and the words used for its output}
\label{sec:statistic}

The primary statistic is formed on extracted spectra, not images or
visibilities. The extraction operates on the QA2-calibrated
measurement set as delivered: per integration and per native channel,
with the archive's QA2 flagging carried through untouched and no
spectral regridding or interpolation at any stage
(Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}, step~3); it returns the Stokes~$I$
intensity at the propagated sky position $\mathbf{x}$, formed from
the two parallel-hand correlations (\S\ref{sec:method}). Baseline
removal is applied \emph{after} extraction (step~4), and the
primary-beam correction enters the quoted thresholds and fluxes
(\S\ref{sec:frames}) rather than the extracted amplitudes the
statistic ranks. The estimator's visibility-domain weighting is a
fixed property of the released code (Data Availability), which
itemises the quantities consumed and emitted per window and is the
reproducibility reference for steps 1--3. For a sky position $\mathbf{x}$, each per-integration
spectrum is baseline-subtracted by a running median along the
\emph{frequency} axis. No temporal median is applied at any stage
(\S\ref{sec:ancillary}). The residual dynamic
spectrum is stacked along each trial linear drift rate $\dot\nu$, and the
statistic is the largest resulting single-channel amplitude in units
of the window's noise,
%
\begin{equation}
T(\mathbf{x})=\max_{\dot\nu,\;\nu}\;
\frac{S(\mathbf{x},\nu,\dot\nu)}{\sigma(\mathbf{x})},
\label{eq:tstar}
\end{equation}
%
where $S$ is the drift-stacked flux density in one native channel and
$\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ a single robust scale per position and window,
estimated as $1.4826\times$ the median absolute deviation of that
position's baseline-subtracted spectrum over all channels: local in
position, global in frequency. The baseline median's width $W$ is
likewise a single fixed channel count for all \NWindows{} windows,
independent of native channel width, so the linewidth ceiling it
imposes scales in frequency with each window's channelisation
(Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). The crossing channel is \emph{not}
excluded from the scale. That is standard for a median-based
estimator, whose 50-per-cent breakdown point makes a single-channel
feature immaterial over a window's hundreds of channels, and the
per-window local recomputation below excludes it explicitly.
$T_\star \equiv T(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ at the proper-motion-propagated
stellar position, and the same operation, drift grid, channelisation and
noise estimator give $\{T_i\}$ at all \NCtrl{} control positions.

\emph{The control geometry, in full.} The controls are placed by
\texttt{probe\_positions()} in the released extraction code, and the
offsets it returns are added to the star's own direction cosines. The
construction is therefore concentric with the \emph{star}, not with the
field phase centre. It is an annulus rather than a ring: \RingNProbe{}
positions drawn uniform in area, $r=\sqrt{U(r_{\rm in}^{2},r_{\rm
out}^{2})}$, and uniform in azimuth, from the fixed seed \RingSeed, so
the layout is exactly reproducible. Radii run
$\RingInFrac$--$\RingOutFrac\,\theta_{\rm PB}$ with $\theta_{\rm
PB}=\RingPbCoefCode\,\lambda/D$ evaluated at each window's own median
frequency, which scales the annulus window by window: across the survey
$\theta_{\rm PB}$ spans \RingThetaMin--\RingThetaMax\,arcsec, $r_{\rm
in}$ \RingRinMin--\RingRinMax\,arcsec and $r_{\rm out}$
\RingRoutMin--\RingRoutMax\,arcsec (Table~\ref{tab:ring}). A separate
inner check-ring of \RingNSrc{} positions, reaching
$\RingCheckFrac\,\theta_{\rm PB}$, serves the point-source test. Ring
centre, $\theta_{\rm PB}$, $r_{\rm in}$, $r_{\rm out}$, the control
count and the seed are columns of the released per-window catalogue.

The geometry is reconstructed from the code, not read from the frozen
products, so two checks establish that it is the layout that ran. The
inner check-ring it generates has exactly the \RingNSrc{} positions the
retired region-max statistic maximised over (\S\ref{sec:aumic}). And
where extended CO emission fills the beam, the released control
statistics fall with the reconstructed radius: Spearman
$\rho=\RingRhoCO$ at $p\sim10^{\RingRhoCOexp}$ in the HD~48370 Band~6
window, against a median $\rho=\RingRhoNull$ over the \RingRhoNullN{}
windows containing no crossing at all. A wrong position-to-index mapping
could not produce that.

\emph{What the geometry costs, and why the test survives it.} A star
near the pointing centre sits at primary-beam response ${\approx}1$,
while the annulus samples \RingGainIn{} at $\RingInFrac\,\theta_{\rm
PB}$ falling to \RingGainOut{} at $\RingOutFrac\,\theta_{\rm PB}$,
area-weighted mean \RingGainMean{} (on ALMA's actual
$\RingPbCoef\,\lambda/D$ beam the same radii give \RingGainInTrue{} to
\RingGainOutTrue). Star and controls are therefore not exchangeable in
flux density. They are exchangeable in signal-to-noise, which is what
the statistic ranks: primary-beam correction divides an extracted
amplitude and its noise by the same $G(r)$, so $T(\mathbf{x})$ in
Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} is invariant under it. That is the honest form of the
argument, and it is stronger than a bound on the gain differential.
What does not cancel is sky brightness, which is why a beam-filling line
raises the ring faster than the star (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). The
residual asymmetry is one-sided: a suppressed control can only make a
star-exceeds-ring outcome more likely under the null.

Neighbouring controls are correlated below the synthesised-beam scale,
so a window's annulus carries $N_{\rm eff}\simeq30$--$43$ independent
spatial trials, as the symmetric reprocessing runs measure directly
(Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}). The synthesised beam itself is not
carried by the frozen products, so radii are quoted in arcsec and in
$\theta_{\rm PB}$, and $N_{\rm eff}$ is the operative count of
independent controls.

Being global in frequency, that scale can misstate the noise
\emph{local} to a crossing wherever a window carries bandpass-edge
structure, atmospheric features or a line forest. The retained products
keep the per-window scale but not the per-channel spectra, so no local

excluding the feature itself, gives local-to-global ratios of 0.97
(CP$-$72~2713), 1.29 (HD~48370), 0.93 ($\beta$~Pic~B3) and 1.02

inflated, and no disposition moves. The retained products keep no
drift-stacked cube, so this tests the noise scale and not $T_\star$
itself, and a per-crossing local scale inside the search is an open item
(\S\ref{sec:futurework}). The same estimator runs at the star and at all
\NCtrl{} control positions, so a badly misstated $\sigma$ would displace
the control distribution, and the pseudo-star ranks are uniform in every
band and at both channelisations (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). The one
departing stratum is the fine-channel one, and that departure is the
four flagged windows themselves.

\emph{Why the statistic is symmetric.} The exchangeability that gives a
rank its meaning requires the identical operation at the star and at
every control. Maximising over an $n_{\rm src}$-position region at the
star while comparing it against single-position controls breaks that.
Under a pure-noise null the region maximum exceeds all $n_{\rm ctrl}$
single controls with probability $n_{\rm src}/(n_{\rm src}+n_{\rm
ctrl})$, which for the $n_{\rm src}=\RingNSrc$ check-ring and
$n_{\rm ctrl}=\NCtrl$ controls is \AsymExpectPct{} per cent rather than
$1/\RankFloor$. The inflation is a property of the estimator and holds
for any window and any noise distribution, so the released statistic
evaluates one position at the star and one at each control.
Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} is that form. An earlier version of this analysis
used the asymmetric region maximum, and \S\ref{sec:aumic} states the
chronology and audits every window the change affected.

no object here is a candidate.

The searched drift-rate range is the larger of (a) a generic
frequency-scaled default (12--13\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$), the
\citet{Mason2024} fiducial with a threefold safety margin, and
(b) for confirmed exoplanet hosts, the maximum line-of-sight orbital
acceleration of the innermost known planet, so a transmitter with a
short-period planet cannot drift out of the window. Drift rate maps
onto line-of-sight acceleration through the first-order Doppler
relation
%
\begin{equation}
a_{\rm los} \;=\; c\,\frac{\dot\nu}{\nu},
\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
%
so the ceiling is most compactly quoted in cross-band-comparable form
$\dot\nu/\nu$: 12--13\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$ is
$\dot\nu/\nu = (1.2$--$1.3)\times10^{-8}$\,s$^{-1}$, i.e.\
$|a_{\rm los}| = 3.6$--$3.9$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:drift}),
independent of observing
frequency. That is the acceleration at ${\sim}0.04$\,au around a
solar-type star. The default is a design boundary and not a claim that
faster drift is implausible. Earth-motion contributions
($\lesssim0.13$\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$) lie two orders of magnitude
below, subsumed by the grid. The grid itself is uniform in $\dot\nu$,
spanning the searched range in $n_{\rm drift}$ steps (2--1944 across
windows, recorded per window in the released file), so the
worst-case mismatch, a carrier midway between trial rates, leaves
a residual drift that traverses at most \DriftGridFineHalf{} of one
native channel over the longest fine-class track
(\DriftGridCoarseHalf{} over the coarse class): the mismatched
carrier stays within a single channel of its trial track throughout,
and the stacked-amplitude loss is bounded by that sub-channel
smearing alone, an order below the recovery slope measured between
$5\sigma$ and $10\sigma$. The injection campaign injected on grid
points, and continuous randomisation between them belongs with the
stratified campaign of \S\ref{sec:futurework}.
Very short-period systems are the ceiling's sacrificial edge. The domain is a
search definition, not a physical prior: an artificial emitter need
not be tied to a known planet at all (a free-flying platform, rotating
structure or deliberately chirped carrier can accelerate as it
chooses), and such emitters are unconstrained, not excluded.
These are \emph{apparent} topocentric drift rates, including deliberate
transmitted chirp and acceleration-induced drift, which the survey does
not distinguish, so the constraints concern linear apparent drift within
the searched range only. For circular orbits the ceiling covers a
transmitter co-orbiting an Earth analogue by three orders of magnitude
and Proxima~b comfortably, while TRAPPIST-1\,d, c and b
($a_{\rm los}=\OrbAccTrapd$, \OrbAccTrapc{} and \OrbAccTrapb\,m\,s$^{-2}$)
sit at or beyond it. Eccentricity raises the periastron value by up to
$(1{+}e)/(1{-}e)^{2}$, $1.9\times$ at $e=0.2$, so borderline cases move
outside the searched domain at modest eccentricity
(Fig.~\ref{fig:accel}).

Across the \NWindows{} windows maximum searched drift rates span
1.1--5.9\,kHz\,s$^{-1}$, native channel widths 15.3\,kHz--31.25\,MHz
(median \ChanMedianMHz\,MHz), trial drift rates per window 2--1944
(median 4). The threefold margin matters: at $1\times$ the search
would have been blind to two of its threshold-crossing windows,
including the AU~Mic crossing; at $5\times$ no disposition changes,
transmitters drifting faster than
${\sim}13$\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$ remaining unconstrained.
Intra-integration smearing is negligible almost everywhere: at most
1.10 channels, median 0.001; only three windows have $\eta_{\rm
smear} < 0.99$ (Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}), the worst the $\beta$~Pic Band~6 fine
window, whose effective threshold is 1.74$\times$ nominal. Channel widths are
effective resolutions, verified against the measurement sets' spectral
window tables (Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}).

\subsection{Frequency and velocity reference frames}
\label{sec:frames}

Every frequency axis here, searched spectra, tabulated ranges and
crossing frequencies alike, is \emph{topocentric} as delivered by the
ALMA correlator, with no velocity-frame shift applied to any data
product at any stage. Velocities enter only in the vetting stage's line
mask, whose conversion chain is in Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}. The
stacking is coherent in frequency and in the drift-rate trial but
preserves no electric-field phase coherence between integrations.

The native channel width is also the linewidth convention for every EIRP
threshold, which is the commonest confusion when comparing
technosignature limits across surveys. For a transmitter intrinsically
narrower than the channel ($\delta\nu_{\rm tx}\ll\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$),
channel dilution spreads its total line flux $F_{\rm line}$ across the
channel, $S_{\nu,\rm ch}=F_{\rm line}/\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$, and
triggering requires $S_{\nu,\rm ch}\geq S_{\min}$, so the minimum
detectable total power is $4\pi d^{2}S_{\nu,\rm ch}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$,
independent of $\delta\nu_{\rm tx}$ below the channel width.
EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ is thus the minimum \emph{total} power of a
channel-confined carrier and not a spectral power density; dividing by
$\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$ converts to the minimum detectable spectral power
for wider transmitters, so a $10^{15}$\,W threshold at 15.625-MHz
channelisation is $6.4\times10^{7}$\,W\,Hz$^{-1}$. The 1-Hz and 3-Hz
limits of Hz-resolution surveys are total powers in the same sense, each
the best case of its own channelisation, which is what makes the axes of
Fig.~\ref{fig:context} commensurable at all. Channelisation is the
dominant price the archive exacts, but we deliberately do not project an
Hz-resolution sensitivity: a radiometer-equation scaling
$P_{\min}\propto\sqrt{\Delta\nu}$ across six orders of magnitude in
spectral resolution is a thought experiment rather than an achievable
ALMA sensitivity, and quoting it invites the misreading this paper
avoids.

The spectral-carrier search covers every spectral window of an
observation, capped at 8 per target to bound compute cost. The cap bound
exactly one target/band, 61~Vir Band~7, four of eight windows searched,
and its frequency-space selection function is tabulated. Of the
\NStarBands{} star/band datasets, 53 have every configured window
searched; the remaining 3 ($\tau$~Cet, Kapteyn's Star, Van~Maanen's
Star) are represented by their deepest single window and are marked in
the released catalogue. Each searched window yields its own
EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ row, and Figure~\ref{fig:context} plots only the
deepest per star.

Velocity coincidence, not drift, is the operative discriminant when a
crossing is checked against the masked species: circumstellar emission
sits at rest offset by the gas's kinematics, whereas a technosignature
may appear at any frequency and drift rate. The searches as executed
used a narrower one-channel-width implementation, which agrees with the
velocity-space form on every disposition here (\S\ref{sec:linecost}).

Both carrier-lane thresholds and continuum fluxes are corrected for
ALMA's primary-beam response at the star's offset from the phase centre,
negligibly for most targets (median factor 1.0001, 90th percentile
1.012, none above 2 per cent). Two sit at large offset.
$\epsilon$\,Eri Band~6 lies at ${\sim}0.7$ primary-beam FWHM, where the
correction reaches $2.9$--$3.4\times$ and the Gaussian beam form fails;
rather than publish provisional values we \emph{withhold} those four
windows, which enter no number here but are released, flagged, in the
catalogue and return once a holography beam model is applied. Sirius~B
is retained and quoted as such, with corrections of 3.6--16 per cent
across bands, a bounded systematic rather than an invalid model.
Excluding it too would change neither endpoint of the quoted EIRP range,
nor the median ($1.3\to1.4\times10^{15}$\,W), nor any conclusion.
\label{sec:benchmarks}

The EIRP thresholds are compared throughout to an Arecibo-like planetary
radar \citep[][EIRP $2\times10^{13}$\,W]{Drake1974,EarthDetectingEarth2025},
which is a technological \emph{power} scale and not a matched
transmitter model. It is an S-band number, and aperture gain scales as
$\nu^{2}$, so the same aperture and transmitter power at 230\,GHz would
radiate ${\sim}4\times10^{17}$\,W. The frequency-matched benchmark is a
12-m aperture radiating 1\,MW at 230\,GHz, EIRP
${\approx}8\times10^{14}$\,W, within a factor of two of the median
Class~A threshold $\EirpMedA$\,W. Both are quoted, and neither is a
model.

\subsection{Ancillary and validation analyses}
\label{sec:ancillary}

Four analyses beyond the spectral-carrier search are executed here, each
summarised only as far as the main text depends on it. Only the
continuum screen enters a disposition.

\emph{Injection-recovery validation} (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}).
Synthetic tones of known amplitude were injected into real visibilities
and recovered with the unmodified pipeline: \NTrials{} trials across six
window configurations, three bands and both resolution classes. The
pipeline performs no temporal subtraction at any stage. Its baseline
step is a per-integration running median along \emph{frequency}
(Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}, step~4), which by construction cannot remove
a feature confined to one or two channels, and its de-drift step is an
inverse-variance-weighted sum over integrations, which accumulates a
persistent carrier coherently. One validation-history item is told in
full once, in Appendix~\ref{app:inject}: the campaign's original report
of 0 of 500 coarse-window recoveries was an artefact of its recovery
\emph{criterion} rather than of the search pipeline. The corrected
criterion has not been re-derived by an independently written
implementation, and that cross-check is an open item
(\S\ref{sec:futurework}). The independent evidence is the dwell campaign
of \S\ref{sec:dwell}, which measures persistent near-static carriers as
\emph{recovered} (Table~\ref{tab:dwell}), the reading the artefact had
reversed.

The primary statistic has no automated injection test on every pipeline
change, and three quantified checks bound undetected pipeline faults
instead: the machinery-only lane (fidelity $-9$/$+14$ per cent, the
bound that caught this artefact), the AU~Mic re-analyses of
\S\ref{sec:aumic} under three treatments, whose recomputation agrees
with the released products, and the pseudo-star rank uniformity
(\NPseudo{} pooled ranks uniform, mean \PseudoMean{} against 0.5, KS
$p=\PseudoKSp$), which any defect of the statistic itself would
displace. For the drifting class on the fine window, recovery is 17,
\RecThresh, 58, 75 and \RecTwice{} per cent at $4$, $5$, $6$, $8$ and
$10\sigma$, measured on the single calibration configuration of one
star and one Band~6 window and transferred by assumption to all \NWinA{}
Class~A windows, an error the bracketing of
Appendix~\ref{app:population} treats as a 0.5--2$\times$ rescaling. That
gives EIRP$_{50}=1.10\,$EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ and, as a bound,
EIRP$_{90}>2.0\,$EIRP$_{5\sigma}$, each with an unbounded transfer
systematic bracketed as $0.5$--$2\times$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:completeness}, Table~\ref{tab:classcomp}). Tables here
stay in nominal EIRP$_{5\sigma}$.

\subsection{Dwell-fraction completeness}
\label{sec:dwell}

The searched class is defined by how long a carrier occupies one native
channel, so we measure recovery against that directly. A carrier was
injected into the retained per-integration spectra of \NDwellWin{} real
windows, six coarse and six fine, at amplitudes of 2--20 times the
per-integration noise and dwell fractions
$f_{\rm dwell}=0.10$--1.00, six realisations each: \NDwellTrials{}
trials, released with one row per injection (Table~\ref{tab:dwell}). Its
detection criterion, recovered when the logged per-integration S/N
reaches five, was independently re-derived from the released rows: over
\XcheckN{} trials and \XcheckWin{} windows the rule and the logged flag
agree \XcheckAgree{} times, with \XcheckMismatch{} mismatches, highest
unrecovered S/N \XcheckFalseMax{} and lowest recovered
S/N \XcheckTrueMin, a clean separating gap. Recovery \emph{rises}
monotonically with dwell. Pooling amplitudes at or above $4\sigma$,
coarse windows recover \DwellCoarseOne{} per cent of continuous
carriers, \DwellCoarseQ{} per cent at $f_{\rm dwell}=0.25$ and
\DwellCoarseT{} per cent at 0.10; fine windows give \DwellFineOne,
\DwellFineQ{} and \DwellFineT{} per cent.

An end-to-end check confirms this through the unmodified pipeline rather
than a re-implementation. Injecting a zero-drift, always-on tone into
the calibrated visibilities of a coarse Band~7 window at 1, 3 and 10
times the per-integration rms and running the released extraction and
search returns $T_\star = 20.5$, $65.3$ and $217.7$, recovered within
one channel of the injected frequency and flagged as detections in every
case. For 639 integrations the ideal coherent gain is $\sqrt{639}=25$,
so a carrier at the per-integration noise level should reach
${\sim}25\sigma$: the measured 20.5 is that, less weighting and flagging
losses. Nothing is being suppressed.

Three distinctions discipline how these numbers are read.
\emph{Injection point}: the dwell campaign injects into retained
per-integration spectra, after the baseline step of
Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}, while the end-to-end check injects into
visibilities, before it. \emph{Drift discrimination}: on Class~B windows
every in-grid drift is sub-channel over the track, so the search recovers
the carrier's amplitude but carries no information on its true drift
rate, and no drift-rate claim is made for the \NWinB{} of \NWindows{}
windows in that class. \emph{The one cell measured only in part}:
through-pipeline recovery of near-static carriers on Class~A windows,
never yet constrained by an end-to-end zero-drift injection
(Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). The earlier claim that the pipeline
suppresses persistent carriers is withdrawn.

\emph{The campaign measures completeness at the survey threshold, across
configurations.} The trigger acts on the stacked statistic and the
de-drift step is an inverse-variance-weighted sum over integrations, so
a carrier of per-integration amplitude $A$ present for a dwell fraction
$f_{\rm dwell}$ of a track of $N_{\rm int}$ integrations accumulates to
%
\begin{equation}
a \;=\; A\,f_{\rm dwell}\sqrt{N_{\rm int}}
\label{eq:stacked}
\end{equation}
%
in units of the per-integration noise. Recovery is a function of $a$
rather than of $A$ and $f_{\rm dwell}$ separately, so binning the
released trials in $a$ unifies the two axes and places all \CompWin{}
injected windows on one axis. Those windows span
$N_{\rm int}=\CompNintLo$--\CompNintHi, a factor of \CompNintFactor,
over \CompTgt{} targets in Bands \CompBands{} and both resolution
classes, so configuration-independence is measurable here rather than
assumed. In the \CompFiftyN{} windows whose amplitude grid straddles the
trigger, the 50 per cent recovery point lies at
$a=\CompFiftyLo$--\CompFiftyHi{} (median \CompFiftyMed), which is the
$5\sigma$ threshold itself and not a value set by channelisation,
integration count or band. In the other \CompSatN{} the weakest
injection already stacks to $a=\CompSatMin$ and at least \CompSatRec{}
per cent of every injection is recovered, consistent but uninformative
about the curve's position. The pooled curves
(Table~\ref{tab:compcurve}) agree between classes within the sampling
noise.

\emph{What this does and does not license.} The injected carriers do not
drift, so what is measured across \CompWin{} configurations is the
persistent and partial-dwell, non-drifting class. On Class~B windows
that is the searched class, which therefore carries a survey-threshold
completeness curve measured on six configurations where before it
carried none. On Class~A windows the searched class is the drifting
carrier, whose curve is still the single calibration configuration of
Appendix~\ref{app:inject}; the fine column of
Table~\ref{tab:compcurve} constrains the near-static cell at the search
stage only, and neither replaces the drifting-class curve nor bounds its
transfer error. The campaign injects after the baseline step, so it
measures the search stage, the visibility-domain injection above
confirming the stage before it on one coarse window. And $a$ is a
derived combination, justified by the form of the statistic and by the
collapse of \CompWin{} configurations onto one curve, rather than
measured independently at every $(A,f_{\rm dwell})$ pair.

\emph{Drift-ceiling caveat.} The fixed $12$\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$
searched ceiling, line-of-sight $|a| = 3.6$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, is uniform
across every window: two orders of magnitude clear of Earth-rotation and

TRAPPIST-1\,b-like orbit. The limits of \S\ref{sec:discussion} are therefore conditional on it
(Figure~\ref{fig:accel}).

\emph{Closure-phase vetting} (Appendix~\ref{app:closure}). A one-sided
point-source test, unavailable to single-dish and beamformed surveys
because it needs multiple independent baselines. It is implemented and
simulation-validated, and at this survey's flux densities it has
\emph{no discriminating power}: per-baseline S/N never exceeds 5.1 in
the positive-control fields, so no flagged window could have failed it
whatever its nature. It is a proof-of-principle diagnostic rather than
an operative filter, and no window is described as having ``survived''
it.

\emph{Extended and cross-target screens}
(Appendices~\ref{app:extended} and \ref{app:freqoccupancy}). One
interference class deserves its own statement: satellite constellations
grew across the 2013--2025 archival baseline. No known ordinary downlink
allocation maps into the searched frequencies, but that excludes neither
harmonics, intermodulation products nor unconventional emitters, and no
exclusion claim rests on allocation alone. The primary defences are the
control ensemble and the time-domain tests. A satellite enters star and
control extractions alike within an integration, the channel-width
mismatch and the drift-stacked statistic dilute transient passes, and a
pass confined to one block and one target would evade the cross-target
occupancy check by construction. A near-field emitter need not share a
celestial source's far-field interferometric response, which is among
the reasons a dedicated per-integration RFI screen and visibility-domain
checks belong in any successor analysis. A chirped (quadratic-drift)
extension and a per-channel periodicity search ran on the 122 retained
spectra as a demonstration rather than a survey-grade screen; null
behaviour is as expected (star-exceeds-ensemble rates 10.7 and 13.1 per
cent against an 11-per-cent chance rate), no instance is a candidate and
no limit derives from them. Separately, crossings towards different
targets were cross-matched for recurrence at the same sky frequency, the
archival substitute for the ON/OFF cadence a single-pointing survey
lacks. Of the 366 star pairs with overlapping searched ranges, none
falls within the matching kernel, subject to one bookkeeping caveat in
the appendix.

\subsection{Data-quality exclusions and open items}
\label{sec:exclusions}

\NStarBands{} target/bands are searched to completion and reported here.
Four classes of entry are absent from the released catalogue and
itemised in Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}: (i) four process-level search
failures, each still carrying a continuum non-detection; (ii) one pair

defect; and (iv) one relabelled entry.

\subsubsection*{The coarse-noise defect, and the bound on its reach}

A reproducible defect produces implausibly small noise in a minority of
coarse-channel windows. The radiometer relation
$\sigma\simeq{\rm SEFD}/\sqrt{N_{\rm bl}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}t_{\rm on}}$
makes $q\equiv\sigma\sqrt{t_{\rm on}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}}\simeq{\rm
SEFD}/\sqrt{N_{\rm bl}}$ a prediction rather than a convention, so a
window far below the sample median is a bookkeeping defect and not a
fluctuation; we exclude such windows at $100\times$ below the median.
The failure mode itself is not identified: it is reproducible and
confined to coarse-channel windows. Six of \NWindows{} windows fail,
across five stars, and their nominal thresholds of
$2.0$--$7.8\times10^{12}$\,W would otherwise have set this paper's
headline depth; excluding them, the best is $1.6\times10^{13}$\,W.

The exclusion does not rest on the retained population being narrow,
which it is not: recomputed from the released products, $q$ runs from
$0.33\times$ to $96\times$ the sample median, a factor of ${\sim}290$,
with a secondary mode formed by the Band~8 windows (band median
$56\times$) and a factor-52 spread within Band~6 alone. The band
dependence is predicted by the same relation through SEFD, and
normalising $q$ per band would turn this screen from an outlier cut into
a physical comparison. The exclusion survives because the six defective
windows sit a factor $1.5\times10^{3}$ below the lowest retained one
across an empty gap of 3.2 decades (Fig.~\ref{fig:noiseqa}), any
threshold between $1.5\times10^{1}$ and $2.2\times10^{4}$ selects the
same six, and the load-bearing argument is one-directional: a
window-level deflation of $\sigma$ acts at the stellar and control
positions alike, so it can only over-state sensitivity and never
manufacture a star-exceeds-ring window. The same holds for a weaker
recurrence hidden inside the retained population. Whether the defect is
window-level rather than position-level is exactly what is not known
while the mechanism is unknown, and the check that would settle it,
whether the \NCtrl{} control $\sigma$ values in a defective window show
the same deflation as the stellar one, needs per-position noise records
the frozen products do not carry.

\NDup{} of the \NExtracted{} extracted rows are duplicate catalogue
entries for the same physical window, the same (star, execution block,
frequency range, channel width) key appearing twice in the archive
query. All are repeated archive products or alternative calibrations of
one execution block rather than independent observations, so one row per
key is the correct statistical unit. The retention rule is deterministic
and quantity-independent, the first-listed row of the key in the archive
query's own identifier order, so it consults no measured value and
cannot prefer a crossing or a deeper threshold. Most repeats are
identical, but two are not: two reductions of the same $\tau$~Ceti
Band~6 window differ by 44 per cent in threshold, and the two HR~1010
Band~6 rows straddle the $5\sigma$ threshold at 5.10 and 4.95, so the
crossing count is rule-dependent at the level of one window. The four
flagged windows are not, no discarded row being both a crossing and
star-exceeds-ring, and every row ships with its status. The audit is
otherwise clean apart from four windows of elevated system temperature,
and every sample identifier re-resolves independently in SIMBAD and
\emph{Gaia}. Item (iv) is a cautionary example for archival work: the
catalogue entry ``$\gamma$~Lupi'' resolved to the F4V debris-disc host
HD~139664, holder of the historical identifier ``*~g~Lup'', a
name-lookup substitution caught by inverting the pipeline distance and
recovering the \emph{Gaia}~DR3 parallax (Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}).

EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ trigger thresholds span $\EirpMin$--$\EirpMax$\,W,

\ref{fig:context}); as expected for a
volume-ordered survey the deepest are the nearest systems'
($1.6\times10^{13}$\,W for the UV~Ceti pair at 2.7\,pc), the shallowest

benchmarks of \S\ref{sec:benchmarks}, only the two resolved components

EIRP.

The statistics are presented strictly before the astrophysics, in three
named stages that do not promote one another. A \emph{crossing} is one
channel$\times$drift cell at the stellar position reaching $T\geq5$:
\NHits{} of the \NWindows{} windows contain one. A \emph{stage-1
statistical flag} is a window whose stellar statistic also exceeds all
\NCtrl{} controls: four do. That is a screening device and nothing more.
The smallest probability it can express is $1/\RankFloor$, two orders of
magnitude above the survey-wide Bonferroni scale $\BonferroniThresh$, so
a stage-1 flag is not a statistically significant detection. It selects a
window for examination. Each of the four is then carried past that floor
by a second-stage local null, described under (vi) below. The second
stage measures one thing: whether a feature exceeds the noise at its own
position. It is not a spatial discriminator, and in \LNNCtrlClear{} of
the \LNNWindows{} windows the control-ring maximum clears the local null
as well, so the first-stage star-versus-control comparison remains the
binding test for candidacy. A \emph{candidate} is a stage-1 flag
surviving every veto, and there are none. Of the four flags, three have independent CO
evidence, matched transitions, velocities and epochs at

\subsubsection*{Survey-level false-alarm calibration}

Each window-level test compares the peak signal-to-noise ratio at the
star's position with the same statistic at \NCtrl{} controls. The drift
grid and the thousands of channels apply identically to both, so the
per-window trials factor is absorbed empirically. The statistic is
symmetric: the on-source quantity is one propagated stellar position,
as each control is one position. Every number here derives from that
frozen statistic; the retired asymmetric variant is development history
(Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}) and contributes nothing. The
false-alarm probability has five components, in the order the procedure
applies them.

\emph{(i) The empirical spatial rank probability.} The per-window
significance quantity is not a Gaussian-tail $p$-value but an empirical
spatial rank probability under the control-position null: under
exchangeability one designated position of \RankFloor{} ranks first with
probability exactly $1/\RankFloor$, a rank statement independent of
whether the noise is Gaussian or heavy-tailed, so heavy tails cannot
explain an excess. The survey expectation is
$\NWindows/\RankFloor=\ExpFlags$ windows, and
$P(\geq\SbrN\,|\,\ExpFlags)=\SbrPobs$ under an independent-Poisson
approximation. That is a real excess over the noise null, for an
astrophysical reason: in \SbrAstro{} of the \SbrN{} windows genuine
celestial line emission sits at the stellar position
(Table~\ref{tab:flagged}), making the star rank first by construction
rather than by chance, so those \SbrAstro{} are not draws from the noise
null. Removing them leaves \SbrResid{} rank-first windows against
\ExpFlags{} expected, $P(\geq\SbrResid\,|\,\ExpFlags)=\SbrResidP$: the
CP$-$72~2713 window, and one towards Barnard's Star whose stellar peak
(4.40 against a ring maximum of 4.38) does not reach the trigger and so
is not a flagged window at all. Three levels stay distinct throughout: an
instrument and noise null, calibrated by the control ensemble; an
astrophysical-source null, addressed by line identification and
kinematics; and only then a technosignature interpretation.

\emph{(ii) The $\geq5\sigma$ amplitude requirement.} Only \NHits{} of
\NWindows{} windows contain any $\geq5\sigma$ crossing at the stellar
position, each contributing at most the exchangeable rate
$1/\RankFloor$. The rank, not the trial count, is the calibrated
quantity: a window's channel$\times$drift grid holds up to
$1.1\times10^{5}$ effective trials (Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}), more
than \NCtrl{} controls can resolve, so the control maximum absorbs
whatever effective trial count the noise realisation carries. Four
flagged windows exceed the budget
($P(\geq4\,|\,\ExpFlags)=\PgeFourPct$ per cent) with no tension:
excluding the three astrophysical ones, it need explain one
unattributed crossing against \ExpNonBP{} expected across the \NNonBP{}
non-$\beta$~Pictoris windows.

\emph{(iii) The line mask.} The velocity-space mask
(Appendix~\ref{app:linemask}) removes the velocity space of known
molecular emission from every crossing before that crossing can count as
unattributed. It is an analysis choice rather than a measurement: it
never lowers the noise-null budget above, and no disposition here rests
on the mask alone, the three CO attributions being kinematic
(Table~\ref{tab:flagged}).

\emph{(iv) The family-level pseudo-target Monte Carlo.} Each trial draws
one control per window uniformly from its annulus as the pseudo-star and
passes it through the operative gates, $T\geq5\sigma$ then the velocity
mask. The expected count of pseudo-target windows flagged by the full
procedure is \PsFlagMu{} (\PsFlagPzero{} per cent of trials flag none,
\PsFlagPgeOne{} per cent at least one). This is the prespecified
family-level procedure, one expectation carrying the threshold, the mask
and the ring geometry simultaneously on real data; the Poisson
expectation of (i) and the Bonferroni reference of (v) are checks
against it rather than independent claims. Worked through: \ExpFlags{}
expected flags from the rank test alone, the amplitude gate and the mask
together removing about three-quarters of that budget, and the Monte
Carlo landing at \PsFlagMu.

\emph{(v) The control-ensemble floor.} The controls can show a crossing
unremarkable but cannot certify one significant survey-wide. The
smallest expressible per-window probability is $1/\RankFloor$, two
orders above the Bonferroni reference $\BonferroniThresh$ over
\NWindows{} windows, and that reference is itself conservative because
windows share execution blocks. Authentication therefore falls to
localisation, recurrence and astrophysical attribution. We pre-commit
that no crossing will be advanced as a candidate on rank statistics
alone. No flagged window is promoted by any number in this account.

\emph{(vi) The second-stage local null.} That floor is a property of the
first stage, and the four flagged windows are carried past it. For each
we take a retained control position, standardise its residual by the
pipeline's own per-integration noise map, give every integration an
independent random circular shift in channel, and re-run the identical
search: same noise map, same weights, same drift grid, same channel
count. A rigid shift preserves each integration's spectral
autocorrelation, measured at \LNLagOne{} at lag one, and the maximum is
taken over the same grid, so the trials factor is the star's.
Independence across integrations leaves nothing coherent along a drift
track, so each realisation is signal-free by construction. Between
$\LNDrawsMin$ and $\LNDrawsMax$ realisations per window resolve
probabilities well below $1/\RankFloor$. The statistic was reimplemented
from the pipeline source and reproduces the published $T_\star$ of all
four windows bit for bit before any null was drawn.

Each null has to reproduce the distribution of the \LNNFirstStage{} real
control maxima first, those being genuine draws of the same statistic at
exchangeable positions. The criteria were fixed before inspection:
\LNValidLo{} to \LNValidHi{} per cent of real controls above the null's
99th percentile, and under 1 per cent above its maximum. Three windows
pass. HD~48370 Band~6 fails, and fails badly. \LNHDnAbove{} of its
\LNNFirstStage{} real controls, \LNHDPctAbove{} per cent, exceed the
maximum of $\LNDrawsMax$ null draws, and those controls sit at median
\LNHDRealMed{} against the null's \LNHDNullMed. The cause is physical.
The field carries extended emission and its imaging residuals, coherent
across integrations, which the scramble destroys by design. A
scrambled-noise probability there would be manufactured significance, so
we quote none for that window.

The two $\beta$~Pictoris windows act as positive controls. No draw
reaches $T_\star$ in either, giving $p<\LNpBpicThree$ in Band~3 and
$p<\LNpBpicSix$ in Band~6. A working statistic should return a very
small probability where a genuine localised astrophysical signal is
present, and here it does, on CO known independently. For
CP$-$72~2713 the value is resolved rather than bounded:
\LNnExceedCP{} of $\LNDrawsMax$ draws equal or exceed $T_\star$, so
$p=\LNpCP$. An extreme-value fit to the \LNNFirstStage{} measured
control maxima gives $p=\LNpCPgev$ independently (95 per cent confidence
$\LNpCPgevLo$ to $\LNpCPgevHi$), agreeing to a factor \LNCPAgreeFac.
Both sit about \LNCPRatio{} times above the survey-wide Bonferroni scale
$\BonferroniThresh$, and a \LNNBootGEV-fold bootstrap puts the chance of
the true value falling below that scale at \LNCPBootPct{} per cent. The
same fit places HD~48370 at $p=\LNpHDgev$ ($\LNpHDgevLo$ to
$\LNpHDgevHi$), unremarkable and consistent with the CO attribution.

Three limits belong with these numbers. A small local-null probability
means only that a feature exceeds the noise at its own position, and in
\LNNCtrlClear{} of the \LNNWindows{} windows the control-ring maximum
clears the local null as well. The retained products keep full dynamic
spectra for \LNNRetained{} of the \LNNFirstStage{} control positions, so
position-to-position heterogeneity rests on \LNNRetained{} draws; that
is what breaks the HD~48370 null, and it is a limit of the retained
products rather than of the method, removable by retaining more control
spectra. Parametric extrapolation of a null tail is not used for any
number quoted here, because for $\beta$~Pictoris Band~3 the natural fits
disagree by \LNTailDex{} orders of magnitude and one implies a bounded
tail and returns exactly zero.

\emph{Is the ensemble exchangeable with the star?} Exchangeability is the
central assumption of the statistic rather than a technical footnote:
the $1/\RankFloor$ expectation holds only if the star and its \NCtrl{}
controls are exchangeable under the null, which spatially varying noise,
sidelobes, correlated pixels or phase-centre-localised calibration
residuals could spoil. The primary-beam term is settled by construction
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}): the annulus is concentric with the star and the
correction divides signal and noise alike, so the ranked statistic is
invariant under it. The rest we measure by stratum
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ctrldiag}). Median per-window control maxima are flat
across bands, 4.46, 4.59, 4.42, 4.52, 4.63 and 4.69 in Bands 3 to 8, and
a Kolmogorov--Smirnov test of the star's rank among its controls against
$U(0,1)$ is consistent in every band ($p=\KsBandLo{}$--$\KsBandHi$) and
in the coarse stratum ($p=\KsCoarse$). Fine-channel windows sit higher
(control maximum 5.76 against 4.42), as their far larger
channel$\times$drift trial count predicts; the test is conditional on
the window, so that is not a violation.

For the stronger question, exchangeability of the ensemble itself, we
ranked each of a window's \NCtrl{} controls against the other
\NCtrl-1 by the statistic and add-one rule applied to the star, pooled
over the survey. The \NPseudo{} pseudo-star ranks are uniform to the
resolution the ensemble can express (mean \PseudoMean{} against 0.5,
$p=\PseudoKSp$) in every band and at both channelisations. Two caveats
belong with that number. The construction compares a control against the
other 511 plus add-one, a different geometry from star-against-ring, so
it cannot detect a centre-versus-annulus asymmetry. And the \NPseudo{}
pooled ranks are not independent, 512 coming from each window's single
noise realisation with $N_{\rm eff}\simeq30$--$43$, so the nominal
pooled $p$-values are optimistic. The relevant check is the two-sample
comparison of the \NWindows{} real stellar ranks against the pooled
pseudo-ranks, $p=\StarPseudoP$: the stellar position behaves like one
more position in its own annulus, and at the amplitude level the medians
coincide (\SymMedStar{} against \SymMedCtrl, $D=\SymKSd$). In
\emph{independent} spatial trials the ensemble delivers only
${\sim}30$--43, so the achievable resolution on a genuinely independent
null is nearer a few$\times10^{-2}$ than $1/\RankFloor$.

One stratum does depart, and is reported rather than pooled away. The
fine-channel windows fail the same $U(0,1)$ test at $p=\KsFine$
($n=\NFine$), containing four windows whose on-star peak exceeds their
own control maximum against 0.22 expected, which are precisely the four
flagged windows of Table~\ref{tab:flagged}. That is consistent with the
astrophysical line features identified below rather than with a
misbehaving ensemble, but the departure is genuine, so fine-window rank
probabilities are slightly optimistic. It is also localised: excluding
the 20 fine windows containing any $\geq5\sigma$ stellar crossing
restores uniformity ($p=\KsFineNoCross$, $n=98$). Two stratifications
the frozen products cannot support are per-control noise records and
per-control gain, and the empirical rank distributions bound the net
effect instead: expected flags \PsFlagMu{} against a rank-only budget of
\ExpFlags, with no excess in any stratum. The windows nearest the beam
edge are not silently affected. Sirius~B sits at
$r\simeq\PbSirRoverTheta\,\theta_{\rm PB}$, inside the annulus's own
radial span, and the four $\epsilon$\,Eri Band~6 windows are withheld
outright (\S\ref{sec:frames}). Constructing controls at equal
holography-model gain would remove the sky-brightness term as well and
is the natural next step. Remaining robustness checks, trials-count
reconciliation under channel adjacency above all, are in
Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.

\subsubsection*{The fully symmetric region-max check}

The SR statistic applies identical source-region, drift-rate
and frequency maximisation at the star's position and at each of the
\NCtrl{} controls under one search rule. The frozen release products
cannot meet that, so its domain is the seven locally reprocessed
windows: six validation windows plus the AU~Mic window's first-epoch
block. All give $T_\star=3.7$--$4.9$ against control maxima of
6.7--7.7, $p=0.81$--1.00. The star is exchangeable with its ring
wherever the test runs at full symmetry.
The single-position (SP) calibration above is therefore the
operative release-wide criterion over all \NWindows{} windows, the SR
statistic a validation subset; the two are never interchanged. The
machinery ships with the release and the full statement is in
Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.

\subsubsection*{Astrophysical end-to-end validation: $\beta$~Pictoris CO}

Two of the four flagged windows are towards $\beta$~Pictoris, a debris
disc with resolved CO emission \citep{Matra2017,Dent2014}: one in Band~3
at \FobsBpicThree\,GHz, \DnuBpicThree\,MHz from the CO($J{=}1{\to}0$)
rest frequency (\LabCOoneGHz\,GHz, \citealt{Pickett1998}), the other in
Band~6 at \FobsBpicSix\,GHz, \DnuBpicSix\,MHz from CO($J{=}2{\to}1$).
Neither coincidence is exact, so the automated one-channel check missed
them. Through the frame chain (Table~\ref{tab:bpicaudit};
Fig.~\ref{fig:bpicvel}) the offsets are one kinematic component,
\StelBpicThree{} and \StelBpicSix\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from stellar systemic and
\BpicPairSepKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ apart, in the two lowest CO rotational
transitions of a system with detected circumstellar CO. The unflagged
lower-resolution Band~6 window, from a third epoch six years earlier,
independently shows CO at \StelBpicCoarse\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the same
frame: all three epochs and both transitions lie within
\BpicSpreadKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of each other, deep inside the
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ mask. The Band~6 window carries 114
formal $\geq5\sigma$ crossings where a channel-confined carrier admits
one, and the retained products do not record whether those channels are
contiguous, so we quote the count and not a line width. Peak flux
densities of \FluxBpicThree{} and \FluxBpicSix\,mJy per channel, ratio
\FluxRatioBpic, are the right order for this belt's spatially integrated
CO but well below the optically thin LTE ratio ($\simeq4$), as
resolved-out flux in the finer configuration would predict; the
comparison with calibrated imaging is indicative, the two weighting the
resolved emission differently.

We attribute both crossings to this kinematically offset CO. The pair is
the survey's astrophysical end-to-end validation, a known signal run
through the same search the null results run through, and what it
recovers is the whole check list: the frequencies to the frame chain's
stated tolerance, the velocity concordance across epochs and
transitions, the localisation (the Band~3 stellar peak exceeding all
\NWindows{} control maxima and the Band~6 peak all but its own CO-filled
ensemble), and the drift class, zero to within the grid. A known line is
the more convincing end-to-end control precisely because nothing about
it was tuned. A three-panel figure of both features and their velocity
alignment would complete the record, but the channel-level spectral
products of those execution blocks are not carried by the frozen
release; Fig.~\ref{fig:bpicvel} carries the velocity alignment, and the
validation rests on the numbers above.

Their empirical significance against the survey background is
nonetheless extreme, so the attribution is no dismissal: the Band~3
region peak of 20.3 exceeds all \NWindows{} per-window control maxima
(add-one $p<0.0044$) and the Band~6 peak of 12.8 all but one, that
window's own CO-filled control ensemble reaching 15.2, the largest here
($p\approx0.0088$). In robust units, the stellar statistic minus the
ring median over $1.4826\times$ the ring median absolute deviation, the
standardised excesses are $\zeta=\ZetaBpicThree$ and \ZetaBpicSix, the
yardsticks against which the remaining flagged windows should be read.
The alternative needs a transmitter coincidentally placed at matched
offsets from two CO transitions in the same star.

\subsubsection*{Dispositions of the remaining flagged windows}

The third, towards HD~48370 in Band~6, is the largest excess here in
absolute and the smallest in relative terms: $T_\star=27.10$ against a
control-ring maximum of 26.83, the star exceeding its ring by one per
cent of the statistic. Its standardised excess, $\zeta=\ZetaHd$, is
near the CP$-$72 value below, though the amplitude and velocity
evidence differ. Star and ring rising together is what emission
filling the primary beam looks like, and the window sits
$-34.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ topocentric from CO($J{=}2{\to}1$). The
attribution is independently published: \citet{Cataldi2023} report
prominent CO($2{\to}1$) and $^{13}$CO($2{\to}1$) emission towards
HD~48370 at $v_{\rm bary}\approx41$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, well separated from
its $23.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ systemic velocity and attributed by them to
foreground cloud contamination; our window recovers it, and the
crossing is consistent with, and very likely caused by, that
previously reported foreground CO emission. Two internal corroborations
make this the most secure rather than the most fragile disposition. The
peak is $\FluxHdFour$\,Jy in one 122-kHz channel with the ring at the
same level: not a marginal statistical excess but a bright line filling
the beam. And the $^{13}$CO($2{\to}1$) window of the same execution block has a
control maximum of \HdThirteenRingMax{} against only \HdThirteenStar{}
at the star, so there is bright $^{13}$CO in the field but not at the
stellar position, exactly as extended foreground emission predicts. The
reconstructed annulus geometry (\S\ref{sec:statistic}) locates that
peak: the control carrying the maximum lies \HdThirteenOffset\,arcsec
from the stellar position, $\HdThirteenOffsetPB\,\theta_{\rm PB}$, and
the ten strongest controls all fall between \HdThirteenTopLo{} and
\HdThirteenTopHi\,arcsec against a median control radius of
\HdThirteenRingMed\,arcsec. The emission is offset and clustered, not
centred on the star. A third window is consistent: all twelve Band~8
windows here belong to $\eta$~Crv, HD~48370 and HD~61005 and are
[C\,\textsc{i}] $^{3}P_{1}$--$^{3}P_{0}$ (\MaskCIGHz\,GHz) tunings, and
HD~48370's carries a $5.05\sigma$ on-star crossing against a ring
maximum of 6.23. Its crossing channel sits \CIcrossKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$
from [C\,\textsc{i}], too far for the repaired mask to exclude it
(\S\ref{sec:linecost}), so it is dispositioned by its control ensemble
rather than by line identification. The coincidence is with
\citeauthor{Cataldi2023}'s quoted $v_{\rm bary}$, not an independent
frame-chain re-derivation on our side. It is also a
stated limitation of the spatial test: this window is identified by the
velocity coincidence and near-equality of star and ring, not the rank
statistic.

\subsubsection*{The unidentified CP$-$72~2713 feature}

The fourth flag, towards CP$-$72~2713 in Band~7, is the only one with no
astrophysical attribution, and it is marginal. The star is not anonymous
and the prior for an astrophysical explanation at it is higher than at a
random sample member: it is a K7/M0 member of the ${\sim}24$\,Myr
$\beta$~Pictoris moving group hosting a cold, dust-rich debris disc
detected in ALMA 1.33-mm continuum \citep{Moor2020}, the same
population and the same class of magnetically active star as AU~Mic.

\emph{What its own data show.} The feature is at \FobsCpSeven\,GHz. That
is worth stating precisely, because it is not the window centre,
\CpTwoFreqGHz\,GHz, quoted in earlier versions of this paper: the
crossing sits ${\sim}0.85$\,GHz below it, 35 channels above the window's
lower edge, which is also a reminder that a global noise scale is least
reliable near a bandpass edge (\S\ref{sec:statistic}). The local
recomputation there gives 0.97 for this window, so the excess is if
anything understated. Peak flux density at the crossing is
\FluxCpSeven\,mJy in one 488-kHz channel of a \CpTwoBwGHz-GHz window,
and two adjacent channels cross threshold. On the numbers,
$T_\star=\CpTwoTstar$ against a ring maximum of \CpTwoRingMax, a margin
of \CpTwoMargin: the stellar position ranks first among the \RankFloor{}
tested spatial positions, but \CpTwoMargin{} is the smallest margin
\NCtrl{} controls can express, a resolution floor and not a measurement
of significance. In robust units its standardised excess is
$\zeta=\ZetaCp$, two orders below the $\beta$~Pictoris controls, and the
margin is smaller than the scatter across this star's own windows, whose
other fine-channel control maxima are 5.16, 5.41, 5.67 and 5.85: its
ensembles reach 5.81 routinely where the star does not cross threshold.
Figure~\ref{fig:cp72ctrl} shows the crossing inside its window's full
\NCtrl-position control distribution, at the upper edge of an
unremarkable ensemble rather than beyond it, one draw of \RankFloor{}
against expected ring maxima whose median is \BootMaxMed{} and 90th
percentile \BootPctNinety; a ring maximum at or above \CpTwoRingMax{}
occurs in \BootPGeRing{} per cent of windows and at or above
\CpTwoTstar{} in \BootPGeStar{} per cent.

\emph{Two of its own windows bear on the astrophysics.} The flagged
Band~7 window covers CO($3{\to}2$) at the stellar velocity and the
Band~6 fine window covers CO($2{\to}1$); neither crosses threshold, so
we report a non-detection of circumstellar CO in the CP$-$72~2713 belt.
That is of independent interest to the debris-disc community and removes
the most obvious circumstellar reading of the flag. The nearest
transition of the masked species lies $\DvCpSeven$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ away
(radio convention; the relativistic convention gives $-1326$), and since
that mask is an automated contamination cut rather than a complete line
catalogue, we re-queried a wider catalogue, whose closest entry is
H$^{13}$CN($J{=}4{\to}3$) at $-195$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, still far outside any
circumstellar tolerance. The repaired mask of \S\ref{sec:linecost}
changes nothing here. The defensible reading is ``no plausible known
transition identified'', a statement about the catalogues consulted and
not a proof that none exists. Atmospheric or calibration spectral
structure near \FobsCpSeven\,GHz is controlled the way everything here
is: it enters star and control positions alike within the one execution
block, so it widens the ensemble rather than mimicking a stellar excess,
and isolating any telluric contribution needs the calibration products
beneath the frozen release. With a single execution block at this
frequency no recurrence test is available.

\emph{What is not done, and why.} Table~\ref{tab:cp72props} lists every
quantity the retained products carry. The rows marked $\dagger$,
polarisation hands, time and baseline splits, visibility-domain
localisation, calibrator comparison and synthesised-beam localisation,
all need re-extraction beneath the frozen products, which do not carry
it. We do not claim tests we have not run. A Gaia/2MASS/WISE
cross-match for an unresolved background source is likewise open rather
than performed: its catalogue half could list sources in the synthesised
beam, but without visibility-domain localisation it could not say which,
if any, the excess belongs to, so a null match would not retire the flag
and a populated match could not promote it.

We record the window as an unclassified stage-1 flag, a statistical or
astrophysical feature and not candidate evidence, with explicit promote
and retire criteria. The second-stage local null replaces the
$1/\RankFloor$ rank resolution with a measured probability,
$p=\LNpCP$ from $\LNDrawsMax$ draws on this window's own retained
dynamic spectrum, and $p=\LNpCPgev$ from an independent extreme-value
fit to its \NCtrl{} measured control maxima. Both lie about
\LNCPRatio{} times above the survey-wide Bonferroni scale, so the
conservative disposition of this window is measured rather than
asserted. \emph{Promote} only on recurrence at an independent epoch,
which a targeted ALMA re-observation at \FobsCpSeven\,GHz would test
directly. No such observation is scheduled or proposed;
Table~\ref{tab:cp72props} states what one would need to carry. An
enlarged control ensemble is no longer the route it was: the
extreme-value fit already estimates where a larger ensemble would land,
and that estimate is above $\BonferroniThresh$, so the excess would have
to grow and not merely be resolved more finely. \emph{Retire} on a clean
second epoch or on identification with catalogued emission. Until
either, it stays released and reported for what it is: unclassified, and
not statistically significant at the survey level.

\subsubsection*{Line exclusion: definition, repair, and cost}
\label{sec:linecost}

The searches as executed used an automated line check matching crossings
to catalogued rest frequencies within one spectral channel at zero
drift. The $\beta$~Pictoris windows showed that inadequate. Both fell
outside its one-channel tolerance, by 10.6 and 21.9\,MHz, while lying,
after the frame chain, within 3.6\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of the stellar systemic
of a resolved, mapped disc, which is exactly the regime a channel-width
tolerance is blind to. Every crossing was therefore reclassified with a
velocity-space mask, $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the stellar frame
around each catalogued transition. Its kinematic justification, its
empirical conservatism and the counter-argument that a communicator
might deliberately choose these natural reference frequencies are in
Appendix~\ref{app:linemask}. The mask is applied here
\emph{retrospectively}, as a post-hoc classification criterion rather
than a detection threshold: the half-width was adopted after the
$\beta$~Pictoris crossings had been seen. A sensitivity test over
half-widths, reclassifying all \NHits{} crossings at $\pm20$, $\pm30$,
$\pm50$ and $\pm100$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, masks \MaskBWTwenty, \MaskBWThirty,
\MaskBWFifty{} and \MaskBWHundred{} per cent of gross bandwidth and
admits no new unattributed flag at any width. At every width the only
unmasked unattributed flag is CP$-$72~2713. Masked regions are reported
throughout as \emph{excluded search space} and never as
searched-and-empty.

\emph{The mask as executed had four defects; all four are repaired
here.} It carried rest frequencies rounded to \MaskRestRoundMHz\,MHz,
worth up to \MaskRoundWorstKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$; it carried no atomic
carbon, although every Band~8 window in the survey is a
[C\,\textsc{i}] $^{3}P_{1}$--$^{3}P_{0}$ tuning; it carried no hydrogen
recombination line, although H30$\alpha$ falls in the standard Band~6
tuning of much of the sample; and it was applied in the stellar frame
only, which is the wrong frame for the Galactic foreground emission
behind one of the three line attributions. The repaired catalogue holds
\MaskNTransNew{} transitions of \MaskNSpecNew{} species at full JPL/CDMS
precision \citep{Pickett1998,CDMS2005}, including [C\,\textsc{i}] at
\MaskCIGHz\,GHz and H30$\alpha$ at \MaskHthirtyGHz\,GHz, against
\MaskNTransOld{} of \MaskNSpecOld{} before, and it is applied in the
LSR frame as well as the stellar frame. The species count also
reconciles the text with the products, which always used
\MaskNSpecOld{} species: earlier versions of this paper said eight,
omitting H$_2$CO.

Repairing a mask is cheap; re-running the survey is not. The repaired
mask is therefore re-applied to the frozen crossing list as a post-hoc
filter, using each crossing channel's own frequency as the pipeline
recorded it, and the result is stated explicitly because
it is what a reader needs. \textbf{No disposition changes.} The two
$\beta$~Pictoris crossings remain inside the stellar-frame tube, at
\StelBpicThree{} and \StelBpicSix\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from CO($1{\to}0$) and
CO($2{\to}1$). HD~48370's crossing remains inside it at
$-17.8$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and is now caught by the LSR-frame tube as well,
at $-23.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, which is the frame its foreground
attribution always implied. CP$-$72~2713's crossing lies
$\DvCpSeven$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from the nearest transition in the topocentric
frame and $-1300$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the LSR frame, so it is excluded by
no tube in any frame and stays unclassified. Across all \NCrossFreq{}
crossing windows the repaired catalogue moves the nearest transition in
exactly \MaskNewCoinc{} case, \CIcrossStar's Band~8 crossing at
\CIcrossGHz\,GHz, whose nearest entry changes from CO($4{\to}3$)
\CIcrossOldKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ away to [C\,\textsc{i}] at
\CIcrossKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$. That is still outside the tube, and the frame
corrections are at most a few tens of km\,s$^{-1}$, so it is not masked
in any frame either; it was never a flag, its own control ensemble
exceeding it. The two new transitions cost a further
\MaskExtraGHz\,GHz of unique searched frequency,
\MaskExtraPct{} per cent.

The masking cost uses one definition: every JPL catalogued transition of
the \MaskNSpecOld{} frozen species (276), displaced by each system's radial velocity,
$\pm50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame half-width, intersected with each
window and merged. Framed on the unique-frequency union, the survey
holds \UnionGHz\,GHz of archival spectral coverage, of which
\SearchedUnionGHz\,GHz is included in the primary technosignature
test and \MaskUnionLostGHz\,GHz (\MaskUnionPct{} per cent) is
deliberately excluded because of molecular-line confusion
(Figure~\ref{fig:waterfall}). That loss of possibly interesting
parameter space is accepted by design and is not a contamination
afterthought.
On the window-summed gross bandwidth the same mask removes
\MaskGrossLostGHz\,GHz of \GrossBwGHz\,GHz (\MaskGrossPct{} per cent),
the per-window usable fraction having median 0.960 (10th percentile
0.790). CN and CO dominate the loss, and the per-species, per-band
breakdown ships in the released veto-mask export. The exposure accounting runs as follows. Gross bandwidth is
\GrossBwGHz\,GHz (Class~A \ExpFineBwPct{} per cent, Class~B
\ExpCoarseBwPct{} per cent), the unique sky-frequency union
\UnionGHz\,GHz (Class~A \ExpUnFine\,GHz), the summed per-star union
371.9\,GHz, and on-source$\times$bandwidth $\ExposureSGHz$\,s\,GHz, or
\ExpoTotalSHG{} star-hour-GHz. The frequency-exposure curve
$C(\nu)=\sum_i t_i(\nu)$ has covered-bin median \ExpoMedSH{} and peak
\ExpoPeakSH{} star-hours in 2-GHz bins, and the archive's tuning
concentrations put \ExpoTopPct{} per cent of all exposure in the five
best-populated bins, which any frequency-prior calculation must weight
accordingly. The mask
governs dispositions, not the noise-derived thresholds, so the quoted
EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ values are unchanged; no signal was found outside the
excluded velocity regions in any window.

The mask is a design selection effect: a signal coincident with a
molecular line is not thereby ``false'' but \emph{excluded from the
technosignature search domain}, the astrophysical prior there being
overwhelming and this pipeline unable to separate the two. We place no
constraint on transmitters in the excluded regions, \MaskUnionPct{} per
cent of the unique-frequency union. Masked channels are retained rather
than discarded, for a line-coincident search with independent
discrimination. Nor is the exposure confined to
the flagged windows: 131 of \NWindows{} windows (30 per cent) contain an
in-band catalogued transition of the masked species, as do all four
flagged windows, yet in none does a crossing fall within one
native channel of a rest frequency, which is precisely the regime the
velocity-space criterion covers.

\subsection{Changing the detection statistic, and the AU~Mic crossing}
\label{sec:aumic}

The estimator is justified on its own terms in \S\ref{sec:statistic},
from the exchangeability the rank test needs and independently of any
dataset. The chronology is this. An earlier region-max statistic
maximised over an $n_{\rm src}=\RingNSrc$-position region centred on the
star against \NCtrl{} single-position controls, and under it a crossing
towards AU~Mic in Band~6 at 230.83\,GHz was carried as a candidate; the
asymmetry was then identified algebraically, the symmetric form of
Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} was substituted, the whole analysis was re-run and
the criterion frozen; only afterwards was any flagged window, AU~Mic's
included, inspected under the new criterion. We state that plainly
because the revision removed the one candidate it was not designed to
remove, and a reader is entitled to weigh the sequence.

What the sequence can be checked against is Table~\ref{tab:bothstats},
which audits every window the original statistic flagged rather than
the AU~Mic window alone. The symmetric criterion flags a strict

ALMA~J1537$-$3319 and HD~14055 drop out, in all three cases because the

rank test, AU~Mic fails on the same evidence: 10 of its \NCtrl{}

was settled on these data, the false-alarm calibration here is

\subsection{Ancillary results}
\label{sec:ancillaryresults}

Three ancillary results are reported in full in the appendices.

\emph{Integration-time audit} (machine-readable release). Window-level
on-source records for 227 of the \NWindows{} windows were checked
against the ALMA archive's own exposure accounting. Two of the 58
star--execution-block groups over-count, AT~Mic by a factor 1.43 and
CD$-$38~10980 by 7 per cent, and the rest are conservative. No EIRP
threshold depends on integration time, every one deriving from the
measured per-channel rms, so no disposition changes and the
exposure-weighted coverage of \S\ref{sec:technosearch} is at worst
optimistic by under 1 per cent.

\emph{Continuum search} (Appendix~\ref{app:contsearch}). An
exploratory by-product over the 57 target/bands processed far enough
for a usable measurement: seventeen $\geq5\sigma$ detections, forty
non-detections reaching 0.036--3.6\,mJy (median 0.52\,mJy); every
detection is astrophysically unsurprising, the auroral emitter
LSR~J1835$+$3259 meriting deeper follow-up. No technosignature
disposition rests on it.

\emph{Serendipitous molecular-line catalogue}
(Appendix~\ref{app:continuum}). The line-exclusion step identifies every
$\geq5\sigma$ outlier channel as a by-product; across the subset scanned, twelve of the outlier groups are single- or
few-channel features with no known transition within 300\,MHz and six
are plausibly real, all reported openly.




\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What signals could ALMA archival data actually detect?}
\label{sec:detectable}

Three concrete cases, one per measured morphology, with real windows and
the completeness entries of Table~\ref{tab:classcomp}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sens2d} maps every window). The denominators count the
\NSystems{} systems whose deepest threshold, in the class that case is
about, lies at or below the case power.

\emph{(i) A continuous, frequency-stationary carrier} in a coarse
channel is the best case rather than the worst. Toward the UV~Ceti pair
(2.7\,pc, deepest window EIRP$_{5\sigma}=1.6\times10^{13}$\,W) a carrier
of that power confined to its channel is recovered at essentially the
ideal $\sqrt{N}$ gain (\S\ref{sec:dwell}), so an unresolved continuous
carrier matching the \emph{total} EIRP of an Arecibo-like planetary
radar is detectable toward the nearest systems even at 15.625-MHz
resolution. That is a power-scale comparison and not a simulated radar
(\S\ref{sec:benchmarks}). \CaseDetCoarseNow{} system of \NSystems{}
qualifies, \CaseDetCoarseTwoNow{} at twice the power.

\emph{(ii) A continuously drifting carrier} in a fine 488-kHz channel
toward $\tau$~Ceti (3.65\,pc, threshold $3.8\times10^{13}$\,W) is
recovered \RecThresh{} per cent of the time at threshold for
$f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$ and $75$--$83$ per cent at $1.6$--$2\times$
threshold (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). A drifting transmitter above
${\sim}8\times10^{13}$\,W is therefore recovered in \RecTwice{} per cent
of trials wherever its drift stays inside the searched ceiling;
\CaseDetFineNow{} systems reach that depth in Class~A.

\emph{(iii) An intermittent carrier, on for a quarter of the track},
toward HD~10647 (17.4\,pc, $1.5\times10^{14}$\,W) is recovered
\DwellFineQ{} per cent of the time in fine channels and \DwellCoarseQ{}
per cent in coarse ones, so a duty-$1/4$ transmitter above
${\sim}2\times10^{14}$\,W is detected toward such mid-distance systems,
\CaseDetDwellNow{} of them. Smaller dwell fractions degrade rapidly
(Table~\ref{tab:dwell}), and below $f_{\rm dwell}\sim0.1$ only the
nearest windows carry information.

\subsection{What does the null result mean?}
\label{sec:null}

No credible technosignature has been found. The EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ values
of the released catalogue are the nominal powers at which a matching
transmitter enters the detection pipeline (boxed rule,
\S\ref{sec:method}), and completeness-calibrated exclusion holds only
where the measured recovery curve applies, which is Class~A. The sample
is ALMA-archive-complete within 40\,pc and processed nearest-first, so it
is sensitivity-ranked rather than an unbiased draw. The flagged-window
dispositions are established in \S\ref{sec:technosearch}.

One exclusion inside the searched space deserves the converse emphasis.
The line mask removes $\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame tubes
around the catalogued transitions, and those rest frequencies are
exactly the `magic' channels \citet{CocconiMorrison1959} argued a
communicator might choose as natural reference points. This survey is
blind by construction to a transmitter parked there in the stellar
frame, a false-positive control purchased with \MaskGrossPct{} per cent
of gross bandwidth and one physically motivated class of signal. Of the
\NExoPlanets{} planets known around the \NExoHosts{} planet-hosting
targets, only TRAPPIST-1\,b reaches the drift ceiling in its edge-on worst case,
on star--planet relative accelerations $GM_\star/r^{2}$ from NASA
Exoplanet Archive planetary-companion parameters accessed 2026
September~9, maximised over orbital phase and inclination.
Figure~\ref{fig:accel} shows the ceiling as an explicit
selection function of planet period and host mass; the host-mass
dependence is weak ($a\propto M_\star^{1/3}$ at fixed period), so it is
effectively a function of orbital period alone.
Table~\ref{tab:searchspace} consolidates the axes on which a transmitter
is or is not detectable and Table~\ref{tab:perband} decomposes the
survey by ALMA band. Coverage eligibility and search completeness
differ: the archive of public usable ALMA data (\S\ref{sec:sample}) sets
eligibility, and every held execution block in scope was searched, so no
EB-level selection lies behind the \NEB{}. A proper-motion-matched ALMA
archive audit (2026 September~9) measures availability directly rather
than inheriting it from bookkeeping, counting 107 unique public on-star
execution blocks and re-finding all but one searched block. The
selection function is Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}.

The survey sits against \citet{Wright2018}'s published haystack axis by
axis. On frequency (10\,MHz--115\,GHz) only Band~3 falls inside, 20.6 of
115\,GHz, 0.18 of the axis, and on it Hz-resolution surveys reach far
more finely; the remaining 66.6\,GHz (Bands~4--8, 125--495\,GHz) lies
wholly outside, and is the genuinely new territory. On transmitted
bandwidth (0--20\,MHz) a native-channel search probes a fraction set by
channel width (window-mean 0.63, median 0.78). On sensitivity the median
threshold (\SminMedian\,mJy across a 15.625-MHz channel,
$\phi\approx1.0\times10^{-21}$\,W\,m$^{-2}$) detects a $10^{13}$\,W
transmitter only within ${\sim}0.9$\,pc. Against the sole prior
ALMA-archival search \citep[][28 bycatch targets at $\geq$1.01\,kpc,
deepest EIRP$_{\rm min}$ $6.91\times10^{17}$\,W]{Mason2024}, the
\NSystems{} systems here, \NSysInTwentyNow{} of them inside 20\,pc,
reach median per-target thresholds ${\sim}\MasonRatio\times$ deeper.
These fractions are not multiplied into a combined coverage: the axes
are strongly coupled, distance and sensitivity above all, so a product
of marginals would misstate the overlap by orders of magnitude.

What the zero detection bounds is fully conditioned: on each target's
own searched frequencies, on the epochs observed and on the searched
morphology class. Turning it into a fraction of systems requires a
per-system detection probability, and that quantity is not a demographic
occurrence rate. We call it the \emph{conditional searched-domain
transmitter fraction} throughout, never ``occurrence'' and never
``transmitter fraction'' unqualified, and we keep its evaluation out of
the interpretive argument: the framework, the numerical demonstration
and its sensitivity analysis are in Appendix~\ref{app:population},
labelled \emph{illustrative calculation only}. They state no survey
result and appear among no headline quantity
(Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}). The result of this paper is the search
sensitivity, the exposure and the null. The reason for the demotion is
measured rather than rhetorical: completeness for the drift-resolved
class rests on one injected configuration, and the error of transferring
it to the other \NWinA{} Class~A windows is not bounded, so a
factor-of-two systematic would rescale every fraction derived from it.

For the broader reader, the plain statement is this. As a by-product of
other science, ALMA has stared at \NStars{} of the nearest stars across
parts of the millimetre and submillimetre band that no technosignature
search had examined. We looked in those data for a narrow,
artificial-looking spectral line at each star, and found none the
surrounding sky could not also produce. The powers we could have seen
are enormous by human standards, and we could look only in the few
gigahertz each star happened to be observed in, during the hours it
happened to be observed. A non-detection on those terms says little
about how common transmitters are, and a good deal about how to search
an archive for them.

Because selection is on ALMA archival coverage alone, useful subsamples
fall out without compromising the archive-complete framing, subject to
the unresolved-pair caveat of \S\ref{sec:sample}. \NExoHosts{} of the
\NStars{} processed stars (\NExoPlanets{} known planets) are confirmed
exoplanet hosts, among them Proxima~Centauri, $\tau$~Ceti,
$\epsilon$~Eridani, $\beta$~Pictoris and the seven-planet TRAPPIST-1
system; HN~Lib and LHS~1140, each with a habitable-zone planet, now
carry an ALMA technosignature non-detection beside their planet-search
literature. Four searched stars are white dwarfs
\citep{GentileFusillo2021}: Sirius~B, Van~Maanen's Star, Wolf~219 and
CD$-$38~10980, all continuum non-detections with nominal
EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ trigger thresholds of
$2.3\times10^{13}$--$8.4\times10^{14}$\,W, among the few mm/submm SETI
constraints for white dwarfs \citep{PollutedWD2026}.

\subsection{ALMA as a technosignature instrument: strengths and limits}
\label{sec:instrument}

ALMA's advantages for this programme are four. Aperture and receiver
temperature give mm-wave sensitivities no single dish reaches. The
tuning range, 125--495\,GHz here, is essentially unsearched territory.
Interferometric spatial discrimination, the control statistic of
\S\ref{sec:statistic}, tests a feature's position against \NCtrl{}
alternatives, which single-dish and beamformed searches cannot. And the
archive supplies \NEB{} public execution blocks re-processable without
new allocations. Each strength has its mirror. Native channelisation
reaches 15.3\,kHz at best, orders coarser than the Hz resolution of
dedicated searches, so the search tests unresolved spectral excess and
not fine structure; coverage is fragmented into \UnionIntervals{}
frequency islands weighted by other science; the line mask deliberately
excludes \MaskUnionPct{} per cent of the union; configurations are
heterogeneous, so no completeness transfers between them; and targeting
is interest-driven rather than uniform (\S\ref{sec:sample}).

The largest unused advantage is the visibilities themselves. ALMA can
test whether an apparent spectral feature carries the interferometric
phase signature of a celestial point source at a known position. For
each surviving candidate one would construct competing likelihoods:
$H_{\star}$, a source at the propagated stellar position,
$V_{ij}=A(t,\nu)\,e^{-2\pi i(u_{ij}l_\star+v_{ij}m_\star)}$; $H_{\rm
sky}$, a source elsewhere; $H_{\rm common}$, correlated or common-mode
interference; and $H_{\rm noise}$, thermal and calibration residual, and
evaluate $\Lambda=\mathcal L(V|H_\star)/\mathcal L(V|H_{\rm
noise}{+}H_{\rm common})$. This uses information single-dish and
beamformed searches do not possess, is strictly more powerful than
ranking a peak against a control ensemble, and would reposition the
control ensemble as a calibration layer rather than the primary
authentication statistic. The extracted-spectrum search here is its
amplitude-only prologue.

\subsection{A next-generation ALMA technosignature search}
\label{sec:futurework}

Four changes would enlarge what this experiment can say, ranked by how
much signal space they buy.

\emph{Drift discrimination on coarse windows.} Within the searched
ceiling every in-grid drift on a 15.625-MHz window is sub-channel over a
track, so \NCoarse{} of \NWindows{} windows measure amplitude but cannot
distinguish a drifting carrier from a stationary one
(\S\ref{sec:dwell}). Finer channelisation on re-observation, or a
longer-baseline drift model spanning multiple execution blocks, would
recover that axis.

\emph{Retain more control spectra.} The two-stage scheme of
\S\ref{sec:technosearch}, \NCtrl{} controls routinely and then a local
null for any window that passes, works where the null can be validated
and fails where it cannot, because full dynamic spectra survive for only
\LNNRetained{} control positions. Retaining all of them, at modest
storage cost, would make the second stage usable in structured fields as
well.

\emph{Extend the injection campaign and the polarisation axis.} The
completeness curve should be measured per band, channel width,
integration time, antenna count and primary-beam position rather than
transferred by construction, and pushed past $10\sigma$ until it
asymptotes, with EIRP$_{90}$ unlocated. The design is a stratified grid
over those axes, injections at continuously randomised drift rates and
channel phases rather than preferentially on grid points, morphologies
extended to sinusoidal acceleration tracks following representative
sample orbits, trial-level logging from the first run, and an
independent re-implementation cross-check of the injection code. The
polarisation axis buys discrimination against weakly polarised
astrophysical backgrounds at no sensitivity cost.

\emph{A boundary on drift linearity.} The search assumes drift linear
over a track. Curvature matters when $\tfrac{1}{2}\ddot\nu T^{2}$
exceeds half a native channel, reached only by the shortest-period
orbits: $P\lesssim1.6$\,d for a typical Class~A window (230\,GHz,
488\,kHz, $T\approx2000$\,s), about an hour for a Class~B one.
TRAPPIST-1\,b, at $1.51$\,d, sits just inside the boundary and is also
the case exceeding the drift ceiling; elsewhere the constant-drift model
is adequate, and the chirp-search extension
(Appendix~\ref{app:extended}) covers the remainder.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This ALMA-archival technosignature survey searched, within 40\,pc, every
star with public usable coverage: \NStarBands{} target/bands, \NStars{}
stars in \NSystems{} independent systems, ALMA-archive-complete and
audited for position and identity (\S\ref{sec:exclusions}), but covering
${\sim}1$ per cent of the catalogued stars in that volume and inheriting the
archive's non-uniform targeting. The archive's channelisation splits
what was searched: \NWinA{} Class~A windows were searched for
drift-resolved spectral carriers and \NWinB{} Class~B windows are
sensitive only to unresolved spectral excess, with no drift
discrimination over \PctCoarse{} per cent of the survey
(\S\ref{sec:statistic}). Four windows raised a stage-1 statistical
screening flag. Three are consistent with, and very likely caused by, CO
emission: two circumstellar components towards $\beta$~Pictoris across
two transitions and three epochs, and one previously reported foreground
cloud towards HD~48370 \citep{Cataldi2023}. The fourth, towards
CP$-$72~2713 at \FobsCpSeven\,GHz, is unclassified and marginal: a
second-stage local null on its own dynamic spectrum gives $p=\LNpCP$,
about \LNCPRatio{} times above the survey-wide Bonferroni scale, so it
is not significant at the survey level. It is held under pinned promote
and retire criteria (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). None survives vetting, so we
report \NCandidates{} credible technosignatures. Barnard's Star and
Wolf~359 carry their first published mm/submm technosignature limits
(\S\ref{sec:barnardswolf}), and forty ancillary continuum measurements
(median $5\sigma$ limit 0.52\,mJy) are non-detections of unquantified
statistical power.

The EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ values are nominal trigger thresholds (boxed rule,
\S\ref{sec:method}): $\EirpMinA$--$\EirpMaxA$\,W over Class~A at
\ChanLoA--\ChanHiA\,kHz native resolution and
$\EirpMinB$--$\EirpMaxB$\,W over Class~B at
\ChanLoB--\ChanHiB\,MHz, deep enough toward the single
nearest system for an unresolved carrier matching the total power of an
Arecibo-like planetary radar, and optimistic by $\times\HanFacBest$ to
$\times\HanFacWorst$ under the instrument's Hanning response. The
conditional searched-domain transmitter fraction stays illustrative
(Appendix~\ref{app:population}). Searched is not the same as
constrained: the \emph{Scope of the constraints} box of
\S\ref{sec:method} fixes what the non-detection binds, and everything
outside it is unbounded. The detection statistic and the line mask were
defined using these data, so the false-alarm calibration here describes
this dataset. The first-priority extensions (\S\ref{sec:futurework}) are
drift discrimination on coarse windows, a visibility-domain likelihood
ratio, control ensembles past the $1/\RankFloor$ rank floor, and a
stratified injection campaign over band, channel width, integration time
and beam offset. By-products ship with the data: a serendipitous
molecular-line catalogue, exoplanet-host and white-dwarf subsample
limits, and a closure-phase diagnostic, demonstrated but without
discriminating power at these flux densities.

\section{Frequency conventions, linewidth reading, and smearing}
\label{app:conventions}

\emph{Reference frames.} The line mask's chain runs topocentric
$\rightarrow$ barycentric (Earth ephemeris at the execution-block
mid-time) $\rightarrow$ stellar rest frame (catalogued systemic radial
velocity), with JPL/CDMS rest frequencies
\citep{Pickett1998,CDMS2005}, and in the LSR frame it stops at the
standard solar motion instead of the systemic velocity
(\S\ref{sec:linecost}). Its residual error is the catalogue
radial-velocity uncertainty alone, at most a few km\,s$^{-1}$ within
40\,pc; second-order relativistic terms at the
\MaskVWidth\,km\,s$^{-1}$ scale are $\lesssim5$\,kHz at 350\,GHz, below
the finest native channel searched (15.3\,kHz). The $\beta$~Pictoris and
AU~Mic attributions use the same chain, so their offsets are comparable
to the exclusion tolerance. Enlarging the drift grid needs no further
frame machinery: the searched rates are topocentric and depend only on
elapsed time and channel width.

\emph{Channel widths, and the instrumental response.} Channel widths
come from the measurement sets' own spectral window tables
(CHAN\_WIDTH). Earlier versions of this appendix reported that those
tables ``carry no EFFECTIVE\_BANDWIDTH column (no online spectral
averaging)'' and read that as evidence that CHAN\_WIDTH is also the
effective resolution. It is evidence of nothing: MS~v2 defines no column
of that name, the required columns being EFFECTIVE\_BW and RESOLUTION,
both always present. The check is withdrawn, and with it the claim that
the quoted channelisations are noise bandwidths.

The correct statement is the opposite. ALMA applies online Hanning
smoothing by default, so the spectral response of such a window is
${\sim}2$ channels wide and adjacent-channel noise is correlated at
$\rho\simeq0.5$. Under the $0.25/0.5/0.25$ kernel a sub-channel tone
retains \HanBest{} of its power in the peak channel when it sits at the
channel centre and \HanWorst{} when it sits on a boundary, median
\HanMed{} over uniform sub-channel placement. Every EIRP$_{5\sigma}$
here is therefore optimistic by $\times\HanFacBest$ to
$\times\HanFacWorst$, median $\times\HanFacMed$, and the released
catalogue carries the nominal threshold with both corrected values per
window; the worst case is the one quoted in the sensitivity summary.
Because the injections deposit an unsmoothed delta-in-channel tone, the
recovery curves of \S\ref{sec:dwell} and Appendix~\ref{app:inject} carry
the same bias, so the end-to-end check (Eq.~\ref{eq:stacked}) cannot see
it. Part of the lag-one correlation attributed to the drift-tracked
statistic ($\rho_{\rm lag1}=0.989$--$0.991$) is instrumental on the same
grounds, and the decomposition is not measured here. We do not rescale
the thresholds, because the per-window smoothing state is recorded in
archive metadata not yet harvested: the honest form of the number is
``nominal, and up to $\times\HanFacWorst$ optimistic if the window is
Hanning-smoothed''.

\emph{Linewidth convention.} The statistic is the flux density in a
single channel after drift correction, so the quoted EIRP$_{5\sigma}$
applies to any transmitter narrower than the native channel, flat in the
assumed linewidth. Two sub-channel qualifications. Flatness is exact for
a tone centred in a channel, a boundary-straddling tone splitting its
power between two at worst a factor of two. And the channel-to-channel
transition follows the correlator's passband shape, not retained in the
frozen products, making the flatness a channel-centred idealisation
accurate at the tens-of-per-cent level. Individual thresholds therefore
carry a sub-channel-position systematic of order tens of per cent, a
tone placed uniformly at random within a channel keeping a mean 0.75 of
its power in the stronger one, and EIRP values are quoted to two
significant figures.

\emph{Transmitters wider than one channel.} The baseline filter is a
running median along frequency, whose transfer function is flat for
features narrower than roughly half the median width $W$ and falls to
zero for structure broader than $W$, so the survey's effective
sensitivity is to channel-confined morphologies and $W$ is the linewidth
ceiling. $W$ is not carried in the frozen per-window products, so it
cannot be printed here; it ships with the tagged release. Within that
ceiling the sensitivity to a top-hat transmitter spanning $N_{\rm ch}$
native channels follows from the statistic's single-channel definition.
The per-channel flux is the total divided by $N_{\rm ch}$ at unchanged
per-channel noise, so to first order
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}(N_{\rm ch}) \;\simeq\; N_{\rm ch}\times
\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}(1), \qquad N_{\rm ch} \lesssim W/2,
\end{equation}
which assumes a top-hat spectral profile and is the same rescaling the
main text applies to signals broader than one channel.

\emph{Worked example: from flux to EIRP.} Take UV~Ceti (G~272-61B)
Band~3. The searched window's per-channel rms is 0.244\,mJy, so
$S_{\rm min} = 5\times0.244 = 1.22$\,mJy in one 15.625-MHz native
channel. At 2.67\,pc that is
$1.22\times10^{-29}$\,W\,m$^{-2}$\,Hz$^{-1}$, and
$\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma} = 4\pi d^{2} S_{\rm min} \Delta\nu = 4\pi
(2.67\,{\rm pc})^{2} \times 1.22\times10^{-29}\,{\rm
W\,m^{-2}\,Hz^{-1}} \times 15.625\,{\rm MHz} = 1.6\times10^{13}$\,W. A
wider transmitter needs $S_{\rm min}$ per channel.

\emph{Intra-integration smearing.} The frequency excursion at the
maximum searched drift rate across one integration,
$\dot\nu_{\max} t_{\rm int}$, is at most 1.10 channels across the
\NWindows{} windows (median 0.001, 90th percentile 0.06). A linearly
drifting signal smeared by $\Delta$ channels within an integration
retains a fraction $\eta_{\rm smear} = \mathrm{sinc}(\pi\Delta/2)$ of
its peak-channel amplitude, so a worst-drift transmitter's effective
threshold is $\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}/\eta_{\rm smear}$. Three windows
have $\eta_{\rm smear}<0.99$: $\beta$~Pic Band~6 ($\Delta=1.10$,
$\eta_{\rm smear}=0.574$), $\eta$~Crv Band~8 (0.585, 0.865) and
$\eta$~Crv Band~7 (0.411, 0.932). Every other window agrees with its
nominal threshold to better than 1 per cent, and $\eta_{\rm smear}$ is a
column of the released catalogue. A signal drifting midway between
adjacent trial rates accumulates at most 0.03 channels of residual drift
across the grid as realised, so no significant sensitivity is lost
between trial rates.

\section{Injection-recovery sensitivity validation}
\label{app:inject}

The quoted limits assume a $5\sigma$ threshold behaves as idealised
Gaussian noise predicts. We test that by injecting synthetic tones of
known amplitude into real calibrated visibilities through the extraction
code's own mechanism, as far upstream of the statistic as retained
products allow, and measuring what the unmodified pipeline recovers.
Three results elsewhere inherit this campaign's single-configuration
limitation: the drifting-class recovery entering the conditional
fraction of Appendix~\ref{app:population}, the machinery and
boundary-placement amplitude factors of \S\ref{sec:validation}, and the
coarse-window dwell reading of \S\ref{sec:dwell}.

Six configurations span three bands and both resolution classes: three
coarse Band~7 spws of AT~Mic, one coarse spw each in Bands~3 and~6 of
AU~Mic, and AU~Mic's 488-kHz flagband window with its 49-trial drift
grid. Into each goes a complex tone at the star position, at amplitudes
$4$--$10\times$ the statistic's own noise, drift fractions
$f_{\rm drift}=0$, $0.25$, $0.5$, $0.75$, $1.0$ of the window's searched
ceiling, on- and half-grid rates, and channel-centred and boundary
placements: 200 trials per configuration, \NTrials{} in all, under a
frozen recovery criterion ($T\geq5$, peak channel within 3 of the
injection, peak drift within one grid step).

The coarse-window variant recovered 0 of 500 injections at every
amplitude from $4\sigma$ to $10\sigma$ under that criterion. This is a
recovery-criterion artefact rather than a pipeline suppression, and it
is the one withdrawn result here: the criterion's drift-matching clause
cannot be satisfied on coarse windows, where every in-grid drift is
sub-channel over the track, so the reported peak drift is arbitrary.
Scoring the same windows on detection alone recovers essentially
everything, and an end-to-end injection through the released pipeline
returns $T_\star$ of 20.5, 65.3 and 217.7 at 1, 3 and 10 times the
per-integration noise, against an ideal coherent gain of
$\sqrt{639}=25$. Class~B limits therefore bound carriers of any dwell
fraction, with the completeness of \S\ref{sec:dwell}; what those windows
genuinely lack is drift discrimination. The fine window fails the same
criterion in the same way, and that cell is unmeasured rather than lost:
carriers with $f_{\rm drift}\leq0.25$ dwell ${\geq}40$ per cent of the
track per channel and fail it, those with $f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$
surviving as the searched class.

The second result is the completeness curve. On the fine window,
drifting carriers ($f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$, pooled over grid phase and
sub-channel placement) are recovered 17, \RecThresh, 58, 75 and
\RecTwice{} per cent of the time at $4$, $5$, $6$, $8$ and $10\sigma$,
saturating by twice threshold power (Fig.~\ref{fig:completeness}). The
machinery alone, with the tone injected after the baseline step, is
amplitude-faithful to within $-9$/$+14$ per cent for channel-centred
tones at every drift fraction. Boundary-straddling placement costs a
factor $0.6$--$0.8$ in the statistic and recovers 0 per cent at
$5\sigma$ against 83 per cent for centred ones (67 and 100 per cent at
$10\sigma$), crossing 50 per cent near $1.4\times$ threshold amplitude.
Survey completeness uses the placement-pooled curve, conservative for
the boundary-straddling case: EIRP$_{50}=1.10\,\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}$
and EIRP$_{90}>2.0\,\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}$, a bound only, and both
carrying an unbounded transfer systematic to every configuration but
this one, bracketed as $0.5$--$2\times$ in
Appendix~\ref{app:population}. Not spanned:
other target environments, primary-beam offsets, integration durations
and edge-phased drift grids.

\emph{Transfer to other windows.} Survey completeness for the drifting
class is measured on one configuration, AU~Mic's 488-kHz Band~6 window,
and applied to all \NWinA{} Class~A windows. The transfer assumption and
its unbounded error are propagated in Appendix~\ref{app:population}, and
bounding it needs the multi-configuration campaign of
\S\ref{sec:futurework}.

\section{Demonstrated diagnostics with no operative role here}
\label{app:closure}

\emph{Closure phase.} The closure phase
$\arg(V_{ij})+\arg(V_{jk})-\arg(V_{ik})$ around a triangle vanishes for
an unresolved point source anywhere on sky and is immune to
per-station calibration errors, so it asks only whether a signal is a
coherent point source at all; a coherent far-sidelobe interferer would
pass. Consistency with zero is scored by a circular (unit-phasor)
z-score of the mean drift-tracked closure phase, its null calibrated on
each observation's own integrations, which also absorbs the
shared-baseline covariance. The implementation is validated on synthetic
visibilities and driven end to end on continuum detections in four local
reprocessing fields, including the resolved AT~Mic Band~7 binary as a
resolving control. At these flux densities it is not useful:
per-baseline S/N is 0.5--0.7 at the median and never exceeds 5.1, with
only 0.02 per cent of triangles having all three baselines above
S/N${\,=\,}5$, so every field's closure phases are indistinguishable
from noise, the binary included. It becomes a discriminator only at
greater depth.

\emph{Chirped drift and periodicity.}
\label{app:extended}
Stacking at constant linear drift rate is blind by design to a
transmitter whose drift rate changes during the observation, and to
pulsed or periodically modulated carriers. We implemented a chirped
(quadratic-drift) extension on a coarser grid of 21 linear-drift
$\times$ 5 chirp-rate trials, and a per-channel periodicity search, a
per-position FFT along each channel's time axis against a robust
per-channel noise estimate. Both vet the star against 8 retained control
positions, a strict subset of that window's \NCtrl-position ensemble and
not an independent one. On the 122 retained spectra this is a
demonstration: the flag rates, 10.7 per cent for chirp and 13.1 per cent
for periodicity against a ${\sim}11$ per cent chance rate, show only
that the extension runs correctly under the null. No instance is treated
as a candidate and no limit derives from it. Extending both to the full
ensemble would promote the screen to survey grade.

\section{Cross-target frequency-occupancy check}
\label{app:freqoccupancy}

With no ON/OFF cadence against radio-frequency interference, this
single-pointing archival survey substitutes the many unrelated stars
sharing similar correlator setups: a channel-confined feature recurring
at the same observed \emph{sky frequency} towards different targets is
almost certainly interference or instrumental. Over every target/spw
result the check pairs both the best-fit-channel frequency and the
formal $\geq5\sigma$ crossings, matching $\nu_1,\nu_2$ towards different
targets when $|\nu_1-\nu_2| < \max(k\,\Delta\nu_1,\; k\,\Delta\nu_2)$
with $k=3$ (${\sim}46$\,kHz). Across the \NWindows{} windows and 107
tabulated crossings, no pair falls within the kernel, or within
0.5\,MHz. The kernel is narrower than the $\beta$~Pictoris line offsets
of 10.6 and 21.9\,MHz, so those windows were dispositioned by
line-catalogue inspection instead.

The null's power is the count of target pairs sharing frequency
coverage: on the $\le$20\,pc subset, 366 of 946 star pairs (39 per cent)
overlap by at least 0.01\,GHz, deepest 14.25\,GHz
($\gamma$~Lep--$\gamma$~Vir), and 109 pairs involve one of the five
stars carrying formal crossings, so every crossing frequency was tested
against genuinely overlapping independent targets. The UV~Ceti and
AT~Mic shared execution blocks are excluded, their intra-pair
coincidences being identities by construction. The interim product's
per-window crossing lists are capped at 50 frequencies, so the true
count is 171 crossings against 107 tabulated. The difference is one
window, $\beta$~Pic Band~6 ultra-fine, with 114 formal crossings of
which the 50 strongest are tabulated; its untabulated 64 could not be
tested, so the null is demonstrated for the tabulated set and unresolved
for the rest. The public release ships uncapped crossing lists.

\section{The velocity-space line mask: justification and retrospective application}
\label{app:linemask}

The one-channel line check failed the $\beta$~Pictoris windows
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}): a native channel spans a fraction of a
km\,s$^{-1}$, while circumstellar kinematics shift real emission by
many channels. The replacement mask takes its tolerance from the
kinematic budget of circumstellar gas. That budget is Keplerian motion
where these molecules survive ($\gtrsim$10\,au), $\lesssim$13\,km\,s$^{-1}$;
cold-gas linewidths $\lesssim$1\,km\,s$^{-1}$; cold dense-gas tracers
with no bulk-wind term for the dwarfs dominating the sample; and an
uncorrected barycentric term bounded by
$\pm$30\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The result is empirically conservative: the largest
attributed circumstellar component ($\beta$~Pic, $-2.7$ to
$-3.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) sits at 7 per cent of it, and \emph{zero}
crossing frequencies lie 13--50\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from their nearest
transition, so a $\pm13$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ Keplerian-only mask would have
suppressed and released the same windows: the wider tolerance costs no
yield and buys robustness against radial-velocity catalogue error.

\emph{The repaired mask.} The four defects of the mask as executed, and
the retrospective re-application that repairs them without re-running
the survey, are in \S\ref{sec:linecost}; no disposition changes. The
rounding defect is also why the released CO($1{\to}0$) offset column and
Table~\ref{tab:bpicaudit} differ: the column is referred to the frozen
mask's rounded entry, \CatCOoneGHz\,GHz, and reads \CatCOerrMHz\,MHz
(\CatCOerrKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$) smaller than the laboratory-referred value
tabulated there. A cheaper and better-founded rule than enumerating
species is available: the archive records the rest frequency each
execution block was tuned to, so masking each window's own tuned line
would catch the $\beta$~Pictoris, HD~48370 and [C\,\textsc{i}] windows
automatically and without tuning, alongside an LSR-frame tube of a few
$\times10$\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

Two of the six remaining crossing windows fall inside the mask (the
unflagged $\beta$~Pic coarse window at $-3.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$; HD~285968
at $+8.7$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, already rejected by its control ring, maximum
10.9 against a star peak of 6.5); the rest lie far outside any
circumstellar tolerance (AU~Mic $+379$, LHS~1140 $+115$\,km\,s$^{-1}$).
Of the \NHits{} windows with an on-star crossing, \NSpatial{} also beat their
control ring, three accounted for by the mask. The other
\NonExcRingGe{} are unremarkable the way ordinary noise crossings are:
in every one of them the window's own control ring reaches or exceeds
the stellar value (ring-maximum median \NonExcRingMed{} against a
crossing median \NonExcHitMed), the expected Gaussian-tailed population
of moderate $5$--$5.5\sigma$ crossings with comparably strong peaks
among \NCtrl{} controls. But the transitions that
make astrophysical false positives cheap are also natural
reference frequencies for a deliberate communicator
\citep{CocconiMorrison1959}, so
the released products keep a known-line-coincident list for a future
search with independent discrimination. It holds every on-source
$5\sigma$ crossing inside the masked intervals, with its offset from
each nearby transition.

\section{AU~Mic: the three tests in full}

recipe (310 integrations against the release pipeline's 249, on a finer
drift grid) and running the symmetric statistic of
\S\ref{sec:statistic} on the crossing window, the star does not beat its
control ensemble. The frozen products agree only asymmetrically: the
source-region peak exceeds the \NCtrl-control maximum by 0.12, below
that statistic's window-to-window scatter, and the crossing fails the
single-position variant too.

\emph{Recurrence.} The one further public Band~6 block covering
230.83\,GHz, the same programme executed 2014 June~5, was searched
identically: no channel within $\pm$10 of the first-epoch flagged
channel exceeds 3.1, and nothing is localised to the stellar position.
Its band rms (1.48\,mJy per 488-kHz channel) matches the first epoch's
1.9\,mJy, so a comparable excess would have been recovered, and the
frequency recurs at no other target. Intermittent or
precisely-scheduled transmissions outside the sampled windows stay
unconstrained.

\emph{Statistical scale.} The on-source peak (5.97) sits at the
\AuPct{}th percentile of the control-maxima distribution over all
\NWindows{} windows (median \CtrlMed, 90th percentile \CtrlPninety):
\AuNge{} windows (\AuFrac{} per cent) match or exceed it, add-one
empirical $p=\AuP$ before any multiple-testing correction, which is a
survey-wide window-level rate and distinct from the within-window rank
of \S\ref{sec:aumic}. The crossing matches no catalogued molecular
transition (287\,MHz from CO $J{=}2{\to}1$), and closure-phase vetting
finds it point-source consistent, one-sided evidence only
(Appendix~\ref{app:closure}).

Two astrophysical contexts are recorded for future re-examination, and
neither grounds the rejection. AU~Mic is an exceptionally active flare
star, but the block's own quiescent continuum (0.15\,mJy) rules out the
later-epoch GHz-wide flares of \citet{MacGregor2020} (16.8 and
6.1\,mJy) as origin: an 11\,mJy channel-confined excess would sit a
factor ${\sim}70$ above a continuum that shows nothing, and the
same-tuning second epoch shows no burst. The controls share the
primary-beam footprint, so smooth structure such as the debris belt
raises star and controls alike and cannot alone produce a localised
excess.

\section{The ancillary continuum lane}
\label{app:continuum}
\label{app:contsearch}

The continuum lane is an exploratory by-product rather than a co-equal
search: 57 measurements, 17 detections and 40 limits, a millimetre
excess being at best an indirect, model-dependent observable of
waste-heat technology. An ``upper limit'' is $5\times$ the
primary-beam-corrected map rms at the star's position, and the deepest
non-detection is towards TRAPPIST-1 in Band~3. Under the combined error
model sixteen of the seventeen detections stay above $5\sigma$, the
exception ($\eta$~Corvi Band~7, $4.7\sigma$ combined) being marginal.
Bright far-from-target peaks ($\epsilon$\,Eri, GJ~674, CD$-$23~14742)
fall outside the 3-arcsec tolerance and are recorded as non-detections.

Every detection is astrophysically unsurprising: the unresolved UV~Ceti
binary; six systems whose millimetre flux is disc or chromospheric
emission, among them $\beta$~Pic, AU~Mic and AT~Mic, which are young and
active, and $\tau$~Cet, which is neither, a ${\sim}5$--8\,Gyr G8V star
whose flux is photospheric and/or its debris disc; six stars at
photospheric flux, among them $\gamma$~Lep, $\gamma$~Vir,
$\chi^{1}$~Ori and $\eta$~Cru; and two late-M dwarfs of documented
magnetic activity, LP~349$-$25 and the auroral emitter
LSR~J1835$+$3259 \citep{Hallinan2015}, which merits deeper follow-up.
Closure phase cannot discriminate structure for any of the seventeen at
these flux densities.

\emph{The anomaly screen.} Each star's flux is tested against its own
photospheric expectation (Planck function with Gaia DR3 parameters,
Pecaut \& Mamajek fallback where GSP-Phot is unavailable) and against
same-$T_{\rm eff}$ stars in coarse bins of at least five, through a
robust median/MAD statistic. The error model, evaluated before any flag,
is $\sigma_{\rm tot}^{2} = \sigma_{\rm map}^{2} + (f_{\rm cal}\,S_\nu)^{2}
+ \sigma_{\rm model}^{2}$, with $f_{\rm cal}$ 5 per cent in Bands~3--6
and 10 per cent in Bands~7--8 and $\sigma_{\rm model}$ propagated from
Gaia temperature and radius errors and the fallback relation's scatter.
The model has \emph{no intrinsic-variability term}, which is adequate
for a photosphere and wrong for an active M dwarf whose millimetre flux
can vary by factors of several, so the screen is uninformative for
exactly the stars a radio-stellar reader cares about. At the five-star
minimum the bin median rests on two or three values, so it flags for
inspection rather than performing per-star hypothesis tests. One outlier
results, $\chi^1$~Ori at Band~3 (excess ratio 2.52, largest of the
seventeen; resampled tail probability 0.53 in its six-star bin, so a
ranked outlier of a small sample and not a significant departure),
consistent with a spectroscopic G0V binary's unresolved companion or a
chromosphere.

\section{False-alarm accounting under the symmetric criterion}
\label{app:falsealarm}

The symmetric statistic gives a per-window rate $1/\RankFloor$ and a
survey expectation $\NWindows/\RankFloor=\ExpFlags$ flags. That floor
falls only if the control ensemble grows, which the primary beam bounds;
and windows are not fully independent, grouping into \NEB{} execution
blocks sharing weather, calibration and correlator setup, so
$N/\RankFloor$ is an expectation over a mildly correlated sample rather
than $N$ independent trials. Both limits are conservative for the
conclusion drawn, that no window warrants candidate status, since they
widen rather than narrow the null range.

The survey evaluates $\TrialCells$ channel$\times$drift cells, and a
Gaussian one-sided $5\sigma$ tail ($2.87\times10^{-7}$) over that count
predicts \GaussCross{} chance crossings against the \NHits{} windows
with an on-star crossing. Raw counts run higher because the
drift-tracked statistic is strongly correlated between adjacent channels
($\rho_{\rm lag1}=0.989$--$0.991$, i.e.\ $8.5\times10^{4}$--$1.1
\times10^{5}$ effectively independent cells), so crossings arrive in
contiguous runs; collapsed to clusters on a kernel of 3 native channels
they leave far fewer independent trials, and that count agrees with the
trials expectation. Beyond the shared primary-beam gain, neighbouring
control positions are correlated below the synthesised-beam scale, so
independent spatial trials fall from \NCtrl{} to $N_{\rm
eff}\simeq30$--$43$, a floor to which the empirical \RateSbr{} per cent
rate, measured on the correlated ensembles themselves, is immune by
construction.

No single stratum drives the star-exceeds-ring rate. The stratification
was run on the $\le$20\,pc subset under the withdrawn asymmetric
statistic, whose counts it quotes, the operative symmetric rate being
too small (\NSbr{} windows) to stratify. Across band, channelisation and
primary-beam offset that rate moved only between 11 and 20 per cent, and
the disc-hosting caveat inverts empirically: disc- or planet-hosting
stars contribute every crossing window and every stage-1 flag but a
\emph{lower} star-exceeds-ring rate, 9.8 against 18.3 per cent for
non-hosts, so circumstellar emission shared by star and ensemble does
not drive the statistic. That rate test is not the relevant one,
however. Every flagged window, and the crossing the statistic revision
removed, belongs to one population: $\beta$~Pic, AU~Mic and
CP$-$72~2713 are $\beta$~Pictoris moving-group members with debris discs
and HD~48370 is a disc host behind a molecular cloud. The flags
concentrate in the systems with the richest astrophysical foreground,
which is what the archive preferentially contains; to a good
approximation the searched sample is the ALMA debris-disc and
young-moving-group archive. That is the worst case for astrophysical
false positives at millimetre wavelengths, and the flag statistics
should be read accordingly. Excluding the \NBPWindows{} $\beta$~Pictoris
windows leaves one unattributed crossing among \NNonBP, against
\ExpNonBP{} expected ($P(\geq1)=\PgeOneNonBPPct$ per cent).

\section{Excluded entries, and the retired statistic}
\label{app:exclusions}

Five pre-freeze changes to the pipeline are logged with their effect on
released products in the repository change-log, which is the auditable
record: the threshold convention (per-channel-width
W\,Hz$^{-1}$ to total power), the search statistic (asymmetric region-max
to the symmetric single-position form of \S\ref{sec:statistic}, the
release re-run and the criterion frozen), the baseline-operator
description (withdrawal of a ``temporal-median subtraction'' claim that
was never in the code), the line-exclusion width (one channel to
$\pm\MaskVWidth$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in velocity) and the framing of the
conditional searched-domain transmitter fraction. Only the second
changes a released number: flagged windows \NSbrOld/\NWindows{}
(\RateSbrOld{} per cent) become \NSbr/\NWindows{} (\RateSbr{} per cent), and the AU~Mic
crossing becomes unflagged. Under a pure-noise null the retired region
maximum over $n_{\rm src}$ positions beats single controls with
probability $n_{\rm src}/(n_{\rm src}+n_{\rm ctrl})=\AsymExpectPct$ per
cent, which is the order of the measured region-max rate
(\RateSbrOld{} per cent against the symmetric \RateSbr, never
interchanged). Nothing derived from the retired statistic enters an
operative false-alarm probability.

\emph{The four classes of excluded entry.} (i) Four target/bands
(Barnard's Star [B7], Wolf~359 [B7], SCR~J1845$-$6357, Wolf~358) have no
carrier result from a process-level failure; each keeps a valid
continuum non-detection and no EIRP threshold. (ii) Giclas~9$-$38A and
Giclas~9$-$38B [B3] are excluded entirely, the pair's only matched MOUS
pointing ${\sim}52$~arcmin from either star, $35\times$ its own
primary-beam FWHM; the carrier step aborts correctly and continuum
imaging lacks that guard. (iii) Six windows across five stars (51~Eri,
HD~10647, HD~139664, TWA~7, HD~92945 twice) fail the noise test of
\S\ref{sec:exclusions}: all are 15.62-MHz coarse windows whose reported
rms is inconsistent with their reported integration by three to four
orders of magnitude and sits a factor \DefectSibFacLo--\DefectSibFacHi{}
below the shallowest sibling of the same block and channel width, which
is a normalisation signature rather than weather or configuration.
Naming the root cause needs quantities that are properties of the raw
execution blocks, and so does checking whether the \NCtrl{} control
$\sigma$ values in a defective window are deflated with the stellar one.
(iv) The catalogue entry ``$\gamma$~Lupi'' resolves to the F4V
debris-disc host HD~139664 at 17.40\,pc, not the naked-eye
$\gamma$~Lupi; all such entries are relabelled and no measured value
changes. The supporting quality audit is clean across all \NWindows{}
windows bar four $\chi^{1}$~Ori Band~3 windows flagged as elevated
(\ChiOriQaLo--\ChiOriQaHi$\times$ the Band~3 median, weakening their
thresholds by ${\lesssim}20$ per cent in amplitude).

\section{The conditional searched-domain transmitter fraction}
\label{app:population}

\emph{Illustrative calculation only.} Nothing in this appendix is a
survey result, and no number in it appears among the headline
quantities of Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}. It is kept because the
framework is reusable, not because the number it returns is
informative.

A zero detection constrains a fraction of systems only through a
per-system detection probability, which factorises as
%
\begin{equation}
C_i(P) \;=\; F_i\;R_i(P)\;D_i\;C_{{\rm morph},i},
\label{eq:conditioning}
\end{equation}
%
with $F_i$ the chance the transmitter's sky frequency lies in system
$i$'s searched intervals, $R_i(P)$ the chance a transmitter of EIRP $P$
would have crossed that system's threshold, $D_i$ the chance it emitted
during the analysed archival interval, and $C_{{\rm morph},i}$ the
measured recovery of a carrier of the searched drift and temporal
morphology (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). The probability of zero credible
detections if a fraction $f$ of systems hosts one is
\begin{equation}
P(N_{\rm det}=0\,|\,f)=\prod_{i=1}^{N_{\rm sys}}\left(1-f\,C_i\right),
\label{eq:prevalence}
\end{equation}
and the one-sided 95 per cent upper limit $f_{95}$ solves
$P(N_{\rm det}=0\,|\,f_{95})=0.05$ over the \NSystems{} systems.

Everything below sets $F_i=D_i=1$ \emph{by conditioning, not by
measurement}, so $f$ is a conditional searched-domain transmitter
fraction and not a demographic occurrence rate. At EIRP
$\geq10^{16}$\,W the measured form gives
$f_{95}\approx\OccMeasuredRound$ per cent over the \NOccSystems{}
systems carrying a Class~A window, with idealised brackets \OccUnit{}
and \OccUniform{} per cent. Folding in the dwell-measured Class~B curve,
so that each window carries the completeness measured for the
morphology it actually searches, extends the calculation to \NOccAll{}
systems and moves the number to \OccAll{} per cent
(\OccAllFifteen{} per cent over \NOccAllFifteen{} systems at
$10^{15}$\,W, \OccAllSeventeen{} per cent over \NOccAllSeventeen{} at
$10^{17}$\,W). Table~\ref{tab:occurrence} carries the ladder.

Three properties bound how far any of this travels. The Class~A recovery
curve is measured on one configuration, AU~Mic's 488-kHz Band~6 window,
and applied to all \NWinA{} Class~A windows spanning Bands~3--8 and
on-source times of \OnsrcMin--\OnsrcMax\,s; the transfer error is not
bounded by the $^{+\RecThreshHi}_{-\RecThreshLo}$ percentage-point
binomial interval, which describes sampling noise within that one run.
Scaling the transferred curve by 0.5--2$\times$ moves the number from
\OccMeasured{} to \OccBracketLo--\OccBracketHi{} per cent, which is the
honest width. The $C_i$ are not independent, \NWindows{} windows
grouping into \NEB{} execution blocks with an intraclass correlation of
\IccRank{} in the control-normalised stellar rank; the implied design
effect moves \OccMeasuredRound{} to \EffFIccPct{} per cent, an order
below the transfer term. And under any transmitter-frequency prior
spread across ALMA's tuning range the bound vanishes: the per-system
Class~A union has median 1.73\,GHz against a ${\sim}75$\,GHz range, so
the conditioning supplies the entire content of the number.

Writing $p_{\rm epoch}$ for the chance a transmitter is on in any one
epoch, a system observed in $n_i$ independent execution blocks is
sampled $n_i$ times, so $D_i=1-(1-p_{\rm epoch})^{n_i}$; \NMultiEpoch{}
of the \NSystems{} systems have $n_i>1$. With at most three epochs at
year-scale separations this is a phase-averaged bound, and an
intermittent transmitter is at least as plausible \emph{a priori}, so
the $p_{\rm epoch}=1$ entries should never be quoted without it.
