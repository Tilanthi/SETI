\begin{abstract}
The millimetre and submillimetre sky has never been searched for narrowband
transmitters toward nearby stars. Public ALMA observations taken for other
purposes record a spectrum of the star at each field centre, so we searched
them: \NWinA{} spectral windows toward \NSysClassA{} archive-selected stellar
systems within 40\,pc, channels fine enough to follow a drift, covering
\CvUnionA\,GHz of union bandwidth in disconnected intervals between
\SurvFreqLoA{} and \SurvFreqHiA\,GHz. Of the \NCross{} threshold crossings,
\NAttributed{} coincide with a catalogued molecular transition --- among them
carbon monoxide in the $\beta$~Pictoris disc, which the search recovered
without being told where to look --- and the remainder are no more numerous
than chance predicts. Each of the \NUnattributed{} unattributed crossings a
later observation covered was tested for recurrence at its predicted stellar-frame frequency and
drift, and no event satisfied the pre-defined independent-recurrence
confirmation criterion. The null has a limit: only \RcConfSysDay{} of the
\NSysClassA{} systems were observed again at the same frequency more than a
day apart, over \CvConfGHzDay\,GHz of the union searched, so the searched
extent is about \PrConfFactor{} the confirmable one. Depth is measured by injecting carriers into the calibrated
visibilities and recovering them: the median system's best window returns nine
in ten at $\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W, a recovery fraction and not a detection
probability. The same data's coarser windows limit an unresolved spectral
excess. For classifying a crossing in the present archival data set,
molecular-line confusion and sparse repeat coverage are more immediate
limitations than thermal sensitivity.
\end{abstract}
