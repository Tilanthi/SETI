\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The public ALMA archive holds thousands of hours of millimetre and
submillimetre observations taken for other reasons, mostly studies of dust
discs and planet formation. This paper searches them for a narrowband
transmitter at the position of a star in the field. No such signal was found.

Searches for \emph{technosignatures} -- detectable imprints of technology,
intended or not \citep{Sheikh2020,SETIreview2025} -- have examined many
thousands of stars over six decades, from the first proposals
\citep{CocconiMorrison1959,Drake1960} through Project Phoenix
\citep{Tarter2001,Backus2002} to the targeted and commensal surveys of today
\citep{Zhang2020,Czech2021,FAST3IATLAS2025,LOFARdualsite2023,Choza2024,Morrison2023,Tremblay2024,SRT2025},
now centred on machine-learning interference rejection
\citep{Ma2023,GLOBULAR2025,AnomalyParkesGBT2025}. The last published
accounting of that effort covers only \HaystackFrac{} of the space of
transmitter position, power, frequency, bandwidth, polarisation and repetition
rate \citep{Wright2018}. Essentially all of it lies below ${\sim}30$\,GHz.

Above about 30\,GHz the record is nearly empty, and that is what makes this
frequency range new. \citet{Steffes1994} observed \SbPriorNStarA{} solar-type
stars within \SbPriorDistA\,pc at \SbPriorFreqGHz\,GHz and
\citet{Mauersberger1996} included $\tau$~Cet and $\epsilon$~Eri among
\SbPriorNStarB{} targets at the same frequency, each covering no more than
\SbPriorBandMHz\,MHz of a single line -- \SbPriorRatio{} times less than the
union searched here -- with no search over drift rate and no
epoch-to-epoch test of persistence. Of ALMA's receiver bands only Band~3 has
been searched for technosignatures \citep{Mason2025}.\footnote{The
\SbBandOneLoGHz--\SbBandOneHiGHz\,GHz band holds observations of
\SbBoneNStar{} stars of this sample, all proprietary until \SbBoneRelYear{}
and all in a continuum mode whose channels are \SbBoneChanRatio{} times
coarser than the narrowest channel searched here, so they cannot carry a
carrier search; the band above it holds none.}

At these frequencies interstellar dispersion and scintillation are
negligible and an aperture delivers far more gain per watt, but system temperatures are
higher, the atmosphere is opaque in places at both ends of the link and the narrow beam --
\BmArcsec{} arcsec for the \BenchDiam\,m benchmark of
\S\ref{sec:mmdifferent} -- must be aimed at the Earth deliberately, so limits
are stated as EIRP, which needs no transmitter model. The archive adds
limitations of its own: the depth and resolution of each spectrum are the
original observer's; only some systems have a second,
separated epoch, so what could be searched and what could be confirmed
differ; and the targets were chosen by other programmes, so the sample
represents no population and supports no bound on the fraction of stars
hosting a transmitter \citep{Wright2018,Margot2023,EarthDetectingEarth2025}
(\S\ref{sec:bias}).

The signal sought is an \emph{unresolved spectral carrier}: emission confined
to a few channels, unresolved at the stellar position, allowed to drift as it
must for a transmitter on a rotating, orbiting body (\S\ref{sec:statistic}),
and present at more than one epoch. ``Narrowband'' describes the assumed
transmitter and not the resolving power of the archived channels, and
broadband planetary-scale leakage \citep{BRaTs2026} is outside the search by
construction. Recurrence, and not brightness, separates an interesting event
from a detection, and a signal of interest needs detailed follow-up
\citep{Sheikh2021}.

The method meets the obstacles \citet{Mason2025} identified -- drift
rates that scale with frequency, smearing unless drift is searched for, and
the molecular line forest -- with a drift search, a line
catalogue against which every event is compared, and a recurrence test in a
separated epoch; and in place of interference rejection it tests the star
against control positions inside its own primary beam, which a single dish
cannot resolve. Every window was re-reduced and re-searched from the raw
archive (\S\ref{sec:method}).

Experiments are compared here on the minimum detectable received flux
$F_{\rm min}=S_{\rm min}\,\delta\nu$ at each one's own channel width: $F_{\rm
min}$ rises as $\delta\nu^{1/2}$, so a coarse window put on a fine channel
would be credited with a depth it never reached. A limit also holds only for
a signal narrower than the native channel that set it, and those channels
span \LtChanSpanDex{} orders of magnitude, from \LtChanNarrow{} to
\LtChanWide. Figure~\ref{fig:context} draws each published threshold beside
its value rescaled to a common $\LtCommonSnr\sigma$ (\LtNRescaled{} of the
\LtNProg{} programmes need it), an arithmetic convention and not a matched
false-alarm probability (Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}); the values, with
each programme's distance, are in \texttt{figures/figure\_data.csv} of the
data archive \citep{SIdeposit}. All are nominal thresholds except this
survey's $\mathrm{EIRP}_{90}$ (open symbols), measured with injected carriers
-- the step \citet{Price2020} name as missing from their own limits and
\citet{Margot2023} measure but do not fold into theirs. None carries a
confirmation criterion, and the \NgNDecBOnly{} stars drawn as grey triangles,
covered in coarse channels alone, constrain an unresolved excess and not a
drifting carrier. Mason et~al.\ reach a median \MasonFluxMed\,W\,m$^{-2}$
(\MasonFluxLo--\MasonFluxHi{} across their four fields) in a
\MasonChanKHz\,kHz channel, against \FluxTrigBest\,W\,m$^{-2}$ in a
\FluxChanBestKHz\,kHz channel for the deepest window that lies outside the
line mask -- the only bandwidth this survey constrains
(\S\ref{sec:summary}) -- both at a bare $\LtCommonSnr\sigma$ threshold, a
factor \FluxNatBestRatio{} deeper than their median field; the median window
here is \FluxTrigMed\,W\,m$^{-2}$ in a \FluxChanMedKHz\,kHz channel, a factor
\FluxNatMedRatio{} shallower. Per channel this survey is the shallower of the
two except in its finest windows; what distinguishes it (panel~b) is its
frequency range and its \NSysClassA{} systems within 40\,pc, against distant
stars in \MasonNCal{} calibrator fields.
