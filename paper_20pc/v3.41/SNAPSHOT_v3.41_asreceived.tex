\begin{abstract}
We search the ALMA Science Archive for technosignatures from nearby
stars: every star within 40\,pc that usable public archival
observations cover (archival bycatch). This nearest-first release is
the development sample of the survey -- the detection statistic and
line mask were developed on its data -- and covers \NStars{} stars
in \NSystems{} systems through \NWindows{} windows over
\UnionGHz\,GHz of unique frequency, in which each stellar position
is compared with \NCtrl{} identically treated controls: \NFine{}
fine-resolution windows are searched for drift-resolved spectral
carriers, and the \NCoarse{} coarse windows are sensitive only to
unresolved spectral excess.
Four windows raise a statistical screening flag, not a candidate
detection: two toward $\beta$~Pic and one toward HD~48370 are
consistent with, and very likely caused by, known circumstellar or
foreground CO emission; one, toward CP$-$72~2713, is unclassified
and not significant at the survey level. No credible
technosignature is found. The searched subsample over-represents
F and G stars ($\times3.1$--$3.4$) and under-represents M dwarfs
($\times0.6$--$0.7$) relative to the 40\,pc stellar census.
Trigger thresholds -- the transmitter powers at which a signal
first exceeds the screening test, not completeness-corrected
limits -- span $\EirpMin$ to $\EirpMax$\,W equivalent isotropic
radiated power at native channel resolution, deep enough, toward
the nearest systems, for an unresolved carrier matching the total
power of an Arecibo-like planetary radar (a power scale, not a
matched transmitter model). Completeness is measured on a single
fine-channel configuration and transferred by assumption, and its
transfer error is unbounded; for the unresolved-excess class,
completeness at the detection threshold is measured across
\CompWin{} window configurations spanning a factor \CompNintFactor{}
in integration count. The survey
constrains nothing outside its searched windows and archival
epochs; the statistic is frozen before the remaining,
pre-registered confirmatory datasets are searched.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A technological civilisation, signalling deliberately or simply going
about its affairs, may leave a detectable imprint on the electromagnetic
spectrum: a \emph{technosignature} \citep{Sheikh2020,SETIreview2025}.
Six decades of radio searches now cover many thousands of stars yet, as
\citet{Wright2018} quantified, sample only an infinitesimal fraction of
the multi-dimensional space of transmitter frequency, bandwidth, power
and duty cycle -- \HaystackFrac{} of the haystack's volume. Three
gaps persist: the millimetre and submillimetre
regime above ${\sim}50$\,GHz is essentially unsearched; most surveys
target interest-selected classes of star, not the local stellar
population; and few systematically reprocess \emph{existing}
interferometric archives.

\medskip
\noindent\rule{\columnwidth}{0.4pt}
\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Status of statistical inference in this release.}
\emph{(i)~The \NStarBands{} datasets processed here are the
development sample of the \NPlanned{}-dataset survey. (ii)~The
symmetric detection statistic and the $\pm50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$
molecular-line mask were both defined, and the statistic once
revised, using these data; the revision was decided on its
algebraic merits before any flagged window was re-examined
(\S\ref{sec:aumic}, Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}). (iii)~Every
rank probability and false-alarm figure in this release is
therefore retrospective and descriptive of the development sample,
not an independent validation of the pipeline false-positive rate;
``confirmed calibration'' is reserved for the confirmatory stage.
(iv)~The remaining datasets form the frozen, held-out
confirmatory sample, to be searched under the frozen statistic,
mask and thresholds (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}).}
\par\smallskip
\noindent\rule{\columnwidth}{0.4pt}
\medskip

This paper addresses all three, asking how strongly existing ALMA
observations can constrain unresolved artificial spectral emission
from nearby stellar systems. In plain terms, what follows
characterises the stars ALMA has happened to observe, not the
40\,pc population as a whole: the searched subsample over-represents
F and G stars and under-represents M dwarfs (\S\ref{sec:sample}), and
every limit below inherits that bias. We take an ALMA archive-complete sample: every star within 40\,pc of
the Sun with public ALMA archival coverage, irrespective of spectral
type, disc-hosting status or known planets, and mine it for
technosignatures in unresolved spectral carriers -- channel-confined
excess at native ALMA resolution, with ``narrowband'' reserved for
the assumed intrinsically narrow transmitter, not for the resolving
power of the data (\S\ref{sec:method}) -- and for anomalous millimetre continuum,
correcting for proper motion and Doppler drift across the adopted
search domain.
The processing sets the search class, and \S\ref{sec:dwell} measures
it: a continuous narrowband beacon is the best case, short-duty and
fast-drifting emitters the worst; coarse-channel windows retain
sensitivity to persistent unresolved excess power only
(\S\ref{sec:method}).
Four archival-specific analyses accompany the classical search --
closure-phase vetting, a continuum anomaly screen, a chirp and
periodicity re-analysis, a cross-target frequency-occupancy check --
of which only the continuum screen enters dispositions; the others
are capability demonstrations (\S\ref{sec:ancillary},
Table~\ref{tab:supp}). One worked case runs through the paper: a
crossing towards $\beta$~Pictoris, the release's largest
star-versus-control contrast, attributed to the disc's known
circumstellar CO across two transitions and three epochs
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}) -- the machinery exists to make that
attribution routine. Per-target tables are
in Appendix~\ref{app:table}; dispositions, false-alarm accounting,
injection completeness and the occurrence machinery consolidate in
Tables~\ref{tab:searchspace} and~\ref{tab:flagged} with
Appendices~\ref{app:falsealarm} and~\ref{app:inject}. We
publish this first release at \PctPlanned{} per cent completion
because the citable content is methodological -- the archive-conditioned
archive-complete sample definition,
the empirically estimated local false-alarm machinery, the measured
completeness. A first set of per-target limits follows, every number
tied to a frozen snapshot that later releases supersede row by row.
``The survey'' is the full \NPlanned-dataset programme; ``this
release'' its \NStarBands{} processed datasets.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

These six decades -- from \citet{CocconiMorrison1959} and
\citet{Drake1960}, through the targeted programmes reviewed by
\citet{Tarter2001} and Project Phoenix \citep{Backus2002} -- divide
into wide-field commensal surveys and targeted
programmes, despite physical
reasons to go higher: interstellar scattering and dispersion fall
steeply with frequency, and a given aperture delivers a tighter beam
per watt of EIRP. Nor is artificial mm-wave emission physically
exotic -- directional communication, radar and beamed power
transmission are all engineered coherent emission at millimetre
wavelengths already, on scales from spacecraft links to laboratory
sources -- so plausibility needs no assumption that anyone operates
Arecibo-scale transmitters at hundreds of gigahertz.
\citep{Zhang2020,Czech2021,Morrison2023,FAST3IATLAS2025,LOFARdualsite2023,SRT2025,Tremblay2024};
machine-learning RFI rejection is now central
\citep{Ma2023,Choza2024,GLOBULAR2025,AnomalyParkesGBT2025,
BRaTs2026} -- a machine-learning capability our classical threshold
search lacks. As \citet{Margot2023} note -- a separate, statistical
point -- a non-detection bounds the transmitter
fraction only if the sample represents the underlying population,
which interest-selected samples do not. Essentially all this activity
lies below ${\sim}10$\,GHz; above it the mm/submm regime has been
searched once, also with ALMA \citep{Mason2024}, despite physical
reasons to go higher: interstellar scattering and dispersion fall
steeply with frequency, and a given aperture delivers a tighter beam
per watt of EIRP. Nor is artificial mm-wave emission physically
exotic -- directional communication, radar and beamed power
transmission are all engineered coherent emission at millimetre
wavelengths already, on scales from spacecraft links to laboratory
sources -- so plausibility needs no assumption that anyone operates
Arecibo-scale transmitters at hundreds of gigahertz. The countervailing costs are real -- atmospheric
transmission windows and receiver noise worsen, a carrier costs more
photons per watt per hertz, diffraction-limited pointing and phase
stability tighten correspondingly, and no demonstrated transmitter
technology delivers mm-wave radar powers -- they price the search, not
forbid it.

\citet{Mason2024} searched archival Band~3 windows toward 28
``stellar bycatch'' stars -- stars observed for other science that a
search can use for free -- placing EIRP$_{\rm min} >
7\times10^{17}$\,W for the nearest and identifying high-frequency
work's challenges: drift rates scale with frequency, smearing erodes
sensitivity without a drift search, and the molecular line forest
confuses. Our pilot (\citealp{White2026}; under review, citation
provisional, manuscript available from the authors on editorial
request) extended that methodology to TRAPPIST-1 across Bands 3, 6
and 7 (EIRP$_{\rm min}\sim5$--$10\times10^{13}$\,W, no detection) and
built the ranked 100-star list this survey grew from; its pipeline is
that design applied to one target, without the
archive-complete sample selection, control-ring calibration or completeness machinery
introduced here. New here
are the archive-complete sample selection (\S\ref{sec:sample}), the \NCtrl-position
control-ring local false-alarm estimation, closure-phase vetting, the
spectral-type-normalised continuum screen, the chirp and periodicity
extension and the cross-target frequency-occupancy check -- each new,
to our knowledge, \emph{in an ALMA archival technosignature survey},
though we claim no wider priority over their centimetre-wave
counterparts.

A non-detection must become a quantitative exclusion statement.
\citet{Wright2018} formalised the ``Cosmic Haystack'' search volume,
\citet{Margot2023} its conversion into an upper bound on the
transmitter-hosting fraction, and \citet{EarthDetectingEarth2025} asked where Earth's technosignatures
would be detectable -- a calibration point for any survey's sensitivity.

Hence the case for an archive-complete sample over interest-selected
lists. Most surveys
select by proximity, spectral type, planets or Earth Transit Zone
membership; the ALMA-specific form, disc selection, skews the sample
young and dynamically active. We instead take
every star within 40\,pc in the usable field of at least one public
ALMA observation (\S\ref{sec:sample}): a ``bycatch'' sample -- stars
in the archive because another programme observed their field, not
because anyone selected them for a search -- of the ALMA-observed
subset of the local stellar population in a fixed volume, not of that
population itself. This removes
technosignature-specific but not archival astrophysical selection; our
\NCensus{} stars are ${\sim}1$ per cent of the catalogued population
within 40\,pc, and that residual coverage selection is characterised
as a completeness function (\S\ref{sec:sample},
Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}).

\section{Sample selection}
\label{sec:sample}

Our sample is every star within 40\,pc of the Sun (Gaia~DR3 parallaxes, distances direct inversions $d = 1/\pi$; every entry $\pi \ge 25$\,mas with $\sigma_\pi/\pi \le 0.8$ per cent) whose
proper-motion-corrected position at the observation epoch falls in the
field of view of at least one public ALMA execution block, not
necessarily at the pointing centre: mosaics and wide pointings
serendipitously cover more than one catalogued star. Disc, planet and
spectral type are descriptive metadata here, not selection criteria.
Throughout, a \emph{star} is one catalogue entry and a \emph{system}
the set of entries bound to it; a \emph{target/band} dataset is one
star in one ALMA band; an \emph{execution block} (EB) one archival
observation; and a \emph{window}
is one spectral window of one execution block. This gives \NCensus{}
unique target stars, processed nearest-first; because trigger
thresholds scale with $d^{2}$ at fixed noise, the order is
sensitivity-ranked, not an unbiased draw, and the prevalence
statements of \S\ref{sec:discussion} are conditional on it.

Composition follows from the archival selection, and is tabulated
against the reference census in Table~\ref{tab:selfunc} rather than
asserted here: of the searched stars carrying a Gaia temperature, 41
per cent are M~dwarfs against 69 per cent of the reference census,
while F and G stars are over-represented by factors of 3.4 and 3.1.
The archive prefers earlier types and nearby systems. The conditional
transmitter-fraction limits of \S\ref{sec:discussion} therefore apply
to this archivally selected mix, which under-represents the M dwarfs
dominating the local volume -- a mismatch of astrobiological priority,
not statistical validity: the non-detection bears on the
ALMA-observed, F/G-enriched mix of Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}, not
preferentially on habitable-zone or M-dwarf prevalence. Eighteen of the \NStars{} processed stars are confirmed exoplanet hosts
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}); the debris-disc hosts ($\beta$~Pic, AU~Mic, $\epsilon$~Eri, $\tau$~Cet)
contribute dusty continua, kept from masquerading as anomalies by the
continuum lane's spectral-type-normalised screen
(\S\ref{sec:method}). Window counts are likewise concentrated:
$\beta$~Pic (\ConcBpicWin{} windows), AU~Mic (\ConcAumicWin{}) and
TRAPPIST-1 (\ConcTrapWin{}) alone carry \ConcThreeWin{} of the
\NWindows{} searched windows (\ConcThreePct{} per cent) from
\ConcThreeSys{} systems, so window-weighted occurrence statements
lean on a few heavily observed young, active or disc-hosting systems.

The \NCensus{} stars are complete with respect to, and conditional only on, the 40-parsec
reference catalogue intersected with the public-archive snapshot (2026
September~8) defining this release.
Against that Gaia~DR3 solar-neighbourhood catalogue
(17\,566 stars within
40\,pc passing the parallax-quality cut of Table~\ref{tab:selfunc})
they are ${\sim}1$\%; that list is itself incomplete at both ends, so
the denominator is a convention and the fraction says only that
coverage is small. Table~\ref{tab:selfunc} leaves the selection
function inspectable. Age and disc-hosting flags are not uniformly available
and are documented qualitatively; a machine-readable
selection-function table ships with the release.

Stellar counts throughout are catalogue-entry counts: the \NCensus{}
sample entries comprise \NSystems{} independent systems among those
searched -- seven designation-linked component pairs (SIMBAD
designations; per-entry rows, distances and unresolved-pair notes in
Appendix~\ref{app:table}). Since the 44 spectrally covered
entries include the unresolved UV~Ceti and AT~Mic pairs, the
\NStarBands{} star/band datasets, \NWindows{} windows and \NEB{}
execution blocks derive from \NSystems{} systems; the paired rows
are bookkeeping, not independent trials.

``Usable public ALMA coverage'' is a five-criterion decision tree,
frozen for this release absent an erratum: (i) at least one public
execution block (EB) whose QA2-calibrated visibilities -- QA2 being
the observatory pipeline's calibrated, quality-scored data products --
cover the
star's epoch-propagated position in the primary beam, with no minimum
integration, sensitivity, antenna count, mosaic-tile count or array
configuration beyond QA2 itself; (ii) that position within one primary-beam FWHM of the field centre (largest accepted offset
${\sim}0.7$ FWHMs, Sirius~B\footnote{Retained with bounded 3.6--16
per cent primary-beam corrections; its EIRP values therefore carry a
systematic the holography-beam recomputation, not yet in the released
snapshot (Data Availability), will settle
(\S\ref{sec:frames}).}); (iii) each spectral window admitted
individually, on the pipeline's quality vetting alone; (iv) no
programme-category restriction, science, calibration and observatory
projects entering on identical terms; (v) a fixed archive snapshot
date (2026 September~8), re-evaluated only at release boundaries. No
star or window was excluded by an unlisted criterion, none for short
integration (the shortest retained are 1.0 and 1.5\,min).

ALMA project metadata quantify pointed versus serendipitous coverage:
almost every spectral-window-covered star is the pointed science target in
at least one execution block, the exceptions a few blocks on another programme's field or
calibrator-only, leaving Wolf~219 the sole genuinely serendipitous star. An archive-complete sample's residual
selection function is the union of ALMA target lists -- a contrast
with interest-selected samples of degree, but of \emph{quantified}
degree: a bycatch fraction of 1 star in 44, 1 EB in 57.

\section{Data and methodology}
\label{sec:method}

Survey vocabulary -- target/band dataset, spectral window, execution
block, control ring, candidate, molecular-line exclusion mask -- is
defined in Table~\ref{tab:glossary}, Appendix~\ref{app:glossary}.

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarises the design: one shared
archive-ingestion stage feeds two independent search lanes. For each
target we mine, within a bounded budget, all calibrated (or locally
recalibrated) archival execution blocks covering the star's
Gaia-propagated position, and search their visibilities in (i) the
\emph{primary} lane, a Doppler-drift-corrected spectral-carrier search following
\citet{Mason2024} and \citet{Enriquez2017}, converting each
non-detection into an equivalent isotropic radiated power limit $\mathrm{EIRP_{5\sigma}} = 4\pi d^{2} S_{\rm min}
\Delta\nu$ ($S_{\rm min}$ five times the searched window's per-channel
rms, primary-beam corrected; $\Delta\nu$ its native channel width; $d$
the target distance; a fully worked numerical example in
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). The quoted thresholds are nominal: their
systematic budget -- the ALMA absolute flux scale (5--10 per cent,
band- and epoch-dependent), the primary-beam model at the stellar
offset (bounded in \S\ref{sec:technosearch}), and visibility
calibration -- multiplies $S_{\rm min}$ and hence
EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ directly, while \emph{Gaia} distances contribute
negligibly at these distances. The same visibilities feed (ii) an \emph{ancillary} line-excluded
continuum lane (Appendix~\ref{app:continuum}). ``5$\sigma$''
throughout is the local instrumental \emph{trigger} threshold of
Table~\ref{tab:nomenclature}, separately and empirically estimated
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). ``Narrowband'' is reserved for the assumed
intrinsic signal morphology, never for the spectral resolving power
of the archival observations: in the majority of windows an
intrinsically Hz-wide carrier is unresolved within a 15.625\,MHz
channel, and the data could not demonstrate that a one-channel excess
is intrinsically narrow. The search is accordingly reported as two
formal survey classes, distinguished by the dimensionless drift
parameter
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\rm drift} \;=\; |\dot\nu_{\max}|\,T\,/\,\Delta\nu_{\rm ch},
\label{eq:etadrift}
\end{equation}
the channel-width fraction the \emph{maximum} searched drift
$|\dot\nu_{\max}|$ traverses over an integration $T$ at native
channel width $\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$: a window is \emph{drift-resolving}
when $\eta_{\rm drift}\gtrsim1$, only then carrying a carrier across
enough channels for its motion to be measured rather than assumed.
The drift ceiling $|\dot\nu_{\max}|$ inside $\eta_{\rm drift}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:etadrift}) is a
boundary of the search grid -- the widest trial drift the stack
evaluates -- not a physical prior on transmitter drift rates: carriers
drifting faster are simply missed, and the resulting selection
function is made explicit only in
\S\ref{sec:detectable} (Table~\ref{tab:searchspace},
Fig.~\ref{fig:accel}).
\emph{Class~A}, the drift-resolved spectral-carrier search: the
\NFine{} windows of fine
channelisation, native widths \ChanAMinKHz--\ChanAMaxKHz\,kHz (median
\ChanAMedKHz\,kHz; \ChanAModePct{} per cent at the \ChanAModeKHz-kHz
mode), spanning $\eta_{\rm drift}=\EtaAmin$--$\EtaAmax$ (median
\EtaAmed) -- the only class with measured end-to-end recovery of a
drifting carrier. \emph{Class~B}, the unresolved spectral-excess search: the
\NCoarse{} coarse windows (73 per cent of the sample), native widths
\ChanBMinMHz--\ChanBMaxMHz\,MHz (\ChanBModeN{} windows at the
\ChanBModeMHz-MHz mode), spanning $\eta_{\rm drift}=\EtaBmin$--$\EtaBmax$
(median \EtaBmed) -- sensitive to persistent near-stationary excess
power, with no survey-threshold completeness curve and no drift
discrimination. Class membership is one pipeline variable, the native
channelisation, and it is bimodal: no window falls between
\ChanAMaxKHz\,kHz and \ChanBMinMHz\,MHz, an empty gap of factor
\ChanGapFac{} (one \ChanStraddleKHz-kHz window sits on the Class~A
side), so any boundary inside the gap assigns identical membership --
figures label the classes by name, never by a boundary frequency. The
physical criterion behind the split is $\eta_{\rm drift}$: only
\EtaBgeOne{} long-integration Class~B windows reach $\eta_{\rm
drift}\geq1$, and then only marginally (maximum \EtaBgeOneMax), where
one-to-two channel traverses still support no discriminative drift
fit after baseline removal; conversely \EtaAltOne{} short Class~A
windows fall below unity and are, in drift terms, conservatively
treated as Class~A anyway. Results are given per class first and combined only
where statistically appropriate (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). Every
limit, disposition, false-alarm
calibration and completeness statement derives from the spectral-carrier
lane; the chirp/periodicity extension and molecular-line census are in
Appendices~\ref{app:extended} and~\ref{app:linecat}.

Both lanes exclude catalogued molecular transitions at tolerances
suited to their statistics. The continuum lane masks the eight common
species (CO, $^{13}$CO, C$^{18}$O, HCN, HCO$^{+}$, CS, CN, SiO) at
native resolution before integrating; the carrier lane applies a
$\pm50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame velocity exclusion mask
(\S\S\ref{sec:frames},~\ref{sec:technosearch}) to any threshold crossing. The mask is \emph{excluded search space}: the unmasked
survey covers \UnionGHz\,GHz of unique sky frequency, the effective
default quoted throughout is the ${\approx}\EffBandGHz$\,GHz
surviving the mask, and no constraint is placed on carriers inside
the excluded velocity regions (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}). Masking
reduces the frequency space a candidate can occupy, not the
per-channel noise, so no threshold in the per-target file changes. All
flux densities and EIRP thresholds are total intensity
(Stokes~$I$), formed from the two parallel-hand cross-correlations
the archive delivers; no cross-hand ($V$/$Q$/$U$) extraction or
circularity test was performed. The construction is nonetheless
polarisation-blind in amplitude: $I$ is the summed parallel-hand
power, so for fixed total transmitter power the response to a
strongly circular or linear artificial signal equals that to an
unpolarised one, to within instrumental cross-polarisation leakage
(a few per cent); what full Stokes analysis would add is
discrimination against weakly polarised astrophysical backgrounds,
not depth (\S\ref{sec:futurework}).

\medskip
\noindent\rule{\columnwidth}{0.4pt}
\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{How to read every limit in this paper.}
\emph{Every EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ value here is a nominal, local
instrumental detection threshold -- the \emph{trigger power}
$P_{\rm trig}$: five times the per-channel rms of
the searched window, at that window's native channel width, for a transmitter at the star's position matching the searched
spectral morphology, with a measured recovery that rises with the
fraction of the track the carrier occupies its channel and is highest
for a continuous one (\S\ref{sec:ancillary},
Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). It is not a calibrated completeness
limit: completeness is the power $P_x$ at which recovery reaches $x$
per cent. $P_{50}=1.10\,P_{\rm trig}$ and $P_{90}>2\,P_{\rm trig}$ for
the calibration configuration's drifting class; for every other
configuration $P_{50}$ and $P_{90}$ are \emph{not determined} -- the
single measured curve is transferred by assumption
(\S\ref{sec:null}) -- so $P_{\rm trig}$ is quoted throughout
rather than any completeness level. It
is a total-power threshold set by ALMA's coarse native
channelisation, not a Hz-resolution narrowband sensitivity. The same
reading applies to every subsequent ``5$\sigma$'' and to the
per-target file (Appendix~\ref{app:table}); it is stated once here and
not repeated at each occurrence.}

\subsection{The search statistic, and the words used for its output}
\label{sec:statistic}

The primary statistic is formed on extracted spectra, not images or
visibilities. The extraction operates on the QA2-calibrated
measurement set as delivered: per integration and per native channel,
with the archive's QA2 flagging carried through untouched and no
spectral regridding or interpolation at any stage
(Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}, step~3); it returns the Stokes~$I$
intensity at the propagated sky position $\mathbf{x}$, formed from
the two parallel-hand correlations (\S\ref{sec:method}). Baseline
removal is applied \emph{after} extraction (step~4), and the
primary-beam correction enters the quoted thresholds and fluxes
(\S\ref{sec:frames}) rather than the extracted amplitudes the
statistic ranks. The estimator's visibility-domain weighting is a
fixed property of the released code (Data Availability), which
itemises the quantities consumed and emitted per window and is the
reproducibility reference for steps 1--3. For a sky position $\mathbf{x}$, each per-integration
spectrum is baseline-subtracted by a running median along the
\emph{frequency} axis -- there is no temporal median at any stage
(\S\ref{sec:ancillary}) -- the residual dynamic
spectrum is stacked along each trial linear drift rate $\dot\nu$, and the
statistic is the largest resulting single-channel amplitude in units
of the window's noise,
%
\begin{equation}
T(\mathbf{x})=\max_{\dot\nu,\;\nu}\;
\frac{S(\mathbf{x},\nu,\dot\nu)}{\sigma(\mathbf{x})},
\label{eq:tstar}
\end{equation}
%
where $S$ is the drift-stacked flux density in one native channel and
$\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ a single robust scale per position and window,
estimated as $1.4826\times$ the median absolute deviation of that
position's baseline-subtracted spectrum over all channels: local in
position, global in frequency. The baseline median's width $W$ is
likewise a single fixed channel count for all \NWindows{} windows,
independent of native channel width, so the linewidth ceiling it
imposes scales in frequency with each window's channelisation
(Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). The crossing channel is \emph{not}
excluded from the scale -- standard for a median-based estimator,
whose 50-per-cent breakdown point makes a single-channel feature
immaterial over a window's hundreds of channels; the per-window
local recomputation below excludes it explicitly.
$T_\star \equiv T(\mathbf{x}_\star)$ at the proper-motion-propagated
stellar position; the same operation, drift grid, channelisation and noise estimator give
$\{T_i\}$ at all \NCtrl{} control positions, which lie on a single
ring centred on the target, evenly spaced in azimuth
($360/\NCtrl=0.70$ degrees apart), at a radius well inside the primary
beam but many synthesised beams from the target. Neighbouring controls
are correlated below the synthesised-beam scale, so a window's ring
carries $N_{\rm eff}\simeq30$--$43$ independent spatial trials, as the
symmetric reprocessing runs measure directly
(Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}). Ranking $T_\star$ against \NCtrl{}
values fixes the smallest per-window $p$-value the design can
express, $1/\RankFloor$: the ring estimates the local false-alarm rate but
cannot authenticate an individual candidate, and the survey-wide
Bonferroni reference $\BonferroniThresh$ lies two orders of magnitude
below the floor (component~v, \S\ref{sec:technosearch}).
Table~\ref{tab:algorithm} sets out the data path in order,
so the statistic can be reimplemented without the appendices.

Being global in frequency, that scale can misstate the noise
\emph{local} to a crossing wherever a window carries bandpass-edge
structure, atmospheric features or a line forest. The retained products keep the per-window scale but not the per-channel
spectra, so no local estimate around a crossing can be recomputed for
this release survey-wide -- but for the four flags we have now
measured it. Re-extracting their retained per-integration spectra and
recomputing the scale locally, within a band around the feature and
excluding the feature itself, gives local-to-global ratios of 0.97
(CP$-$72~2713), 1.29 (HD~48370), 0.93 ($\beta$~Pic~B3) and 1.02
($\beta$~Pic~B6). The global scale is thus accurate to a few per cent
except towards HD~48370, where the local value sits 29 per cent higher
because that field is filled with CO: rescaling both the statistic and
its ring by the local value leaves it flagged
($T_\star=\HdCorrectedT$ against a corrected ring maximum of
\HdCorrectedRing) -- the disposition already assigned to it. For CP$-$72~2713 the local scale is slightly
\emph{lower}, so its excess is marginally understated rather than
inflated, and no disposition moves. The retained products keep no
drift-stacked cube, so this tests the noise scale and not $T_\star$
itself; a per-crossing local scale inside the search is a
next-release item (\S\ref{sec:futurework}). The same estimator runs at
the star and at all \NCtrl{} control positions, so a badly misstated
$\sigma$ would displace the control distribution, and the pseudo-star
ranks are uniform in every band and at both channelisations
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}) -- the one departing stratum is the fine-channel one, and that departure
is the four flagged windows themselves.

The output vocabulary nests strictly (Table~\ref{tab:nomenclature}); no
object of this release is a candidate.

The searched drift-rate range is the larger of (a) a generic
frequency-scaled default (12--13\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$), the \citet{Mason2024} fiducial with a threefold safety margin -- a
design boundary, not a claim that faster drift is implausible -- and
(b) for confirmed exoplanet hosts, the maximum line-of-sight orbital
acceleration of the innermost known planet, so a transmitter with a
short-period planet cannot drift out of the window. Drift rate maps
onto line-of-sight acceleration through the first-order Doppler
relation
%
\begin{equation}
a_{\rm los} \;=\; c\,\frac{\dot\nu}{\nu},
\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
%
so the ceiling is most compactly quoted in cross-band-comparable form
$\dot\nu/\nu$: 12--13\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$ is
$\dot\nu/\nu = (1.2$--$1.3)\times10^{-8}$\,s$^{-1}$, i.e.\
$|a_{\rm los}| = 3.6$--$3.9$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:drift}),
independent of observing
frequency -- the acceleration at ${\sim}0.04$\,au around a solar-type
star. Earth-motion contributions
($\lesssim0.13$\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$) lie two orders of magnitude
below, subsumed by the grid. The grid itself is uniform in $\dot\nu$,
spanning the searched range in $n_{\rm drift}$ steps (2--1944 across
windows, recorded per window in the released file), so the
worst-case mismatch -- a carrier midway between trial rates -- leaves
a residual drift that traverses at most \DriftGridFineHalf{} of one
native channel over the longest fine-class track
(\DriftGridCoarseHalf{} over the coarse class): the mismatched
carrier stays within a single channel of its trial track throughout,
and the stacked-amplitude loss is bounded by that sub-channel
smearing alone, an order below the recovery slope measured between
$5\sigma$ and $10\sigma$. The injection campaign injected on grid
points; continuous randomisation between grid points is pre-registered
with the stratified campaign of \S\ref{sec:futurework}.
Table~\ref{tab:orbital} grounds the
ceiling physically: a transmitter co-orbiting an Earth analogue trails
it by three orders of magnitude, Proxima~b sits well inside, and the
innermost TRAPPIST-1 planets sit at or beyond the adopted domain --
very short-period systems are its sacrificial edge. The domain is a
search definition, not a physical prior: an artificial emitter need
not be tied to a known planet at all (a free-flying platform, rotating
structure or deliberately chirped carrier can accelerate as it
chooses), and such emitters are unconstrained, not excluded.
These are \emph{apparent} topocentric drift rates, including deliberate
transmitted chirp and acceleration-induced drift, which the survey does
not distinguish; our
constraints concern linear apparent drift within the searched range
only.

\subsection{Frequency and velocity reference frames}
\label{sec:frames}

Every frequency axis here -- searched spectra, tabulated ranges,
candidate frequencies -- is \emph{topocentric}, as delivered by the ALMA correlator, no velocity-frame shift applied to
any data product at any stage. Velocities enter only in the vetting stage's
stellar-frame line-exclusion mask, whose conversion chain is in
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}. The stacking is coherent in frequency
and in the drift-rate trial but preserves no electric-field phase
coherence between integrations.

The native channel width is also the linewidth convention for every
EIRP threshold here -- the commonest confusion when comparing
technosignature limits across surveys. The derivation is explicit:
for a transmitter intrinsically narrower than the channel
($\delta\nu_{\rm tx}\ll\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$), channel dilution spreads
its total line flux $F_{\rm line}$ across the channel,
$S_{\nu,\rm ch}=F_{\rm line}/\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$; triggering requires
$S_{\nu,\rm ch}\geq S_{\min}$, so the minimum detectable total power
is $4\pi d^{2}S_{\nu,\rm ch}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$ -- independent of
$\delta\nu_{\rm tx}$ below the channel width. EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ is thus
the minimum \emph{total} power of a channel-confined carrier, not a
spectral power density; dividing by $\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}$ converts to
the minimum detectable spectral power for wider transmitters (a
$10^{15}$\,W threshold at 15.625-MHz channelisation is
$6.4\times10^{7}$\,W\,Hz$^{-1}$, and the same division applies
wherever the two quantities appear together below). The 1-Hz and
3-Hz limits of Hz-resolution surveys are total powers in the same
sense, each the best case of its own channelisation, which is what
makes the axes of Fig.~\ref{fig:context} commensurable at all.
Channelisation is the dominant price the archive exacts, but we
deliberately do not project an Hz-resolution sensitivity: a
radiometer-equation scaling $P_{\min}\propto\sqrt{\Delta\nu}$ across
six orders of magnitude in spectral resolution is a thought
experiment, not an achievable ALMA sensitivity, and quoting it invites
the misreading this paper avoids. The worked example (UV~Ceti,
Band~3), sub-channel
qualifications, smearing table and channel-width verification are in
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}.

The spectral-carrier search covers every spectral window of an observation,
capped at 8 per target to bound compute cost; the cap bound exactly
one target/band (61~Vir Band~7: four of eight windows searched),
whose frequency-space selection function is therefore tabulated. Of
the \NStarBands{} star/band datasets, 53 have every configured window
searched; the remaining 3 ($\tau$~Cet, Kapteyn's Star, Van~Maanen's
Star) are represented by their deepest single window, marked in
Appendix~\ref{app:table}, full multi-window coverage awaiting the next
release. Each searched window yields its own EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ row;
Figure~\ref{fig:eirp} plots only the deepest per target/band.

Velocity coincidence, not drift, is the operative discriminant when a
crossing is checked against the eight masked species
(\S\ref{sec:method}): circumstellar emission sits at rest offset by
the gas's kinematics, whereas a technosignature may appear at any
frequency and drift rate. The searches as executed used a narrower
one-channel-width implementation, agreeing with the velocity-space
form on every disposition here; its justification and the bandwidth
removed are in \S\ref{sec:technosearch}.

Both carrier-lane thresholds and continuum fluxes are corrected for
ALMA's primary-beam response at the star's offset from the phase
centre, negligibly for most targets (median factor 1.0001, 90th
percentile 1.012, none above 2\%). Two sit at large offset.
$\epsilon$\,Eri Band~6 lies at ${\sim}0.7$ primary-beam FWHM, where
the correction reaches $2.9$--$3.4\times$ and the Gaussian beam form
fails; rather than publish provisional values we \emph{withhold} those
four windows, which enter no number here but are released, flagged, in
the per-target file and return once the holography beam model is
applied (that recomputation is not yet in the released snapshot, Data
Availability). Sirius~B is retained and quoted as such: corrections
3.6--16 per cent across bands, a bounded systematic rather than an
invalid model. Excluding it too would change neither endpoint of the
quoted EIRP range, nor the median ($1.3\to1.4\times10^{15}$\,W), nor
any conclusion.

\subsection{Ancillary and validation analyses}
\label{sec:ancillary}

Four analyses beyond the spectral-carrier search are executed here, each
specified in full in Table~\ref{tab:supp} and summarised only as
far as the main text depends on it.

\emph{Injection-recovery validation} (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}).
Synthetic tones of known amplitude were injected into real
visibilities and recovered with the unmodified pipeline (\NTrials{}
trials across six window configurations, three bands and both
resolution classes; trial-level records were not retained for that
campaign, and the dwell campaign of \S\ref{sec:dwell} now supplies
them). The pipeline performs no temporal subtraction at any stage:
its baseline step is a per-integration running median along
\emph{frequency} (Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}, step~4), which by
construction cannot remove a feature confined to one or two channels,
and its de-drift step is an inverse-variance-weighted sum over
integrations, which accumulates a persistent carrier coherently.
One validation-history item must be stated plainly: the campaign's
original report of 0 of 500 coarse-window recoveries was an artefact
of its recovery \emph{criterion}, not of the search pipeline -- a
drift-matching clause that could not be satisfied on coarse windows
by construction. It was caught by a quantitative bound, not by
inspection: the all-zero return arrived at injected amplitudes of
$20$--$200\sigma$, where the machinery-only lane's measured amplitude
fidelity of $-9$/$+14$ per cent guarantees a detection, so no working
criterion can return zero there; that impossibility isolated the
fault to the criterion. The identification is logged as a pre-freeze
validation change (Table~\ref{tab:validation},
Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}), its wall-clock record the same
versioned audit trail as the statistic revision of
\S\ref{sec:aumic}. On code paths, stated plainly: the campaign and
the machinery-only lane share one pipeline and one trial list; the
faulty clause sat in the criterion applied to that pipeline's
output, so the lane is the same path with the clause's decision step
bypassed -- an internal control isolating the clause, not an
independent implementation. The corrected criterion has not been
re-derived by an independently written implementation even on a
subset of trials; it is retained as a regression fixture, and the
cross-check is recorded as an open item (\S\ref{sec:futurework}).
The independent evidence is the dwell campaign of \S\ref{sec:dwell},
whose post-baseline detection criterion is separately derived and
which measures persistent, near-static carriers as \emph{recovered},
not suppressed (Table~\ref{tab:dwell}) -- the reading the artefact
had reversed. The primary search statistic has no
automated injection test on every pipeline change; three quantified
checks bound undetected pipeline faults instead: the machinery-only
lane (fidelity $-9$/$+14$ per cent -- the bound that caught this
artefact), the AU~Mic re-analyses of Table~\ref{tab:aumic} (three
treatments, recomputation agreeing with the released products), and
the pseudo-star rank uniformity (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}: \NPseudo{}
pooled ranks uniform, mean \PseudoMean{} against 0.5, KS
$p=\PseudoKSp$), which any defect of the statistic itself would
displace. For the drifting class
on the fine window recovery is 17, 42, 58, 75 and 83 per cent at
$4$, $5$, $6$, $8$ and $10\sigma$ -- measured on the single
calibration configuration (one star, one Band~6 window) and
transferred by assumption to all \NFine{} fine windows, an error the
bracketing of \S\ref{sec:null} treats as a 0.5--2$\times$
rescaling -- giving EIRP$_{50}=1.10\,$EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ and, as a
bound, EIRP$_{90}>2.0\,$EIRP$_{5\sigma}$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:completeness}, Table~\ref{tab:classcomp}). Tables here
stay in nominal EIRP$_{5\sigma}$; the conditional in-band transmitter
fraction of \S\ref{sec:discussion} is the one place measured
completeness is folded in, Band~8 completeness being transferred by
shared construction, not measured.

\subsection{Dwell-fraction completeness}
\label{sec:dwell}

The searched class is defined by how long a carrier occupies one
native channel, so we measure recovery against that directly. A
carrier was injected into the retained per-integration spectra of
\NDwellWin{} real windows, six coarse and six fine, at amplitudes of
2--20 times the per-integration noise and dwell fractions
$f_{\rm dwell}=0.10$--1.00, six realisations each: \NDwellTrials{}
trials, released with one row per injection
(Table~\ref{tab:dwell}). Its detection criterion -- recovered when
the logged per-integration S/N reaches five -- was independently
re-derived from the released rows: over \XcheckN{} trials and
\XcheckWin{} windows the rule and the logged flag agree
\XcheckAgree{} times (\XcheckMismatch{} mismatches; highest
unrecovered S/N~\XcheckFalseMax, lowest recovered
S/N~\XcheckTrueMin{} -- a clean separating gap). Recovery
\emph{rises} monotonically with dwell. Pooling amplitudes at or above $4\sigma$, coarse windows
recover \DwellCoarseOne{} per cent of continuous carriers,
\DwellCoarseQ{} per cent at $f_{\rm dwell}=0.25$ and \DwellCoarseT{}
per cent at 0.10; fine windows give \DwellFineOne, \DwellFineQ{} and
\DwellFineT{} per cent.

An end-to-end check confirms it through the unmodified pipeline rather
than a re-implementation. Injecting a zero-drift, always-on tone into
the calibrated visibilities of a coarse Band~7 window at 1, 3 and 10
times the per-integration rms and running the released extraction and
search returns $T_\star = 20.5$, $65.3$ and $217.7$, recovered within
one channel of the injected frequency and flagged as detections in
every case. For 639 integrations the ideal coherent gain is
$\sqrt{639}=25$, so a carrier at the per-integration noise level
should reach ${\sim}25\sigma$: the measured 20.5 is that, less
weighting and flagging losses. Nothing is being suppressed.

Three distinctions discipline how these numbers are read.
\emph{Injection point}: the dwell campaign injects into retained
per-integration spectra, after the baseline step of
Table~\ref{tab:algorithm}; the end-to-end check injects into
visibilities, before it. Both recover static and near-static carriers, so coarse-window
amplitude is characterised by the dwell experiment -- 83--100 per cent
recovery at $\geq$4$\sigma$ per-integration amplitude
(Table~\ref{tab:dwell}). \emph{Drift discrimination}: on
coarse windows every in-grid drift is sub-channel over the track, so
the search recovers the carrier's amplitude but carries no information
on its true drift rate -- the coarse majority (\NCoarse{} of
\NWindows{} windows) is drift-undiscriminating, and no drift-rate
claim is made for it. \emph{The one cell measured only in part}:
through-pipeline recovery of near-static carriers on \emph{fine}
windows, constrained below at the search stage but never yet by an
end-to-end zero-drift injection, the 1200-trial campaign having failed
them under a criterion mixing amplitude and drift matching
(Appendix~\ref{app:inject}). The earlier claim that the pipeline suppresses persistent
carriers is withdrawn (\S\ref{sec:ancillary},
\S\ref{sec:validation}).

\emph{The campaign measures completeness at the survey threshold, and
measures it across configurations.} The trigger acts on the stacked
statistic, and the de-drift step is an inverse-variance-weighted sum
over integrations, so a carrier of per-integration amplitude $A$
present for a dwell fraction $f_{\rm dwell}$ of a track of $N_{\rm
int}$ integrations accumulates to
%
\begin{equation}
a \;=\; A\,f_{\rm dwell}\sqrt{N_{\rm int}}
\label{eq:stacked}
\end{equation}
%
in units of the per-integration noise. Recovery is a function of $a$,
not of $A$ and $f_{\rm dwell}$ separately, so binning the released
trials in $a$ both unifies the amplitude and dwell axes and places all
\CompWin{} injected windows on one axis -- and those windows span
$N_{\rm int}=\CompNintLo$--\CompNintHi{}, a factor of
\CompNintFactor, over \CompTgt{} targets in Bands \CompBands{} and
both resolution classes. Configuration-independence is therefore
measurable here rather than assumed. In the \CompFiftyN{} windows
whose amplitude grid straddles the trigger, the 50 per cent recovery
point lies at $a=\CompFiftyLo$--\CompFiftyHi{} (median
\CompFiftyMed), i.e.\ at the $5\sigma$ threshold itself and not at a
value set by channelisation, integration count or band; in the other
\CompSatN{} the weakest injection already stacks to
$a=\CompSatMin$ and at least \CompSatRec{} per cent of every injection
is recovered, consistent but uninformative about the curve's position.
The pooled curves (Table~\ref{tab:compcurve}) agree between the
classes within the sampling noise.

\emph{What this does and does not license.} The injected carriers do
not drift, so what is measured across \CompWin{} configurations is the
\emph{persistent and partial-dwell, non-drifting} class. On Class~B
windows that is the searched class, since every in-grid drift is
sub-channel over the track: those windows now carry a survey-threshold
completeness curve measured on six configurations, where before they
carried none. On Class~A windows the searched class is the drifting
carrier, whose curve is still the single calibration configuration of
Appendix~\ref{app:inject}; the fine column of
Table~\ref{tab:compcurve} constrains the near-static cell of
Table~\ref{tab:classcomp} at the search stage only, and neither
replaces the drifting-class curve nor bounds its transfer error. Two
further limits: the campaign injects after the baseline step, so what
is measured across configurations is the search stage, with the
visibility-domain injection above confirming the stage before it at
three amplitudes on one coarse window; and $a$ is a derived
combination, justified by the form of the statistic and supported by
the collapse of \CompWin{} configurations onto one curve, not by an
independent measurement at every $(A,f_{\rm dwell})$ pair.

\emph{Drift-ceiling caveat.} The fixed $12$\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$
searched ceiling, line-of-sight $|a| = 3.6$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, is uniform
across every window: two orders of magnitude clear of Earth-rotation and

TRAPPIST-1\,b-like orbit. The limits of \S\ref{sec:discussion} are therefore conditional on it
(Figure~\ref{fig:accel}).

\emph{Closure-phase vetting} (Appendix~\ref{app:closure}).
A one-sided point-source test -- evidence against celestial
point-source behaviour, never an authentication -- unavailable to
single-dish and beamformed surveys, needing multiple independent
baselines. Implemented and simulation-validated, it has at this
survey's flux densities \emph{no discriminating power}: per-baseline
S/N never exceeds 5.1 in the positive-control fields, so no flagged
window could have failed it whatever its nature. It is a proof-of-principle diagnostic for deeper releases, not an
operative filter here, and no window is described as having ``survived''
it.

\emph{Extended and cross-target screens}
(Appendices~\ref{app:extended} and \ref{app:freqoccupancy}). One
interference class deserves its own statement: satellite
constellations grew across the 2013--2025 archival baseline. No known
ordinary downlink allocation maps directly into the searched
frequencies, but that excludes neither harmonics, intermodulation
products nor unconventional emitters, and no exclusion claim rests on
allocation alone: the primary defences are the control ring and the
time-domain tests. A satellite enters star and ring extractions alike
within an integration; the channel-width mismatch and the
drift-stacked statistic dilute transient passes; a pass confined to
one block and one target would still evade the cross-target occupancy
check by construction. A near-field emitter, moreover, need not share
a celestial source's far-field interferometric response -- among the
reasons the next release adds a dedicated per-integration RFI screen
and visibility-domain checks. A chirped (quadratic-drift) extension and per-channel periodicity search
ran on the 122 retained spectra as a demonstration, not a survey-grade
screen; null behaviour is as expected (star-exceeds-ensemble rates
10.7 and 13.1 per cent against an 11-per-cent chance rate), no
instance is a candidate, and no limit derives from them. Separately,
crossings towards different targets were cross-matched for recurrence
at the same sky frequency -- the archival substitute for the ON/OFF
cadence this single-pointing survey lacks. Of the 366 star pairs with
overlapping searched ranges, none falls within the matching kernel,
subject to one bookkeeping caveat (64 crossings of one $\beta$~Pic
window untested) in the appendix.

\subsubsection*{The coarse-noise defect, and the bound on its reach}

A reproducible defect produces implausibly small noise in a minority of
coarse-channel windows; naming the two affected did not generalise as
the sample grew, so item (iii) is now caught by a physical test. The
radiometer relation $\sigma\simeq{\rm SEFD}/\sqrt{N_{\rm bl}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}t_{\rm on}}$
makes $\sigma\sqrt{t_{\rm on}\Delta\nu_{\rm ch}}\simeq{\rm SEFD}/\sqrt{N_{\rm bl}}$
a prediction, not a convention: across a heterogeneous archive it
varies by factors of a few, so a window $100\times$ below the sample
median is a bookkeeping defect, not a fluctuation; we exclude such
windows. The failure mode itself is not yet identified -- reproducible,
confined to coarse-channel windows, and to be named in the completed
survey's pipeline release, whose first change promotes this physical
test from a release-time screen to a pre-count regression gate on
every window -- but two positive facts bound it. First,
the retained population's radiometric quantity is continuous from
$0.33\times$ to ${\sim}4\times$ the sample median with no secondary
mode: a tenth-strength version of the defect would sit inside the
same empty gap -- between $0.1\times$ and $0.33\times$ the median --
where no retained window lies. Second, as a window-level bookkeeping
defect it deflates $\sigma$ at the stellar and control positions
alike, so it can only over-state sensitivity, never manufacture a
star-exceeds-ring window; the six sit three decades below the retained
population across an empty gap and are absent from the per-target
file and every count. Could the defect recur undetected among the
retained windows? The test screens each window on its own measured
quantity, so a recurrence with the observed signature cannot pass it;
a recurrence an order of magnitude weaker would pass, but would then
sit inside the continuous retained population, where its only effect
is the same one-directional over-statement of sensitivity.
Six of \NWindows{} windows
fail, across five stars, a factor $1.5\times10^{3}$ below the lowest
retained across an empty gap of 3.2 decades (Figure~\ref{fig:noiseqa});
the threshold is not fine-tuned, any value between $1.5\times10^{1}$ and
$2.2\times10^{4}$ selecting the same six. Their nominal thresholds,
$2.0$--$7.8\times10^{12}$\,W, would otherwise have set this paper's
headline depth; excluding them, the best is $1.6\times10^{13}$\,W.
\NDup{} of the \NExtracted{} extracted rows are duplicate catalogue
entries for the same physical window -- the same (star, execution
block, frequency range, channel width) key appearing twice in the
archive query. All are repeated archive products or alternative
calibrations of the same execution block, never independent
observations, so one row per key is the correct statistical unit.
The retention rule is deterministic and quantity-independent: the
first-listed row of the key in the archive query's own identifier
order, a rule that consults no measured value and so cannot prefer a
crossing or a deeper threshold; it is frozen unchanged for the
confirmatory sample. Most repeats are
identical, but two are not -- two reductions of the same
$\tau$~Ceti Band~6 window differ by 44 per cent in threshold, and the
two HR~1010 Band~6 rows straddle the $5\sigma$ threshold (5.10 and 4.95)
-- so the crossing count is rule-dependent at the level of one window. The
four stage-1 statistical flag windows are not: no discarded row is both a
crossing and star-exceeds-ring, and every row, kept or discarded, ships with
its status, making the choice auditable row by row. The audit is otherwise clean apart from four windows of
elevated system temperature, and every sample identifier re-resolves
independently in SIMBAD and \emph{Gaia} (Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}).

Item (iv) is a cautionary example for archival work: the catalogue
entry ``$\gamma$~Lupi'' resolved to the F4V debris-disc host
HD~139664, holder of the historical identifier ``*~g~Lup'' -- a
name-lookup substitution caught by inverting the pipeline distance and
recovering the \emph{Gaia}~DR3 parallax (Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}).

\subsubsection*{Survey-level false-alarm calibration}

Each window-level test compares the peak signal-to-noise ratio at the
star's position with the same statistic at \NCtrl{} controls; the
drift grid and thousands of channels apply identically to both, so the
per-window trials factor is absorbed empirically. The statistic is
\emph{symmetric}: the on-source quantity is the single propagated
stellar position, as each control is. Every number in this account
derives from that frozen statistic. The retired asymmetric variant
that maximised over a small region at the star only -- and every
diagnostic it produced, from its \RateSbrOld-per-cent
star-exceeds-ring rate to the 4--19 per cent bracket it induced on
$P(F{\geq}3)$ -- is development history, labelled as such and
quarantined in Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}; it contributes nothing
to the operative false-alarm probability. That probability has five
components, in the order the procedure applies them.

\emph{(i) The empirical spatial rank probability.} The per-window
significance quantity of this survey is not a Gaussian-tail p-value
but an \emph{empirical spatial rank probability under the
control-position null}: under exchangeability one designated position
of \RankFloor{} ranks first with probability exactly $1/\RankFloor$
-- a rank statement independent of whether the
noise is Gaussian or heavy-tailed, so heavy tails cannot explain an
excess. The survey expectation is
$\NWindows/\RankFloor=\ExpFlags$ windows, and
$P(\geq\SbrN\,|\,\ExpFlags)=\SbrPobs$ under an independent-Poisson
approximation. That is a real excess over the noise null, for an
astrophysical rather than statistical reason: in \SbrAstro{} of the
\SbrN{}, genuine celestial line emission sits at the stellar position
(Table~\ref{tab:flagged}), making the star rank first by construction
rather than by chance, so those \SbrAstro{} are not draws from the noise
null at all. Removing them leaves \SbrResid{} rank-first windows against
\ExpFlags{} expected, $P(\geq\SbrResid\,|\,\ExpFlags)=\SbrResidP$ --
entirely unremarkable: the CP$-$72~2713 window of
\S\ref{sec:technosearch}, and one towards Barnard's Star whose stellar
peak (4.40 against a ring maximum of 4.38) does not reach the trigger
threshold and so is not a flagged window at all.

Three levels stay distinct throughout: an \emph{instrument/noise} null,
calibrated by the control ring; an \emph{astrophysical-source} null,
addressed by line identification and kinematics; and only then a
technosignature interpretation. The chance budget must account for
flags unattributed after the first two, not every window in which
the star happens to be brightest. Two statistics appear: the
\emph{SP statistic} of Eq.~\ref{eq:tstar} -- the single stellar
position against \NCtrl{} single-position controls -- is operative
over all \NWindows{} windows and the source of every count in this
paper; the \emph{SR statistic} additionally maximises over the source
region at star \emph{and} controls and, needing raw-visibility
reprocessing, exists for seven windows only, as a consistency check.

\emph{(ii) The $\geq5\sigma$ amplitude requirement.} The joint
criterion gating a candidate is dominated by its threshold leg: only
\NHits{} of \NWindows{} windows contain any $\geq5\sigma$ crossing at
the stellar position, each contributing at most the exchangeable rate
$1/\RankFloor$ -- the \ExpFlags{} expectation of component (i). The
rank, not the trial count, is the calibrated quantity: a window's
channel$\times$drift grid holds up to $1.1\times10^{5}$ effective
trials (Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}), more than \NCtrl{} controls
could resolve individually, so the control maximum absorbs whatever
effective trial count the noise realisation carries and the per-window
probability is expressed as a rank with resolution $1/\RankFloor$
rather than a Gaussian tail at some $N_{\rm eff}$. Four flagged
windows exceed the budget ($P(\geq4\,|\,\ExpFlags)=0.9$ per cent) with
no tension: excluding the three astrophysical, it need explain one
unattributed crossing against 0.78 expected across the 402
non-$\beta$~Pictoris windows. The estimate absorbs execution-block
systematics into the fixed block margins, assuming only that crossing
and star-exceeds-ring status are exchangeable within a block.

\emph{(iii) The line mask.} The velocity-space mask
(Appendix~\ref{app:linemask}) removes, from every crossing, the
velocity space of known molecular emission before that crossing can
count as unattributed. It is an analysis choice, not a measurement:
it never lowers the noise-null budget above, and no disposition in
this paper rests on the mask alone -- the three CO attributions are
kinematic (Table~\ref{tab:flagged}). Statistic, mask and thresholds
were frozen and deposited before the confirmatory sample is processed
(the repository tag named in the Data Availability statement carries
the frozen code and its timestamp); what that deposit protected
against is quantified as development history in
Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}.

\emph{(iv) The family-level pseudo-target Monte Carlo.} A Monte-Carlo
calibration applies the full selection, not the rank alone, to
pseudo-targets: each trial draws one control per window uniformly from
its ring as the pseudo-star and passes it through the operative gates
($T\geq5\sigma$, then the velocity mask). The expected count of
pseudo-target windows flagged by the full procedure is \PsFlagMu{}
(\PsFlagPzero{} per cent of trials flag none, \PsFlagPgeOne{} per cent
at least one). This is the \emph{prespecified family-level procedure}
for the release -- one expectation carrying the threshold, the mask
and the ring geometry simultaneously, on real data; the Poisson
expectation of component (i) and the Bonferroni reference of
component (v) are checks against it, not independent claims
(Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}). Worked: \ExpFlags{} expected flags
from the rank test alone; the amplitude gate and the mask together
remove about three-quarters of that budget; and the Monte-Carlo
procedure -- which carries threshold, mask and ring geometry
simultaneously -- lands at \PsFlagMu{} expected flagged windows. On the \PsHoldWin{}
windows of stars outside the development set (\S\ref{sec:intro}) the
same procedure expects \PsFlagMuHold{} and the control ranks remain
uniform ($p=\KsNoDev$) -- the held-out behaviour the confirmatory
sample will test.

\emph{(v) The control-ensemble floor.} The controls can show a
crossing unremarkable but cannot certify one significant survey-wide:
the ring's smallest expressible per-window probability is
$1/\RankFloor$, two orders above the Bonferroni reference
$\BonferroniThresh$ over \NWindows{} windows -- and that reference is
itself conservative, windows sharing execution blocks, so it is a
threshold to compare against, not the calibrated family-wise
probability (\S\ref{sec:method}; box below). Authentication therefore
falls to the localisation, recurrence and astrophysical attribution
that dispositions each flag below. Stated in advance for later
releases: $N$ windows contribute ${\sim}N/\RankFloor$ chance flags, so
the full ${\sim}2000$-window programme expects ${\sim}4$, and one
unattributed marginal crossing per release is the \emph{expected}
outcome, not an accumulating anomaly. We pre-commit that no crossing in
this programme will be advanced as a candidate on rank statistics
alone: promotion needs evidence the ensemble cannot supply --
recurrence at an independent epoch, or a ring enlarged until the rank
probability resolves survey-wide significance ($\gtrsim10^{4}$
positions for a Bonferroni threshold over the completed survey;
\S\ref{sec:futurework}) -- and until then every such crossing is
reported with its disposition stated explicitly, and released with its
products. No flagged window is promoted by any number in this account:
the three CO dispositions rest on kinematic attribution to emission
resolved, mapped or independently published, and the CP$-$72~2713
standing is a rank statement, invariant to the bookkeeping.

\noindent\rule{\columnwidth}{0.5pt}\par\nointerlineskip
\textbf{The trials chain, start to finish.} The release evaluates
$\TrialCells$ channel$\times$drift cells; the measured lag-one
correlation of the drift-tracked statistic
($\rho_{\rm lag1}=0.989$--$0.991$) collapses these to
$8.5\times10^{4}$--$1.1\times10^{5}$ effectively independent cells
per window. Lag one is sufficient for the use this number is put to:
the effective-$N$ feeds only the Bonferroni robustness figure below,
while every operative calibration -- the ring ranks and the
pseudo-target Monte-Carlo above -- is empirical, reproducing the
observed flag counts from real control positions with no
effective-$N$ input at all. The control ring calibrates the resulting maximum
empirically -- no Gaussian tail is taken at any $N_{\rm eff}$ --
leaving per-window resolution $1/\RankFloor$. Across \NWindows{}
windows: Bonferroni reference $\BonferroniThresh$ (conservative);
rank-budget expectation $\ExpFlags$ flags; family-level Monte-Carlo
expectation \PsFlagMu. Each number's provenance is tabulated in
Table~\ref{tab:trials}.

\emph{Is the ring exchangeable with the star?} Exchangeability is
the central assumption of the statistic, not a technical footnote:
the $1/\RankFloor$
expectation holds only if the star and its \NCtrl{} controls are
exchangeable under the null, which spatially varying noise, sidelobes,
correlated pixels or phase-centre-localised calibration residuals could
spoil; we measure it by stratum (Fig.~\ref{fig:ctrldiag}). The median
per-window control maximum is flat across bands -- 4.46, 4.59, 4.42,
4.52, 4.63 and 4.69 in Bands 3 to 8 -- and a Kolmogorov--Smirnov test of
the star's rank among its controls against $U(0,1)$ is consistent in
every band ($p=\KsBandLo{}$--$\KsBandHi$) and the coarse-channel stratum
($p=\KsCoarse$).
Fine-channel windows sit higher (control maximum 5.76 against 4.42), as
expected from their far larger channel$\times$drift trial count; not a
violation, the test being conditional on the window.

Those tests are conditional on the stellar rank; for the stronger
question -- exchangeability of the ensemble itself -- we ranked each of
a window's \NCtrl{} controls against the other \NCtrl-1 by the
statistic and add-one rule applied to the star, pooled over the
release. The \NPseudo{} pseudo-star ranks are uniform to the resolution
the ensemble can express (mean \PseudoMean{} against 0.5; the
Kolmogorov--Smirnov $D=1/\NCtrl$ is the discreteness floor of a
\NCtrl-point rank statistic, $p=\PseudoKSp$), in every band and at both
channelisations, and a two-sample comparison of the \NWindows{} real
stellar ranks with that pooled distribution gives $p=\StarPseudoP$: the
stellar position behaves like one more position in its own ring -- the
null calibration the control-ring argument needs, measured not assumed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:symcdf} makes the same comparison at the amplitude
level).

One stratum does depart, reported rather than pooled away: the
fine-channel windows fail the same $U(0,1)$ test at $p=\KsFine$
($n=\NFine$), containing four windows whose on-star peak exceeds their
own control maximum against 0.22 expected -- precisely the four flagged
windows of Table~\ref{tab:flagged}. That is consistent with the
presence of the astrophysical line features identified below, not
a misbehaving ring; but the departure is genuine, so fine-window rank
probabilities are slightly optimistic. It is also localised, not
global: excluding the 20 fine windows that contain any $\geq5\sigma$
stellar crossing restores uniformity ($p=\KsFineNoCross$, $n=98$), and
excluding the development-set stars entirely leaves
$p=\KsNoDev$ over the remaining \PsHoldWin{} windows
($p=\KsFineNoDev$ in their fine stratum) -- the departure is carried
by the flagged and crossing windows, not by a miscalibrated ring. Two stratifications the retained
products cannot support, storing no per-control radial position or
primary-beam gain -- so the per-control gain evaluation below is
analytic, not measured. The control ring shares the star's radius
from the phase centre exactly (positions enter the extraction as
exact phase centres, not pointing estimates), which makes the
exchangeability violation a property of the beam's
\emph{azimuthal} structure at fixed radius: for a circularly
symmetric beam it is identically zero, at any offset. The
radially symmetric component is bounded per band from the Gaussian
form $G=\exp[-2.77(r/\theta_{\rm PB})^{2}]$ with $\theta_{\rm
PB}=1.13\,\lambda/D$: the 12-m primary beams span
\PbThetaBandThree{} arcsec in Band~3, \PbThetaBandSix{} in Band~6,
\PbThetaBandSeven{} in Band~7 and \PbThetaBandEight{} in Band~8, so
at the median stellar offset ($0.1$\,arcsec) the star-versus-ring
gain differential is at most \PbSuppMedPct{} per cent even in the
highest-frequency band -- the effect lives entirely in the beam's
departure from circularity, not in the offset itself. At the largest
retained offset (Sirius~B, $r\simeq\PbSirRoverTheta\,\theta_{\rm
PB}$) the Gaussian gain changes by $\PbSirGradPct$ per cent per
$0.01\,\theta_{\rm PB}$ of effective radius, so percent-level
holography or ellipticity terms at that radius map to percent-level
differentials; the withheld $\epsilon$\,Eri windows
(\S\ref{sec:frames}) sit beyond the regime entirely. The sign of any
true differential is one-sided and conservative: the primary beam
attenuates an unresolved carrier's extracted amplitude by $G(r)$
while the visibility-domain thermal noise it is measured against
carries no such factor, so a suppressed control can only
\emph{inflate} star-exceeds-ring outcomes under the null -- the safe
direction for every disposition drawn here. The empirical rank
distributions themselves (Fig.~\ref{fig:ctrldiag}, and the
pseudo-target calibration of \S\ref{sec:technosearch}) bound the
net effect: expected flags \PsFlagMu{} against a rank-only budget of
\ExpFlags, with no excess in any stratum. Constructing controls at
\emph{equal gain} -- sampling ring azimuth at fixed holography-model
gain rather than fixed radius -- is pre-registered as the default for
the completed survey, which removes the term by construction.
Remaining robustness checks -- trials-count reconciliation under
channel adjacency, control-ring geometry -- are in
Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.

\subsubsection*{The fully symmetric region-max check}

The SR statistic applies identical source-region, drift-rate
and frequency maximisation at the star's position and at each of the
\NCtrl{} controls under one search rule. The frozen release products
cannot meet that, so its domain is the seven locally reprocessed windows
-- six validation, plus the AU~Mic window's first-epoch block -- all
giving $T_\star=3.7$--$4.9$ against control maxima of 6.7--7.7,
$p=0.81$--1.00: the star
is exchangeable with its ring wherever the test runs at full symmetry.
The single-position (SP) calibration above is therefore the
operative release-wide criterion over all \NWindows{} windows, the SR
statistic a validation subset; the two are never interchanged. The
machinery ships with the release; the full statement, with the
family-wise trials factors of Table~\ref{tab:trials}, is in
Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm}.

\subsubsection*{Astrophysical end-to-end validation: $\beta$~Pictoris CO}

Two of the four flagged windows are towards $\beta$~Pictoris, a debris
disc with reported CO emission \citep{Matra2017}: one in Band~3 at
115.2606\,GHz, 10.56\,MHz below the CO($J{=}1{\to}0$) rest frequency,
the other in Band~6 at 230.5161\,GHz, 21.87\,MHz below
CO($J{=}2{\to}1$). Neither coincidence is exact, so the automated
check missed them. Through the frame chain (Table~\ref{tab:bpicaudit};
Fig.~\ref{fig:bpicvel}) the offsets are one kinematic component --
$-2.7$ and $-3.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from stellar systemic,
0.74\,km\,s$^{-1}$ apart -- in the two lowest CO rotational
transitions of a system with detected circumstellar CO; the unflagged
coarse Band~6 window, from a third epoch six years earlier,
independently shows CO at $-3.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the same frame --
all three epochs and both transitions within $0.9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of
each other, deep inside the $\pm50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ mask. We attribute
both crossings to this kinematically offset CO. The pair is the
survey's astrophysical end-to-end validation -- a known signal run
through the same search that the null results run through -- and what
it recovers is the check list: the
frequencies, to the frame chain's stated tolerance; the velocity
concordance across epochs and transitions; the localisation, the
Band~3 stellar peak exceeding all \NWindows{} control maxima and the
Band~6 peak all but its own CO-filled ring; and the drift class, zero
to within the grid. Not quantified is flux against calibrated
imaging \citep{Matra2017}: line fluxes are not among the retained
carrier products. A known line remains the more convincing
end-to-end control precisely because nothing about it was tuned.
A three-panel figure -- the Band~3 and Band~6 features and both
aligned in stellar velocity -- would complete the record, but the
channel-level spectral products of those execution blocks are not
carried by the frozen release, and re-extracting them is the same
declined visibility-level operation as the CP$-$72~2713 tests
(Table~\ref{tab:cp72props}); Fig.~\ref{fig:bpicvel} carries the
velocity alignment, and the validation itself rests on the numbers
above.

Their empirical significance under the release background is
nonetheless extreme, so the attribution is no dismissal: the Band~3
region peak of 20.3 exceeds all \NWindows{} per-window control maxima
(add-one $p<0.0044$), the Band~6 peak of 12.8 all but one -- that
window's own CO-filled control ring, reaching 15.2, by far the release
maximum ($p\approx0.0088$). In robust units -- the stellar statistic
minus the ring median, over $1.4826\times$ the ring median absolute
deviation -- the standardised excesses are $\zeta=\ZetaBpicThree$ and
\ZetaBpicSix, the yardsticks against which the remaining flagged
windows should be read. The alternative needs a transmitter coincidentally placed at matched
offsets from two CO transitions in the same star.

\subsubsection*{Dispositions of the remaining flagged windows}

The third, towards HD~48370 in Band~6, is the release's largest excess
in absolute and smallest in relative terms: $T_\star=27.10$ against a
control-ring maximum of 26.83, the star exceeding its ring by one per
cent of the statistic -- a standardised excess of $\zeta=\ZetaHd$,
near the CP$-$72 value below, though its amplitude and velocity
evidence differ. Star and ring rising together is what emission
filling the primary beam looks like, and the window sits
$-34.2$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ topocentric from CO($J{=}2{\to}1$). The
attribution is independently published: \citet{Cataldi2023} report
prominent CO($2{\to}1$) and $^{13}$CO($2{\to}1$) emission towards
HD~48370 at $v_{\rm bary}\approx41$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, well separated from
its $23.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ systemic velocity and attributed by them to
foreground cloud contamination; our window recovers it, and the
crossing is consistent with, and very likely caused by, that
previously reported foreground CO emission. The
coincidence is with their quoted $v_{\rm bary}$, not an independent
frame-chain re-derivation on our side. It is also a
stated limitation of the spatial test: this window is identified by the
velocity coincidence and near-equality of star and ring, not the rank
statistic.

\subsubsection*{The unidentified CP$-$72~2713 feature}

The fourth, towards CP$-$72~2713 in Band~7, is the only flagged window
with no astrophysical attribution, and is marginal: $T_\star=\CpTwoTstar$
against a ring maximum of \CpTwoRingMax, a margin of \CpTwoMargin: the
stellar position ranks first among the \RankFloor{} tested spatial
positions, but the available control ensemble is too small to
establish survey-level statistical significance -- \CpTwoMargin{} is
the smallest margin \NCtrl{}
controls can express, a resolution floor, not a measurement of
significance; in the
robust units above its standardised excess is $\zeta=\ZetaCp$, two
orders below the $\beta$~Pictoris controls. That margin
is smaller than the scatter across this star's own windows, whose other
fine-channel control maxima are 5.16, 5.41, 5.67 and 5.85: its rings
reach 5.81 routinely where the star does not cross threshold. The
nearest transition of the eight masked species lies 1326\,km\,s$^{-1}$
away; since that mask is an automated contamination cut, not a complete
line catalogue, and a chemically rich or circumstellar environment can
present many further ALMA-band transitions, we re-queried a wider
catalogue, whose closest entry is H$^{13}$CN($J{=}4{\to}3$) at
$-195$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, still far outside any circumstellar tolerance. The
defensible reading is ``no plausible known transition identified'', a
statement about the catalogues consulted, not a proof that none exists;
and with a single execution block at this frequency no recurrence test
is available. Atmospheric or calibration spectral structure near
345.115\,GHz is controlled the same way everything here is: it enters
star and ring positions alike within the one execution block, so it
widens the ensemble rather than mimicking a stellar excess, and
isolating any telluric contribution needs the calibration products
beneath the frozen release. None of these facts is decisive alone.
Figure~\ref{fig:cp72ctrl} shows
the crossing within its window's full \NCtrl-position control
distribution: $T_\star$ sits at the upper edge of an unremarkable
ensemble, not beyond it -- one draw of \RankFloor{} against the
release's expected ring maxima (median \BootMaxMed, 90th percentile
\BootPctNinety; a ring maximum at or above \CpTwoRingMax{} occurs in
\BootPGeRing{} per cent of windows, at or above \CpTwoTstar{} in
\BootPGeStar{} per cent). We record it as an unclassified stage-1
flag -- a statistical or astrophysical feature, not candidate
evidence -- with
explicit promote/retire criteria: \emph{promote} only on recurrence at
an independent epoch -- a targeted ALMA re-observation at
345.1152\,GHz, specified in Table~\ref{tab:cp72props} but not yet
scheduled, tests it directly -- or on an
enlarged control ensemble resolving the rank probability below the
survey-wide Bonferroni threshold ($p<\BonferroniThresh$, requiring of
order $\PromoCtrlNeeded$ control positions against the present
$1/\RankFloor$ floor);
\emph{retire} on a clean second epoch or on identification with
catalogued emission. One further check is open rather than performed:
a Gaia/2MASS/WISE cross-match for an unresolved background source.
Its catalogue half could list sources in the synthesised beam, but
without the visibility-domain localisation it could not say which, if
any, the excess belongs to -- a null match would not retire the flag,
and a populated match could not promote it -- so the check is
scheduled with that localisation rather than run as a catalogue query
that cannot move the disposition. Until either, it stays released and
reported for what it is: unclassified, and statistically
non-significant at the survey level. The enlargement and the
visibility-level tests of Table~\ref{tab:cp72props} are not deferred
for want of interest: each needs re-extraction beneath the frozen
products, which they do not carry, while the re-observation at
345.1152\,GHz needs no reprocessing and is the faster arbiter.

\subsubsection*{Line exclusion: definition, cost, and limits}

The searches used an automated line check matching crossings to
catalogued rest frequencies within one spectral channel at zero drift.
The $\beta$~Pictoris windows showed that inadequate: both fell outside
its one-channel tolerance (by 10.6 and 21.9\,MHz) while lying, after
the frame chain, within 3.6\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of the stellar systemic of a
resolved, mapped disc -- exactly the regime a channel-width tolerance
is blind to. Every crossing was therefore reclassified before
publication with a velocity-space mask ($\pm50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in the
stellar frame around each catalogued transition), the pipeline's
line-exclusion definition from this release on. Its kinematic
justification, empirical conservatism, that reclassification and the
counter-argument that a communicator might
deliberately choose these natural reference frequencies are in
Appendix~\ref{app:linemask}. The mask's application to this release
is \emph{retrospective} -- a post-hoc classification criterion, not a
detection threshold: the half-width was adopted after the
$\beta$~Pictoris crossings had been seen, and it excludes exactly
those two windows, changing no other disposition. A sensitivity test
over half-widths: reclassifying all \NHits{} crossings and recomputing
masked coverage at $\pm20$, $\pm30$, $\pm50$ and $\pm100$
km\,s$^{-1}$ (nearest-catalogued-line approximation, one tube per
window regardless of in-band position -- an upper bound, exceeding
the full-catalogue \MaskGrossPct{} per cent at $\pm50$) masks
\MaskBWTwenty, \MaskBWThirty, \MaskBWFifty{} and \MaskBWHundred{} per
cent of gross bandwidth, leaves \MaskHitTwenty, \MaskHitThirty,
\MaskHitFifty{} and \MaskHitHundred{} of the \NHits{} crossings and
\MaskExcTwenty, \MaskExcThirty, \MaskExcFifty{} and \MaskExcHundred{}
of the \NSpatial{} star-exceeds-ring windows unmasked. At every width the only
unmasked unattributed flag is CP$-$72~2713 (at
$-1326$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, masked at no plausible width); the CO
attributions rest on kinematics and published detections, not on the
mask; no width admits a new unattributed flag. The half-width
moves which CO crossings are masked, not any conclusion. We therefore
pre-register the same $\pm50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame
definition, unchanged, for all remaining planned datasets: the
confirmatory sample tests a frozen rule, not a tuned one, and masked
regions are reported throughout as \emph{excluded search space},
never as searched-and-empty. Two standing policies accompany the
pre-registration. First, the eight-species mask is the automated
first-pass screen only: crossings outside it will in future releases
be scored against a Splatalogue/CDMS/JPL query of all catalogued
transitions in the searched interval, assigning each an
astrophysical-line probability from source and environment rather
than a binary species veto -- the procedure CP$-$72~2713 would have
needed had it fallen in a line-rich field. Second, the mask width and
any other post-hoc-adjusted parameter will be applied and audited by
a team member not involved in the original crossing identification.

The mask is a design selection effect: a signal coincident with a
molecular line is not thereby ``false'' but \emph{excluded from the
technosignature search domain}, the astrophysical prior there being
overwhelming and this pipeline unable to separate the two. We place no
constraint on transmitters in the excluded regions --
\MaskUnionPct{} per cent of the unique-frequency union -- and a
search for deliberately line-adjacent carriers would need a
complementary strategy, pre-registered here: masked channels are
retained, not discarded, and future releases will score them in a
separate \emph{line-coincident} technosignature catalogue, discriminating
molecular from technological emission by the axes this survey already
measures per window -- spatial extent against the control ring, line
ratios across transitions, recurrence across epochs, linewidth, and
drift -- rather than by molecular proximity alone. Nor is the exposure
confined to the flagged windows: 131 of \NWindows{} windows (30 per
cent) contain an in-band catalogued transition of the eight species, as
do all four flagged windows, yet in none does a crossing fall within one
native channel of a rest frequency -- precisely the regime the
velocity-space criterion covers.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What signals could ALMA archival data actually detect?}
\label{sec:detectable}

Three concrete cases, one per measured morphology, with real windows
and the completeness entries of Table~\ref{tab:classcomp}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sens2d} maps every window).

\emph{(i) A continuous, frequency-stationary carrier} in a coarse
channel: the best case, not the worst. Toward the UV~Ceti pair
(2.7\,pc, deepest window EIRP$_{5\sigma}=1.6\times10^{13}$\,W), a
carrier of that power confined to its channel is recovered at
essentially the ideal $\sqrt{N}$ gain (\S\ref{sec:dwell}) -- an
unresolved, continuous carrier matching the \emph{total} EIRP of an
Arecibo-like planetary radar is detectable toward the nearest systems
even at 15.625-MHz channel resolution (a power-scale comparison, not a
simulated radar; \S\ref{sec:benchmarks}).

\emph{(ii) A continuously drifting carrier} in a fine 488-kHz channel
toward $\tau$~Ceti (3.65\,pc, threshold $3.8\times10^{13}$\,W):
recovery is \RecThresh{} per cent at the threshold for
$f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$ and $75$--$83$ per cent at $1.6$--$2\times$
threshold (Appendix~\ref{app:inject}), so a drifting transmitter above
${\sim}8\times10^{13}$\,W -- twice threshold -- is recovered in
\RecTwice{} per cent of trials wherever its drift stays inside the
searched ceiling.

\emph{(iii) An intermittent carrier, on for a quarter of the track},
toward HD~10647 (17.4\,pc, $1.5\times10^{14}$\,W): dwell recovery at
$f_{\rm dwell}=0.25$ is \DwellFineQ{} per cent (fine) and
\DwellCoarseQ{} per cent (coarse), so a duty-$1/4$ transmitter above
${\sim}2\times10^{14}$\,W is detected toward such mid-distance
systems. Smaller dwell fractions degrade rapidly (Table~\ref{tab:dwell});
below $D\sim0.1$ only the nearest windows carry information. The
denominators, counting systems whose deepest relevant threshold lies
below each case power: \CaseDetCoarse{} of \NSystems{} at the
case-(i) power (\CaseDetCoarseTwo{} at twice it),
\CaseDetFine{} at the case-(ii) power and \CaseDetDwell{} at the
case-(iii) power.

\subsection{What does the null result mean?}
\label{sec:null}

This first stand-alone processed release is preliminary by construction:
\NStarBands{} of \NPlanned{} planned target/bands (\PctPlanned{} per
cent) are complete, and that subset is sensitivity-ranked, not an
unbiased draw; the full
sample will reach larger distances and shallower faint-end limits. No credible
technosignature has been found; the EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ values of
Appendix~\ref{app:table} are the nominal powers at which a matching
transmitter enters the detection pipeline (boxed rule,
\S\ref{sec:method}) -- completeness-calibrated exclusion holds only
where the measured recovery curve applies (Class~A; the framework
below). The flagged-window dispositions are established in
\S\ref{sec:technosearch} and not repeated here.

The searched space uses one definition throughout
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}, Figure~\ref{fig:waterfall}). One exclusion
in it deserves the converse emphasis: the line mask removes
$\pm50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ stellar-frame tubes around eight common
species, and those rest frequencies are exactly the `magic' channels
\citet{CocconiMorrison1959} argued a communicator might choose as
natural reference points. This survey is blind by construction to a
transmitter parked there in the stellar frame -- a false-positive
control purchased with \MaskGrossPct{} per cent of gross bandwidth and one
physically motivated class of signal, accepted knowingly and only
partially narrowed by the pre-registered catalogue-probability screen
of \S\ref{sec:technosearch}. Of the \NExoPlanets{} planets known around the \NExoHosts{}
planet-hosting targets only TRAPPIST-1\,b
reaches the drift ceiling, in its edge-on worst case at
$\sim$13\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$\,GHz$^{-1}$; GJ~581\,e, 61~Vir\,b, and
TRAPPIST-1\,c reach roughly half, the remaining 33 well inside the
searched grid.\footnote{Star--planet relative accelerations
$GM_\star/r^{2}$ from NASA Exoplanet Archive planetary-companion
parameters (Mission Star/Planet table, accessed 2026 September),
maximised over orbital phase and inclination.}
Table~\ref{tab:searchspace} (\S\ref{sec:results}) consolidates the axes on which a transmitter is or is not detectable;
Table~\ref{tab:perband} decomposes the release
by ALMA band. Coverage eligibility and search completeness differ. The archive of
public usable ALMA data (\S\ref{sec:sample}) sets eligibility; every held execution block in scope was searched, so no EB-level
selection lies behind the \NEB{}. Availability is measured directly, not inherited
from release bookkeeping: a proper-motion-matched ALMA archive audit
(2026 September~9; public rows, all bands, pointing within
30\,arcsec of the propagated stellar position at the observation
epoch) counts 107 unique public on-star EBs over the subset run so far,
re-finding all but one searched EB -- TRAPPIST-1's Band~7 execution
A002/Xcccc19/X5564, which \texttt{obscore} carries only through its
calibrator-field row, an archive-representation artefact biasing that
star's availability low by one. Availability exceeds scope by the
recurrence pool drawn on by the AU~Mic second-epoch test
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}); the ledger
\texttt{v323\_star\_coverage.json} gives per-star distance,
availability, scope and searched frequency intervals, and the
selection funnel itself is Table~\ref{tab:funnel} with the selection
function of Table~\ref{tab:selfunc}.

Figure~\ref{fig:accel} shows the drift ceiling as an explicit selection function of planet
period and host mass: the search covers the drift of a transmitter on
any known planet in the sample bar the innermost TRAPPIST-1 planets,
while a faster drift falls outside the searched grid, sacrificed by
design
(Appendix~\ref{app:extended} recovers some non-linear morphologies).
The host-mass dependence is weak ($a \propto M_\star^{1/3}$ at fixed
period), so the ceiling is effectively a function of orbital period
alone; a transmitter not co-located with a \emph{known} planet is
covered only if its drift falls in the searched grid (Scope box,
\S\ref{sec:method}).

The release sits against \citet{Wright2018}'s published haystack axis
by axis. On frequency (10\,MHz--115\,GHz) only our Band~3 falls inside
(20.6 of 115\,GHz, 0.18 of the axis, and on it Hz-resolution surveys
reach far more finely); the remaining 66.6\,GHz (Bands~4--8,
125--495\,GHz) lies wholly outside -- the genuinely new territory. On
transmitted bandwidth (0--20\,MHz), a native-channel search probes a
fraction set by channel width (window-mean 0.63, median 0.78). On
sensitivity, our median threshold ($7.3$\,mJy across a 15.625-MHz
channel, $\phi\approx1.1\times10^{-21}$\,W\,m$^{-2}$) detects a
$10^{13}$\,W transmitter only within ${\sim}0.9$\,pc. Against the
sole prior ALMA-archival search \citep{Mason2024} (28 bycatch targets
at $\geq$1.01\,kpc, deepest EIRP$_{\rm min}$ $6.91\times10^{17}$\,W),
this release's \NSystems{} systems, \NSysInTwenty{} of them inside
20\,pc, reach median per-target thresholds
${\sim}\MasonRatio\times$ deeper. These fractions are not multiplied
into a combined coverage: the axes are strongly coupled (distance and
sensitivity above all), so a product of marginals would misstate the
overlap by orders of magnitude.


What the zero-detection result bounds is stated once here and
carried everywhere below: fully conditioned, on each target's own
searched frequencies, epochs and morphology class. The occurrence
formalism that will be applied once configuration-dependent
completeness is established for the completed survey factorises the
per-system detection probability into four terms,
%
\begin{equation}
C_i(P) \;=\; F_i\;R_i(P)\;D_i\;C_{{\rm morph},i},
\label{eq:conditioning}
\end{equation}
%
\begin{list}{}{\leftmargin=3.1em\labelwidth=2.7em\labelsep=0.4em
\itemsep=0pt\parsep=0pt\topsep=2pt\partopsep=0pt}
\item[$F_i$:] \emph{frequency occupancy} -- the transmitter's sky frequency lies in
system $i$'s searched intervals;
\item[$R_i(P)$:] \emph{amplitude recovery} -- a transmitter of EIRP
$P$ would have crossed system $i$'s threshold had it emitted there;
\item[$D_i$:] \emph{epoch activity} -- it emitted during the analysed archival interval;
\item[$C_{{\rm morph},i}$:] \emph{morphology match} -- the measured
recovery of a carrier of the searched drift and temporal morphology
(Appendix~\ref{app:inject}).
\end{list}
%
The product treats the four factors as independent; for one strategy
the assumption is strained (a continuously transmitting narrowband
emitter trivially satisfies high $D_i$ while carrying small $F_i$),
for which the thresholded form, using $R_i$ alone, is the honest one.
The bound below sets $F_i=D_i=1$ \emph{by conditioning, not by
measurement}. With that convention the probability of zero credible
detections if a fraction $f$ of systems hosts one is
\begin{equation}
P(N_{\rm det}=0\,|\,f)=\prod_{i=1}^{N_{\rm sys}}\left(1-f\,C_i\right),
\label{eq:prevalence}
\end{equation}
and the one-sided 95\% upper limit $f_{95}$ solves
$P(N_{\rm det}=0\,|\,f_{95})=0.05$, treating the \NSystems{} systems
as independent. The formulation differs from the transmitter-fraction
limits of \citet{Enriquez2017} and \citet{Margot2023}: those surveys
quote occurrence over samples with roughly uniform per-target
frequency coverage, whereas this framework conditions on each
target's own archival frequency support.

\medskip
\noindent\rule{\columnwidth}{0.4pt}
\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Reading rule.}
\emph{Among the ALMA-observed systems, fewer than $f_{95}(P)$ per cent host a transmitter of EIRP $\geq P$
emitting during the archival observations, at a sky frequency in the
particular spectral intervals searched for that system, with drift and
temporal morphology in the searched class.}
\par\smallskip
\noindent\rule{\columnwidth}{0.4pt}
\medskip

\noindent The numerical demonstrations -- the $f_{95}$
evaluation, its bracketing, duty-cycle variants and
transfer-error analysis -- are consolidated in
Appendix~\ref{app:population}, labelled \emph{illustrative
calculation only}: they state no survey result, appear among no
headline quantities (Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}), and should
be cited, if at all, only with the $F_i{=}D_i{=}1$ conditioning
and the single-configuration completeness transfer attached --
never as a measured occurrence limit, and never as a statement
about the 40\,pc stellar population.

Because selection is on ALMA archival coverage alone, useful
subsamples fall out without compromising the archive-complete framing, subject
to the unresolved-pair caveat of \S\ref{sec:sample}. First, \NExoHosts{} of the \NStars{} processed stars
(\NExoPlanets{} known planets) are confirmed exoplanet hosts
(Proxima~Centauri, $\tau$~Ceti, $\epsilon$~Eridani, $\beta$~Pictoris and
the seven-planet TRAPPIST-1 system among them), extractable as a
sub-survey from Appendix~\ref{app:table}; HN~Lib and LHS~1140, each with
a habitable-zone planet, now carry an ALMA technosignature non-detection
beside their planet-search literature.
Second, four searched stars are white dwarfs
\citep{GentileFusillo2021} (Sirius~B, Van~Maanen's Star,
Wolf~219, CD$-$38~10980), all continuum non-detections with
nominal EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ trigger thresholds of
$2.3\times10^{13}$--$8.4\times10^{14}$\,W --
among the few mm/submm SETI constraints for white dwarfs
\citep{PollutedWD2026}, a channel motivated by post-main-sequence
survival and the compact debris discs of metal-polluted ones.

\subsection{ALMA as a technosignature instrument: strengths and limits}
\label{sec:instrument}

ALMA's advantages for this programme are four: aperture and
receiver temperature give mm-wave sensitivities no single dish
reaches; the tuning range, 125--495\,GHz here, is essentially
unsearched territory; interferometric spatial discrimination --
the control-ring statistic of \S\ref{sec:statistic} -- tests a
feature's position against \NCtrl{} alternatives, which
single-dish and beamformed searches cannot; and the archive
itself supplies \NEB{} public execution blocks re-processable
without new allocations. Each strength has its mirror. Native
channelisation reaches 15.3\,kHz at best, orders coarser than
the Hz resolution of dedicated narrowband surveys, so the
search tests unresolved spectral excess, not fine structure;
coverage is fragmented into \UnionIntervals{} frequency islands
weighted by other science; the molecular-line mask deliberately
excludes \MaskUnionPct{} per cent of the union
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}); configurations are heterogeneous,
so no completeness transfers between them (\S\ref{sec:null});
and targeting is interest-driven, not uniform (\S\ref{sec:sample}).
The largest unused advantage is the visibilities themselves:
ALMA's
fundamental advantage for this programme is not only mm-wave
sensitivity but the ability to test whether an apparent spectral
feature carries the interferometric phase signature of a celestial
point source at a known position. For each surviving candidate,
construct competing likelihoods -- $H_{\star}$: a source at the
propagated stellar position,
$V_{ij}=A(t,\nu)\,e^{-2\pi i(u_{ij}l_\star+v_{ij}m_\star)}$;
$H_{\rm sky}$: a source elsewhere on the sky; $H_{\rm common}$:
correlated or common-mode interference; $H_{\rm noise}$: thermal and
calibration residual -- and evaluate
$\Lambda=\mathcal L(V|H_\star)/\mathcal L(V|H_{\rm noise}{+}H_{\rm common})$.
This uses information that single-dish and beamformed searches do not
possess, is strictly more powerful than ranking a peak against a
control ring, and repositions the control ring as a calibration layer
rather than the primary authentication statistic. We regard this
visibility-domain likelihood analysis as potentially the programme's
most important methodological contribution; the extracted-spectrum
search of this release is its amplitude-only prologue.


\subsection{A next-generation ALMA technosignature search}
\label{sec:futurework}

Three changes would enlarge what this experiment can
say, ranked by how much signal space they buy -- the natural
extensions of \S\ref{sec:instrument}'s ledger of strengths and limits.

\emph{Drift discrimination on coarse windows.} Within the
searched ceiling every in-grid drift on a 15.625\,MHz window is
sub-channel over a track, so \NCoarse{} of \NWindows{} windows
measure amplitude but cannot distinguish a drifting carrier from a
stationary one (\S\ref{sec:dwell}). Finer channelisation on
re-observation, or a
longer-baseline drift model spanning multiple execution blocks, would
recover that axis.

\emph{Enlarge the control ensemble for interesting windows.}
The $1/\RankFloor$ rank floor (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}) cannot
certify significance across a survey of thousands of windows. A
two-stage scheme -- \NCtrl{} controls routinely, then
$10^{4}$--$10^{5}$ local null evaluations for any window that passes
-- would settle candidates statistically rather than hand each to
astrophysical vetting.

\emph{Extend the injection campaign and the polarisation
axis.} The completeness curve should be measured per band, channel width,
integration time, antenna count and primary-beam position rather than
transferred by construction, and pushed past $10\sigma$ until it
asymptotes, with EIRP$_{90}$ unlocated. That campaign is pre-registered
here as the completed survey's completeness design: a stratified grid
over those configuration axes; injections at continuously randomised
drift rates and channel phases rather than preferentially on grid
points; injected morphologies extended to sinusoidal acceleration
tracks following representative sample orbits, whose linear-chirp
fitting loss the present campaign cannot measure; trial-level logging
from the first run; and an independent re-implementation cross-check
of the injection code, closing the open item of
\S\ref{sec:validation}. The polarisation axis is as noted in
\S\ref{sec:method}: discrimination against weakly polarised
astrophysical backgrounds, at no sensitivity cost.

\emph{A boundary on drift linearity.} The search assumes drift linear
over a track. Curvature matters when $\tfrac{1}{2}\ddot\nu T^{2}$
exceeds half a native channel, reached only by the shortest-period
orbits: $P\lesssim1.6$\,d for a typical fine-channel window (230\,GHz,
488\,kHz, $T\approx2000$\,s), about an hour for a coarse one.
TRAPPIST-1\,b, at $1.51$\,d, sits just inside the boundary and is also
the case exceeding the drift ceiling; elsewhere the constant-drift
model is adequate, and the chirp-search extension
(Appendix~\ref{app:extended}) covers the remainder.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This first processed release of an ALMA-archival technosignature
survey searched, within 40\,pc, every star with public usable
coverage: \NStarBands{} of \NPlanned{} planned target/bands,
\NStars{} stars in \NSystems{} independent systems, complete within
the archive and audited for position and identity
(\S\ref{sec:exclusions}), but covering ${\sim}1$\% of the catalogued
stars there and inheriting the archive's non-uniform targeting. The
archive's channelisation splits what was searched: \NFine{}
fine-resolution windows were searched for drift-resolved spectral
carriers, \NCoarse{} coarse windows are sensitive only to unresolved
spectral excess (\S\ref{sec:statistic}). Four windows raised a
stage-1 statistical screening flag: three are consistent with, and
very likely caused by, CO emission -- two circumstellar components
towards $\beta$~Pictoris across two transitions and three epochs,
one previously reported foreground cloud towards HD~48370
\citep{Cataldi2023} -- and one, towards CP$-$72~2713, is
unclassified, marginal and non-significant at the survey level, held
under pinned promote/retire criteria (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}).
None survives vetting: this release reports \NCandidates{} credible
technosignatures. Among the searched stars, Barnard's Star and
Wolf~359 carry their first published mm/submm technosignature limits
(\S\ref{sec:barnardswolf}); forty ancillary continuum measurements
(median $5\sigma$ limit 0.52\,mJy) are non-detections of
unquantified statistical power.

Its EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ values are nominal trigger thresholds (boxed
rule, \S\ref{sec:method}): $\EirpMin$--$\EirpMax$\,W, deep enough,
toward the nearest systems, for an unresolved carrier matching the
total power of an Arecibo-like planetary radar; the occurrence
demonstration stays illustrative (Appendix~\ref{app:population}).
Searched is not constrained: the \emph{Scope of the
constraints} box of \S\ref{sec:method} fixes the frequency, epoch,
linewidth, drift and threshold box inside which the non-detection
binds -- everything outside it, from the masked velocity space to
any transmitter-frequency prior spanning ALMA's tuning range, is
unbounded, and every EIRP statement concerns continuous or
near-continuous emitters (item~iii there).

This release is developmental by construction: the detection
statistic and line mask were established on these data, so the
false-alarm calibration is retrospective, and the remaining
\NPlanned$-$\NStarBands{} planned datasets form the frozen,
pre-registered confirmatory sample -- the completed survey will test
the frozen method on data it has never shaped and supersede this
release row by row without invalidating it. First-priority growth
(\S\ref{sec:futurework}): drift discrimination on coarse windows,
a visibility-domain likelihood ratio, control ensembles past the
$1/\RankFloor$ rank floor, and a stratified injection campaign over
band, channel width, integration time and beam offset. By-products
ship with the release: a serendipitous molecular-line catalogue,
exoplanet-host and white-dwarf subsample limits, and a first
technosignature-survey use of a closure-phase diagnostic --
demonstrated, with no discriminating power at these flux densities.

\section{Frequency conventions, linewidth reading, and smearing}
\label{app:conventions}

\subsection*{Reference frames}

The line-exclusion mask's chain -- topocentric $\rightarrow$
barycentric (Earth ephemeris at the execution-block mid-time)
$\rightarrow$ stellar rest frame (catalogued systemic radial velocity;
JPL catalogue rest frequencies, Appendix~\ref{app:linecat}) -- has as
residual error the catalogue radial-velocity uncertainty alone (at most
a few km\,s$^{-1}$ within 40\,pc); second-order relativistic terms at
the 50\,km\,s$^{-1}$ scale are $\lesssim5$\,kHz at 350\,GHz, below the
finest native channel searched (15.3\,kHz). The $\beta$~Pictoris and
AU~Mic kinematic attributions (\S\ref{sec:technosearch},
Fig.~\ref{fig:bpicvel}) use the same chain, so their offsets are
comparable to the exclusion tolerance. Enlarging the drift-rate grid needs no further frame machinery: the
searched rates are topocentric and depend only on elapsed time and
channel width, not sky position.

\subsection*{Channel widths and their verification}

Channel widths come from the measurement sets' own spectral window
tables (CHAN\_WIDTH). For the local reprocessing set we verified these are the \emph{effective} resolution: the tables carry no
EFFECTIVE\_BANDWIDTH column (no online spectral averaging) and the full-resolution windows are
named FULL\_RES, so the 15.625-MHz coarse and 488.28125-kHz fine
channelisations are the noise bandwidths the thresholds mean.

\subsection*{Linewidth convention, and the running-median transfer
function}

The detection statistic is the flux density in a single channel after
drift correction, so the quoted EIRP$_{5\sigma}$ applies to any
transmitter narrower than the native channel, flat in the assumed
linewidth. Two sub-channel qualifications: flatness is exact for a tone
centred in a channel (a boundary-straddling tone splits its power
between two, at worst a factor of two; measured in
Appendix~\ref{app:inject}); and the channel-to-channel transition
follows the correlator's passband shape, not retained in the frozen
products, making the flatness a channel-centred idealisation accurate
at the tens-of-per-cent level. Analytically, a tone placed uniformly at
random within a channel keeps a fraction $\max(f,1{-}f)$ of its power
in the stronger channel, $f$ uniform over the channel, so the
amplitude cost is uniform between 0.5 and 1 (mean 0.75): individual
thresholds carry a sub-channel-position systematic of order tens of
per cent, and EIRP values are therefore quoted to two significant
figures (a randomised through-pipeline robustness test
remains pending; \S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\emph{Transmitters wider than one channel.} The baseline filter is a
running median along frequency, whose transfer function is flat for
features narrower than roughly half the median width $W$ and falls to
zero for structure broader than $W$: the survey's effective
sensitivity is therefore to channel-confined morphologies, and $W$
(in channels, fixed per setup in the released code) is the linewidth
ceiling. Within that ceiling the sensitivity to a top-hat transmitter
spanning $N_{\rm ch}$ native channels follows from the statistic's
single-channel definition: the per-channel flux is the total divided
by $N_{\rm ch}$ at unchanged per-channel noise, so to first order
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}(N_{\rm ch}) \;\simeq\; N_{\rm ch}\times
\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}(1), \qquad N_{\rm ch} \lesssim W/2,
\end{equation}
the same rescaling the main text applies to signals broader than one
channel. Engineered signals need not be intrinsically Hz-wide; this
function is how the quoted thresholds answer for them. For the deepest
window (UV~Ceti Band~3), $1.6\times10^{13}$\,W at a 15.625-MHz
channel corresponds to a minimum detectable spectral power of
$1.0\times10^{6}$\,W\,Hz$^{-1}$: channelised at 1\,Hz from the start
the same observation would reach $4\times10^{9}$\,W, and the factor
${\sim}4000$ between them is the unavoidable price of archival
channelisation (\S\ref{sec:frames}).

\medskip
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Worked example: from flux to EIRP.} Take UV~Ceti
(G~272-61B) Band~3. The searched window's per-channel rms is
0.244\,mJy, so $S_{\rm min} = 5\times0.244 = 1.22$\,mJy in one
15.625-MHz native channel. At 2.67\,pc that is
$1.22\times10^{-29}$\,W\,m$^{-2}$\,Hz$^{-1}$, and
$\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma} = 4\pi d^{2} S_{\rm min} \Delta\nu = 4\pi
(2.67\,{\rm pc})^{2} \times 1.22\times10^{-29}\,{\rm
W\,m^{-2}\,Hz^{-1}} \times 15.625\,{\rm MHz} =
1.6\times10^{13}$\,W. A wider transmitter needs $S_{\rm min}$ per channel.

\subsection*{Intra-integration smearing}

The frequency excursion at the maximum searched drift rate across one
integration, $\dot\nu_{\max} t_{\rm int}$, is evaluated for every
window: at most 1.10 channels across the \NWindows{} windows (median
0.001, 90th percentile 0.06). A linearly drifting signal smeared by
$\Delta$ channels within an integration retains a fraction $\eta_{\rm
smear} = \mathrm{sinc}(\pi\Delta/2)$ of its peak-channel amplitude, so
a worst-drift transmitter's effective threshold is
$\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}/\eta_{\rm smear}$; the three affected windows
of \NWindows{} are those of Table~\ref{tab:smear}, the median window
essentially unsmeared. A signal drifting midway between adjacent trial rates
accumulates at most 0.03 channels of residual drift across the grid as
realised, so no significant sensitivity is lost between trial rates.

\section{Injection-recovery sensitivity validation}
\label{app:inject}

The quoted limits assume a $5\sigma$ threshold behaves as idealised
Gaussian noise predicts. We test that by injecting synthetic
narrowband signals of known amplitude into real calibrated visibilities
via the extraction code's own mechanism, as far upstream of the
statistic as retained products allow, and measuring what the unmodified
pipeline recovers. Three results elsewhere in the paper rest on this
campaign and inherit its single-configuration limitation: the
$17\rightarrow\RecTwice$ per cent drifting-class recovery entering the
measured completeness of \S\ref{sec:null} and
Table~\ref{tab:classcomp}; the machinery and boundary-placement
amplitude factors quoted in \S\ref{sec:validation}; and the
coarse-window dwell-completeness reading of \S\ref{sec:dwell}.

Six configurations span three bands and both resolution classes: three coarse Band~7 spws of AT~Mic, one coarse spw each in Bands~3
and~6 of AU~Mic, and AU~Mic's 488-kHz flagband window (49-trial drift
grid). Into each goes a complex tone at the star position, at
amplitudes $4$--$10\times$ the statistic's own noise (the five levels
of Fig.~\ref{fig:completeness}), drift fractions
$f_{\rm drift}=0$, $0.25$, $0.5$, $0.75$, $1.0$ of the window's searched
ceiling, on- and half-grid rates, and channel-centred and boundary
placements: 200 trials per configuration, \NTrials{} in all, every
marginal in the tables below pooling the grid-phase and placement
draws of these 200, under a
frozen recovery criterion ($T\geq5$, peak channel within 3 of the
injection, peak drift within one grid step;
Fig.~\ref{fig:completeness}).

The coarse-window variant of this campaign recovered 0 of 500
injections at every amplitude from $4\sigma$ to $10\sigma$ under the
frozen criterion -- a recovery-criterion artefact, not a pipeline
suppression; the full history and its withdrawal are audited in
Appendix~\ref{app:exclusions}. In brief: the criterion's drift-matching
clause cannot be satisfied on coarse windows, where every in-grid
drift is sub-channel over the track, so the reported peak drift is
arbitrary; scoring the same windows on detection alone recovers
essentially everything, and an end-to-end injection through the
released pipeline returns $T_\star$ of 20.5, 65.3 and 217.7 at 1, 3
and 10 times the per-integration noise. The coarse-window limits
therefore bound carriers of any dwell fraction, with the completeness
of \S\ref{sec:dwell}, and what those windows genuinely lack is drift
discrimination. The fine window measures the same
boundary in drift space: carriers with $f_{\rm drift}\leq0.25$ dwell
${\geq}40$ per cent of the track per channel and fail the criterion,
those
with $f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$ surviving as the searched class.

The second result is the completeness curve. On the fine window,
drifting carriers ($f_{\rm drift}\geq0.5$, pooled over grid phase and
sub-channel placement) are recovered 17, 42, 58, 75 and 83 per cent of
the time at $4$, $5$, $6$, $8$ and $10\sigma$, saturating by twice
threshold power (Fig.~\ref{fig:compsurface}). The machinery alone (tone
injected after the baseline step) is amplitude-faithful to within
$-9$/$+14$ per cent for channel-centred tones at every drift fraction;
boundary-straddling placement costs a factor $0.6$--$0.8$ in the
statistic and recovers 0 per cent at $5\sigma$ against 83 per cent for
centred ones (67 and 100 per cent at $10\sigma$), crossing 50 per cent
near $1.4\times$ threshold amplitude and recovering fully at
${\sim}1.6\times$ threshold power. Survey completeness uses the
placement-pooled curve, conservative for the boundary-straddling case.
For that class EIRP$_{50} = 1.10\,\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}$ (the
interpolated half-recovery point, $5.5\sigma$), while EIRP$_{90} >
2.0\,\mathrm{EIRP}_{5\sigma}$ is a bound only, the curve reaching 83 per
cent at the largest amplitude injected. Coarse windows carry no
searched-class EIRP$_{50}$ by construction; Table~\ref{tab:classcomp}
collects the per-class reading. Band~8 windows share the coarse-channel
construction, so the static-carrier result holds there by the same
mechanism, but their drifting-class completeness is transferred rather
than measured (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). Not spanned: other target
environments, primary-beam-offset positions, integration durations and
edge-phased drift grids; campaigns on Band~8 data and on the
$\beta$~Pictoris windows are infeasible with the measurement sets
retained locally.

\emph{Transfer to other windows.} Survey completeness is measured on
one configuration -- AU~Mic's 488-kHz Band~6 window -- and applied to
all \NFine{} fine windows; the transfer assumption and its unbounded
error are stated in \S\ref{sec:discussion}, and bounding it needs the
multi-configuration campaign of \S\ref{sec:futurework}.

\section{Closure-phase point-source vetting}
\label{app:closure}

A threshold crossing faces one further automated test, available only
to an interferometer: the closure phase
$\arg(V_{ij})+\arg(V_{jk})-\arg(V_{ik})$ around a triangle vanishes
for an unresolved point source anywhere on sky and is immune to
per-station calibration errors -- it asks only whether the signal is
a coherent point source at all, so a coherent far-sidelobe interferer
would pass. Consistency with zero is scored by a circular
(unit-phasor, phases wrapping at $\pm\pi$) z-score of the mean
drift-tracked closure phase, its null calibrated empirically on each
observation's own integrations, which also absorbs the
shared-baseline covariance that makes one observation's triangles
non-independent. Validated on synthetic visibilities and driven end
to end on continuum detections in four local reprocessing fields
(including the resolved AT~Mic Band~7 binary as a resolving control
and the AU~Mic Band~6 crossing frequency), the capability is banked
but not yet useful: per-baseline S/N is 0.5--0.7 at the median and
never exceeds 5.1 (0.02 per cent of triangles have all three
baselines above S/N${\,=\,}5$), so every field's closure phases are
indistinguishable from noise, including the binary -- a flagged
window's formal point-source consistency carries no evidential weight
at these flux densities (\S\ref{sec:ancillary}). The test triggers
only on threshold crossing, at negligible cost, and becomes a
discriminator only for deeper releases.

\section{Chirped-drift and periodicity screen: summary and full specification}
\label{app:extended}

Stacking at constant linear drift rate makes the primary search blind
by design to a transmitter whose drift rate changes during the
observation, and to pulsed or periodically-modulated carriers. We
implemented a chirped (quadratic-drift) extension of the same stacking
on a coarser grid of 21 linear-drift $\times$ 5 chirp-rate trials, and
a per-channel periodicity search -- a per-position FFT along each
channel's time axis, its peak power against a robust per-channel noise
estimate. Both vet the star's dynamic spectrum against 8 retained
control positions, a strict subset of that window's \NCtrl-position
ring, not an independent ensemble (\S\ref{sec:method}). On the 122
retained spectra this is a demonstration rather than survey-grade,
establishing only that the extension runs and behaves correctly under
the null: the flag rates (10.7\% chirp, 13.1\% periodicity,
against the $\sim$11\% chance rate) are as expected, no instance is
treated as a candidate, and no limit derives from it. Extending both to
the full \NCtrl-position ring -- the spectra being retained -- would
promote the screen to survey grade.

\section{Cross-target frequency-occupancy check: summary, kernel, coordinates, and scope}
\label{app:freqoccupancy}

With no ON/OFF cadence against radio-frequency interference, this
single-pointing archival survey substitutes the many unrelated stars
sharing similar correlator setups: a channel-confined feature recurring at
the same observed \emph{sky frequency} towards different targets is
almost certainly RFI or instrumental. Over every target/spw result the
check pairs both the best-fit-channel frequency and the formal
$\geq5\sigma$ crossings, matching $\nu_1,\nu_2$ towards different
targets when $|\nu_1-\nu_2| < \max(k\,\Delta\nu_1,\; k\,\Delta\nu_2)$
with $k=3$ ($\sim$46\,kHz; $k$ between $\sim$0.2 and $\sim$330 in
native channels spans the original 5\,MHz tolerance). Across the
\NWindows{} windows and 107 tabulated crossings, no pair falls within
the kernel, or within 0.5\,MHz; the kernel is narrower than the
$\beta$~Pictoris line offsets of 10.6 and 21.9\,MHz, so those windows
were dispositioned by line-catalogue inspection instead.

\emph{The three frequency coordinates.} A crossing's \emph{sky frequency},
reconstructed from archive metadata in the observation's recorded
frame; its topocentric frequency; and its \emph{correlator channel
index}. We probe the first, so a frame-convention inconsistency, or an
artefact fixed in the second, would defeat it; correlator-frame
artefacts recur at fixed intermediate frequency or channel number, not
fixed sky frequency. Matching on the third coordinate (same channel,
identical correlator setup) also returns zero pairs; a full
intermediate-frequency reconstruction across tunings is not
recoverable from the retained metadata.

\emph{Exposure and caveats.} The null's power is the count of target
pairs sharing frequency coverage: on the $\le$20\,pc subset run so
far, 366 of 946 star pairs (39\%) overlap by at least 0.01\,GHz
(deepest 14.25\,GHz, $\gamma$~Lep--$\gamma$~Vir), and 109 pairs
involve one of the five stars carrying formal crossings, so every
crossing frequency was tested against genuinely overlapping
independent targets. The UV~Ceti and AT~Mic shared execution blocks
(\S\ref{sec:sample}) are excluded, their intra-pair ``coincidences''
being identities by construction. The interim product's per-window crossing
lists are capped at 50 frequencies, so the true count is 171 crossings
against 107 tabulated -- the difference is one window ($\beta$~Pic
Band~6 ultra-fine, 114 formal crossings, the 50 strongest tabulated)
whose untabulated 64 could not be tested: the null is
demonstrated for the tabulated set, unresolved for the rest, a
limitation of the retained product that the public release removes by
shipping uncapped crossing lists (Data Availability).

\section{The velocity-space line mask: justification and retrospective application}
\label{app:linemask}

The one-channel line check failed the $\beta$~Pictoris windows
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}): a native channel spans a fraction of a
km\,s$^{-1}$, while circumstellar kinematics shift real emission by
many channels. The replacement mask takes its tolerance from the
kinematic budget of circumstellar gas -- Keplerian motion where these
molecules survive ($\gtrsim$10\,au) $\lesssim$13\,km\,s$^{-1}$;
cold-gas linewidths $\lesssim$1\,km\,s$^{-1}$; the eight masked
species cold dense-gas tracers, no bulk-wind term for the dwarfs
dominating the sample; the uncorrected barycentric term bounded by
$\pm$30\,km\,s$^{-1}$ -- and is empirically conservative: the largest
attributed circumstellar component ($\beta$~Pic, $-2.7$ to
$-3.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$) sits at 7 per cent of it, and \emph{zero}
crossing frequencies lie 13--50\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from their nearest
transition, so a $\pm13$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ Keplerian-only mask would have
suppressed and released the same windows: the wider tolerance costs no
yield and buys robustness against radial-velocity catalogue error.

Two of the six remaining crossing windows fall inside the mask (the
unflagged $\beta$~Pic coarse window at $-3.6$\,km\,s$^{-1}$; HD~285968
at $+8.7$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, already rejected by its control ring, maximum
10.9 against a star peak of 6.5); the rest lie far outside any
circumstellar tolerance (AU~Mic $+379$, LHS~1140 $+115$\,km\,s$^{-1}$).
Of the \NHits{} windows with an on-star crossing, \NSpatial{} also beat their
control ring, three accounted for by the mask. The other
\NonExcRingGe{} are unremarkable the way ordinary noise crossings are:
in every one of them the window's own control ring reaches or exceeds
the stellar value (ring-maximum median \NonExcRingMed{} against a
crossing median \NonExcHitMed), the expected Gaussian-tailed population
of moderate $5$--$5.5\sigma$ crossings with comparably strong peaks
among \NCtrl{} controls. But the transitions that
make astrophysical false positives cheap are also natural
reference frequencies for a deliberate communicator
\citep{CocconiMorrison1959}, so
the released products keep a known-line-coincident list -- every
on-source $5\sigma$ crossing inside the masked intervals, with its
offset from each nearby transition -- for a future search with
independent discrimination.

\section{AU~Mic: the three tests in full}
\label{app:aumic}

\emph{Localisation.} Reprocessing the first-epoch block from the raw
science data model with the observatory pipeline's own calibration
recipe (310 integrations against the release pipeline's 249, on a finer
drift grid) and running the symmetric star-versus-control statistic of
\S\ref{sec:technosearch} on the crossing window, the star does not beat
its control ring. The frozen products agree only asymmetrically: the
source-region peak exceeds the \NCtrl-control maximum by 0.12, below
that statistic's window-to-window scatter, and the crossing fails the
single-position variant too.

\emph{Recurrence.} The one further public Band~6 block covering
230.83\,GHz, the same programme executed 2014 June~5, was searched
identically: no channel within $\pm$10 of the first-epoch flagged
channel exceeds 3.1, and nothing is localised to the stellar position.
Its band rms (1.48\,mJy per 488-kHz channel) matches the first epoch's
1.9\,mJy, so a comparable excess would have been recovered, and the
frequency recurs at no other target. Intermittent or precisely-scheduled
transmissions outside the sampled windows stay unconstrained, a caveat
quantified by the dwell-fraction and epoch-activity analysis of
\S\ref{sec:discussion}.

\emph{Statistical scale.} The on-source peak (5.97) sits at the \AuPct{}th percentile of the control-maxima distribution over all
\NWindows{} windows (median \CtrlMed, 90th percentile \CtrlPninety;
Fig.~\ref{fig:aumic}): \AuNge{} (\AuFrac{} per cent) match or exceed
it, add-one empirical $p=\AuP$, before any multiple-testing
correction -- a release-wide window-level rate, distinct from the
within-window rank quoted in \S\ref{sec:aumic}. It matches no
catalogued molecular transition (287\,MHz from CO $J{=}2{\to}1$);
closure-phase vetting finds it point-source consistent, one-sided
evidence only (Appendix~\ref{app:closure}).

Two astrophysical contexts are recorded for future re-examination;
neither grounds the rejection. AU~Mic is an exceptionally active flare
star, but the block's own quiescent continuum (0.15\,mJy) rules the
later-epoch GHz-wide flares of \citet{MacGregor2020} (16.8 and
6.1\,mJy) out as origin: an 11\,mJy channel-confined excess would sit a
factor ${\sim}70$ above a continuum that shows nothing, and the
same-tuning second epoch shows no burst. The controls share the
primary-beam footprint, so smooth structure such as the debris belt
raises star and controls alike and cannot alone produce a localised
excess.

\section{The ancillary continuum lane: search, detections and population screen}
\label{app:continuum}
\label{app:contsearch}

The continuum lane is an exploratory by-product, not a co-equal
search: 57 measurements (17 detections, 40 limits; comparison bins of
4--7 stars), a millimetre excess being at best an indirect,
model-dependent observable of waste-heat technology. An ``upper
limit'' is $5\times$ the primary-beam-corrected map rms at the star's
position, the per-target rms of Appendix~\ref{app:table} rescaling it
to any confidence level; the deepest non-detection is towards
TRAPPIST-1 in Band~3. Under the combined error model sixteen of the
seventeen detections stay above $5\sigma$, the exception ($\eta$~Corvi
Band~7, $4.7\sigma$ combined) being marginal. Bright far-from-target
peaks ($\epsilon$\,Eri Band~6 at 10.2$\sigma$, 18\,arcsec off the
phase centre; GJ~674 and CD$-$23~14742 at 10.7 and 9.9$\sigma$) fall
outside the 3-arcsec tolerance and are recorded as non-detections,
though the retained products cannot say whether the $\epsilon$\,Eri
peak is real emission or a primary-beam artefact (\S\ref{sec:method}).

Every detection is astrophysically unsurprising: the unresolved
UV~Ceti binary; six young active systems hosting discs or
chromospheres ($\tau$~Cet, $\beta$~Pic, AU~Mic, AT~Mic); six stars at
photospheric flux ($\gamma$~Lep, $\gamma$~Vir, $\chi^{1}$~Ori,
$\eta$~Cru); and two late-M dwarfs of documented magnetic activity,
LP~349$-$25 and the auroral emitter LSR~J1835$+$3259
\citep{Hallinan2007}, which merits deeper follow-up. Closure phase
cannot discriminate structure for any of the seventeen at these flux
densities (Appendix~\ref{app:closure}).

\emph{The anomaly screen.} Each star's flux is tested against its own
photospheric expectation (Planck function with Gaia DR3 parameters,
Pecaut \& Mamajek fallback where GSP-Phot is unavailable -- close to
half the sample; rerunning with fallback parameters imposed on every
star leaves the sole flag and the bin scales essentially unchanged)
and against same-$T_{\rm eff}$ stars in coarse bins of at least five,
through a robust median/MAD statistic with the bin width a fixed
pipeline constant. The error model, evaluated before any flag, is
$\sigma_{\rm tot}^{2} = \sigma_{\rm map}^{2} + (f_{\rm cal}\,S_\nu)^{2}
+ \sigma_{\rm model}^{2}$: $f_{\rm cal}$ 5 per cent in Bands~3--6 and
10 per cent in Bands~7--8; $\sigma_{\rm model}$ propagated from Gaia
temperature and radius errors and the fallback relation's scatter,
dominating for fallback stars as the calibration term does for the
brightest detections. The per-target file's $\sigma_{\rm tot}$ column
carries map and calibration terms only. At the five-star minimum the
bin median rests on two or three values, so the screen flags for
inspection, not per-star hypothesis tests. One outlier results:
$\chi^1$~Ori at Band~3 (excess ratio 2.52, largest of the seventeen;
resampled tail probability 0.53 in its six-star bin -- a ranked
outlier of a small sample, not a significant departure), consistent
with a spectroscopic G0V binary's unresolved companion or a
chromosphere. Per-bin medians and log-scatters ship in the
machine-readable release; two robustness reruns are pending.

\section{A serendipitous molecular-line catalogue}
\label{app:linecat}

The continuum search's line-exclusion step (\S\ref{sec:method}) flags
every $\geq5\sigma$ outlier channel as a by-product; no existing
mm/submm line survey of the solar neighbourhood rests on a
distance-limited sample of archival coverage. Twelve of the outlier
groups scanned to date are single- or few-channel features with no
known transition within 300\,MHz, most plausibly statistical; six are
plausibly real:
CO($J{=}1{\to}0$) and CO($J{=}2{\to}1$) towards $\beta$~Pictoris (the
stellar-frame component of Table~\ref{tab:bpicaudit}); a marginal
CO($J{=}2{\to}1$)-consistent feature recurring across two epochs
towards HD~285968, tentative only; and a single-channel feature
195\,MHz from SiO($J{=}5{\to}4$) towards HD~53143, more likely
statistical than real. The machine-readable catalogue ships with the
tagged public release.

\section{Lessons for archival pipelines}
\label{app:lessons}

Three of the four audit items of \S\ref{sec:exclusions} are failure
modes generic to automated archive mining. First, every lane needs
its own geometric guard: item (ii)'s pointing mismatch aborts the
carrier step but not continuum imaging. Second, window-level
bookkeeping anomalies yield to cross-target pattern recognition, not
per-target sanity checks: item (iii) recurred with matching
correlator parameters across two unrelated targets. Third, name
resolution is not position resolution: the $\gamma$~Lupi entry
(item~iv below) was misidentified through the SIMBAD identifier list
and caught only by recovering the Gaia~DR3 parallax digit-for-digit;
that check should be standard.

\section{False-alarm accounting under the symmetric criterion}
\label{app:falsealarm}

Earlier drafts carried a four-model bookkeeping ladder
(permutation, hypergeometric, block bootstrap and a Poisson rate
heuristic, $P(F{\geq}3)=6.1$--9.2 per cent) calibrating a false-alarm
rate for the \emph{asymmetric} statistic; the symmetric statistic of
\S\ref{sec:technosearch} removes that need, giving a per-window rate
$1/\RankFloor$ and survey expectation $\NWindows/\RankFloor=\ExpFlags$
flags, so the ladder is retired, not updated.

The $1/\RankFloor$ rank floor of \S\ref{sec:technosearch} falls only if
the control ring grows, which the primary beam bounds; and windows are
not fully independent, grouped into \NEB{} EBs sharing weather,
calibration and correlator setup, so $N/\RankFloor$ is an expectation
over a mildly correlated sample, not $N$ independent trials. Both limits
are conservative for the conclusion drawn -- that no window warrants
candidate status -- widening, not narrowing, the null range.

\subsection*{The symmetric star-versus-control null in full}

The per-window $p$-value of the test of \S\ref{sec:technosearch} is
$p = (1+\#\{T_i\geq T_\star\})/(N+1)$, and its seven measured windows
are the six validation windows of the local reprocessing set plus the
AU~Mic crossing's reprocessed first-epoch window (\S\ref{sec:aumic}).

Table~\ref{tab:trials} consolidates the family-wise trials factors:
every per-window factor applies identically at the \NCtrl{} controls,
making the control ring and its empirical star-exceeds-ring rate the
operative calibration, not any analytic correction.

\subsection*{Robustness of the empirical calibration}

The release evaluates $\TrialCells$ channel$\times$drift cells; a
Gaussian one-sided $5\sigma$ tail ($2.87\times10^{-7}$) over that
count predicts 11.4 chance crossings against the \NHits{} windows with
an on-star crossing. Raw counts run higher because the drift-tracked
statistic is strongly correlated between adjacent channels
($\rho_{\rm lag1}=0.989$--$0.991$, i.e.\ $8.5\times10^{4}$--$1.1
\times10^{5}$ effectively independent cells), so crossings arrive in
contiguous runs; collapsed to clusters (kernel of 3 native channels)
they leave far fewer independent trials, and that count agrees with
the trials expectation.

No single stratum drives the star-exceeds-ring rate. The
stratification was run on the $\le$20\,pc subset under the withdrawn
asymmetric statistic, whose counts it quotes -- the operative
symmetric rate is too small (\NSbr{} windows) to stratify. Across
band, channelisation and primary-beam offset that rate moved only
between 11 and 20 per cent, and the disc-hosting caveat inverts
empirically: disc- or planet-hosting stars contribute every crossing
window and every stage-1 statistical flag but a \emph{lower}
star-exceeds-ring rate (9.8 against 18.3 per cent for non-hosts), so
circumstellar emission shared by star and ring does not drive the
statistic. Excluding the \NBPWindows{} $\beta$~Pictoris windows leaves
one unattributed crossing among \NNonBP, against 0.78 expected
($P(\geq1)=54$ per cent).

Beyond the shared primary-beam gain, neighbouring control positions
are correlated below the synthesised-beam scale, so independent
spatial trials fall from \NCtrl{} to $N_{\rm eff}\simeq30$--$43$, as
the symmetric reprocessing runs measure directly -- a floor to which
the empirical \RateSbr{} per cent rate, measured on the correlated
ensembles themselves, is immune by construction. A continuum source
in the ring raises the carrier statistic only if it also emits a
narrow line in-window, and Appendix~\ref{app:freqoccupancy} covers
recurring frequencies.

\section{Integration-time audit: method and exceptions}
\label{app:audit}

The archive cross-check of \S\ref{sec:ancillaryresults} uses the ALMA
Science Archive's \texttt{ivoa.obscore} service (queried 2026
September~8), whose \texttt{t\_exptime} rows give the science-intent
exposure per observing unit set and pointed science source, summed
over its executions, covering every window-level record of the subset
by star and EB. For each of the 57 searched EBs the pointed source
came from the archive's field list (aliases resolved by hand: Sirius~B
through the Sirius~A pointings; the UV~Ceti/G~272-61 and AT~Mic pairs
share fields), its on-source time compared with the archive exposure.
\texttt{obscore} counts science-intent scans only, hiding target time
in calibrator-intent scans, so the archive figure is a lower bound and
any pipeline over-count conservative; per-execution durations are not
exposed, so multi-execution sets are compared through their aggregate.
The audit is conservative for both flagged hosts
(\S\ref{sec:technosearch}): $\beta$~Pictoris's 1824--1845\,s sit
inside an archive exposure of 47\,444\,s, AU~Mic's inside 109\,334\,s,
no disposition changing, and the archive values lower the
exposure-weighted coverage total by under 1 per cent.

Two of the 58 star--execution-block groups over-count: AT~Mic's wide
windows in block A002\_Xcd97a1\_X3f1e (pipeline 1258\,s against the
archive's 877\,s, ratio 1.43) and CD$-$38~10980 in A002\_Xe5808e\_X80a1
(617 against 575\,s, 1.07); the 53 remaining groups are all at or
below the archive exposure, three more (Wolf~28, the narrow AT~Mic
window, G~272-61) agreeing to within 1 per cent. The AT~Mic block
shows the signature of a dump-counting defect: three windows report
208 integrations where 145 exist, and more on-source time than the
block's own 1140\,s elapsed span. The duty table uses the
archive-consistent value for both AT~Mic entries; the over-counted
values survive only in per-window machine-readable fields. The CD$-$38~10980 excess is within the
intent-filter's conservative margin and its limit is unaffected,
thresholds deriving from measured rms rather than integration time.

\subsection*{Historical false-alarm diagnostics of the retired statistic}

Kept so the main-text account (\S\ref{sec:technosearch}) can be
symmetric-only; nothing here enters the operative false-alarm
probability. Under a pure-noise null the region maximum over

star-exceeds-ring rate (\RateSbrOld{} against the symmetric

interchanged); bookkeeping built on the asymmetric rate expected
\NSbrOld{} flagged windows against the symmetric \ExpFlags, with
secondary models spanning $P(F{\geq}3)=1.0$--9.2 per cent. That
bracket was the sensitivity of the pre-freeze accounting to the
statistic choice -- the deposit the freeze protected against. This
history mixes three failure kinds, kept distinct: a \emph{design
error} (the asymmetric statistic), a \emph{data/bookkeeping defect}
(the noise-defect windows below), and \emph{analysis choices} frozen
with their alternatives stated.

\section{Population-inference framework: derivation and demonstration tables}
\label{app:population}

This appendix consolidates the numerical demonstrations
condensed out of \S\ref{sec:null}, labelled \emph{illustrative
calculation only} throughout. Everything here is conditional on
Eq.~\ref{eq:conditioning} with $F_i{=}D_i{=}1$, and $f$ is an
\emph{effective conditional in-band transmitter fraction given
occupancy of each target's own observed frequencies} -- defined on
the \NSystems{} systems processed here, not the \NCensus{}-star
archive-complete sample and still less the 40\,pc stellar population.

\emph{Illustrative calculation only.} The demonstration
number and its sensitivity analysis follow; none of it is a
survey result. At EIRP $\geq10^{16}$\,W the measured form gives
$f_{95}\approx\OccMeasuredRound$ per cent over the \NOccSystems{}
systems with a fine-channel window -- conditional on in-band,
always-on emission of the searched morphology ($F_i{=}D_i{=}1$ by
conditioning, not measurement); on a drifting-class recovery curve
measured on one fine-channel configuration and transferred by
assumption to all \NFine{} fine windows, whose error is bracketed
below; and on the archivally observed subsample, in which F and G stars are
over-represented ($\times3.1$--$3.4$) and M dwarfs under-represented
($\times0.6$--$0.7$) relative to the 40\,pc census
(\S\ref{sec:sample}), so no demographic statement about the solar
neighbourhood follows. Under any transmitter-frequency prior spread
across ALMA's tuning range the bound vanishes entirely: the
per-system fine-channel union has median 1.73\,GHz against a
${\sim}75$\,GHz range, so the conditioning supplies the entire
content of the number. The full derivation, the thresholded and
bracketing forms (idealised brackets \OccUnit{} and \OccUniform{}
per cent at $10^{16}$\,W), the epoch-activity degradation
($p_{\rm epoch}$ and the intra-track dwell fraction are distinct
axes; panel~b of Table~\ref{tab:occurrence}), the
transfer-error analysis, a Bayesian cross-check (\OccBayes{} per
cent) and Table~\ref{tab:occurrence} are
consolidated in this appendix.

\emph{Independence.} The $C_i$ are not independent: \NWindows{}
windows group into \NEB{} execution blocks sharing weather,
calibration and correlator setup. Across the \EbsRankAll{} execution
blocks of the frozen export -- \EbsRetained{} of them contributing
searched windows (Table~\ref{tab:searchspace}), the withheld
$\epsilon$\,Eri Band~6 block \EbsExtraUid{} accounting for the
difference -- the intraclass correlation of the
window noise floor is $\IccRms$ (shared weather and configuration),
of the control-normalised stellar rank \IccRank, and of the raw
stellar statistic \IccStat. Residual dependence at this level is
one-sided -- positive correlation between detections inflates
the true $f_{95}$ above the independent product. Quantified for the
rank correlation: over the \EffEbsFine{} execution blocks carrying
fine-channel windows (mean \EffKbar{} windows each), \IccRank{}
implies a design effect of \EffDeff, moving the demonstration number
only from \OccMeasuredRound{} to \EffFIccPct{} per cent -- an order
below the completeness-transfer term that dominates the bracket
above.



\emph{Two forms.} Eq.~\ref{eq:prevalence} is evaluated on the
\NSystems{} independently observed systems (\NStars{}
carrier-searched stars; six bound pairs share observations and
count once each). The \emph{thresholded} form is the step function
the retained products support, $R_i(P)=1$ when system $i$'s deepest
threshold lies below $P$, else 0. The \emph{measured} form
integrates the injection-recovery curve of Appendix~\ref{app:inject}
over each system's searched fine windows (the transferred curve
existing only there), folding in the $17\rightarrow\RecTwice$ per
cent recovery of the searched class measured between four and ten
times the noise; it is defined on the \NOccSystems{} systems with a
fine-channel window.

\emph{Transfer error, and why this is a pilot number.} The
drifting-class recovery curve is measured on one fine-channel
configuration, then applied to all \NFine{} fine windows, which span
Bands 3--8, on-source times of \OnsrcMin--\OnsrcMax\,s and a range
of primary-beam offsets. Achieved noise, integration time and
drift-grid density shift the curve mainly in \emph{position}; the
channelisation and median-subtraction dwell fraction that control
its \emph{shape} are common to every fine window by construction.
None of this has been measured, so the transfer is an assumption,
and its error is not bounded by the
$^{+\RecThreshHi}_{-\RecThreshLo}$ percentage-point binomial
interval, which describes only sampling noise within the one
configuration run. One term is bounded analytically: coherently
averaging $N_{\rm int}$ on-source integrations suppresses noise as
$\sqrt{N_{\rm int}}$, so the amplitude anchor can shift by at most
$\sqrt{\OnsrcMax/\OnsrcMin}\approx\IntGainSpan$ between the shortest
and longest windows, and the calibration configuration
(\CalibOnsrc\,s on source) sits a factor \CalibGainRatio{} in
$\sqrt{t_{\rm on}}$ from the median fine window
(\FineOnsrcMedian\,s). Propagated as a bracket: shifting the
amplitude anchor over that factor moves the 95-per-cent limit by
only \OccAnchorShift{} points (most fine windows sit on the
plateau); scaling the transferred curve itself by 0.5--2$\times$
moves it from \OccMeasured{} to \OccBracketLo--\OccBracketHi{} per
cent. The v3.28 products preserve that campaign's pooled recovery
fractions but not its trial-level records, so its denominators come
from the design, not a log; the dwell campaign (\S\ref{sec:dwell})
releases one row per injection and is re-derivable, and it supplies
what the coarse class previously lacked entirely -- a survey-threshold
completeness curve, measured on six coarse configurations
(Table~\ref{tab:compcurve}). Folding it in, so that each window
carries the completeness measured for the morphology it actually
searches -- the drifting curve on Class~A, the dwell curve on
Class~B -- extends the measured form from the \NOccSystems{}
fine-calibrated systems to \NOccAll{}, and moves the demonstration
number from \OccMeasured{} to \OccAll{} per cent
($\OccAllDhalf$ per cent at $p_{\rm epoch}=0.5$, \OccAllDtenth{} per
cent at 0.1; \OccAllFifteen{} per cent over \NOccAllFifteen{} systems
at $10^{15}$\,W and \OccAllSeventeen{} per cent over
\NOccAllSeventeen{} at $10^{17}$\,W). It remains a \emph{pilot} estimate -- the Class~A
curve is still single-configuration and its transfer error still
unbounded, and $F_i{=}D_i{=}1$ throughout -- and stays secondary to
the non-detection and threshold range.

\emph{Which number is which.} The measured form carries the only
measured recovery fraction; the thresholded form (\OccUnit{} per
cent) assumes unit recovery, a uniform $C=0.42$ gives \OccUniform{}
per cent, and a Bayesian treatment with a uniform prior on $f$,
$p(f\,|\,{\rm data})\propto\prod_i(1-f\,C_i)$, gives \OccBayes{} per
cent -- bracketing calculations, none a prevalence measurement, and
each applying to transmitters above its $P$, not one well-defined
class.

\emph{Epoch-activity ladder.} Writing $p_{\rm epoch}$ for the chance
a transmitter is on in any one epoch, a system observed in $n_i$
independent execution blocks is sampled $n_i$ times, so
$D_i=1-(1-p_{\rm epoch})^{n_i}$; \NMultiEpoch{} of the \NSystems{}
systems have $n_i>1$ (13 with two epochs, five with three). The
Bernoulli form assumes independent epochs; the release spans
2013--2025 at year-scale separations ($\beta$~Pictoris: 2013, 2019,
2022), so orbit-tied beacons enter at effectively random phase and,
with at most three epochs, the ladder is a phase-averaged bound.
An intermittent transmitter is at least as plausible \emph{a
priori}, so the $p_{\rm epoch}=1$ numbers should never be quoted
without this dependence.

\emph{Series-level error control.} The prespecified family-level
false-alarm procedure for this release is the Monte-Carlo
pseudo-target calibration of \S\ref{sec:technosearch} (expected
\PsFlagMu{} flags against a rank-only budget of \ExpFlags); the
Bonferroni, Wilson and Poisson calculations of
Appendix~\ref{app:falsealarm} are robustness checks against it, not
independent claims. Across releases the cumulative look-elsewhere
effect of component~(v) applies: each release's unattributed flags
will be reported against the \emph{cumulative} expected count (a
Poisson tail on the running total), the series-level
false-discovery rate controlled by Benjamini--Hochberg on per-window
empirical spatial rank probabilities (the $1/\RankFloor$ floor limits their resolution)
and promoted flags keeping the per-release pre-commitment of
\S\ref{sec:technosearch}. The rule is provisional and will be fixed,
with power simulations, before the second release.
