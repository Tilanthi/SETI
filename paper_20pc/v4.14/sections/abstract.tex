\begin{abstract}
The published radio technosignature effort stops near 30\,GHz; no search
above it has reached a star within 40\,pc. The ALMA archive reaches that
band incidentally: observations of dust discs and planet formation record a
spectrum of the star at every field centre. Re-reduced uniformly from the
raw visibilities they search for a drifting, unresolved carrier at the
stellar position in \NWinA{} fine-channel windows toward \NSysClassA{}
stellar systems, with a coarse-channel bound on an unresolved spectral
excess that discriminates no carrier. Nothing met the confirmation
criterion. Of the \EvNCrossRaw{} threshold crossings, \PrFlagXrossWord{}
arise in one execution block that fails a quality criterion fixed in
advance, and \EvNAttrQp{} of the rest coincide with a catalogued molecular
transition, among them $\beta$~Pictoris's carbon monoxide, recovered blind. Every
unattributed crossing was tested for recurrence in the star's own frame, in
each later covering window at every drift searched; none returned, including
\PrRankClause. That excludes a carrier holding its frequency inside the
window searched and radiating through both observations, not a persistent
transmitter of any kind; and the remainder is consistent with chance, to the
accuracy with which control positions stand in for the star. Depth is a recovery
fraction, not a threshold: injected carriers come back nine times in ten at
$\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W for the median system, over a union bandwidth about
\PrConfFactor{} what recurrence could test. Molecular-line confusion and
sparse repeat coverage, not thermal sensitivity, limit a search of this
kind.
\end{abstract}
