The order in which these two things happened is a fair question, and the record
answers it against us. AU~Mic was flagged as a candidate under the region-max
statistic on 2026 August~31 (commit \texttt{8e00f90e8f8c}, 11:55:04~UTC), in the
machine-readable aggregate of that version's search products and in the version's
own text. The asymmetry in that statistic was identified, and the symmetric
replacement adopted, nine days later on 2026 September~9 (commit
\texttt{0c465f2661bd}, 16:14:07~UTC). We have looked for an earlier trace and
there is none: the first committed text that even diagnoses the asymmetry is
dated 2026 September~9 as well, 15:39:59~UTC. The redesign therefore postdates
the flag by nine days, and no part of it can be dated before the window that it
removed.

What carries the argument instead is that the replacement follows from the
definition of the test rather than from anything seen in this window. Under a
pure-noise null with exchangeable positions, the maximum over $n_{\rm src}$
source-region positions exceeds the maximum over $n_{\rm ctrl}$ single controls
with probability $n_{\rm src}/(n_{\rm src}+n_{\rm ctrl})$, which for
$n_{\rm src}=\RingNSrc$ and $n_{\rm ctrl}=\NCtrl$ is
\AsymExpectPct{}~per~cent. That is a property of every window in the survey and
was computed for none of them individually. Two further facts bear on it. The
released record of the flagged window already contains the symmetric quantity,
and it is unremarkable: the single propagated stellar position reached
$\mathrm{S/N}=5.22$ against a control maximum of $5.85$, while the region
maximum, $5.97$, was the value that exceeded it. The symmetric test would not
have selected this window even on the data as they then stood. And the set of
windows flagged under the symmetric criterion is a strict subset of the set
flagged under the region maximum, so the revision removed candidates generally
and was not shaped around one of them. Neither fact establishes priority, and we
do not offer them as though they did.

The retirement criterion can be anchored in the same record, with one limitation
stated plainly. The programme-wide rule that no crossing would be advanced on
rank statistics alone was committed on 2026 September~9 (commit
\texttt{db1e2ee6f7c0}, 19:42:21~UTC), in the same commit that first reports this
window, so the rule is contemporaneous with the flag rather than earlier than it.
The paired promote-and-retire form, including retirement on a clean second epoch,
was committed on 2026 September~11 (commit \texttt{119a68a6ab50}, 11:33:40~UTC).
The second-epoch search began later that day, at 20:19:23~UTC, and wrote its
first product at 21:30:07~UTC. The criterion actually exercised was therefore on
the public record eight hours and forty-six minutes before the second-epoch data
were touched, and the programme-wide rule two days before that. The ordering that
can be demonstrated is criterion before search; the ordering criterion before
flag cannot be demonstrated and we do not claim it.
