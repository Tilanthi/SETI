\subsection{A stellar-frame stacked search}
\label{sec:stack}

Combining a star's epochs in its own rest frame deepens the limit, provided
the emitter holds still in that frame. A frequency fixed in the stellar
frame does not stay fixed at the telescope, since the barycentric term
alone swings by a median \StkVcorrSwingMed\,km\,s$^{-1}$ between the epochs
of one star, many channels at the \StkRefChanKHz\,kHz Class~A width. Each
epoch is therefore
transformed to the stellar rest frame, divided by its own primary-beam
response and combined with the others by inverse variance over the channels
of common coverage. The transform is checked on \StkPersistLineStar's line,
which \StkRegNEpoch{} epochs observe \StkRegTopoKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ apart and
place at one stellar-frame frequency to \FrEpochStelKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The
criterion is applied in the same way at the stacked control positions, and
every stacked limit is measured by injection into the stack.

Of the surveyed stars, \SxStkStarCensus{} can be stacked, \SttNStkOneEb{} of
them with blocks beyond the census, and over \SyStkNGroup{} stacked groups
the flux-density limit improves on each star's best single epoch by a median
factor \SyStkGainMed{} and by \SyStkGainMax{} at most. A further
\SxStkStarOutside{} stars, all control stars beyond 40\,pc, are stacked and left
out of those figures. The gain is \StkGainVsN{} of what $\sqrt{N}$ equal
epochs would give, the shortfall being unequal epoch depth.
In the stack, \StkNSurvive{} groups pass the full criterion and both are
known lines, the $\beta$~Pictoris carbon monoxide of \S\ref{sec:bpicval} in two
transitions, so the positive control passes in the stack as well, and the
survey's own $\pm\StkAttrKms$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ mask leaves \StkUnattrClause.

Every stacked limit holds only for an emitter steady in the stellar frame to
within one of its own channels. No crossing of \S\ref{sec:etacrv} drifts
slowly enough to survive a fine-channel stack, which admits far less
stellar-frame excursion than a coarse one, so the single-epoch limits of
\S\ref{sec:pninetysel} are the ones that apply generally. The deepest
stacked limit is Proxima Centauri's, $\SzProxEirpCensus$\,W from
\SxProxEbCensus{} census blocks of coarse \ChanBModeMHz\,MHz channels, and
like every Class~B value it is a limit on an unresolved narrowband spectral
excess. It also needs a qualification of its own. A transmitter on
\PxPlanet{} would swing over \PxPPKms\,$\sin i$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in its orbit
\citep{AngladaEscude2016,Kervella2017,NEA2013}, some \PxNChanWalk{} channels
at $\sin i=1$. Spread over those channels, such a signal would be diluted
in the stack by roughly the number of channels it crosses, unless the orbit
is seen nearly face-on, with $\sin i\le\PxSiniMax$, which holds for
\PxIsoPct{} per cent of random orientations. Within one block, by contrast, the planet moves at most
\PxBlockPct{} per cent of a channel, so the host's best census single-epoch
limit, $\PxEirpCensB$\,W in a Class~B window, is the one that applies to it.
