\begin{abstract}
Do nearby stars show artificial narrowband carriers? We searched \NWinA{} fine-channel ALMA windows toward
\NSysClassA{} systems within 40\,pc, covering \CvUnionA\,GHz in
\CovNIslands{} separate intervals; \NWinB{} coarser windows constrain only unresolved
emission. Of \CsNCross{} threshold crossings, \MkNCoinc{}
coincide with catalogued lines (\LnAttrSupp{} with further
evidence of line emission). Given the adopted mask and background, the
other \CeObs{} are consistent with chance (\CeExp{} expected from control
positions, $P=\CeProbNull$). No carrier is detected or confirmed: for the
crossings, a non-detection, not an exclusion. Repeat coverage, not depth,
limits confirmation: \ZcNoRepNoSep{} systems were never observed in two separated
sessions and \ZcNoRepNoFreqWord{} more only at different frequencies. For a
continuous carrier narrower than one channel, outside the mask and within the
searched drift, the limits are ninety per cent single-epoch trigger
completeness levels, not deterministic exclusions. The median is
$\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W of equivalent isotropic radiated power, about
\AroMedMult{} times Arecibo's planetary radar. Within the frequency range of
a system's most sensitive window, a carrier at that system's limit would have produced
a crossing in nine trials in ten, atmospheric losses aside. Drifting intermittent or broader signals, faster
drifts, beams aimed elsewhere, other frequencies and unobserved stars
remain open.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Technosignatures, Search for extraterrestrial intelligence, Submillimeter astronomy, Radio spectroscopy, Debris disks}
