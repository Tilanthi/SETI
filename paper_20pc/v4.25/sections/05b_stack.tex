\subsection{A stellar-frame multi-epoch stacked search}
\label{sec:stack}

Combining the epochs of a star in that star's own rest frame deepens the
limit. \EpMulti{} of the \NSystems{} surveyed systems hold more than one
searched execution block, and \SxStkStarCensus{} of the surveyed stars can be
stacked, \SttNStkOneEb{} of them from blocks beyond the census. Over \SyStkNGroup{} stacked groups, \SyStkNEpochComb{} epoch-windows and
\SyStkHours\,h, the flux-density limit improves on each star's best single epoch by a
median factor \SyStkGainMed{} and by \SyStkGainMax{} at most.
\SxStkStarOutside{} further stars stack too, all of them control stars beyond
40\,pc, and are excluded from those figures; over all \StkNStar{} stacked
stars, whose stacks are mostly shallow two-epoch ones, the median gain is
\StkLimitGainMed. Every
stacked limit is measured by injection into the stack itself and never read
off a stacked spectrum as a threshold.

\emph{A fixed stellar-frame frequency is not a fixed observed frequency, so
the epochs are registered before they are added.} The barycentric term alone
swings by a median \StkVcorrSwingMed\,km\,s$^{-1}$ between the epochs of one
star and by as much as \StkVcorrSwingMax{} -- many channels at the
\StkRefChanKHz\,kHz Class~A width -- so epochs added as observed would
dilute. Each epoch is transformed to the stellar rest frame,
divided by its own primary-beam response and combined by inverse variance
over the channels of common stellar-frame coverage, keyed on the star's
catalogued identity and not the directory the search ran in. Spectra with
pathological archived visibility weights are excluded first
(\StkBadWeight{} of \StkNProd, which would otherwise dominate the sum). The transform is verified on a real line. Over \StkRegNEpoch{} epochs
spanning \StkRegSpanD\,d, HD~285968's molecular line collapses from a
\StkRegTopoChan-channel topocentric spread to \StkRegStellarChan{}
channels.

The criterion is a stacked
$Z\ge\StkZmin$ above every stacked control maximum, no epoch supplying more
than \StkDomMax{} of the weighted sum, and at least \StkJkMin{} of the signal
surviving removal of the strongest epoch. Controls are stacked identically
and every clause evaluated at them, measuring the whole test's
false-alarm rate at \StkCtrlFap{} per cent against a \StkFapCeil{} per cent
ceiling.

\emph{The gain is $\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}$ and not $\sqrt{N}$}, where
$N_{\rm eff}=\sigma_{\min}^{2}\sum_e\sigma_e^{-2}$ is the equivalent number of
equal-depth epochs and the median $N_{\rm eff}/N$ is \StkNeffFrac. A
stack realises $\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}$ divided by the measured noise of its
own stacked spectrum, and that noise follows the inverse-variance prediction
to \StkGainVsNeff{} in the median, with a per-stack scatter of
\StkMadZScatterPct{} per cent and nine stacks in ten between \SzNoiseLo{} and
\SzNoiseHi. Against $\sqrt{N}$ the realised gain is \StkGainVsN, the
shortfall being unequal epoch depth, so an epoch count alone misleads.
\StkWorstStar{} stacks \StkWorstN{} epochs for a gain of only
\StkWorstGain. Its $N_{\rm eff}$ is \StkWorstNeff, so
$\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}$ is \SzWorstSqrtNeff, and its stacked spectrum came out
\SzWorstNoise{} times as noisy as predicted.

\emph{The deepest stacked limit is Proxima Centauri's, and it is a Class~B
limit.} Proxima is covered by \StkProxN{} blocks over \StkProxHours\,h
spanning \StkProxSpanYr\,yr, all of them coarse \ChanBModeMHz\,MHz windows,
so the stack constrains an unresolved spectral excess and discriminates no
drift. \SxProxEbCensus{} of those blocks belong to the primary census,
\SzProxEbSearched{} were searched afterwards from the same public archive and
\SzProxEbHold{} come from the reserved hold-out, all observed over the census
blocks' own years. Stacking the census blocks alone, the set every other limit here uses,
gives $\mathrm{EIRP}_{90}=\SzProxEirpCensus$\,W. Adding the rest of the
archive deepens that by \SzProxDeepen{} to $\StkProxEirpCal$\,W. The best
single epoch, on the same nominal injection-calibrated convention, gives
$\StkProxEirpSingle$\,W. Its $N_{\rm eff}$ is only \StkProxNeff, so
$\sqrt{N_{\rm eff}}=\SxProxSqrtNeff$ of the measured \StkProxGain{} is what
combining the epochs buys, the rest being this spectrum's own noise.
The limit holds for an emitter steady in the stellar frame to within one
\ChanBModeMHz\,MHz channel, which is \StkProxTolKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$ peak to
peak, and \PxPlanet{} does not satisfy it. At \PxPorbD\,d about a star of
\PxMassMsun\,M$_\odot$ -- the archive's default parameter set
\citep{NEA2013}, for the planet of \citet{AngladaEscude2016} and a host mass
agreeing with \citet{Kervella2017} -- the planet moves at
\PxVorbKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$, so a
transmitter on it swings over \PxPPKms$\sin i$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, some
\PxNChanWalk{} channels at $\sin i=1$. The stack cancels such a
carrier instead of building it unless the orbit is seen nearly face-on,
$\sin i\le\PxSiniMax$ or $i\le\PxIncMaxDeg^\circ$, which is \PxIsoPct{} per
cent of randomly oriented orbits. The \PxNEpoch{} epochs sample
\PxFracSampled{} per cent of that excursion at the orbital phase least
favourable to the statement, so the shortfall is in these observations and
not in an idealised orbit. Within a single block the same transmitter gains
at most \PxBlockDvKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$, \PxBlockPct{} per cent of one channel,
so the single-epoch limit of $\PxEirpSingle$\,W is the one that applies to
it -- \PxEirpRatio{} times shallower than the stack, and the deepest limit
this survey places on the nearest plausible transmitter site. It also
requires no inter-epoch drift above
\StkProxDriftHzS\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$, which is that same \ChanBModeMHz\,MHz channel
crossed once over the span of the epochs. At \StkProxDistPc\,pc that depth is mostly
geometry; the gain is what stacking bought.

\emph{\StkNSurvive{} groups pass the full criterion and both are known
lines.} They are both $\beta$~Pictoris carbon monoxide, in the star's rest
frame and on the sign convention of Appendix~\ref{app:linemask}: CO
$J=1\!-\!0$ stacks \StkBpCoOneOff\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from the transition over
\StkBpCoOneN{} epochs, at $Z=\StkBpCoOneZ$, and CO $J=2\!-\!1$
\StkBpCoTwoOff\,km\,s$^{-1}$ from it at $Z=\StkBpCoTwoZ$ -- both inside the
\BpicDvCoHi\,km\,s$^{-1}$ of the single-epoch crossings of the same star
(\S\ref{sec:bpicval}). The two agree to \StkBpCrossKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$, or
\StkBpCrossChan{} of a CO(1--0) channel: the astrophysical positive control,
passing in the stack as well. The
survey's own $\pm\StkAttrKms$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ mask leaves
\StkUnattrClause. \StkPersistLineStar{} reaches
$Z=\StkPersistLineZ$ on the line used above as the registration standard and is
rejected by its own controls at \StkPersistLineCtrl: the emission is extended,
as a cloud is and a transmitter is not.

Every stacked limit above holds only for a carrier that stays inside one
channel. At CO(2--1) one \StkRefChanKHz\,kHz channel spans
\StkTolFineKms\,km\,s$^{-1}$, the whole excursion a stacked carrier is
allowed, peak to peak. Take a transmitter on a short-period giant planet, its line-of-sight
velocity swinging by $\pm\StkReflexAmpKms$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. It crosses
\SzReflexPPChanFine{} of those channels peak to peak and de-registers
completely, against \SzReflexPPChanCoarse{} of a \ChanBModeMHz\,MHz window:
the coarse stacks buy reflex tolerance with bandwidth. Drift divides the same way, a
stack in \StkRefChanKHz\,kHz channels being confined to
\StkDriftTolHzS\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$ over the median inter-epoch span of
\StkSpanMedD\,d. No crossing of \S\ref{sec:etacrv} drifts that slowly. The slowest measured
is \SttDriftMinHzS\,Hz\,s$^{-1}$, which leaves one channel in
\SttDriftMinMin{} minutes, and in \SttNIncDisc{} of \NUnattributed{} cases
the stack covering a crossing contains its discovery block. Toward the
\SttNHostStar{} stars carrying them the stack is a deeper limit on a
non-drifting carrier, not a test of these events. Each
stacked limit therefore constrains only emitters stable in the stellar frame to
within its own channel; the single-epoch limits of \S\ref{sec:excludes} apply
generally.
