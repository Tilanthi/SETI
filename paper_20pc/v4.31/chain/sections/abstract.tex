\begin{abstract}
Do nearby stars show narrowband, drifting artificial radio carriers in the
ALMA archive, and how deep and complete can such a search be? We searched
\NWinA{} fine-channel windows toward \NSysClassA{} systems within 40\,pc,
covering \CvUnionA\,GHz in \CovNIslands{} separate intervals between
\SurvFreqLoA{} and \SurvFreqHiA\,GHz, comparing drift-corrected stellar spectra
with nearby control positions; \NWinB{} coarser windows constrain
only unresolved emission. Of \CsNCross{} windows reaching
$T_\star\geq\StageNullTrig$, \MkNCoinc{} coincide with catalogued molecular
lines, \LnAttrSupp{} of them with further evidence of line emission. Given the
adopted line mask and background model, the other
\CeObs{} are consistent with chance, against \CeExp{} expected
from control positions ($P=\CeProbNull$), which allows up to \CeAddLim{} further crossings. For a
carrier narrower than one channel, ninety per cent single-epoch recovery
needs an equivalent isotropic radiated power with a median, over systems, of
$\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W, \AroMedMult{} times the Arecibo planetary
radar's \citep{EarthDetectingEarth2025}, and at best
$\EirpNinetySysLoA$\,W, still \AroDeepMult{} times Arecibo's; \NSysEfifteen{}
of \NSysALimSum{} systems reach $\CovEirpRefTex$\,W. Confirmation needs a
repeat at a covered frequency, which \ZcNoRepSys{} systems lack, so coverage,
more than depth, limits the search. No carrier is detected or confirmed: a
non-detection, not an exclusion.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Technosignatures, Search for extraterrestrial intelligence, Submillimeter astronomy, Radio spectroscopy, Debris disks}
