\begin{abstract}
Do nearby stars show artificial narrowband carriers? We searched \NWinA{} fine-channel ALMA archival windows toward
\NSysClassA{} systems within 40\,pc, covering \CvUnionA\,GHz in
\CovNIslands{} separate intervals between \SurvFreqLoA{} and
\SurvFreqHiA\,GHz; \NWinB{} coarser windows constrain only unresolved
emission. Of \CsNCross{} threshold crossings, \MkNCoinc{}
coincide with catalogued lines, \LnAttrSupp{} of them with further
evidence of line emission. Given the adopted mask and background, the
other \CeObs{} are consistent with chance (\CeExp{} expected from control
positions, $P=\CeProbNull$). No carrier is detected or confirmed: for the
crossings, a non-detection, not an exclusion. Coverage, more than depth,
limits the search: \ZcNoRepNoSep{} systems were never observed in two separated
sessions and \ZcNoRepNoFreqWord{} more only at different frequencies. For a carrier narrower than one channel, ninety per cent
single-epoch trigger completeness, not the chance of confirming a signal,
needs a median equivalent isotropic radiated power of
$\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W, about \AroMedMult{} times the Arecibo planetary radar's. Continuous
carriers above each system's limit, outside the mask and within
the searched drift, are excluded at the single-epoch trigger level toward these
systems and frequencies. Intermittent or broader signals, faster
drifts, beams aimed elsewhere, other frequencies and unobserved stars
remain open.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Technosignatures, Search for extraterrestrial intelligence, Submillimeter astronomy, Radio spectroscopy, Debris disks}
