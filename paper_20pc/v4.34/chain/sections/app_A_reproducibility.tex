\section{Spectral response, retention and the survey ledger}
\label{app:conventions}

\subsection{The response of a channel to a carrier}

A carrier seldom falls at the centre of a channel. ALMA's online Hanning
smoothing \citep{ALMAHandbook} leaves \HanBest{} of an unresolved carrier's
flux in the peak channel at a channel centre and \HanWorst{} on a boundary,
and this loss enters the limits once, through the injections. Each synthetic
carrier is deposited at a sub-channel phase drawn afresh and recovered
through the unmodified trigger, so the measured completeness already
contains it. The Class~A factor separates into two parts,
$\EirpNinetyMultA\simeq\RpRespFac\times\RpMarginNinety$. The first is
$1/\InjDepMean$, from the phase-averaged share of a carrier's flux that the
$0.25/0.5/0.25$ kernel places in the peak channel. The second is the margin
that a peak channel needs above the nominal trigger for nine carriers in ten
to return. $P_{\rm trig}$ and $S_{\min}$ therefore stay nominal, comparable
with other programmes' thresholds, and the sensitivity carries the
correction,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EIRP}_{90}=\frac{\kappa\,\rho_N}{R_{\rm px}\,\eta_{\rm smear}}\,
4\pi d^{2}S_{\min}\,\Delta\nu,\qquad S_{\min}=\frac{5\sigma}{A},
\label{eq:eirp90}
\end{equation}
where $4\pi d^{2}S_{\min}\Delta\nu$ is $P_{\rm trig}$, $\kappa$ is the class
factor, $\rho_N$ the response ratio of the window's averaging mode, and $A$, $R_{\rm px}$ and $\eta_{\rm smear}$ are the
primary-beam, parallax and smearing retentions. The noise $\sigma$ is that of the
time-stacked spectrum at the star, in each native channel. In every
integration and channel it is the median absolute deviation over the control
positions, scaled to a Gaussian standard deviation, and the integrations are
then combined by inverse variance (\texttt{\LrSigFile}, lines
\LrSigLineMad--\LrSigLineSig{} and \LrSigLineComb).

The kernel is an approximation. A delivered channel is the mean of $N$
Hann-windowed raw channels ($N=1$ in \SvNWinNOne{} Class~A windows, online
averaging in \SvNWinNAvg), so carriers were also injected with each window's
own response, carrier for carrier against the kernel, into \IjNWin{} windows
of every averaging mode. The ratio $\rho_N$ of the two ninety-per-cent points
is $\IjRatioNOne$, $\IjRatioNTwo$ and $\IjRatioNFour$ at $N=1$, 2 and 4 in
Class~A, and $\IjRatioBNOne$ and $\IjRatioBNTwo$ at $N=1$ and 2 in Class~B.
Smearing within one integration, which the injections do not model, is
divided out as $\eta_{\rm smear}$, the mean channel response over the sweep,
and is never worse than $\times\SmcFacWorst$.

\subsection{Primary-beam and positional retention}
\label{app:pbresp}

The response applied, $A$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:eirp90}, is a Gaussian primary beam
of the documented full width $\RingPbCoef\,\lambda/D$ \citep{ALMAHandbook}
at each window's own dish diameter. Archival fields are pointed at catalogue
J2000 positions, so these nearby, fast-moving stars sit off axis by a median
of \PbOffMed$''$ and at most \BmOffMax$''$. At the furthest census offset the
Gaussian agrees with the blocked-aperture response of \textsc{casa}
\citep{CASA2022} to \BmCensusWorstPct{} per cent. The statistic, ranks and
attribution are ratios within one window, so $A$ enters only $S_{\min}$.
$R_{\rm px}$ is the modelled share of a carrier's amplitude that survives the
annual parallax where the extraction omitted it, and the model errs on the
safe side, since carriers injected at the full apparent position of the
\SensNReextWin{} furthest-out windows retain \SensPxSmallMeas{} and
\SensPxLargeMeas{} where it predicts \SensPxSmallPred{} and
\SensPxLargePred. $\epsilon$\,Eridani has no parallax record, and its limit
may be optimistic by up to \BmEpsPxBoundPct{} per cent.

Propagating a position also assumes straight-line motion. Of the
\OmNStar{} searched positions, the \OmNCensusStar{} census stars and
\OmXStar, \OmNComponent{} have a companion within
\OmRadius\,arcsec \citep{wds,orb6}. The displacement, from a visual orbit,
from the separation with masses from \citet{pecaut2013}, or from the
proper-motion anomaly \citep{kervella2022}, moves no system's limit by more
than \OmSysMovedMaxPct{} per cent. \OmFlagStars{} are different. Their
astrometric solutions are disturbed, yet they have no known companion and no
Hipparcos epoch, so the positional loss in their \OmNWinFlag{} windows has no
bound and their limits hold only at the adopted positions. They are marked
in Table~\ref{tab:persys} and left out of the median, the count at
$10^{15}$\,W and Fig.~\ref{fig:classasens} (\S\ref{sec:pninetysel}).

\subsection{The survey ledger}

Every headline count is read from Table~\ref{tab:blockledger}. The data
archive repeats the table row for row in \texttt{catalogues/\allowbreak
block\_ledger.csv}, and \texttt{catalogues/\allowbreak count\_accounting.csv}
itemises each of its rows under the same number and says which rows it is
the sum of. The planet counts are
from the \SfHostCatalogue{} \SfHostTable{} table \citep{NEA2013}, accessed
\SfHostDate, and Table~\ref{tab:projcodes} lists the ALMA proposals the
searched blocks belong to. 

Two counts of reprocessed blocks appear in the paper, and they are not of
the same blocks. The recalibration with ALMA's final flags, described in
\S\ref{sec:method}, was planned on the blocks that then set a quoted limit or held a crossing.
The fresh extraction with the final extractor was planned later, on the
blocks that held a crossing, a best limit or a statistic close to the
threshold in any set, as the Data availability statement describes. Table~\ref{tab:recalrecon} sets
the two side by side, block by block, and adds every block that holds a
crossing or a system's best limit now.
