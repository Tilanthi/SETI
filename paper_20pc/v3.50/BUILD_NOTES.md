## 3. Control-ring geometry (brief §2.2, referees A2 and B-M3)

The geometry was never in the frozen products, only in the code. It is
deterministic, so `v342_calc.py` reconstructs it exactly rather than describing
it: `seti_extract_generic.py::probe_positions(pb_arcsec, seed=20260825)`, whose
offsets the caller adds to the star's own direction cosines
(`lam = l_star + dl`). Hence:

* the annulus is **concentric with the star**, not with the field phase centre;
* it is an **annulus**, 512 positions uniform in area
  (`r = sqrt(U(r_in², r_out²))`) and uniform in azimuth, seed `20260825`;
* radii `0.14–0.78 θ_PB` with `θ_PB = 1.22 λ / 12 m` at each window's own
  median frequency: θ_PB 12.7–69.5″, r_in 1.8–9.7″, r_out 9.9–54.2″;
* a separate inner check-ring reaches `0.06 θ_PB`.

**Two independent checks that this is the layout that actually ran**, because a
reconstruction is a claim and not a record:

1. the inner check-ring the reconstruction generates has exactly **135**
   positions, which is the `n_src` the retired region-max statistic maximised
   over — a number that appears in the manuscript and was never used to build
   the reconstruction;
2. in windows filled by extended CO the released control statistics **fall with
   the reconstructed radius**: Spearman ρ = −0.49 at p ≈ 7×10⁻³³ for the
   HD 48370 Band 6 window and ρ = −0.30 at p ≈ 7×10⁻¹² for its ¹³CO window,
   against a median ρ = **0.009** over the 304 windows containing no crossing.
   A wrong position-to-index mapping could not produce that.

Three claims in v3.41 were **wrong** and are corrected: the controls do not lie
"on a single ring of fixed angular radius"; they are not "evenly spaced in
azimuth (0.70 degrees apart)"; and the primary-beam argument's stated
assumption (ring concentric with the phase centre) is the opposite of what the
code does.

The exchangeability argument is restated in the form the brief specifies and
the one the code supports: the annulus samples primary-beam response 0.95 at
0.14 θ_PB down to 0.19 at 0.78 θ_PB (area-weighted mean 0.47), so star and
controls are **not** exchangeable in flux density, but the primary-beam
correction divides signal and noise by the same `G(r)`, so the ranked statistic
is invariant under it and **exchangeability holds in S/N**. A nuance neither
referee raised: the annulus is defined in units of the pipeline's own
`1.22 λ/D`, whereas ALMA's actual 12 m primary-beam FWHM is nearer `1.13 λ/D`,
so the *physical* response over the same radii is 0.94 → 0.14. Both are given.

By-product: the reconstruction **locates the HD 48370 ¹³CO peak**, which
referee B asked for. The control carrying that window's ring maximum sits
9.2″ (0.32 θ_PB) from the star, and the ten strongest controls all fall between
7.4″ and 11.1″ against a median control radius of 15.9″.

## 4. Line mask: repaired and re-applied (brief §2.3, referee A3, referee B minor)

The repaired catalogue holds **17 transitions of 11 species** at full JPL/CDMS
precision (was 15 of 9, stored rounded to 1 MHz), adding
**[C I] ³P₁–³P₀ at 492.160651 GHz** and **H30α at 231.900928 GHz**, and it is
applied in the **LSR frame** as well as the stellar frame. The worst rounding
error the frozen mask carried is 1.59 km s⁻¹ (HCO⁺(1−0)), not the 1.3 km s⁻¹
quoted in v3.41, which was the CO(1−0) value only. The eight-versus-nine
species discrepancy is reconciled in favour of the products: nine, the text was
wrong to say eight.

The mask is re-applied to the frozen crossing list as a post-hoc filter, not by
re-running the survey. Frame chains for the four flagged windows are computed
with astropy from the real EB coordinates and epochs (recovered from the
retained `*_srcspec.npz` products into `ebmeta_v342.json`); the barycentric
corrections reproduce the paper's own tabulated values to 0.07 and 0.01 km s⁻¹,
which validates the chain.

**No disposition changes**, and the paper says so explicitly:

| window | ν_cross (GHz) | stellar frame | LSR frame | disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| β Pic B3 | 115.260644 | −2.8 km/s, masked | −1.0, masked | circumstellar CO |
| β Pic B6 | 230.516128 | −3.4, masked | −1.6, masked | circumstellar CO |
| HD 48370 B6 | 230.511674 | −17.8, masked | **−23.9, now also masked** | foreground CO |
| CP−72 2713 B7 | 344.269746 | −1323, not masked | −1300, not masked | unclassified |

The LSR result for HD 48370 is the one that moves: a Galactic foreground
feature is now caught in the frame its attribution always implied.

Across all 20 crossing windows the repaired catalogue moves the nearest
transition in exactly one case, HD 48370's Band 8 crossing at 491.949074 GHz,
whose nearest entry changes from CO(4−3) 20 098 km s⁻¹ away to [C I] at
−129 km s⁻¹. That is **still outside** the ±50 km s⁻¹ tube, so adding [C I]
does *not* dispose of that window, and the manuscript's previous claim that
"foreground [C I] is the obvious reading" is softened accordingly. It was never
a flag: its own control ensemble exceeds it.

The two new transitions cost a further 0.24 GHz of unique searched frequency
(0.3 per cent).

**Open item #2 from v3.41's BUILD_NOTES is closed, not fixed:** the two
mask-loss computations that "disagree by ~7×" are not in conflict. 0.64 GHz is
the loss over the 15 principal transitions; 4.96 GHz is the loss over all 276
JPL-catalogued transitions of the same species. Different denominators,
different quantities, both correct.

## 5. A defect neither referee raised

**The CP−72 2713 re-observation frequency in v3.41 was the window centre, not
the feature.** The manuscript twice named 345.1152 GHz — once as the frequency
of a targeted re-observation, once as the frequency at which atmospheric
structure was discussed. The crossing is at **344.269746 GHz**, 845 MHz lower.
Verified three ways: the frozen export's own `line_off` arithmetic; the
pipeline's recorded `star_peak_freq_GHz`; and the window's channel grid
(channel 35 of 3543, 17 MHz above the lower band edge). A re-observation
centred on the published number would have missed the feature.

The band-edge proximity is itself worth having: the manuscript's own §4.1 warns
that a global noise scale is least reliable near a bandpass edge. The local
recomputation there gives 0.97 for this window, so the excess is if anything
understated, and the disposition does not move.

`pipeline_peakfreq_v342.json` now carries the crossing frequency, channel,
drift and integration count for all 431 windows, harvested from the pipeline's
own `*_result.json`. It adds no new measurement: every `star_peak_snr` in it
reproduces the frozen export's value, all 431 matched (the three that initially
did not are the "γ Lupi" → HD 139664 relabelling of the paper's own item (iv),
which is a pleasing confirmation of that story).

## ★ Traps met in this cycle

1. **Appendix prose cuts do not move the page count.** Measured twice: a
   589-character appendix cut left the total at exactly 29.49 pages, and a
   230-character one changed nothing. The appendix region is
   float-quantised. Main-text cuts propagate until the main region is
   float-packed too, after which they stop as well.
2. **What closed the last page was float geometry, not prose.** Roughly
   9,000 characters of prose cuts moved the document from 30 pages to 30
   pages. Reducing `fig:funnel` from 2.05 to 1.78 inches and two appendix
   figures by about 12 per cent took it to 29 in one step.
3. **Moving a float between regions helps even when it saves nothing.**
   Moving `tab:configdist` from the appendix into §4.4 left the content
   total unchanged and filled 0.22 pages of main-region slack.
4. **A shorter abstract can create a new gate failure.** Cutting 166
   characters left `\enlargethispage{-\baselineskip}` on page 1
   overshooting by 0.31 pt, reported as an overfull vbox. Now
   `-2\baselineskip`.
5. **`.fuzzysub.py` rewraps a replacement into a single paragraph**, so
   every `\subsection*` heading and paragraph break inside a multi-paragraph
   replacement is silently destroyed. Caught on the first build of the new
   appendix. Later stages write paragraph breaks as `@@PARA@@` markers and
   restore them in a post-pass.
6. **A matplotlib axis label can be silently clipped by the figure's own
   bbox.** Two attempts at the referee A8 axis warning lost their tail
   ("(W)", then "old,") with no warning from matplotlib and no visible
   damage at page scale. Verify a figure's text by extracting it back out
   of the figure PDF, not by eye.
