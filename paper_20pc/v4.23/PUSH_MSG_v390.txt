Two referee reports. Both treat the same question as a condition for
acceptance: which statistic is primary, and can it be justified?

1. REFEREE 2 WAS RIGHT: THE PAPER CONTRADICTED ITSELF.
   S4.2 said the radius-corrected statistic was primary; Appendix G.10.2
   said in terms that it was not. v3.89 changed the front of the paper and
   left the back stating the opposite. Separately, the conclusions printed
   the FROZEN CO count (9) beside the CORRECTED totals (10 stage-1, 2
   unattributed), so 9 + 2 = 11 did not equal 10. Both fixed. New gate
   consistency_v390.py now asserts that both count identities close, that
   the text does not simultaneously adopt and reject the corrected
   statistic, and that the abstract's counts come from whichever statistic
   is declared primary. Nothing in gate.sh could see any of this before.

2. REFEREE 1'S CONDITION: THE CORRECTION FAILS OUT OF SAMPLE.
   New radval_v390.py freezes the correction and applies it unmodified to
   778 windows it played no part in building -- the pre-registered hold-out
   and the prospective extension -- at a level fixed in advance (p >= 0.05).

        stellar rank, out of sample   median      D        p
        uncorrected                    0.409    0.105   <1e-4
        radius-corrected               0.407    0.097   <1e-4

   The departure from uniformity is essentially undiminished and the median
   moves FURTHER FROM 0.5, not toward it. Holds in each sample separately.

   Methodological note: the pseudo-star test does NOT discriminate.
   Probe-against-probe ranks are near-uniform with or without the
   correction (uncorrected p = 0.16 pooled) because every probe is drawn
   from the same radial distribution. The star is at the phase centre,
   inside the annulus, so only the STELLAR rank tests the asymmetry the
   correction exists to repair. Both are reported; the verdict rests on the
   discriminating one.

3. WHAT FOLLOWS. Per referee 1's own instruction, the corrected statistic
   is therefore not primary. The frozen screen returns as the primary
   candidate-generation device -- it at least was fixed in advance --
   described consistently as a prioritisation and never as a significance
   test, and every disposition now rests on physical evidence: visibility
   localisation, stellar-frame line coincidence, and recurrence. Counts are
   consistent throughout at 13 flagged / 9 CO / 4 unattributed, with the
   corrected 10 / 8 / 2 as a documented robustness comparison. Appendix
   G.10.2's position, which v3.89 had overridden, was correct; its
   justification is now empirical rather than procedural.

   v3.89 over-corrected on the previous round's instruction. The validation
   this round's referee 1 demanded has settled it.
