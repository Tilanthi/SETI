\begin{abstract}
Do nearby stars show artificial narrowband carriers? We searched \NWinA{}
fine-channel ALMA windows toward \NSysClassA{} systems within 40\,pc, covering
\CvUnionA\,GHz in \CovNIslands{} separate intervals, and \NWinB{} coarser windows
for unresolved emission. Of \CsNCross{} threshold crossings, \MkNCoinc{}
coincide with catalogued lines (\LnAttrSupp{} also showing line emission).
Given the adopted mask, counting rule and background, the other \CeObs{} are consistent with
chance (\CeExp{} expected from control positions, $P=\CeProbNull$). No carrier
is detected or confirmed: for the crossings, a non-detection, not an exclusion.
Confirmation is limited by repeat coverage (\ZcNoRepNoSep{} systems lack two
separated sessions, \ZcNoRepNoFreqWord{} more a shared frequency), by repeat depth
and, for a drifting carrier, by an uncertain repeat frequency. At a system's
limit, a continuous carrier narrower than one channel, outside the mask and
within the searched drift, would have produced a crossing in nine trials in ten
in its best window. Though corrected for decorrelation, the
limits remain optimistic by any residual. Their median over the \NSysALimSum{}
systems with a bounded limit is $\EirpNinetySysMedA$\,W of equivalent isotropic
power, about \AroMedMult{} times Arecibo's planetary radar. Drifting intermittent
or broader signals, faster drifts, beams aimed elsewhere, other frequencies and
unobserved stars remain open.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Technosignatures, Search for extraterrestrial intelligence, Submillimeter astronomy, Radio spectroscopy, Debris disks}
